# Liechtenstein cross-border tax preparation v0.3

Independent source/editorial review evidence and correction status are in the [dated whole-pack review record](../../../docs/research/2026-10-24-liechtenstein-pack-review.md). Apply its exact revision and scope limits. Named Liechtenstein accountant review remains open.

## Scope and order of work

This 2026 source-cited draft prepares the domestic-law and relief workpaper. Determine person/entity, residence and source, the relevant income/asset, domestic tax, treaty allocation and then the actual filing/claim route. Do not start with a presumed zero withholding rate, 183-day exemption or EEA entitlement. The [Tax Act][law] and [Tax Ordinance][ordinance] sources below were read in their current 1 July 2026 consolidations; individual treaty/protocol and complete within-year amendment review remain open.

## Residence and PE screens

| Question | Domestic rule to investigate | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Individual domicile | Article 2(1)(b): place of stay with intention of permanent remaining | Homes, occupancy, intention/conduct, family/work records; registration alone is insufficient |
| Habitual abode | Article 2(1)(c): nontemporary stay; a connected stay **over six months** counts from its beginning, ignoring short interruptions | Complete stay chronology and purpose; the statute has separate school/institution and up-to-twelve-month cure/holiday exceptions |
| Individual liability dates | Articles 6–7: domicile/habitual-abode resident gate, domestic wealth/income for nonresidents, and actual start/end events | Arrival/departure, assets, source-income dates, and any dual-residence/treaty analysis |
| Legal-entity residence | Article 44: domestic seat **or** actual management; article 2 defines management centre | Governing documents, substantive decision-making, people and management records |
| Domestic PE | Article 2(1)(a): fixed place and listed examples; construction/installation **exceeding six months**, plus dependent-agent and insurer provisions | Actual activity/site/project duration, authority, premises, staff, contracts and insurance premium facts |

These are domestic screens, not a treaty residence tie-breaker or treaty PE conclusion. A treaty can alter the taxing right. Do not turn “over six months” into a generic 183-day domestic test or make a PE solely from a tax/VAT registration. Preserve all potentially applicable states, treaty dates and actual activity facts. [SteG, articles 2, 6–7 and 44–46][law]

## Nonresident category before rate

Article 6(4)–(5) identifies domestic property/PE wealth and income categories, employment/replacement income, specified board functions, pension/freedom-of-movement benefits and deemed yield. Its employment-source provision also addresses certain treaty-assigned remote/international transport work. Do not assume all work physically abroad is automatically outside Liechtenstein source taxation. Treaty and actual workday facts must be resolved. [SteG, article 6][law]

Article 23 separates:

- Certain property/business/deemed-yield categories: simplified assessment at the statutory 4% plus the specified municipality surcharge, subject to the ordinary-assessment routes and linked deductions.
- Specified wages/board/pension categories: source tax can discharge liability, subject to ordinary assessment.
- Ordinary assessment triggers/applications: category-specific CHF 211,400 tests, the exclusive-treaty-taxing-right employment rule and the application route below the stated limit. Do not apply one gross threshold to all categories. Ordinary assessment also has global-rate/expense-allocation rules and different surcharge sources.

This Guide does not compute those specialised nonresident assessments or the annual Finance Act surcharge in article 23(5)(b). Refer the exact category to the current return/notice and treaty before calculating. [SteG, article 23][law]

## Source withholding and treaty relief

Residents' wages also have withholding. Use [payroll](li-payroll-social.md) for the current ordinary wage schedule, not a final individual-tax rate. SteG article 25 separately provides 12% for its specified board/pension categories. Article 63a provides 12% on the specified nonresident **corporate** board-function income, with its own ordinary-assessment application. A blanket “no withholding” statement about investment distributions does not resolve a board, pension or employment payment. [SteG, articles 24–25 and 63a][law]

For an individual article-24 payment, article 26 provides authority confirmation on application where the treaty gives exclusive taxing rights abroad or caps the domestic rate. Excess treaty withholding has an application/refund route, with its **two-year period from the due date of the source-withholding amounts**. Collect the authority confirmation and use it within its actual conditions; do not substitute a private residence assertion. Payer liability/certification under article 27 remains separate. The [February 2026 wage-tax bulletin and January treaty annex][wage] are an administrative screen, not proof that a named employee meets commuter, workday, employer, cost-bearing or public-service conditions.

For a concrete treaty, retain its signed text, protocols, entry into force/application dates, covered person/tax/income articles, residence, permanent establishment/employment conditions and the actual claim evidence. The [authority treaty collection][treaties] is the starting register; this draft has not independently applied every treaty or established a rate for an unnamed country.

## Foreign relief: exemption is different from a credit

SteG article 22 distinguishes treaty/reciprocity exemption (with the stated progression cross-reference) from credit relief against the domestic state/municipality tax attributable to the relevant wealth/income. It does not grant an unrestricted deduction of every foreign payment. [SteG, article 22][law]

For the nonbusiness individual credit route, SteV articles 18a–18c require the treaty/reciprocity and domestic-tax gates. They exclude expenditure-tax cases and cases where the total foreign taxes do not exceed CHF 50; self-employment uses separate articles 36a–36c. Article 18b requires exhausting source-state tax reduction opportunities and limits credit to domestic tax on the relevant income. For wealth income, build the limit from the affected assets, allocated debt, Sollertrag, allocated deductions and actual domestic average rate. Other income uses its net/allocated-deduction base and actual average rate. Do not use gross foreign cash receipts as the credit ceiling.

Credit is application-only, no earlier than the end of the relevant income year, with a three-year claim expiry after that year; it is applied to that income year. The worked excess below is an uncredited amount, not an established carryforward/refund right. Corporate relief and business-income credit need separate provisions. [SteV, articles 18a–18c][ordinance]

## Corporate exit and corresponding adjustments

SteG article 51 treats exclusion/restriction of domestic taxing rights over asset disposal/use as an arm's-length disposal/use event. The stated foreign-PE transfer/departure/seat-transfer deferral runs interest-free until actual realisation only with secured enforcement and annual proof. Subsequent value reductions have their own cap and no-foreign-duplication evidence. This is not an automatic tax-free move or a universal EU instalment election. Reconcile market/book values, deduction/loss interactions, retained domestic rights and the actual security/evidence before assessing tax or deferral. [SteG, article 51][law]

SteG article 63 separately addresses treaty/reciprocity relief and specified corresponding TP adjustments. For final assessments, article 124a's qualifying foreign-adjustment route has a 90-day period after foreign finality and an outer ten-year period after the relevant tax year. Establish the exact qualifying rule and dates; this is not a general reopening right for any foreign notice. Refer treaty disputes/MAP, valuations and contested claims. [SteG, articles 63 and 124a][law]

## Filing, payments and sanctions

Maintain a separate calendar for the domestic return, source remittance, foreign-credit application, treaty-refund application, foreign finality and annual deferral evidence. The different periods above cannot be substituted for each other. SteG article 94 requires filing notwithstanding absence of a personal form; articles 113–114 govern maturity/payment and special departure events. A deferral must be established under its own rule, rather than inferred from a return extension. Article 135's procedural fine concerns its post-reminder intentional/negligent breach; do not estimate a penalty from its maximum. Current individual/foreign filing dates, treaty forms, complete sanctions and live execution remain open. [SteG, cited articles][law]

## Worked controls

### Assumed eligible nonbusiness foreign-credit ceiling

Assume a resident individual with all article 18a eligibility gates, no excluded/special category, exhausted source relief, CHF 1,600 qualifying foreign tax and a separately established CHF 1,200 domestic limit under article 18b. Credit is CHF 1,200. The CHF 400 excess is not treated as an automatic carryforward or refund.

```oa-check
name: Liechtenstein assumed eligible foreign tax credit ceiling
inputs:
  established_qualifying_foreign_tax: 1600
  established_domestic_credit_limit: 1200
steps:
  available_credit: min(established_qualifying_foreign_tax, established_domestic_credit_limit)
  uncredited_foreign_tax: established_qualifying_foreign_tax - available_credit
expected:
  available_credit: 1200
  uncredited_foreign_tax: 400
```

### Specified nonresident individual board payment

Assume a CHF 10,000 gross payment within article 24(2)(b), no treaty reduction/authority exemption and no other issue. Article 25(3)'s 12% gives CHF 1,200 withheld and CHF 8,800 paid before any separately applicable deductions. An ordinary employment wage does not inherit this rate.

```oa-check
name: Liechtenstein assumed specified nonresident board withholding
inputs:
  established_gross_board_payment: 10000
  established_statutory_rate: 0.12
steps:
  source_tax: established_gross_board_payment * established_statutory_rate
  cash_after_source_tax_only: established_gross_board_payment - source_tax
expected:
  source_tax: 1200
  cash_after_source_tax_only: 8800
```

### Corporate asset exit workpaper

Assume article 51 applies to one asset with CHF 100,000 established arm's-length value and CHF 60,000 tax carrying value. The assumed taxable uplift is CHF 40,000; at 12.5%, the isolated tax effect is CHF 5,000 before equity/loss/other interactions. Whether/when it is paid or deferred must be decided separately from the valuation arithmetic.

```oa-check
name: Liechtenstein assumed corporate asset exit uplift
inputs:
  established_arm_length_value: 100000
  established_tax_carrying_value: 60000
  ordinary_corporate_rate: 0.125
steps:
  assumed_taxable_uplift: established_arm_length_value - established_tax_carrying_value
  isolated_tax_before_other_rules: assumed_taxable_uplift * ordinary_corporate_rate
expected:
  assumed_taxable_uplift: 40000
  isolated_tax_before_other_rules: 5000
```
