OLD:**Self-employed (SZČO).** From 1 January 2026 a person aged 18 or over is an SZČO for social insurance if they hold a licence to do business or, by an honest declaration, carry on self-employed work that needs no licence (for example an artist or author). Income no longer decides this status (Sociálna poisťovňa, changes from 1 January 2026, item 22).
- **New entrants** (no licence in the last 60 months) become compulsorily insured from the first day of the sixth calendar month after the month the licence starts.
- **Returning entrants** (a licence ended less than 60 months ago) are insured from the day the licence starts.
- Insurance now lasts until the licence ends. The old income test at 1 July no longer ends it.
- **Transition:** to 30 June 2026 an SZČO who held a licence before 1 January 2026 stays under the old rules. Under those rules insurance started on 1 July 2025 (1 October 2025 with an extended tax deadline) if 2024 income was above EUR 8,580 (Sociálna poisťovňa, 2026 tables, note 3).

CURRENT [29, 37] **Self-employed (SZČO): status and compulsory coverage are separate.** A person aged 18 or over has SZČO status under §5 through the relevant business authorisation or, for work needing no authorisation such as an artist/author, the statutory declaration. Status alone does not establish compulsory insurance. The January 2026 SP overview's account of income-independent coverage was superseded from 1 July 2026 by [Act 122/2026, Art.I points 1–3/9 and Art.II](https://static.slov-lex.sk/pdf/SK/ZZ/2026/122/ZZ_2026_122_20260701.pdf), which amended §21 and added §293gnb. Apply the current [binding Act 461/2003, §§21/293gma/293gnb](https://static.slov-lex.sk/pdf/SK/ZZ/2003/461/ZZ_2003_461_20260901.pdf), not the superseded overview, to coverage:

- **First entry or return after more than 60 months.** Section 21(1)/(3) normally starts compulsory sickness/pension insurance on the first day of the sixth calendar month after the authorisation month. For licence-free work, count from the declared start month, but no earlier than the sixth following month after SP receives the declaration. A first authorisation on 15 January 2026 therefore starts coverage 1 July 2026; one on 15 February starts 1 August. Exactly 60 months since the last activity ended belongs to the returning branch below, not the exception for a gap longer than 60 months.
- **Return within 60 months, including exactly 60, or resumption after interruption.** Section 21(3) starts on renewed authorisation or the declared activity start, no earlier than declaration delivery; interruption resumes the day after it ends. First apply §21(4)'s exception: use the 1 July assessment, or 1 October for an extended return deadline, preceding the relevant return/resumption and the preceding calendar year's business income. Coverage does not start under this returning rule if that income does not exceed 10.5 times the adult subsistence amount at 1 January of the income year, including the specified low-income non-filer. For 2025 income assessed in 2026 that threshold is EUR 2876.90: EUR 2876.90 does not exceed it; EUR 2876.91 does. Non-filing because there was no SZČO activity in that prior year is a different fact and does not itself qualify for this low-income exception. SP's current worked case of activity in January–June 2026, none in 2025, then a return 1 August 2026 starts coverage on that August return.
- **Annual starts and ends.** Under §21(5), coverage also starts 1 July following a year above the 10.5-times income threshold, or 1 October with an extended filing deadline. A July start also applies where the person was not SZČO in the prior year, except while §21(1)'s initial waiting period is still running. Conversely, coverage ends 30 June, or 30 September for the extended deadline, where prior-year income does not exceed the threshold, including the statutory low-income non-filer. Ending the authorisation/declared activity remains a separate end route under §21(2). A supplementary return or tax assessment changes coverage under §21(6) only if it affects current insurance, from the first day of the following calendar month. Compute each income year's 10.5-times threshold using its own 1 January adult subsistence amount and income-tax rounding (§21(7)); EUR 2876.90 is the 2025-income threshold, not a permanent annual amount.
- **January–June 2026 transition.** For §293gma(1)'s defined pre 2026 population, the old law applies through 30 June 2026, except where more than 60 months elapsed since the last activity ended. Under that old assessment,2024 income above EUR 8580 started insurance 1 July 2025, or 1 October 2025 with an extended deadline. This does not preserve the old EUR 8580 coverage threshold for the July 2026 assessment.
- **July/October 2026 transition.** Section 293gnb, rather than superseded §293gma(2)/(3)/(5), governs from 1 July. For the defined transition population uninsured on 30 June and still SZČO on 1 July, coverage starts 1 July if 2025 income exceeds EUR 2876.90 or there was no 2025 SZČO activity. With an extended 2025 return deadline, an uninsured person still SZČO on 1 October starts then if 2025 income exceeds that threshold. For someone insured on 30 June, a non-extended case continues after June only if 2025 income exceeds the threshold; an extended case continues July–September while still SZČO, retains the June base for those months, and continues after September if still SZČO on 1 October and 2025 income exceeds the threshold. The annual low-income end rule also applies. Do not automatically charge the special base to every low-income licence holder.

The [current SP July 2026 calculator instructions and returning/new-entrant cases](https://www.socpoist.sk/nastroje-sluzby/kalkulacky/kalkulacky-pre-szco/kalkulacka-na-informativny-vypocet-poistneho-pre-5) and [SP's 8 October 2026 assessment notice](https://www.socpoist.sk/news/v-oktobri-2026-dostane-takmer-92-tisic-zivnostnikov-szco-od-socialnej-poistovne-oznamenia-o) confirm the distinction. First decide whether and from when insurance exists; only then compute its base. For an ordinary continuing insured person assessed on 2025 income, EUR 2876.91–9144 inclusive takes the 2026 special base; EUR 9144.01 and above takes the income-derived route with its applicable floor/ceiling. Initial new-entrant coverage under §21(1) uses the special base when the income-derived route does not apply, subject to subsequent annual reassessment.

CURRENT [77, 77] **Employee social base.** Pay from the work, except income that is not taxable or is exempt from income tax, and except employer contributions to supplementary pension saving (Act 461/2003 §138(1)). The income-tax exemption does not remove qualifying registered-trainer income from the social base: that income is included before its separate trainer deduction. For an employee's qualifying legal relationship as a trainer entered in the register of natural persons in sport, §138c gives a EUR 300 monthly deduction, limited to that relationship's assessment base if lower. Employer accident and guarantee insurance use the employee base before that deduction (§138(6)); do not reduce those two employer bases by the trainer deduction. A profit share paid to an employee who holds no capital is included. The base is capped at EUR 16,764 a month; employer accident insurance runs on the full pay (§138(10)). For remuneration for 2026 periods, the employee and employer pay social insurance on assessment-base income accounted during sickness, maternity or care leave: the old employee exclusion periods are abolished. If pay is accounted after 31 December 2025 for a period before 1 January 2026 containing the then-excluded days, adjust the base under the law effective through 31 December 2025, as the authority's transition instruction requires (Sociálna poisťovňa, changes from 1 January 2026, item 26). Where someone has several jobs, social contributions run from the highest base down (§138(12)).

CURRENT [96, 96] **SZČO health advance.** 16% of the business tax base from two years earlier (2024 for 2026), taken before contributions paid, divided by the months of business in that year and by 1.486. The ordinary minimum is EUR 121.92, or EUR 60.96 with a disability, where §16(8)(a) applies. The insurer states the advance in the annual reconciliation. For an SZČO who is concurrently an employee or state-insured under §11(7), use the calculated §16(2)(b) advance without the ordinary minimum (§16(6), (8)(e)); that concurrent person need not remit an advance below EUR 3 (§16(9)). Exactly EUR 3 does not meet the below-EUR-3 exception. This concerns advances, not an assumed zero annual reconciliation.
