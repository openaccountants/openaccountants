**Rounding.** Social insurance: each assessment base and each separate contribution is rounded down to the cent (Act 461/2003 §138(14); 2026 tables, closing note). Health: bases and advances are rounded down to the cent (Act 580/2004 §13(17), §16(1)). Income tax: amounts are rounded half up to two decimals (Act 595/2003 §47).
CURRENT [77, 96] **Employee social base.** Pay from the work, except income that is not taxable or is exempt from income tax, and except employer contributions to supplementary pension saving (Act 461/2003 §138(1)). The income-tax exemption does not remove qualifying registered-trainer income from the social base: that income is included before its separate trainer deduction. For an employee's qualifying legal relationship as a trainer entered in the register of natural persons in sport, §138c gives a EUR 300 monthly deduction, limited to that relationship's assessment base if lower. Employer accident and guarantee insurance use the employee base before that deduction (§138(6)); do not reduce those two employer bases by the trainer deduction. A profit share paid to an employee who holds no capital is included. The base is capped at EUR 16,764 a month; employer accident insurance runs on the full pay (§138(10)). For remuneration for 2026 periods, the employee and employer pay social insurance on assessment-base income accounted during sickness, maternity or care leave: the old employee exclusion periods are abolished. If pay is accounted after 31 December 2025 for a period before 1 January 2026 containing the then-excluded days, adjust the base under the law effective through 31 December 2025, as the authority's transition instruction requires (Sociálna poisťovňa, changes from 1 January 2026, item 26). Where someone has several jobs, social contributions run from the highest base down (§138(12)).

**Partial-month social insurance.** The monthly figures above assume a full contribution month. If an employee's sickness, pension and unemployment insurance lasts only part of the calendar month, adjust the §138(9) ceiling for the contribution days. For a compulsorily insured SZČO, adjust the applicable income-derived or special base and its applicable minimum/maximum under §138(2), (3), (8), (9) when insurance lasts only part of the month or §140 excludes contribution days. Divide the applicable monthly base by the actual calendar days in that month, round the daily base down to cents, then multiply by the days for which contributions are payable; round each separate fund contribution down to cents. This does not create an employee social minimum or restore the employee sickness/maternity exclusion abolished in 2026. The employer base follows §138(5)/(6), with the separate §138(10) ceiling and uncapped accident insurance. For concurrent activities, §138(12) gives employee income priority over compulsory SZČO income; among employee bases, take the highest first and, if equal, the insurance that started earlier, subject to the aggregate §138(9) ceiling. For example, the authority's May 2026 SZČO case uses EUR 1200 ÷31 → EUR 38.70 daily, then ×20 payable days = EUR 774 base and EUR 256.57 across the four funds, rather than prorating the final monthly payment ([Act 461/2003 §138(12)–(14)](https://static.slov-lex.sk/static/SK/ZZ/2003/461/20260901.html#paragraf-138); [SP partial-month instructions and worked case](https://www.socpoist.sk/socialne-poistenie/platenie-poistneho/poistne-szco-dpo-po-prerusenom-poisteni), read 2026-10-27).

**Employee health base.** Employment income under the health law's own §10b and §13(1), with no ceiling (Act 580/2004; VšZP, employer page). The employer's 11% runs on the gross before any deductible amount (§13(4), §16(2)(c)). Start with the EUR 45.45 combined minimum only for a full month without excluded days. Under §16a(2), reduce that minimum proportionately for calendar days when the person is self-employed, state-insured under §11(7)/(8), disabled, declared missing, or not an employee. Count each excluded calendar day once. Compare the reduced minimum with the actual employee-plus-employer advance, after the applicable deductible amount; only a positive shortfall increases the employee share (§16a(3)/(4)). An employee with concurrent employers can elect not to apply that shortfall increase and must notify the employer within eight days of the employment/concurrency starting, and within eight days of a change. The employee also communicates excluded-day status where another body is not required to notify it; retain the prescribed written notice (§16a(5)). With EUR 200 pay, no deductible amount and no excluded days, ordinary advances are EUR 10 employee plus EUR 22 employer; the EUR 13.45 shortfall increases the employee advance to EUR 23.45. If the same person is state-insured for the entire month, the minimum reduces to zero: actual advances remain EUR 10 and EUR 22, with no minimum shortfall. State-insured status does not itself erase the contributions on employment ([Health Act §16a](https://static.slov-lex.sk/static/SK/ZZ/2004/580/20260701.html#paragraf-16a)).

**Wage tax advance.** Taxable wage = gross taxable pay less the employee's social and health contributions, less EUR 497.23 if the employee has signed the declaration (vyhlásenie) with this employer (Act 595/2003 §35(1), (4), §36(6)). Only one employer may hold that declaration. The spouse allowance and pension savings deductions wait for the annual reconciliation (§35(1)(b)). Apply the monthly bands to the taxable wage, then reduce the advance by the child tax bonus (§35(2), (5)).

**Child tax bonus in payroll.** Granted only by the employer holding the declaration. It is capped at the §33(6) share of the month's employment tax base (gross less employee contributions), and the §33(11) taper applies to that monthly base (§35(5)). If the bonus exceeds the advance, the employer pays the difference from the tax it withheld for all staff. If that is not enough, it pays from its own money and claims the balance from the tax office after the quarter (§35(7)).

**Net pay** = gross − employee social − employee health − tax advance after bonus. **Employer cost** = gross + employer social (with accident) + employer health.

**SZČO social base** (Act 461/2003 §138(2), (3), (8), (9) and (14)). First establish coverage under §21 and the 2026 transition above; income at or below EUR 9144 alone does not establish insurance. Keep the income-derived cycle separate from the calendar-year limits:
1. For the cycle beginning July 2026, or October 2026 with an extended filing deadline, test 2025 business income (not profit) against EUR 9,144: 50% of the 2024 general assessment base of EUR 18,288. Exactly EUR 9,144 takes the special-base route; income above it takes the 1.486 route. For that route, take the business tax base before social and health contributions, make the statutory adjustments for income and expenses excluded from the assessment base, divide by 12 and by 1.486, and round down to the cent. This income-derived figure runs July to June, or October to September. With an extended return deadline, July–September retains the base used through June (§138(3)).
2. Apply the minimum and maximum valid for the month being paid. During 2026 the ordinary minimum is EUR 914.40 and the ceiling EUR 16,764. Those amounts reset on 1 January using the general assessment base from two years before the contribution year; the income-derived July/October figure continues until its cycle ends. Do not carry the 2026 floor or ceiling into January 2027, or retest the cycle's income threshold merely because January arrives.
3. If the income-derived route does not apply, including a new entrant once compulsory insurance starts, use the special base: 26% of one twelfth of the general assessment base from two years before the contribution year. It is EUR 396.24 in 2026 and also resets in January. The 2026 annual reference wage is EUR 1,524: `1,524 × 60% = 914.40`, `1,524 × 11 = 16,764`, and `1,524 × 26% = 396.24`. Future-year amounts require that year's reference wage and law, not reuse of these figures.

Each component (4.4%, 18%, 6%, 4.75%) is rounded down separately. Apply the partial-month social method above where relevant. The existing transition through June 2026 above still applies.

**SZČO health advance.** 16% of the business tax base from two years earlier (2024 for 2026), taken before contributions paid, divided by the months of business in that year and by 1.486. The ordinary minimum is EUR 121.92, or EUR 60.96 with a disability, where §16(8)(a) applies. The insurer states the advance in the annual reconciliation. For an SZČO who is concurrently an employee or state-insured under §11(7), use the calculated §16(2)(b) advance without the ordinary minimum (§16(6), (8)(e)); that concurrent person need not remit an advance below EUR 3 (§16(9)). Exactly EUR 3 does not meet the below-EUR-3 exception. This concerns advances, not an assumed zero annual reconciliation.
