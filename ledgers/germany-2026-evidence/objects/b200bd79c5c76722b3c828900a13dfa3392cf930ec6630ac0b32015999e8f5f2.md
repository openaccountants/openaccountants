## DE-R23: EU small-business host-state eligibility (material ordinary eligibility)
Current annual Guide LF39 says an EU business can use Germany’s exemption on the same EUR100,000 EU test plus its home-state number; LF129 repeats only those gates. Own §19(4) explicitly incorporates §19(1), so the German prior-year EUR25,000/current-year EUR100,000 domestic-turnover conditions also apply. Conversely §19a(1) no.3 requires the other host state’s exemption conditions and no.4 bars duplicate registration for the procedure.

Source: https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/ustg_1980/__19.html, reader Ueb043bd52ca19a0a.reader.txt, actually read literal LF7–18; key original words: “Für im übrigen Gemeinschaftsgebiet ansässige Unternehmer gilt Absatz 1 entsprechend”. §19a: https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/ustg_1980/__19a.html, U475fd171437e5353.reader.txt LF7–18 actually read in full selected scope, including “die Voraussetzungen ... des Mitgliedstaates ... erfüllt”.

Option A (recommended): qualify both directions in LF39 and the German incoming route LF129. German exemption needs the incorporated German turnover limits, the separate EU100,000 prior/current test and the valid home-state number. Outgoing German use also requires the actual host-state conditions and §19a registration gates. Preserve existing figures and filing mechanics.
Option B: keep the BZSt reporting method and replace the purported sufficient eligibility sentence with an explicit host-state eligibility referral, rather than approving exemption on EU100,000 alone.
Separating control: an established EU business with qualifying EU turnover80,000 and German domestic turnover30,000 in2025 does not obtain Germany’s2026 exemption merely from its EU number and EU total. German prior-year25,000 fails. At25,000 it passes that one limb, subject to every other gate.

## DE-R24: financial/insurance turnover exclusion is conditional (material ordinary threshold)
Current LF86 subtracts exempt financial and insurance supplies without the statutory ancillary-supply qualification. Own §19(2) no.2 excludes §4 no.8(a–h),9(a),10 only “wenn sie Hilfsumsätze sind”; §19(2) no.1 has a different unconditional listed set. Do not merge the two.
Source same §19 reader, actual LF8–12. Exact phrase: “Umsätze, die nach § 4 Nummer 8 Buchstabe a bis h, Nummer 9 Buchstabe a und Nummer 10 steuerfrei sind, wenn sie Hilfsumsätze sind.”
Option A (recommended): replace LF86’s blanket financial/insurance subtraction with the two actual statutory categories or concise exact no.1/no.2 references, expressly retaining the Hilfsumsätze condition for no.2. Keep receipt basis, fixed-asset exclusion and reverse-charge distinctions.
Option B: state that only exempt turnovers expressly excluded by §19(2) are subtracted, adding that the cited financial/insurance group is excluded only where ancillary; refer classification to actual facts.
Control:30,000 receipts of a §4 no.10 exempt main insurance activity are not automatically subtracted from Gesamtumsatz; an independently qualifying Hilfsumsatz in that group is excluded. No invented numerical ancillary test.
