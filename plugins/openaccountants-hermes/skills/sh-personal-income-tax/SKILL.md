# Saint Helena island: ordinary personal income tax

## Scope and allowance

This Guide concerns ordinary income of individuals on Saint Helena island, not Ascension or Tristan da Cunha. The statutory tax year runs from 1 April to 31 March. Section 10(1) sets a £7,000 annual personal allowance. Section 10(3) allows one twelfth for each month of residence, or, for a non-resident receiving income from employment derived in Saint Helena, for each month receiving that employment income. Do not grant a full year merely because the person meets a residence test at some point. [Income Tax Ordinance, sections 2 and 10](https://www.sainthelena.gov.sh/documents/Income-Tax-Ord-Updated-011021).

The government reports that the 2026 Income Tax Amendment Bill did not pass. The proposed £7,150 allowance is therefore not the enacted replacement used here. The published [bill PDF](https://www.sainthelena.gov.sh/app/uploads/2026/03/20260303_Income-Tax-Amendment-Ordinance-2026_FINAL.pdf) has uncompleted execution fields despite its filename. [Government announcement](https://www.sainthelena.gov.sh/category/press-releases/page/14/).

## Ordinary annual calculation

For ordinary chargeable income, Schedule 1 paragraph 1(1)(a)(ii) sets 26% on the first £18,000 and 31% on the excess. The bands apply **after** relevant deductions and allowances. Section 7 floors chargeable income at nil. [Statute](https://www.sainthelena.gov.sh/documents/Income-Tax-Ord-Updated-011021); [current tax-office rates](https://www.sainthelena.gov.sh/public-services/income-tax/).

Assume twelve months of allowance entitlement, ordinary taxable income only, no further deduction, pension allowance, credit or relief:

| Ordinary taxable income | Personal allowance used | Chargeable income | Annual tax |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| £6,000 | £6,000 | £0 | £0 |
| £7,000 | £7,000 | £0 | £0 |
| £20,000 | £7,000 | £13,000 | £3,380 |
| £25,000 | £7,000 | £18,000 | £4,680 |
| £30,000 | £7,000 | £23,000 | £6,230 |

At £30,000, tax is £18,000 × 26% plus £5,000 × 31%. For a separately established six qualifying months, the personal allowance is £7,000 × 6/12 = £3,500. On £10,000 ordinary taxable income and those assumptions, chargeable income is £6,500 and tax £1,690. These are annual liability examples, not payroll table instructions.

## Filing depends on the income and reason for filing

Section 38 removes the ordinary annual-return requirement where the only taxable income is employment income and PAYE has been correctly deducted and paid. The Commissioner retains powers to require a return under section 37. An employee filing solely for excess PAYE has the section 39(1)(a) deadline of three months from receipt of the deduction certificate; the ordinary other-person deadline is three months after tax-year or approved accounting-period end. Departure rules are separate. [Sections 37–39](https://www.sainthelena.gov.sh/documents/Income-Tax-Ord-Updated-011021).

For the year **1 April 2025 to 31 March 2026**, the [11 June 2026 official reminder](https://www.sainthelena.gov.sh/reminder-filing-of-tax-returns/) states 30 June 2026 for self-employment, business, property and capital-gain/loss returns and 31 July 2026 for employees claiming PAYE refunds. Those are the dates for that completed year, not the return dates for income earned in 2026/27.

## Cases needing another computation

Schedule 1 has separate rules for dividends, gains and specified production activities. Mixed ordinary and preferential business income is subject to paragraph 1(3), including allocation of common deductions and the band interaction. Do not grant two independent £18,000 lower bands. Pension allowance limits, special exemptions, investment credits and cross-border relief require a separate calculation. [Sections 7–11 and Schedule 1](https://www.sainthelena.gov.sh/documents/Income-Tax-Ord-Updated-011021).
