# Succession, forced heirship and inheritance tax compared: France, Italy, Spain, Germany, Switzerland, Malta, UK, UAE and US

Figures are for tax year 2026. This Guide is for a family deciding who gets what on a death and what it will cost. It keeps two questions apart for every country. First, civil law: does the law reserve a share of the estate for children or a spouse, whatever the will says? Second, tax: what does the country charge on death, at what rates, with what allowances, and who is caught (residence, domicile or where the asset sits)? Each figure comes from an official page linked in its table. Where no allowed page could be read for a rule, the Guide says "not printed on an allowed page" instead of guessing.

Sibling Guides carry the detail this Guide does not repeat: `mt-inheritance-and-succession` (Malta duty on death and the reserved portion), `trusts-vs-foundations-by-country` and `mt-foundations-and-trusts` (vehicles and forced heirship claims), `exit-taxes-when-a-family-moves` and `family-office-residency-regimes-compared` (moving country), and `us-estate-gift-706-709` (US federal estate and gift tax).

## The method, step by step

1. Fix the facts at death for the person who will die first: nationality, where they live and for how long, marital status, every child (including adopted children), and where each asset sits. Each country below tests different facts.
2. Find which country's succession law governs the estate. For Malta, the Civil Code says the EU Succession Regulation applies to cross-border successions from 17 August 2015 ([Civil Code, Cap. 16, article 958B](https://legislation.mt/eli/cap/16/eng/pdf)). Spain's Código Civil article 9.8 names the law of the deceased's nationality ([Código Civil](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-1889-4763)); whether article 9.8 or the EU Succession Regulation decides a cross-border Spanish case is not settled on an allowed page read here, so refer it. France's service-public page states the residence-or-chosen-nationality rule ([F606](https://www.service-public.gouv.fr/particuliers/vosdroits/F606)). How the Regulation's own choice-of-law rules work is not printed on an allowed page; refer it to a lawyer.
3. Apply that law's reserved shares (the civil-law tables below). A will that leaves less than a reserved share can be cut back on a claim by the heir.
4. For each country that can tax, test its basis (the residence or nationality of the deceased or of the heir, or the location of the asset), then apply that country's allowance and rates to each heir's share (the tax tables below).
5. Where two countries tax the same share, check for a treaty or a credit. GOV.UK says an executor "might be able to reclaim tax through a double-taxation treaty if Inheritance Tax is charged on the same assets by the UK and the country where you lived" ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/inheritance-tax/when-someone-living-outside-the-uk-dies)). Germany credits, on application, foreign tax on foreign assets where no treaty applies and the liability is unlimited; the foreign tax must have been assessed, fall on the heir, have been paid, and not be subject to a claim for reduction. The credit covers only the foreign assets that also bear German tax, and only if the German tax arose within five years of the foreign tax ([§ 21 ErbStG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/erbstg_1974/__21.html)). Spain lets an heir taxed on worldwide receipts deduct the lower of two amounts: the tax actually paid abroad under a similar tax, and the Spanish average effective rate applied to the foreign assets taxed abroad by a similar tax ([Ley 29/1987, article 23](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-1987-28141)). No allowed page read here prints a French, Italian or Swiss credit, or any treaty's terms: refer those.
6. For a US person, or US assets, stop at `us-estate-gift-706-709`. For Malta assets, use `mt-inheritance-and-succession`.

## Layer 1: civil law, country by country

### France

Where French law governs the succession, children cannot be disinherited: "Si la loi française est applicable à votre succession, vous ne pouvez pas déshériter vos enfants" ([service-public F606](https://www.service-public.gouv.fr/particuliers/vosdroits/F606)). The reserved heirs ("héritiers réservataires") cannot be excluded. The testator may leave freely only the "quotité disponible". An heir who has not received the reserved share can bring an "action en réduction" ([service-public F770](https://www.service-public.gouv.fr/particuliers/vosdroits/F770)).

| Item | Reserved share (réserve héréditaire) | Note (verbatim, service-public F606) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Source | all rows below | https://www.service-public.gouv.fr/particuliers/vosdroits/F606 |
| One child | one-half to the child; one-half free | "1 1/2 1/2" |
| Two children | two-thirds to the children; one-third free | "2 2/3 1/3" |
| Three or more children | three-quarters to the children; one-quarter free | "3 ou plus 3/4 1/4" |
| No child, deceased married | one-quarter to the spouse; three-quarters free | "Défunt marié 1/4 (conjoint) 3/4" |
| No child, deceased not married | no reserved share; all free | "Défunt non marié Aucune Tout" |

F606 states which law applies as France reads it. The law of the country of habitual residence applies in principle, but a person may choose the law of a nationality held when the will is made or at death, by saying so clearly in a valid will ("vous pouvez décider d’appliquer la loi de l’État de votre nationalité plutôt que la loi de votre pays de résidence"). For a succession opened on or after 1 November 2021, a child disinherited by a foreign law may take the equivalent out of assets situated in France at the death (the "prélèvement compensatoire"). Both conditions must hold: the deceased or at least one child was a national or habitual resident of an EU member state at the death, AND "La loi étrangère applicable à la succession ne doit pas prévoir de mécanisme réservataire protecteur des enfants". The page cites a European Commission letter of 4 June 2026 on how France applies this right; refer every such case.

### Italy

Under the Codice civile, the "legittimari" are the spouse, the children and the ascendants; adopted children rank with children, and descendants of a child who come in that child's place take the same rights ([art. 536](https://www.normattiva.it/uri-res/N2Ls?urn:nir:stato:regio.decreto:1942-03-16;262:2~art536!vig=)).

### Germany

A descendant excluded by a will may claim the "Pflichtteil" from the heir: one-half of the value of the statutory share. Parents and the spouse have the same right when excluded by a will ([§ 2303 BGB](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/bgb/__2303.html)). The Pflichtteil is a money claim against the heir, not a share of the assets. The statutory share itself depends on the other heirs and, for the spouse, on the marital property regime (§ 2303(2) keeps § 1371 BGB in force); work it out before halving it.

### United Kingdom

No allowed page read for this Guide states a fixed reserved share for children. Instead, where a person "dies domiciled in England and Wales", the Inheritance (Provision for Family and Dependants) Act 1975 lets a listed person (spouse or civil partner, a former spouse or civil partner who has not formed a new marriage or partnership, a cohabitant as below, a child, a person treated as a child of the family, or a person the deceased was maintaining) apply to court for an order on the ground that the will, the intestacy rules or both do not make "reasonable financial provision" for them. A person who lived in the same household as the deceased, as if married or civil partners, for the whole two years before a death on or after 1 January 1996 is within the Act ([section 1](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1975/63/section/1)). A claim is decided by a court, not by a fixed fraction. This Guide does not cover Scotland or Northern Ireland.

### United Arab Emirates

Personal status law for non-Muslims, Sharia shares, and how a will is registered are not printed on an allowed page. The allowed UAE host is tax.gov.ae, which covers taxes, not succession. Refer to a UAE lawyer.

### United States

No federal reserved share for children appears on an allowed page. A surviving spouse's elective share against a will is a state-law question; this Guide does not cover it. Federal estate and gift tax is in `us-estate-gift-706-709`, a Guide by a named accountant; this Guide does not repeat it.

### Spain: Impuesto sobre Sucesiones y Donaciones (state level)

Spain's tax is ceded to the regions. Where the deceased was resident in a region, a non-resident heir may apply that region's rules. Regional rates and reductions are not covered here; flag them.

### Switzerland

The federal direct tax act lists as tax-free income "der Vermögensanfall infolge Erbschaft, Vermächtnis, Schenkung" ([DBG art. 24](https://www.fedlex.admin.ch/filestore/fedlex.data.admin.ch/eli/cc/1991/1184_1184_1184/20260101/de/pdf-a/fedlex-data-admin-ch-eli-cc-1991-1184_1184_1184-20260101-de-pdf-a.pdf)). ### Malta

Malta has no inheritance tax as such; it charges duty on documents on Maltese immovables and shares passing on death (and, where the heir is ordinarily resident and domiciled in Malta, on some shares and partnership interests registered outside Malta), and tax when the heir later sells. Basis from 6 April 2025 ([GOV.UK, long-term UK residents](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/inheritance-tax-if-youre-a-long-term-uk-resident)). The domicile and deemed domicile rules were replaced by long-term UK residence. A person is long-term UK resident in a tax year if UK tax resident for the previous 10 consecutive years, or for a total of 10 years or more within the previous 20. Inheritance Tax is charged on overseas assets owned outright on death when the person is long-term UK resident. Long-term residence can continue for up to 10 tax years after leaving; it is shorter if the person was not UK resident for all of the previous 20 years (for 10 to 13 years of residence, it ends 3 years after leaving). Do not use this for a death before 6 April 2025.

A person who is not long-term UK resident pays only on UK assets ("Inheritance Tax is only paid on your UK assets, for example property or bank accounts in the UK"), and 'excluded assets' such as foreign currency accounts, overseas pensions and holdings in authorised unit trusts and open-ended investment companies are not taxed ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/inheritance-tax/when-someone-living-outside-the-uk-dies)). There are two transitional rules on the long-term residence page. First, a person is not long-term UK resident if all three hold: on 30 October 2024 they did not have UK domicile or deemed domicile status, they are non-resident for the tax year 6 April 2025 to 5 April 2026, and they do not return. Second, a person who had deemed UK domicile on 30 October 2024, is non-resident for that tax year and does not return stops being long-term UK resident after 3 years of non-residence. Overseas assets in a trust the person set up or added to are charged "even when you were not a long-term UK resident". The page lists three conditions for trust assets to escape tax on the death (placed in the trust while non-UK domiciled; overseas on 30 October 2024; overseas at the death or when the rights ended) but does not say AND or OR: refer every trust case.

## Traps a family office meets

- **A trust or foundation does not beat forced heirship everywhere.** For a Maltese foundation, Malta's succession rules, "especially the indefeasible shares of spouses, ascendants and descendants", prevail over the foundation's terms, unless the foundation has no Malta connection through the founder's domicile at endowment or the situs of immovable property ([Civil Code, Cap. 16, Second Schedule, article 29A](https://legislation.mt/eli/cap/16/eng/pdf)). In France the reserved heirs cannot be excluded and may bring an "action en réduction" ([F770](https://www.service-public.gouv.fr/particuliers/vosdroits/F770)). See `trusts-vs-foundations-by-country` and `mt-foundations-and-trusts`.
- **Gifts during life are not outside the count.** France takes certain earlier gifts into account against the child allowance (F14198 above). Malta adds gifts back when working out the reserved portion (`mt-inheritance-and-succession`).
- **A move does not end the old country's tax at once.** The UK keeps long-term residence for up to 10 tax years after leaving (it ends after 3 years of non-residence for someone who had deemed UK domicile on 30 October 2024, is non-resident for the tax year 6 April 2025 to 5 April 2026 and does not return). Germany treats a German national as an Inländer for five years abroad without a German home. See `exit-taxes-when-a-family-moves` and `family-office-residency-regimes-compared`.
- **The heir's residence matters too.** Spain taxes a resident heir on everything received, wherever it is. Germany taxes worldwide if the heir is an Inländer, even when the deceased was not.
- **A will valid in one country may still owe the reserved share of another.** Where Spain's article 9.8 applies, a will valid under the testator's national law stays valid, but the legítimas follow the law that governs the succession. Under French law, a child disinherited by a foreign law may take the equivalent from French assets in the cases set out in the France section.
- **Spain's state figures are the fallback.** Regional reductions and rates usually decide the Spanish bill.

## Ask the client first

- Where did the person who may die first live in each of the last 20 years, and what nationalities do they hold?
- Where does each heir live, and for how long, and what nationality does each hold?
- Where is each asset (land, company shares, bank and broker accounts), and is any of it in a trust, foundation or company?
- Is there a will in each country, and has the person chosen a governing law in it?
- Is there a spouse or registered partner, under which marital property regime, and any divorce pending?
- What gifts were made during life, to whom and when?

## When to refuse or refer

- Refer every governing-law question (EU Succession Regulation, renvoi, a choice of nationality law) to a succession lawyer in each country concerned. This Guide does not print the Regulation's rules.
- Refer Italian, Swiss cantonal and UAE tax, and French territorial rules: no allowed page read here prints them.
- Refer any Spanish case to someone who applies the region's own rules.
- Refer US federal estate and gift tax to `us-estate-gift-706-709`, and US state law to a state adviser.
- Refuse to give a final figure where two countries tax the same share and step 5 has not been worked through.
- Refuse to say a trust or foundation defeats a reserved share without a lawyer's opinion for each country concerned.
