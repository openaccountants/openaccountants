# A UK company's first employee in Germany: what the employer needs to arrange

## Scope

Tax year 2026 (calendar year). For a UK company directly hiring an ordinary employee who will habitually work in Germany. This Guide sets out the German registrations and decisions needed before payroll starts and the deadlines that run from the start date. Net-pay calculations are in `de-payroll`; UK PAYE treatment and immigration permission need their own work.

Your first German employee brings several decisions before the first payroll: where social insurance is due, which employer registrations are needed, who handles German records and whether the company must deduct German wage tax. A UK payroll arrangement cannot answer those questions by itself. The company also needs a separate assessment of any German corporate-tax presence. [DVKA: foreign employers](https://www.dvka.de/de/arbeitgeber-erwerbstaetige/faq/), [§ 38 EStG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/estg/__38.html), [§ 12 AO](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/ao_1977/__12.html)

For an ordinary direct hire, the steps below show how those decisions fit together. A temporary posting or regular work in several countries needs a different social-insurance assessment before the same setup can be used.

## Ask the client first

- Will the person work habitually in Germany, or divide their work between countries?
- Which company employs them, and is a German company or office involved in their work? Does any German group company bear, or should it bear, the pay?
- When do they start, what will they do, and is any foreign social-insurance coverage (a certificate) being claimed?
- Is the employee in statutory or private health insurance, and do they have other jobs?
- Will the employee conclude contracts, negotiate or take orders for the company in Germany? Will they work from a home office the company uses?

These facts establish the route. The payroll provider can then request the specific information needed at each step.

## The method, step by step

1. **Establish where social insurance is due.** German employment generally falls within German social-insurance law; international coordination can change that result. The employee's work pattern and existing insurance establish which system and insurance branches apply. The payroll provider needs the start date, work locations, other jobs and health-insurance details to make that assessment. A fresh local hire is not proved to be a UK posting by keeping a UK bank account or employment contract. Where foreign coverage is claimed, the certificate's legal basis, issuer, dates and underlying assignment facts need to support it. A posting or multi-country arrangement should be resolved with the competent institution before payroll treats it as exempt. [§ 3 SGB IV](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/sgb_4/__3.html), [§ 6 SGB IV](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/sgb_4/__6.html), [DVKA coordination](https://www.dvka.de/de/arbeitgeber-erwerbstaetige/geltungsbereiche/koordination.html)

2. **Appoint the German representative and keep records.** An employer with no domestic seat must appoint a representative resident in Germany for the statutory wage-record duties. The appointment should make clear who holds the records, where they are accessible and who answers an audit. Wage records are kept for each employee, by calendar year, in Germany and in German, until the end of the calendar year after the last audit. A payroll software subscription does not itself make this appointment. [§ 28f(1), (1b) SGB IV](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/sgb_4/__28f.html)

3. **Arrange statutory accident insurance.** The business must notify the competent accident insurer within one week of the start of the undertaking. For an existing UK undertaking starting German activity, the insurer or adviser should confirm when that duty begins. A timely qualifying trade notification can meet the notification duty. The insurer issues the Unternehmensnummer needed for the next registration. A foreign undertaking must also deal with its accident-insurance representative duty; the representative's appointment should expressly cover that role. [§ 192 SGB VII](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/sgb_7/__192.html), [§ 130(2) SGB VII](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/sgb_7/__130.html)

4. **Obtain the Betriebsnummer.** The employer or an authorised agent applies electronically through the Federal Employment Agency's Betriebsnummern-Service. The application needs the employment-location address, activity classification, legal form and Unternehmensnummer so the right employment establishment is registered. The Unternehmensnummer comes from the accident insurer and is different from the Betriebsnummer and the tax number. The number belongs to the employer's establishment, not to the payroll provider. Keep its confirmation and report changes promptly. [Agency application guidance](https://www.arbeitsagentur.de/unternehmen/betriebsnummern-service/alles-wichtige/beantragung), [Agency prerequisites](https://www.arbeitsagentur.de/unternehmen/betriebsnummern-service/alles-wichtige), [§ 18i SGB IV](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/sgb_4/__18i.html)

5. **Set up the collecting institution and reports.** For ordinary insured employment the statutory health fund normally collects health, care, pension and unemployment contributions. Private health insurance does not remove pension or unemployment contributions that remain due; those still need a collection route. Minijobs use their own collecting institution and a different calculation route. The employee's social-insurance number, insurer details, duties and other employment let the payroll provider pick the reporting codes and the collection account. The collecting institution can settle doubts about insurance liability. [§ 28i SGB IV](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/sgb_4/__28i.html), [§ 28h SGB IV](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/sgb_4/__28h.html)

6. **Register the employee on time.** The start of insured employment is reported with the first following payroll, and at the latest within six weeks of the start. Separately check whether the sector needs a Sofortmeldung (immediate report) by the start of work; that report does not replace the ordinary registration. The payroll provider should keep the accepted registration and clear any rejection before treating the employee as registered. [§ 6 DEÜV](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/de_v/__6.html), [§ 28a SGB IV](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/sgb_4/__28a.html)

7. **Decide whether the employer must deduct German wage tax.** § 38 EStG sets the employer conditions: German residence, management, seat, establishment or permanent representative. Foreign commercial labour leasing has its own branch. In an international assignment, a German host can be the domestic employer if it bears, or under arm's-length principles should bear, the pay. So no German company does not prove there is no withholding. Equally, German social-insurance registration alone does not establish wage-tax withholding. Contracts, management locations, the employee's role and any host-company recharges show who is responsible. Where the result is uncertain, the tax adviser can ask for an Anrufungsauskunft, a binding wage-tax ruling, before payroll starts. [§ 38 EStG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/estg/__38.html), [§ 42e EStG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/estg/__42e.html)

8. **Put the wage-tax arrangement in place.** Where withholding applies, the payroll provider needs a tax-office account and authenticated reporting access. The employee's tax ID, date of birth and main-or-second job information allow retrieval of the ELStAM, the electronic wage-tax details. Changes and exceptions need checking each payroll. The filing period is set by the wage tax of the previous year; for a new establishment, by the wage tax of the first full calendar month after it opened, multiplied to a year (table below). A small headcount does not by itself mean quarterly filing. The return and payment are due on the tenth day after the end of each period. [§ 39e EStG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/estg/__39e.html), [§ 41a EStG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/estg/__41a.html)

   Where the employer has no German withholding duty, the employee still needs advice on German income-tax returns and advance payments. German residence can create unlimited income-tax liability, and the tax office sets advances by notice. Any UK payroll consequences need separate UK advice. [§ 1 EStG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/estg/__1.html), [§ 37 EStG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/estg/__37.html)

9. **Agree who runs payroll and funds the payments.** The employer owes the total social-insurance contribution. Employee shares are normally recovered by wage deduction, with statutory limits on catching up missed deductions. The company should agree with its provider who does calculations, returns, funding and reconciliation of notices. Salary, benefits, variable pay, hours and contract terms are needed for the payslip once the insurance and tax decisions are settled; `de-payroll` covers those calculations and the routine payroll cycle. [§ 28e SGB IV](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/sgb_4/__28e.html), [§ 28g SGB IV](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/sgb_4/__28g.html)

   Contributions can fall due before salary is paid: the contribution statement goes in two working days before the due date, and the expected contribution is due by the third-last bank working day of the month, with a reconciliation the following month. The provider's collection calendar should leave the company time to fund those payments. [§ 28f(3) SGB IV](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/sgb_4/__28f.html), [§ 23 SGB IV](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/sgb_4/__23.html)

10. **Assess the company's tax presence and the employee's right to work.** German fixed-place-of-business and permanent-representative rules need their own facts; a worker who habitually concludes or negotiates contracts or takes orders needs specific review. The corporate-tax adviser needs office or home-office access arrangements, hiring and sales authority, negotiation activity and where management decisions are made. Those facts support the separate domestic and UK to Germany treaty assessment of permanent establishment and company residence. Employer registration and social-insurance certificates do not settle it. Employment-law terms and immigration permission also need review before work starts. [§ 12 AO](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/ao_1977/__12.html), [§ 13 AO](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/ao_1977/__13.html)

## Worked cases

These cases show how the decisions can differ; they do not calculate a payslip.

- **Local hire, no German subsidiary:** an ordinary employee works indefinitely only in Germany for a UK company. No foreign-coverage exception is evidenced. German social-insurance registration is needed, while wage-tax withholding needs the separate § 38 assessment. A German subsidiary is not needed for those obligations to arise. [DVKA foreign employers](https://www.dvka.de/de/arbeitgeber-erwerbstaetige/faq/)
- **Insurance applies, withholding does not:** the adviser confirms on fully documented facts that no § 38 employer, host or leasing trigger applies. German insurance registration and contributions continue. The employee needs income-tax and advance-payment advice even though the employer does not withhold. [§ 38 EStG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/estg/__38.html), [§ 37 EStG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/estg/__37.html)
- **UK payroll, German economic host:** a German host should bear the assigned worker's pay but the group has not posted a recharge. The missing invoice does not remove the need to assess the host's wage-tax duties. [§ 38(1) EStG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/estg/__38.html)
- **Certificate claimed, facts changed:** a certificate covers an earlier temporary assignment, but the employee is now hired indefinitely in Germany. The changed facts need a fresh decision on which insurance law applies; the old certificate cannot be assumed to cover the new arrangement. [§ 6 SGB IV](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/sgb_4/__6.html), [DVKA coordination](https://www.dvka.de/de/arbeitgeber-erwerbstaetige/geltungsbereiche/koordination.html)
- **Late first payroll:** the first payroll falls after the six-week limit. The employer cannot wait for payday to register. In a listed immediate-report sector, the start-of-work deadline was earlier still. [§ 6 DEÜV](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/de_v/__6.html), [§ 28a SGB IV](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/sgb_4/__28a.html)
- **Filing period for the new German establishment (example inputs):** the employee starts on 15 March 2026; wage tax for April 2026, the first full month, is EUR 412. Multiplied to a year that is EUR 4,944: more than EUR 1,080 and not more than EUR 5,000, so the employer files quarterly in 2026, with each return and payment due on the tenth day after the quarter. [§ 41a(2) EStG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/estg/__41a.html)

### Before the first payroll

The company and its provider should be able to name the insurance arrangement, the German representatives, the accident insurer, the employer number, the collecting fund and the accepted employee registration. They should also know who handles wage tax (or the employee's own income-tax duties where there is no withholding) and who funds each payment.

Keep the registrations, mandates, certificates and tax advice together so a change of payroll provider or an authority enquiry does not mean rebuilding the setup. Review the arrangement when the employee changes workplace, travel pattern, host company or contract authority, or when coverage expires. Rates and reporting rules need checking each new calendar year.

## When to refuse or refer

- Resolve uncertain insurance coverage, required representatives, missing registrations and the withholding decision before treating the payroll setup as complete.
- Temporary UK postings, regular work in several countries and certificate disputes need an assessment under the coordination rules that apply. EU telework arrangements cannot be assumed to cover a UK case.
- Labour leasing, directors and shareholders, employment-status disputes, equity pay and regulated sectors need specialist advice beyond this ordinary direct-hire route.
- Company tax presence, profit attribution, corporate residence, employment-law terms and immigration permission need their own conclusions alongside payroll registration.
