# Medical professional accounting v2.0

## Scope

For accountants working with doctors, dentists, allied-health professionals, locums, clinics and group practices. Covers patient, insurer, public-system and membership income, costs and practice acquisitions. It provides no medical, clinical or treatment advice.

This is a cross-country method for 2026. Establish the entity, country, tax year, reporting framework and permitted accounting basis before using it. Financial-reporting recognition is separate from income-tax deductions, capital allowances and VAT/GST. The IFRS principles below use the Australian adopted paragraph text as an official public reference; Australian-specific paragraphs and local simplified rules are not automatically rules in another country. Match the compilation and local adoption to the reporting period. Do not silently apply this method as US GAAP or a country's cash-basis tax computation.

## Ask the client first

- Which person/entity earns the income, owns the assets and signs the contracts? Obtain the legal structure, residence, business locations and reporting dates.
- Which reporting framework and tax accounting basis apply? What registrations, elections, prior balances and tax losses exist?
- Obtain contracts, invoices, credit notes, bank and platform statements, withholding certificates, refunds, chargebacks and opening/closing balances.
- What is sold, when and to whom? Identify customer location and status, performance obligations, intermediaries, private use and any money held for another person.
- Obtain cost invoices, asset ownership/use records, funding agreements, payroll/contractor documents, related-party dealings and the actual terms of insurance and licences.

## The method, step by step

1. Establish legal ownership and accounting/tax scope before coding transactions. Keep each business entity, owner and client-money account separate.
2. Map each contract to the actual promised goods/services and the consideration the business is entitled to. Determine recognition from performance and control. Billing, payout and cash dates are evidence, not universal recognition rules.
3. Reconcile each income source from transactions to returns/refunds, fees, withheld tax, reserves and bank settlement. Review principal/agent status before choosing gross or net reporting. Do not double-count a payout and the underlying sales.
4. Reconcile receivables, conditional contract assets, customer deposits, contract liabilities, deferred fees and client funds. Separate price concessions from credit losses. Obtain the applicable impairment standard before quantifying a loss allowance.
5. Establish business purpose, private proportion, capital versus revenue treatment and timing for every cost. Obtain the current country law before claiming a tax deduction; an accounting expense is not proof of tax deductibility.
6. Prepare an asset register with invoices, costs, ownership, available-for-use date, residual value, useful life, method and disposal proceeds. Maintain a separate tax-capital-allowance schedule. The prior purchase-price and useful-life ranges were unsupported planning estimates, not statutory values, and are held below.
7. Apply the country's VAT/GST/sales-tax rules by supply, customer, location and registration. Include reverse charges/imported services where relevant. Do not reclaim tax on exempt/private activity just because a supplier charged it.
8. Test payroll/contractor status, withholding, permanent establishment, cross-border reporting and elections using current country jobs. A platform tax form, incorporation or a merchant-of-record arrangement does not remove every obligation.
9. Deliver reconciled journals and schedules, source-linked tax adjustments, missing facts and a review checklist. Require the relevant professional review before a return, tax payment or material accounting judgment is relied on.

## Clinical boundary and practice records

This method must never be used to make a diagnosis, select treatment, determine clinical necessity, substitute for professional healthcare judgment or let tax/financial considerations dictate care. Clinical decisions remain with qualified healthcare professionals. A VAT category is not a medical recommendation. Refer clinical questions to the responsible healthcare professional.

Establish the practitioner, partnership, company, clinic and associate arrangements separately. Obtain patient-service contracts without unnecessary clinical personal data, payer/panel terms, professional registration, permissions, public-system statements, fee schedules, claims, rejections, bank settlements, pension deductions and premises/equipment records. Confirm who supplies care, who is entitled to fees and who is legally liable for payment.

The patient is not automatically liable for every rejected insurer claim. Identify the actual contract, statutory restrictions, authorisation conditions, permitted balance billing and payer liability before moving a receivable. Distinguish a price concession or lack of entitlement from a credit loss on a valid receivable. Do not inflate revenue to a standard fee and then automatically call every contractual reduction a bad debt.

For NHS, Medicare and other public arrangements, reconcile capitation, activity, quality incentives, adjustments, notional/actual reimbursements, deductions and remittances. Do not treat the bank amount as the entire earned revenue. Establish whether a particular receipt is a customer service, grant or other arrangement before choosing its accounting standard.

### Worked case: unequal stand-alone values in a treatment package

Fabricated currency units. Assume a valid contract with two distinct services: an assessment with stand-alone price 200 and treatment with stand-alone price 400. A package sells for 450. Allocate 150 to assessment and 300 to treatment by relative stand-alone values. If only assessment transfers at the reporting date and the whole package was prepaid, earned revenue is 150 and the remaining contract liability is 300. Do not allocate half to each service simply because there are two visits. This case establishes no clinical indication, tax rate or refund right. [AASB 15, paragraphs 27-30 and 73-86](https://standards.aasb.gov.au/aasb-15-dec-2022).

```oa-check
name: Medical package allocation using relative stand-alone prices
inputs:
  package_price: 450
  assessment_standalone: 200
  treatment_standalone: 400
steps:
  total_standalone: assessment_standalone + treatment_standalone
  assessment_revenue: package_price * assessment_standalone / total_standalone
  treatment_allocation: package_price * treatment_standalone / total_standalone
  remaining_liability: package_price - assessment_revenue
expected:
  total_standalone: 600
  assessment_revenue: 150
  treatment_allocation: 300
  remaining_liability: 300
```

Boundary: if the clinical services form one integrated non-distinct promise, determine its transfer pattern rather than applying this distinct-service allocation. Current payer, refund and variable-fee conditions must also be assessed.

## Clinical and practice cost checklist

These categories identify records to obtain; they do not establish automatic deductions, compulsory worldwide amounts or clinical compliance:
### Clinical premises

- Clinic/surgery rent or mortgage interest (per country skill's property rules)
- Business rates / property taxes on clinical premises
- Utilities (electricity, gas, water :  higher than typical office due to sterilization, lighting)
- Clinical waste disposal (specialist contractor required)
- Cleaning services (clinical-grade cleaning standards)
- Building maintenance and repairs
- Premises insurance (buildings and contents)
- Security systems and medical storage compliance (controlled drugs safe, etc.)

### Medical equipment and instruments

- Diagnostic equipment (stethoscopes, otoscopes, ophthalmoscopes)
- Treatment-specific equipment (dental chairs, physiotherapy tables, ultrasound machines)
- Surgical instruments and sterilization equipment (autoclaves)
- IT systems (patient management software, clinical systems)
- Medical imaging equipment (X-ray, dental radiography, ultrasound)
- Laboratory equipment (pathology, on-site testing)
- Consumable medical supplies (gloves, masks, swabs, needles, dressings)

### Professional regulation and indemnity

- Medical indemnity / malpractice cover (confirm the actual practitioner, employer, insurer and regulator requirements)
- Professional registration fees (GMC, GDC, AHPRA, state medical boards)
- DBS/background checks (determine which roles and local rules require them)
- CQC registration fees (UK) / equivalent regulatory body fees
- Controlled drugs license
- Radiation protection advisor fees (dental/radiology)

### Staff costs (for practices with employees)

- Nurses, hygienists, therapists, healthcare assistants
- Reception and administrative staff
- Practice manager
- Locum costs (temporary cover for leave/illness)
- Staff training (mandatory and CPD)
- Employer pension contributions
- Staff uniforms/scrubs
- Occupational health for staff (Hep B vaccination, needle-stick protocols)

### Consumables and disposables

- Medical supplies (per patient usage :  significant volume)
- Dental materials (composite, amalgam, impressions, crowns from lab)
- Pharmaceuticals and medications dispensed
- PPE (personal protective equipment)
- Laboratory and diagnostic test kits
- Sterilization supplies and chemicals

**Classification:** distinguish stock on hand, items consumed in treatment, purchased lab services and general practice costs. Apply the actual framework, inventory/expense recognition and presentation policy; payment for a stock purchase is not necessarily full current consumption.

### Professional development (CPD)

- Confirm the relevant regulator's actual CPD/revalidation requirements for the profession and period. No universal hours or cycle is established here
- Course fees, conference attendance, study days
- Medical journals and subscriptions (BMJ, Lancet, specialty journals)
- Medical textbooks and reference materials
- Online CPD platforms and e-learning subscriptions
- Examination fees for specialist qualifications
- Travel and accommodation for CPD events

### Locum and cover costs

- Locum doctors/dentists/physios to cover holidays, sickness, maternity
- Determine employment/contractor status from the actual arrangement and country rules; do not presume self-employment
- Locum cost: establish the business, payer, period, payroll/withholding and local deduction requirements
- Locum agency fees: establish the actual service, timing, VAT/GST and local deduction treatment

## Protocols, systems, permissions and acquisitions

Identify the actual accounting treatment of clinical protocols, research outcomes, purchased treatment systems, booking/patient portals, perpetual software and hosted services. Developing something during normal practice does not by itself settle recognition, and small-practice size does not force all software development to expense. Clinical research can be subject to a different standard or fail recognition; document the actual reason rather than presuming it has no value.

For teaching materials or external courses, distinguish inventory/content, licences and the service sold. Apply the relevant framework, rights and recognition tests. Internal training, professional education and regulatory permissions need their own business-purpose, period and recognition analysis. An annual regulatory fee, acquired transferable right and business-combination permission are not interchangeable. Do not call every registration fee an intangible or rule out every permission automatically.

For practice names, brands, websites and domains, no generic dollar cutoff or trademark-registration life applies. Separate purchased rights from internally generated brands and actual marketing/service expenditure. For acquisitions, separate business-combination goodwill, patient/customer relationships, equipment, property, contracts and liabilities. A purchased patient list is not always the same thing as goodwill, and the former instruction that IFRS goodwill must always be amortised is wrong.
## Asset, lease and intellectual-property checks

Do not copy the former asset-price and useful-life ranges into an accounting or tax register. Use actual acquisition cost and the applicable framework; review private use, ownership, finance, impairment and disposal separately. For a lessee applying IFRS 16 there is no general exemption merely because the business is a sole practitioner or the lease is called operating. Specific short-term and low-value exemptions have conditions. [AASB 16, paragraphs 5-8 and 22](https://standards.aasb.gov.au/aasb-16-nov-2022).

Research costs and internally generated goodwill/brands are not automatically capitalisable. Development requires all recognition criteria, including technical feasibility, intent, ability to use/sell, probable benefits, resources and reliable cost measurement. Once the criteria are met, qualifying subsequent development costs are not excluded merely because the developer is self-employed. Previously expensed expenditure is not reinstated as an asset later. Finite-life intangibles are amortised from availability for use using a supported life and pattern; indefinite-life intangibles are not amortised and require impairment review. Purchased rights, service subscriptions and business-combination goodwill require separate classification. [AASB 138, paragraphs 48-71 and 97-110](https://standards.aasb.gov.au/aasb-138-dec-2022).

For an IFRS business combination, goodwill is not routinely amortised; it is carried subject to impairment, with annual testing. Separately identified finite-life customer lists or other intangibles can have amortisation. Other local GAAP and tax amortisation may differ. Do not post tax relief or a generic goodwill amortisation period without identifying the regime. [AASB 3, paragraph B63](https://standards.aasb.gov.au/aasb-3-dec-2022-0); [AASB 136, paragraphs 9-10 and 90](https://standards.aasb.gov.au/aasb-136-jan-2026-0).

## Platform, withholding and foreign-currency reporting

Identify the legal payer and contract separately for every platform or processor. Reconcile gross entitled consideration, platform fees, refunds, taxes withheld, rolling reserves, exchange conversion and settlements. A platform statement can include non-revenue transfers; a tax report can report gross payments that differ from accounting income. Check refunds, duplicated forms and the actual issuing entity. Do not assume every named platform issues the same tax form.

The former US Form 1099-K shorthand is not a taxability threshold. Current IRS guidance distinguishes payment-card transactions from third-party settlement organisations, notes that a form may be issued below the statutory reporting threshold, and explains that a form alone does not decide taxable income. Retain the reporting question in the current US job; this global method does not introduce a new US return computation. [IRS, Understanding Form 1099-K](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/understanding-your-form-1099-k).

For an international payment, establish the income's character, source, work location, beneficial recipient, withholding documentation and any treaty claim. A US payer does not make every payment a US-source royalty. A platform's withholding is not automatically the final liability, and a credit requires its own evidence. Record foreign-currency recognition at the applicable transaction date and review the framework's translation and settlement requirements; invoice date is not universal where performance occurred earlier. Exact exchange-rate methods, treaty rates and withholding forms remain country-specific items below.

## United Kingdom: expense boundary

For a UK sole trader or individual in a business partnership, use the business-expense rules and private-use allocation, with the correct cash or traditional basis. Do not apply the sole-trader overview as company tax law. The owner's drawings, loan principal and an invented charge for the proprietor's own unpaid time are not ordinary paid business costs. [HMRC self-employed expenses](https://www.gov.uk/expenses-if-youre-self-employed).

Do not use the former global half-deduction default for client meals: UK client entertaining is not generally allowable under the cited self-employed guidance. Business advertising, qualifying subscriptions, professional/legal costs and training have their own conditions. The UK training guidance can cover new skills supporting the existing business, not only maintaining old skills; training to start a new unrelated business is different. Accountancy fees for the business and the individual's own Self Assessment preparation/submission are also distinguished. Review the actual cost and its purpose. [HMRC marketing/entertainment](https://www.gov.uk/expenses-if-youre-self-employed/marketing-entertainment-subscriptions); [HMRC legal/financial costs](https://www.gov.uk/expenses-if-youre-self-employed/legal-financial); [HMRC training](https://www.gov.uk/expenses-if-youre-self-employed/training-courses).


## United Kingdom: healthcare VAT is conditional

HMRC's medical-services exemption requires the relevant registered professional scope and a primary purpose of protecting, maintaining or restoring the person's health. A medical report principally enabling a legal/insurance decision, staff supply, goods, cosmetic service or room letting cannot be labelled exempt from the professional's job title alone. Distinguish clinical care, goods supplied with treatment, dispensing and other activities. [VAT Notice 701/57, sections 2-7](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/health-professionals-pharmaceutical-products-and-vat-notice-70157).

Also read the specific 2026 locum update before applying the general staffing rule. HMRC recognises that a supply of a **GMC-registered locum doctor performing a medical-practitioner role** may fall under the deputy exemption, including via an employment business. It does not extend to all healthcare workers, GMC registrants in other roles or generic staffing. Any past-output-tax claim requires the supplier's evidence and review of input-tax/partial-exemption effects and unjust enrichment; do not promise an automatic refund. [Revenue and Customs Brief 6 (2026)](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/revenue-and-customs-brief-6-2026-vat-liability-of-the-supply-of-temporary-medical-staff-locum-doctors/vat-liability-of-the-supply-of-temporary-medical-staff-2026).

## Australia: GST-free is different from input taxed

The current GST Act separately provides for qualifying medical services and their exclusions. A service rendered for cosmetic reasons without the specified Medicare basis is not automatically GST-free; identify the statutory definition, recipient and applicable health category. Do not import the UK exemption test. GST-free treatment does not itself prohibit input credits: a creditable acquisition requires the actual enterprise purpose, taxable acquisition, consideration and registration conditions, with private/input-taxed limitations. [GST Act, sections 11-5 to 11-30 and 38-7](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2004A00446/2026-01-01/2026-01-01/text/original/epub/OEBPS/document_1/document_1.html).

## Other countries and recurring traps

- **EU and other jurisdictions:** check the actual healthcare exemption, recognised practitioner, therapeutic purpose, goods, education, premises, staff supply and input-recovery rules under current national law. Mixed medical, cosmetic and retail activity requires supply-by-supply analysis and any applicable allocation; do not apply a universal “cannot register” rule.
- **Public-system and panel receipts:** reconcile earned fees to remittance adjustments, lab charges, pension/superannuation and prior-period corrections. Deductions from a statement are not automatically all tax deductions, and pension amounts must not be claimed twice.
- **Associates and locums:** employment, genuine independent services, facility fees, commission and payer/recipient of care require separate contracts and actual conduct. A customary self-employed label does not prevent payroll/social-security obligations.
- **Private use:** document clinical/private equipment and premises allocation, staff benefits and owners' drawings. The clinic's income and the practitioner's personal income are separate where the legal structure makes them so.
- **Lab and materials:** identify who supplies the patient, who contracts with the lab, inventory/consumption and gross/net analysis. Do not automatically net every lab charge off revenue or call every related acquisition tax recoverable.
- **Goodwill and tax:** maintain separate accounting, acquisition-allocation and tax-relief schedules. An accounting impairment is not automatically a tax deduction, and a permitted tax allowance is not necessarily accounting amortisation.

## Insurance, professional requirements and growth

Obtain current registration, firm/individual authorisation, specialty permissions, claims-made/occurrence cover, indemnity limits, retroactive dates and run-off requirements from the actual regulator and policy. The former worldwide mandatory statements, premiums and run-off multiples were not supported. Review professional boards such as GMC/GDC/AHPRA and relevant state boards only where they apply, not as equivalent worldwide licences.

Keep professional memberships (BMA, BDA, AMA, ADA, RCGP, RCS and specialty colleges), examinations, appraisal/revalidation, safeguarding, Basic Life Support, infection-control, audit/peer-review, CQC and information-governance costs as separate records. Confirm legal necessity and tax treatment locally. Neither mandatory status nor business relevance makes every training, travel or personal tax cost automatically deductible.

For incorporation or group structure, assess professional-ownership restrictions, clinical liability, public contracts, partnership/LLP options, retained profit and eventual goodwill/property disposal. Incorporation does not automatically separate every personal clinical liability or preserve a public contract. Assess a practice acquisition using patient retention, contract transfer, equipment, premises lease, staff/associate retention, pensions and claims history. A new location needs actual cash-flow forecasts; the old fixed profitability ramp was not evidence.

Consider reception/admin, clinical assistants, other practitioners, a practice manager and further associates from actual demand, safe capacity, supervision, economics and worker status. Prior admin-hour, staff-number and revenue milestones are not legal thresholds. Plan pensions, succession, valuation, phased retirement, patient-record transfer and run-off with the responsible professionals; no common pre-retirement period is assumed.

## When to refuse or refer

Refuse clinical advice, inappropriate disclosure of patient data, a declaration of professional fitness or a tax-driven treatment recommendation. Do not certify exemption, claim a refund, write off an insurer balance against a patient or apply goodwill relief without the actual contract/law. Refer the unresolved accounting/tax issue with minimal necessary records to an appropriately credentialed medical-sector practitioner. All clinical decisions remain exclusively with qualified healthcare professionals using their professional judgment.
