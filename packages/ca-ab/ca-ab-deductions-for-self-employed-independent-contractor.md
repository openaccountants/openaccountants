# Deductions for a self-employed independent contractor in Alberta

## Deductions for a self-employed independent contractor in Alberta

An independent contractor in Alberta who is not incorporated reports business income and expenses as a sole proprietor on Form T2125, Statement of Business or Professional Activities, filed with the T1 personal return for the calendar year. Deductible amounts are the expenses incurred to earn that business income, reduced by the personal-use portion and by the statutory limits set out below. Figures are for tax year 2026. Alberta has no separate provincial business-expense rules on the pages used here; the deduction limits are federal and apply to an Alberta contractor in the same terms. Where a table cites a CRA page that states a different year or a different filing cycle, the table says so in the Note column.

## Scope

Self-employed individuals carrying on a business or profession in Alberta as a sole proprietor or partner :  contractors, consultants, tradespeople, gig and platform workers :  who report on Form T2125. It is not for employees claiming employment expenses, who use Form T777 with Form T2200 completed by the employer, and it is not for incorporated contractors, who file a T2 corporate return. Passenger vehicle limits below apply to a vehicle designed or adapted primarily to carry people on highways and streets that seats a driver and no more than eight passengers; the CRA states that passenger vehicles and zero-emission passenger vehicles are subject to limits on the amount of CCA, interest and leasing costs that may be deducted.

## Ask the client first

- Is the work carried on as a sole proprietor or partner, or through a corporation? A corporation files a T2 and none of the T2125 method above applies.
- Have worldwide taxable supplies exceeded the small supplier threshold in any single calendar quarter or over the last four consecutive calendar quarters, and on what date? This fixes the effective date of GST/HST registration and the date charging must begin.
- Is the vehicle owned or leased, and is it a passenger vehicle, a zero-emission passenger vehicle or another motor vehicle? The applicable ceiling, and whether a ceiling applies at all, turns on this.
- On what date was the vehicle acquired, or the lease or loan entered into? The 2026 CCA ceiling applies to vehicles acquired on or after January 1, 2026, and the leasing and interest limits to new leases and loans entered into on or after that date.
- Was net tax owing above the instalment threshold in 2026 and in either 2025 or 2024, and was an instalment reminder received?
- Is the meal, beverage or entertainment cost rebilled to the client and shown on the bill, or otherwise within the exceptions listed on the Line 8523 page? That determines whether the 50% limit bites.

### Filing workflow

1. Determine whether GST/HST registration is required before anything else, by testing worldwide taxable supplies against the small supplier threshold in the table above, in a single calendar quarter and over the last four consecutive calendar quarters :  see [When to register for and start charging the GST/HST](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html), which sets the 29-day registration deadline. Include worldwide taxable and zero-rated supplies of the person and associates, excluding financial services, sales of capital property and goodwill. A single-quarter crossing charges the crossing supply; a rolling-quarter crossing without a single-quarter crossing stops small-supplier status at the end of the month following that quarter, with registration no later than the first subsequent supply. Taxi and commercial ride-sharing operators have special mandatory registration rules even below the ordinary threshold. Exempt-only supplies do not create an ordinary registration entitlement.
2. Keep books and records that, in the CRA's words, "provide enough detail for the CRA to determine your tax obligations and entitlements," retaining them for the period in the records table :  see [Keeping records](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/rc188/keeping-records.html).
3. Sort each cost into a current operating expense or a capital outlay. Current operating expenses are listed and defined at [Types of operating expenses](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed-income/business-income-tax-reporting/business-expenses/types-operating-expenses.html); capital repairs are not deductible as current expenses there, and capital cost allowance is dealt with in Chapter 4 of Guide T4002.
4. Apply the 50% restriction to food, beverages and entertainment, and calculate the allowable business-use part, following [Line 8523 - Meals and entertainment](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/sole-proprietorships-partnerships/report-business-income-expenses/completing-form-t2125/line-8523-meals-entertainment-allowable-part-only.html), which lists the exceptions where the limit does not apply, including where you bill your client or customer for the meal and entertainment costs and show these costs on the bill.
5. Identify the vehicle type before claiming any vehicle cost, using [Type of vehicle you own](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/sole-proprietorships-partnerships/business-expenses/motor-vehicle-expenses/type-vehicle.html), then apply the 2026 CCA, interest and leasing ceilings in the vehicle table. Motor vehicle expenses are entered as described at [Motor vehicle expenses](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed-income/business-income-tax-reporting/business-expenses/motor-vehicle-expenses.html), with capital cost allowance entered on Line 9936.
6. Report gross income and the deducted expenses on Form T2125, Statement of Business or Professional Activities, using the line-by-line instructions in [Guide T4002, Self-employed Business, Professional, Commission, Farming, and Fishing Income](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/t4002/t4002-5.html).
7. Compute CPP on the resulting net business income at the self-employed rate and ceilings in the CPP table :  see [Contributions to the Canada Pension Plan](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html). The CRA's payroll page states that a self-employed worker pays both the employer and employee contribution up to the self-employed maximum as part of their personal income tax return: [CPP contribution rates, maximums and exemptions](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/payroll/payroll-deductions-contributions/canada-pension-plan-cpp/cpp-contribution-rates-maximums-exemptions.html).
8. Test whether instalments are required against both years named in the instalment table, and diarise the four due dates :  see [Who has to pay instalments](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/payments/payments-cra/individual-payments/income-tax-instalments/who-pays-instalments.html), which directs you to the calculation chart for instalment payments.
9. File by the self-employed filing date and pay any balance owing by the earlier payment date, both in the filing table :  see [Important dates for individuals](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/individuals/topics/important-dates-individuals.html). Filing late does not extend the payment date.

### Complete the deduction computation

For an ordinary T2125 business, deduct only the reasonable business portion supported by invoices; do not deduct personal living costs, capital acquisitions as current expenses, or the same GST/HST recovered as an input tax credit. Meals are the lesser of actual or reasonable cost, then normally 50%; apply a documented statutory exception where relevant, such as separately billed client reimbursement. Do not extend an employee mileage allowance to a sole proprietor: use actual vehicle costs multiplied by documented business kilometres / total kilometres, plus allowable business parking, then apply the passenger-vehicle CCA, loan and lease restrictions. The monthly lease ceiling is subject to the full passenger-vehicle lease calculation and business-use fraction, not an automatic $1,100 deduction. [T4002 chapter 3](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/t4002/t4002-5.html) and [motor vehicle expenses](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed-income/business-income-tax-reporting/business-expenses/motor-vehicle-expenses.html).

For a workspace in a home, it must be the principal place of business, **or** used exclusively to earn business income and regularly and continuously to meet clients, customers or patients. Allocate maintenance, heating, electricity, insurance, rent or mortgage interest and property taxes reasonably by area, and by time for shared use; mortgage principal is not an expense. Claim on **T2125 line 9945** without duplicating another line. The claim cannot create or increase a business loss; carry qualifying unused expenses forward while the same conditions continue. CCA on a home requires a separate capital-gain/recapture review. The T4002 examples currently print 2025 amounts; the continuing test is in **ITA 18(12)**. [T4002 line 9945](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/t4002/t4002-5.html), [ITA 18](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/I-3.3/section-18.html).

Report ordinary vehicle operating expenses on **line 9281**, CCA separately on **line 9936**, and unreimbursed partner expenses, if eligible, on **line 9943**. Complete T2125 income and expense lines, Part 5 reconciliation and net income; transfer the correct business/professional income category to the T1. Finish Schedule 8 for CPP. Do not treat CPP as a second T2125 operating expense. Retain the accepted submission confirmation and a copy of the return; check the notice of assessment and pay separately by the earlier balance date. [T4002](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/t4002/t4002-5.html).

The general six-year record rule has exceptions: late-filed returns run from actual filing, long-term property/share-registry records may require indefinite retention, and a CRA preservation notice extends retention. Obtain permission before early destruction or keeping records outside the permitted Canadian location. [CRA records](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/keeping-records/where-keep-your-records-long-request-permission-destroy-them-early.html).

## Worked cases

### Case 1: ordinary contractor, meals and vehicle costs

My client has 2026 gross receipts of $100,000, ordinary deductible costs of $20,000, reasonable business meals of $2,000 with no exception, and actual vehicle running costs of $10,000. A log records 6,000 business kilometres out of 20,000 total; no CCA, interest, lease, parking, GST/HST recovery or home-office claim enters this example. Meals allowed are $1,000 and the vehicle claim is $3,000, leaving T2125 profit of $76,000. For a full-year CPP contributor with no employment contributions, first-tier CPP is $8,460.90 and CPP2 on $1,400 is $112, total $8,572.90. This computes deductions and CPP, not the T1 income tax or refundable credits. [Meals](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/sole-proprietorships-partnerships/report-business-income-expenses/completing-form-t2125/line-8523-meals-entertainment-allowable-part-only.html), [vehicle](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed-income/business-income-tax-reporting/business-expenses/motor-vehicle-expenses.html), [CPP](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html).

```oa-check
name: Alberta contractor deductions and CPP, full-year 2026
inputs:
  revenue: 100000
  ordinary: 20000
  meals: 2000
  vehicle_cost: 10000
  business_km: 6000
  total_km: 20000
steps:
  - meals_allowed: meals * 0.50
  - vehicle_allowed: vehicle_cost * business_km / total_km
  - profit: revenue - ordinary - meals_allowed - vehicle_allowed
  - cpp_first: max(0, min(profit, 74600) - 3500) * 0.119
  - cpp_second: max(0, min(profit, 85000) - 74600) * 0.08
  - cpp_total: cpp_first + cpp_second
expected:
  profit: 76000
  cpp_first: 8460.90
  cpp_second: 112
  cpp_total: 8572.90
```

### Case 2: home-office loss limit and maximum CPP boundary

My client's qualifying, exclusively business-used principal workspace is 15 square metres in a 100-square-metre rented home for the full year. Eligible household costs are $20,000, giving $3,000 allocated costs. Business profit before this claim is only $2,000, so line 9945 deducts $2,000 and carries $1,000 forward; it cannot produce a loss. Separately, a full-year self-employed client with $85,000 profit and no employment CPP reaches $8,460.90 plus $832, total $9,292.90. [Home office](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/t4002/t4002-5.html), [ITA 18(12)](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/I-3.3/section-18.html), [CPP](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/publicpensions/cpp/contributions.html).

```oa-check
name: Home-office ceiling and upper CPP boundary, 2026
inputs:
  home_costs: 20000
  work_area: 15
  total_area: 100
  profit_before_home: 2000
  separate_cpp_profit: 85000
steps:
  - apportioned: home_costs * work_area / total_area
  - home_deduction: min(apportioned, max(0, profit_before_home))
  - carryforward: apportioned - home_deduction
  - cpp_first: (min(separate_cpp_profit, 74600) - 3500) * 0.119
  - cpp_second: max(0, min(separate_cpp_profit, 85000) - 74600) * 0.08
  - cpp_total: cpp_first + cpp_second
expected:
  apportioned: 3000
  home_deduction: 2000
  carryforward: 1000
  cpp_total: 9292.90
```

## When to refuse or refer

- Incorporated contractors, personal services business questions, and any question about whether the contractor is in fact an employee rather than self-employed :  the pages used here do not decide worker status.
- A home-office claim where the principal-place-of-business or exclusive-use/regular-client-meeting test is not established, or proposed CCA on a home without reviewing the capital-gain and recapture consequences.
- Capital cost allowance rates and class determinations beyond the vehicle ceilings tabled above :  the operating expenses page points to Chapter 4 of Guide T4002 and no CCA rates are quoted here.
- The precise maximum annual self-employed base CPP contribution dollar amount for 2026 :  the pages read for this draft state the rate, ceiling and exemption but that dollar maximum is not quoted here, and it must not be calculated.
- Alberta provincial tax rates, brackets and credits, GST/HST filing frequency and return due dates for registrants, and Quebec instalment rules :  none are covered by the tables above.
- Farming and fishing income, which has its own instalment date, and any partnership required to file a partnership information return.
