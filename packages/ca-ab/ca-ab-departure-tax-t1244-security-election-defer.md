# Deferring departure tax with Form T1244 and providing security in Alberta

## Deferring departure tax with Form T1244 and providing security in Alberta

When an individual ceases to be a resident of Canada, the CRA treats certain property as sold at fair market value and immediately reacquired, and the resulting capital gain is commonly called departure tax. An individual who does not want to pay that tax in the year of departure can elect under subsection 220(4.5) of the Income Tax Act, on Form T1244, to defer payment until the property is actually sold or otherwise disposed of; above the federal tax amount in the table below, the election must be backed by adequate security acceptable to the CRA. This Guide covers the election and the security requirement for a person who emigrated from Alberta, for the return covering the year of departure. Figures are for tax year 2026, except that the CRA pages read here are written for the 2025 return year; the amounts below are quoted exactly as those pages state them, and where a year is attached to a statement it is the CRA page's own year (2025).

## Scope

Individuals who ceased to be a resident of Canada for income tax purposes while living in Alberta, who have reported a deemed disposition of property on Form T1243, and who want to defer payment of the tax on that deemed-disposition income rather than pay it with the return for the year of emigration. The CRA states that the election can be made regardless of the amount of tax involved, and that the deferred tax is paid later, without interest, when the property is sold or otherwise disposed of. It is not for the deemed disposition of an employee benefit plan, which the CRA states the election does not apply to. It is not for corporations or trusts, and it does not decide whether the person is in fact a non-resident.

## Ask the client first

- On what date were Canadian residential ties severed, and does the client accept non-resident status from that date, or is residency still in question?
- Which properties appear on Form T1243, and is the Alberta real estate among the property the CRA's dispositions page treats as deemed disposed, or among the property it does not?
- What is the federal tax owing on the deemed-disposition income, so we can tell whether it exceeds the security threshold in the first table above?
- Was the client a resident of Quebec immediately before leaving Canada, which changes the threshold figure that applies?
- What is the client's unencumbered equity in the Alberta property, who else is registered on title, and are there existing mortgages, caveats or matrimonial-property claims against it?
- Does the client expect to return to Canada, which would bring the unwind election and the return of security into play?

### Filing workflow

- **Step 1 :  Fix the date of emigration** :  Fix the date of emigration and record it on the return. Enter the date of departure from Canada on page 1 of the return in the "Residence Information" area, as set out on the CRA's emigrants page.  _(https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/international-non-residents/individuals-leaving-entering-canada-non-residents/leaving-canada-emigrants.html)_
- **Step 2 :  List the property owned on departure** :  List the property owned on departure. Apply the T1161 exclusions before the $25,000 test: exclude cash/deposits, the listed registered/employee plans and trust rights, each personal-use item worth less than $10,000, and qualifying pre-arrival/inherited property for a resident of 60 months or less in the prior 10 years only where that property is not taxable Canadian property. Canadian real estate can require T1161 even though it is excluded from automatic departure tax. Attach the form by the normal filing due date even if no T1 is otherwise required.  _(https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/international-non-residents/individuals-leaving-entering-canada-non-residents/leaving-canada-emigrants.html)_
- **Step 3 :  Identify which property is subject to the deemed disposition** :  Identify which property is actually subject to the deemed disposition. The CRA's dispositions page sets out the property that is and is not treated as disposed of on emigration; work from that list rather than assuming that every Canadian asset, including Alberta real estate, is caught.  _(https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/international-non-residents/individuals-leaving-entering-canada-non-residents/dispositions-property.html)_
- **Step 4 :  Compute and report the deemed disposition** :  Compute and report the deemed disposition. Complete Form T1243, Deemed Disposition of Property by an Emigrant of Canada, and include the resulting capital gains or losses on Schedule 3, Capital Gains (or Losses), of the return for the year of departure.  _(https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/international-non-residents/individuals-leaving-entering-canada-non-residents/dispositions-property.html)_
- **Step 5 :  Make the deferral election** :  Make the deferral election. Complete Form T1244, Election, under Subsection 220(4.5) of the Income Tax Act, to Defer the Payment of Tax on Income Relating to the Deemed Disposition of Property. The CRA states this form is completed by a person who ceased to be a resident of Canada for income tax purposes in the year and is electing to defer payment of tax on income relating to the deemed disposition of a property indicated on Form T1243.  _(https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/forms/t1244.html)_
- **Step 6 :  Meet the election deadline** :  Meet the election deadline in the first table above.  _(https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/international-non-residents/individuals-leaving-entering-canada-non-residents/dispositions-property.html)_
- **Step 7 :  Provide adequate security** :  Provide adequate security if the federal tax owing on the deemed-disposition income exceeds the threshold in the first table, and be ready to provide security for applicable provincial tax as well.  _(https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/international-non-residents/individuals-leaving-entering-canada-non-residents/dispositions-property.html)_
- **Step 8 :  Settle the form of the security with the CRA** :  Settle the form of the security with the CRA before relying on it. IC98-1 lists bank letters of guarantee, standby letters of credit and mortgages; bank instruments should come from a Schedule I or II Canadian financial institution. Security must be liquid, cash-equivalent or near cash-equivalent, and realisable on demand without third-party claims. The related non-resident disposition page directs enquiries to Revenue Collections, but its section 116 interim arrangement is not the T1244 election itself. Get written acceptance for the particular property, amount and documentation.  _(https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/international-non-residents/information-been-moved/disposing-acquiring-certain-canadian-property.html)_
- **Step 9 :  Pay the deferred tax when property is disposed of** :  Pay the deferred tax when the property is sold or otherwise disposed of, as the CRA describes in the first table above.  _(https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/international-non-residents/individuals-leaving-entering-canada-non-residents/dispositions-property.html)_
- **Step 10 :  Consider unwind election on returning to Canada** :  If the client later re-establishes Canadian residency, consider the election to unwind the earlier deemed disposition, made in writing by the deadline in the first table and including a list of the properties owned and the fair market value of each property the election applies to; the CRA states some or all of the security provided may be returned.  _(https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/international-non-residents/individuals-leaving-entering-canada-non-residents/dispositions-property.html)_

### Complete the form and maintain the security

On T1244 identify the taxpayer, mailing address and emigration date. Take taxable income from **T1 line 26000 to line 1**; enter taxable capital gains on emigration at **line 2**, other deemed-disposition income at **line 3**, related deductions at **line 5**, and the net amount at **line 6 / 68900**. **Line 7** is taxable income less line 6, floored at zero. Compute federal tax twice, on lines 1 and 7, and enter the difference at **line 10**; do the same for Alberta Form 428 at **lines 11-13**. The total is **line 14**. This is a tax-with/without comparison, not the whole gain multiplied by the client's last marginal rate. Former Quebec residents leave lines 11-13 blank. [T1244 form](https://www.canada.ca/content/dam/cra-arc/formspubs/pbg/t1244/t1244-22e.pdf).

On page 2 list the selected properties where only some T1243 property is elected; enter the federal and provincial amounts elected, no greater than lines 10 and 13, in **68910** and **68920**. The client signs and dates. The amount/type/expiry of security area marked **Do not use this area** is for CRA. Attach a copy to the departure return and arrange acceptable security before **April 30, 2027** for a 2026 departure; the business-owner June filing extension does not extend T1244. Keep delivery proof, the signed form, valuations, CRA's written security acceptance and any instrument expiry/renewal dates. Do not treat a return acceptance as acceptance of the mortgage. [T1244](https://www.canada.ca/content/dam/cra-arc/formspubs/pbg/t1244/t1244-22e.pdf), [ITA 220(4.5)](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/I-3.3/section-220.html).

The CRA page's $16,500 trigger is not a flat tax rate or a licence to set security from property equity alone. **ITA 220(4.51)** provides deemed security by reference to tax on $50,000 of trust income; **220(4.5)** determines the deferrable amount, including the statutory tax-payment adjustment and continued ownership test, and **220(4.52)** limits the amount. Obtain CRA's actual security requirement rather than assuming that its required collateral always equals either the entire tax or simply the excess over the trigger. Security may need increasing if its value falls: **220(4.53)** gives 90 days after notice to furnish replacement security, subject to **220(4.54)** extensions. Hardship relief under **220(4.7)** is discretionary, not automatic. [ITA 220](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/I-3.3/section-220.html).

When the property is actually disposed of, notify **Non-Resident T1 Adjustments, Winnipeg Tax Centre**, with the property description matching T1243, share count if relevant and actual disposal date. Pay any released deferred amount by **April 30 of the following year**. Do not promise that a partial disposal releases all security or removes destination-country tax. On returning, request any unwind election in writing by the filing due date for that year, with properties still owned and their FMVs. [Dispositions](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/international-non-residents/individuals-leaving-entering-canada-non-residents/dispositions-property.html).

## Worked cases

### Case 1: below the federal security trigger

My Alberta client leaves in 2026. The completed federal return computes $20,000 with the departure income and $8,000 without; Alberta Form 428 computes $7,000 and $4,000 respectively. T1244 lines 10 and 13 are $12,000 and $3,000; line 14 is $15,000. The federal amount is below $16,500, so the CRA form says no federal security is required, but the provincial treatment must still be checked and T1244 is still required by April 30, 2027. These are supplied, fully computed tax results, not invented 2026 tax brackets. [T1244](https://www.canada.ca/content/dam/cra-arc/formspubs/pbg/t1244/t1244-22e.pdf).

```oa-check
name: Alberta T1244 tax differences below federal security trigger
inputs:
  federal_with: 20000
  federal_without: 8000
  provincial_with: 7000
  provincial_without: 4000
steps:
  - line10: federal_with - federal_without
  - line13: provincial_with - provincial_without
  - line14: line10 + line13
  - excess_over_trigger: max(0, line10 - 16500)
expected:
  line10: 12000
  line13: 3000
  line14: 15000
  excess_over_trigger: 0
```

### Case 2: security required, mortgage not automatically accepted

My client has federal tax with/without the deemed income of $40,000/$15,000 and Alberta tax of $15,000/$5,000. The federal difference is $25,000 and provincial difference $10,000, total $35,000. The federal difference exceeds the trigger by $8,500, which establishes the security-review branch; it is **not** a determination that $8,500 alone is the required mortgage. Obtain CRA's statutory security computation and written acceptance of title, priority, equity and terms, with an Alberta lawyer arranging any mortgage. If the client cannot supply acceptable security, do not assert that merely filing T1244 defers the whole bill. [T1244](https://www.canada.ca/content/dam/cra-arc/formspubs/pbg/t1244/t1244-22e.pdf), [ITA 220](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/I-3.3/section-220.html), [IC98-1](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/ic98-1/tax-collections-policies.html).

```oa-check
name: Alberta T1244 above trigger, collateral amount not determined
inputs:
  federal_with: 40000
  federal_without: 15000
  provincial_with: 15000
  provincial_without: 5000
steps:
  - line10: federal_with - federal_without
  - line13: provincial_with - provincial_without
  - line14: line10 + line13
  - trigger_excess: max(0, line10 - 16500)
expected:
  line10: 25000
  line13: 10000
  line14: 35000
  trigger_excess: 8500
```

## When to refuse or refer

- Whether the CRA will accept a charge, mortgage or pledge over Alberta real estate as adequate security, and on what loan-to-value or documentation terms: IC98-1 lists mortgages as a possible form, but it does not approve this property, its priority or a loan-to-value; obtain written CRA acceptance through the collections officer.
- Determining residency status itself, including treaty tie-breaker analysis and Form NR73 requests.
- Valuing the Alberta property or any other asset for the deemed disposition; this needs a qualified valuator or appraiser.
- Drafting, registering or discharging a mortgage, caveat or other charge at Alberta Land Titles; that is legal work for an Alberta lawyer.
- The deemed disposition of an employee benefit plan, which the CRA states the deferral election does not apply to.
- Emigration of corporations, trusts, or estates, and deemed dispositions arising other than on an individual's emigration.
- Later actual sale of Canadian property by the non-resident, including section 116 notification and clearance certificate procedures.
- Tax consequences in the destination country, and any foreign step-up, exit or credit rules.
- Quebec provincial filings and security for a former Quebec resident, which are administered by Revenu Québec and need a separate Quebec method.
