# Trusts versus foundations for family wealth, compared by country

This 2026 source-cited working paper compares legal ownership, tax attribution and register duties. For the UK, current income-tax figures refer to 6 April 2026 to 5 April 2027; the dated Swiss circular and US Code edition are identified separately below. Tax years and charging events must be checked for the actual vehicle and person. This Guide is for a family and its adviser choosing between a trust and a foundation, and choosing where. It covers Malta, the United Kingdom, Switzerland, Liechtenstein, Luxembourg, Cyprus, the United States and Ireland. Jersey and Guernsey remain outside this comparison: ordinary official-page recovery did not produce operative rules for this update. See the individual source limits at the end. It is a working paper for a qualified adviser, not advice.

Read it with three sibling Guides: `succession-and-forced-heirship-compared` (forced heirship and clawback claims against a trust or foundation), `family-holding-company-location-compared` (holding companies) and `mt-foundations-and-trusts` (Malta in full). For US filing duties use `us-foreign-trust-reporting` and `us-estate-gift-706-709`. This Guide does not repeat them.

## The method, step by step

1. Fix the facts first: where the settlor or founder lives and is tax resident, where each beneficiary lives, where the assets sit, and where the trustee or council members will be. Each country below taxes on its own connecting factor, and more than one may apply at once.
2. Decide what the family needs the vehicle to be. A trust has no legal personality: the trustee owns the assets for the beneficiaries. A foundation is a legal person that owns its own assets. The Swiss federal tax administration's circular puts it this way: "Die Stiftung erwirbt mit ihrer Errichtung die juristische Persönlichkeit. Dagegen fehlt es dem Trust an einer eigenen Rechtspersönlichkeit" ([ESTV circular 20 with circular 30 of the Swiss Tax Conference](https://www.estv.admin.ch/dam/de/sd-web/oFjV8C0p36UV/dbst-ks-2008-1-020-dv-de.pdf)).
3. For the place of the vehicle, read that country's section below: Malta ([Income Tax Act, Cap. 123](https://legislation.mt/eli/cap/123/eng/pdf)), the UK ([Trusts and Inheritance Tax](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/trusts-and-inheritance-tax)), Liechtenstein ([Steuergesetz](https://www.gesetze.li/konso/pdf/2010340000?version=38)), Luxembourg ([SPF page](https://impotsdirects.public.lu/fr/az/s/spf.html)), Ireland ([Discretionary Trust Tax](https://www.revenue.ie/en/gains-gifts-and-inheritance/discretionary-trust-tax/index.aspx)).
4. Then test the settlor's country and each beneficiary's country separately. The same trust can be ignored in one country and taxed as a separate taxpayer in another. Switzerland, for example, never attributes trust assets to the trust: they go to the settlor or the beneficiaries, or, for an irrevocable discretionary trust whose settlor lived abroad at creation, to nobody until a distribution ([ESTV circular](https://www.estv.admin.ch/dam/de/sd-web/oFjV8C0p36UV/dbst-ks-2008-1-020-dv-de.pdf)). A US person may be the owner of a foreign trust for income tax under the grantor trust rules ([IRS foreign trust page](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/international-businesses/foreign-trust-reporting-requirements-and-tax-consequences)).
5. Check the settlor-interest trap in every relevant country: if the settlor or founder can revoke, or can benefit, most of these systems keep taxing the settlor (UK, Switzerland, Liechtenstein and the US sections below).
6. List the register duties: UK Trust Registration Service, Malta's trust beneficial owner register at the MFSA, Ireland's CRBOT, Cyprus's CyTBOR, and Liechtenstein's foundation registration or formation notice. Each has its own trigger and deadline, set out below.
7. Check forced heirship before funding. A transfer that cuts an heir's reserved share can be challenged after death. Use `succession-and-forced-heirship-compared`.
8. Write down which country taxes what, at which event (funding, each year, distribution, termination, death), and which points are open.

## Malta

Malta has both vehicles. A trust exists where a trustee holds property under an obligation to deal with it "for the benefit of persons (called the beneficiaries)" ([Trusts and Trustees Act, article 3](https://legislation.mt/eli/cap/331/eng/pdf)). A foundation is an organisation consisting of a "universality of things constituted in writing", and its assets and liabilities are distinct from those of its founder, administrators and beneficiaries ([Civil Code, Second Schedule, article 26](https://legislation.mt/eli/cap/16/eng/pdf)). Legal personality "shall only be acquired by an organisation on its registration with the Registrar for Legal Persons", unless another law grants it ([Civil Code, Second Schedule](https://legislation.mt/eli/cap/16/eng/pdf)). For a private foundation, unless the founder has expressly waived confidentiality, "all documents, statements or declarations submitted to the Registrar" are not open to third parties without the administrators' or supervisory council's written consent "duly authenticated by a Notary Public", or the court's permission for someone with "a legitimate interest"; the Note of Initial Registration that must be filed "shall be accessible to the public" (Second Schedule, article 31C).

Tax on a trust ([Income Tax Act, articles 27B to 27D](https://legislation.mt/eli/cap/123/eng/pdf)):

- **Scope.** Tax is payable on income attributable to a trust "Where at least one of the trustees of a trust is a person" resident in Malta (article 27B). The trustee answers for the tax; two or more trustees are jointly and severally answerable (article 27C).
- **Option 1, taxed as a company.** A Malta-resident trustee that is authorised under article 43(3) of the Trusts and Trustees Act, or does not need that authorisation under article 43(6), may elect irrevocably to have the trust's income computed "as if such income was derived by a company ordinarily resident and domiciled in Malta". The election is open only where the trust instrument limits the trust's income to royalties, dividends, capital gains, interest, rents or other investment income. The form must reach the Commissioner within thirty days of the trust's establishment or of the resident trustee's appointment, whichever is later. Distributions are then treated as dividends (article 27D(1)).
- **Final tax option.** A trust using article 27D(1) is an entity eligible for the separate final-tax election under [S.L. 123.217, regulations 2–3](https://legislation.mt/eli/sl/123.217/eng/pdf). The headline rate is 15% on eligible chargeable income, but the tax cannot be below ordinary tax less all shareholders’ article 48(4)/(4A) refunds claimed or claimable, applying article 43(6) where relevant. Existing-final-tax income and dividends from profits not allocated to another Malta company’s final-tax account are excluded from this base. The election applies from the assessment year elected, available from assessment year 2025, by the Commissioner’s form and deadline. It binds for at least five assessment years; returning to ordinary treatment also binds for at least five consecutive assessment years. The tax is final, carries no credit/set-off/refund, and the relevant profits move to the final-tax account. Do not promise a trust-beneficiary refund: eligibility for a particular distribution has not been established here. Where no refund is claimable, the floor is ordinary tax; the 15% headline does not guarantee a saving. Refer before electing; see `mt-foundations-and-trusts`.
- **Look-through for foreign income and non-resident beneficiaries.** Without the company election or a temporary-trust determination under article 27D(2), where all the trust's income arises outside Malta (or is income under article 12(1)(c)), and all beneficiaries are not ordinarily resident in Malta or not domiciled in Malta, or are fully exempt, the income is treated as derived directly by the beneficiaries and not by the trust. Receipt in Malta by the trustee is deemed receipt in Malta by those beneficiaries; the trustee must notify their income and duties (article 27D(3)(b)). A separate article 27D(3)(c) route covers its specified foreign/exempt income and Malta taxed-account dividends where every beneficiary is nonresident and the trustee supplies the article 5(3) certificate. Neither route follows merely from calling a trust foreign.
- **Default.** Absent those elections, determinations or direct-attribution routes, the trust is taxed as if it were a person ordinarily resident and domiciled in Malta, but amounts allocated to beneficiaries are excluded, provided the trustee files the return and gives the beneficiaries a certificate (article 27D(3)(e) and (4)). For amounts vested in or granted to beneficiaries but not distributed by 31 March after the year, the exclusion also needs a tax payment on the beneficiaries' behalf at the article 56(6) rate. The trustee owes it by 30 June after that year, and the beneficiary gets a credit (article 27D(4)(b)(iii)).
- **Rate.** The trust rate is "the rate specified in article 56(6)", which the Act writes as "thirty-five cents (0.35) on every euro of the chargeable income" (articles 27D(5) and 56(6)) Article 27D(5) excepts article 56(4): its 20% clergy-income class has a further exemption for the part actually applied to Malta-resident clergy or ecclesiastical communities to the Commissioner’s satisfaction; that is a separate restricted route, not the ordinary family-trust rate.
- **Beneficiaries.** Allocated amounts are added to the beneficiary's other income, and "Income distributed to beneficiaries shall retain its character as to type and country of source" (article 27D(8)). That paragraph also covers trusts without a Malta-resident trustee where the stated beneficiary residence/domicile or Malta-receipt nexus applies. Article 27D(10) lets the Commissioner disregard a trust used with the sole or main purpose of reducing a person’s tax.
- **Capital gains on settlement.** Putting property into a written trust is not always a neutral act. Article 5(18) deems no transfer only "where the sole settlor is also the sole beneficiary". The direct-donation limb requires the instrument’s irrevocable vested right to all settled property, only the specified article 5(2)(e)(i)/(ii) family classes relative to every settlor (possibly with the settlor), and an existing beneficiary. The no-gain/no-loss limb instead requires only the article 5(2)(e)(i) class (possibly the settlor), an existing beneficiary, and the statutory incapacity, impairment, inability or irregular/dissolute-lifestyle condition with the Commissioner’s required evidence. The exact incorporated family definitions and any later transfer charge need the transaction-specific check. Read `mt-foundations-and-trusts` before settling Maltese property.

Tax on a foundation ([Foundations (Income Tax) Regulations, S.L. 123.114](https://legislation.mt/eli/sl/123.114/eng/pdf)):

- A foundation is treated, for the purposes of the Income Tax Acts, "in the same manner as a company that is ordinarily" resident and domiciled in Malta, at the article 56(6) rate (regulation 3(1)). This does not apply to a foundation enrolled under the Voluntary Organisations Act, or a non-profit social-purpose foundation, unless it opts irrevocably in writing; otherwise it is taxed at the article 56(1)(d) rate, subject to article 56(9)(b) (regulation 3(5)).
- Distributions to beneficiaries "shall be treated as if they were dividends distributed to shareholders of a company" (regulation 3(4)).
- The administrators may, by notice in writing to the Commissioner, irrevocably elect that a foundation "shall be taxed under the provisions of the Act applicable to trusts" (regulation 4(1)).

Register: a trustee authorised or registered under articles 43 or 43B of the Trusts and Trustees Act, or a private trustee under article 43A, must send the MFSA a declaration of beneficial ownership within fourteen (14) days of "being appointed as a trustee of an express trust" ([S.L. 331.10, regulation 3](https://legislation.mt/eli/sl/331.10/eng/pdf)). A change is notified within fourteen days after the trustee records it (regulation 5). A trustee established or residing outside the EU who enters into a business relationship or acquires real estate in Malta as trustee files the same declaration within fourteen days (regulation 3A). Every trustee confirms each year, by 31 January, that nothing changed in the previous calendar year other than notified changes (regulation 5(2)). The current compilation includes L.N. 252 of 2026. A company trustee’s declaration/change/annual confirmation needs at least two directors’ signatures; another corporate trustee needs at least two managers. Regulation 4 permits the MFSA to accept another Member State’s register certificate/excerpt for the stated co-trustee or multiple-business-relationship cases; this is not an automatic exemption. The class-beneficiary birth/marriage rule has its own three-month notification period under regulation 3(3), rather than the general 14-day change period.

**Entry charge.** For most relevant trusts, aggregate the transfer and prior seven years’ chargeable transfers against GBP 325,000; 20% is the lifetime rate if trustees bear the tax. Settlor-paid tax increases the loss to the estate and requires grossing. A death within seven years can cause recalculation at 40% with credit for lifetime tax and applicable taper relief, rather than a universal extra 20%. The old summary page gives conflicting payer descriptions. [HMRC IHTM14592](https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm14592) distinguishes the transferor’s immediately-chargeable liability from the transferee’s liability for additional charges; [IHTM14012](https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm14012) confirms primary transferor/secondary transferee liability on entry, grossing, and no transferor liability for the further death charge. Do not assign that further charge automatically to the personal representative merely from the old summary wording; establish the liable persons for the particular transfer.

**Ten-year and exit charges.** Trustees pay a charge on every 10-year anniversary "if your trust contains relevant property with a value above the Inheritance Tax threshold", on the net value the day before the anniversary. Exit charges arise when relevant property leaves the trust, with exceptions, including transfers "within 3 months of setting up a trust, or within 3 months following a 10 year anniversary". The rate on each is a computed rate up to the maximum in the tables, not a flat rate. For a chargeable event on or after 6 April 2014, trustees pay and report on IHT100 by the end of the sixth month after the event ([Trusts and Inheritance Tax](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/trusts-and-inheritance-tax)).

**Not every trust is relevant property.** The exceptions on the gov.uk page are property put into an interest in possession trust before 22 March 2006, transitional serial interests, interest in possession trusts made by will or intestacy, and trusts for a disabled person, a bereaved minor or an "18 to 25" trust ([Trusts and Inheritance Tax](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/trusts-and-inheritance-tax)).

**Residence-based rules from 6 April 2025.** For foreign settled property, test the living settlor’s long-term UK residence at the charge. For a settlor dying on or after that date, the relevant status is fixed at death; for one who died earlier the old domicile-at-settlement test remains. A qualifying interest-in-possession beneficiary can impose an additional residence test. Transitional relief for property already excluded on 30 October 2024 is separate. A settlor ceasing to be long-term UK resident can trigger a proportionate exit charge ([IHTM47050](https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm47050); [IHTM47052](https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm47052)). The general test uses at least 10 of the previous 20 tax years, but the post-departure tail can be three to ten years: ten years’ nonresidence is the manual example, not a universal waiting period. Retain the 30 October 2024 non-dom/deemed-dom and 2025/26 nonresident/non-return transitional branches, and the reset after ten consecutive nonresident years ([HMRC long-term residence guidance](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/inheritance-tax-if-youre-a-long-term-uk-resident)).

**Enacted charge cap.** [Finance Act 2026 section 74, inserting IHTA 1984 section 75B](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2026/11/pdfs/ukpga_20260011_en.pdf?timeline=false) applies retrospectively from 6 April 2025, replacing the old proposed/elective description. It covers section 64/65 charges on property already in the settlement before 30 October 2024 and excluded immediately before that date under former section 48(3)/(3A), which immediately before the charge is either outside the UK and outside Schedule A1 paragraphs 2/3, or an authorised-unit-trust holding/OEIC share. The first relevant period runs from 6 April 2025 to the first subsequent ten-year anniversary: its cap is GBP 125,000 times the whole successive quarters in that period. Each later ten-year period has a GBP 5 million cap. Reduce the otherwise chargeable tax to the cap less earlier section 65 charges in that period on qualifying property, never below zero. This is not a cap for every trust or all its assets. Section 73 separately disapplies specified exit exemptions after its defined long-term-residence change, effective 26 November 2025; the old three-month exception cannot be assumed without that overlay.

**Income tax.** For income used to pay qualifying trust-management expenses the source gives 10.75% on dividends (8.75% through 5 April 2026) and 20% on other income; this is distinct from the general trust rates. Trustees of accumulation or discretionary trusts pay at the rates in the third table. The tax-free amount is a cliff: "Tax is due on the full amount if the income is more than the tax-free amount", and trustees do not get the dividend allowance. With several such trusts, the settlor's tax-free amount is divided by the number of trusts, down to the per-trust floor in the table ([Trusts and Income Tax](https://www.gov.uk/trusts-taxes/trusts-and-income-tax)).

**Settlor-interested trusts.** A trust is settlor-interested where "the settlor or their spouse or civil partner benefits from the trust" ([Types of trust](https://www.gov.uk/trusts-taxes/types-of-trust)). "The settlor is responsible for Income Tax on these trusts, even if some of the income is not paid out to them." The trustees still pay as income arrives and give the settlor a statement ([Trusts and Income Tax](https://www.gov.uk/trusts-taxes/trusts-and-income-tax)). For inheritance tax, a settlor who gives assets into trust but keeps a benefit pays the entry charge and the gift still counts in the estate; HMRC applies only the higher of the two charges ([Trusts and Inheritance Tax](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/trusts-and-inheritance-tax)).

**Capital gains allowance.** The ordinary 2026/27 allowance is GBP 1,500, with GBP 3,000 for the disabled-beneficiary class. Multiple-settlement division applies under its conditions; the source’s worked GBP 1,200 cap is an old-year illustration, not the 2026 allowance ([HMRC trust CGT guidance, allowance section](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/trusts-and-capital-gains-tax)).

**Trust Registration Service.** Every UK resident express trust must register unless it is excluded under Schedule 3A. A non-UK resident trust must register if it becomes liable to income tax, capital gains tax, inheritance tax or the UK land transaction taxes on UK assets or income, or if it is an express trust with listed UK links, such as acquiring UK land after 6 October 2020. The current general Schedule 3A exclusion requires all of: no UK land; no appreciable-worth assets over GBP 2,000; no cumulative property over GBP 10,000; annual income not over GBP 5,000; no UK tax liability. Only one such trust per settlor qualifies, ignoring trusts under other exclusions. Other Schedule 3A categories remain separate and a taxable trust may still need registration. A non-taxable trust created after 6 October 2020 registers within 90 days of being created or becoming liable, or by 1 September 2022 if later. A taxable trust created on or after 6 April 2021 registers within 90 days of becoming liable, or by 1 September 2022 if later. For a taxable trust created before 6 April 2021 the deadline is 5 October or 31 January after the tax year, depending on the tax and on whether the trust was liable for income tax or capital gains tax before. A non-UK trust that acquired an interest in UK land before 6 October 2020 and still held it on 30 June 2026 must register by 1 September 2027, but the Trust Registration Service "currently will not let you register these trusts" ([Register a trust](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/check-if-you-need-to-register-a-trust)).

## Switzerland

The operative trust basis read is ESTV circular 20 dated 27 March 2008, adopting SSK circular 30 of 22 August 2007, not a newly enacted 2026 trust code. Its sections 2.5, 3.7, 4–6 distinguish recognition from tax: the Hague Convention took effect on 1 July 2007 without determining tax. A trust has no separate personality/taxpayer status in that circular. Trustee/protector fees are taxable, but the fund and its income are not theirs merely because of their office. Classification follows actual retained powers, not an “irrevocable” label. [ESTV / SSK circular](https://www.estv.admin.ch/dam/de/sd-web/oFjV8C0p36UV/dbst-ks-2008-1-020-dv-de.pdf)

- **Revocable:** attribute fund and income to the settlor at their residence. A payment to another beneficiary is treated as a gift, with cantonal gift-tax jurisdiction/rate separately established.
- **Irrevocable fixed interest:** attribute the relevant share of fund/income to the beneficiary; funding is a gift to that beneficiary. Income is recognized on receipt or an enforceable claim. Proven contributed capital and private-asset capital gains can be distributed without income tax; without proof the receipt is income. Contributed capital follows distribution of all trust income.
- **Irrevocable discretionary, Swiss-resident settlor at creation:** the fund and income remain attributed to the settlor, subject to the circular’s expenditure-tax/control-computation qualification and anti-abuse reservation.
- **Irrevocable discretionary, foreign-resident settlor at creation:** the circular attributes the fund to neither settlor nor beneficiary. Funding is a gift; a Swiss-taxable beneficiary is taxed on receipt or a firm claim, generally as income, except proven contributed capital already treated as a gift. Income is distributed before capital. This beneficiary cannot exclude a distribution as their private capital gain merely because the trust realized gains.
- **Disclosure:** Swiss-taxable settlors, trustees and beneficiaries must supply the trust instruments and relevant evidence. A lawyer acting as trustee cannot invoke professional secrecy for this tax-audit task.

Those are the circular’s bounded estate-planning/asset-protection fact patterns, not all commercial trusts. The [Federal Office of Justice project record](https://www.bj.admin.ch/de/einfuehrung-eines-schweizer-trusts) records the domestic-trust project being abandoned and the motion closed on 27 February 2024; it is dated history, not proof of every subsequent private-law change. The April 2026 [ESTV corporate-tax dossier, sections 3.3 and 4.5](https://www.estv2.admin.ch/stp/ds/d-besteuerung-juristischen-personen-gesamter-text-de.pdf) separately treats a Swiss foundation as a legal person: ordinary federal net-profit tax is 4.25%, with profits strictly below CHF 5,000 exempt; no federal capital tax, but cantonal profit/capital regimes and exemptions vary. Endowments do not form taxable profit. A charitable/public-purpose exemption requires its substantive exclusive/irrevocable-purpose conditions; a family label does not confer it. Current individual cantonal tariffs and post-project domestic-trust enactment are not established here.

## Liechtenstein

Liechtenstein has both. A foundation is a legally and economically independent purpose fund: the PGR calls it a "verselbständigtes Zweckvermögen, welches als Verbandsperson (juristische Person) durch die einseitige Willenserklärung des Stifters errichtet wird" ([PGR Art. 552 § 1](https://www.gesetze.li/konso/pdf/1926004000?version=136)). The trustee of a Treuhänderschaft receives assets or a right from the settlor (Treugeber) "mit der Verpflichtung zuwendet, dieses als Treugut im eigenen Namen als selbständiger Rechtsträger zu Gunsten eines oder mehrerer Dritter (Begünstigter) oder für einen bestimmten Zweck" to manage or use them ([PGR Art. 897](https://www.gesetze.li/konso/pdf/1926004000?version=136)). The trustee, not the trust, is the legal holder. PGR Art. 552 §14 requires commercial-register entry for charitable foundations and private-benefit foundations conducting the specially permitted commercial business; other private-benefit foundations may register voluntarily. A foundation that need not be entered in the commercial register must deposit a formation notice with the Office of Justice "innerhalb von 30 Tagen ab Errichtung", and a change notice within 30 days of a change (PGR Art. 552 § 20).

## United States

The US distinguishes grantor and non-grantor trusts for income tax. Where the grantor trust rules treat the grantor or another person as owner of any portion of a trust, that person's taxable income includes the items of income, deductions and credits of that portion ([26 U.S.C. 671, official 2024 edition](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title26/html/USCODE-2024-title26-subtitleA-chap1-subchapJ-partI-subpartE-sec671.htm)). The remaining portion is subject to subparts A–D under section 671; non-grantor trusts have a separate estate/trust rate table. The official Code text recovered is the 2024 edition; the current IRS foreign-trust guidance and [Rev. Proc. 2025-32 section 4.01 Table 5 for 2026](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/rp-25-32.pdf) corroborate these bounded distinctions. The full rate computation is in `us-form-1041-trust-and-estate-income`.

For foreign trusts, "a U.S. person who is treated as the owner of a foreign trust under the grantor trust rules (IRC sections 671-679) is taxed on the income of that trust", and a US beneficiary of a foreign non-grantor trust reports their share of distributable net income ([IRS foreign trust page](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/international-businesses/foreign-trust-reporting-requirements-and-tax-consequences)). Who owns a foreign trust, the throwback tax and Forms 3520 and 3520-A are in `us-foreign-trust-reporting`. ## Traps

- **The settlor is still taxed.** A UK settlor-interested trust leaves income tax with the settlor. A Swiss settlor who creates even an irrevocable discretionary trust while resident in Switzerland keeps the assets and income for Swiss tax. A Liechtenstein revocable foundation is the founder's. A US grantor trust's income is the grantor's. Ask who can revoke and who can benefit before choosing the vehicle (sections above).
- **A beneficiary in a high-tax country.** A low-tax vehicle does not lower the beneficiary's own tax. A Swiss beneficiary of a foreign-settlor discretionary trust is taxed on distributions as income unless they prove a return of contributed capital. A Malta beneficiary's allocation keeps its character and source. A US beneficiary of a foreign non-grantor trust faces DNI and throwback rules (`us-foreign-trust-reporting`).
- **Moving changes the answer.** A UK settlor who becomes long-term UK resident brings foreign trust property into inheritance tax, and one who stops being long-term resident triggers an exit charge (UK section). Leaving the UK does not necessarily end long-term residence at once: the manual’s ten-consecutive-year example is subject to the shorter tail, transitional and reset branches above.
- **Malta election deadline.** The Malta trust-as-company election must be filed within thirty days and cannot be undone (Malta section).
- **Irish DTT is a charge on capital.** Both DTT charges are on the market value of the trust assets, not on income. A beneficiary's CAT on a benefit appointed out of the trust is separate (Ireland section); income tax on trust income is not covered here.
- **Forced heirship.** In a country with reserved shares for heirs, a trust or foundation funded during life can face a clawback claim after death, whatever its tax status. Use `succession-and-forced-heirship-compared`.
- **Registers are separate from tax.** A trust with no tax to pay may still have to register (UK, Malta, Ireland, Cyprus). The UK penalty for failing to register is in the UK table.

## Ask the client first

- Where are the settlor or founder and each beneficiary resident? For the UK, obtain the full previous 20-tax-year history, departure/return dates and 30 October 2024 domicile/deemed-domicile status, rather than using “about ten years” as the legal test.
- Must the settlor or founder be able to revoke the vehicle, change beneficiaries, or benefit from it?
- What assets go in (listed shares, a trading company, real estate, and where the real estate is) and do any beneficiaries need fixed rights or only discretionary hope?
- Where will the trustee, council members or administrators be resident, and where will the vehicle be managed?
- Does any heir have a reserved share under the law of the settlor's nationality, residence or the asset's location?
- Has anyone already made chargeable gifts, or set up other trusts on the same day or earlier?

## When to refuse or refer

- Refer every choice of vehicle and place to a qualified adviser in the settlor's country and in each beneficiary's country before funding. This Guide compares rules; it does not design a structure.
- Refer the UK computed rates for ten-year and exit charges, the 2025 residence-based transition, and the enacted section 75B cap’s property eligibility, first-period quarters and prior-charge aggregation; refer transaction-specific application, not enactment that is now established.
- Refer any Malta final tax election, and any promise that beneficiaries of a Malta trust or foundation can take an article 48(4A) refund: the page does not settle the refund, and without one the final tax saves nothing (`mt-foundations-and-trusts`).
- Refuse to state the unresolved Cyprus trust tax, current Swiss cantonal foundation tariff/domestic-trust enactment, Jersey or Guernsey rules from this Guide. Luxembourg subscription tax and the bounded federal Swiss foundation treatment are now source-supported above; they do not establish every vehicle’s eligibility or tax outcome.
- Refer any forced heirship exposure to a succession lawyer (`succession-and-forced-heirship-compared`).
- Refer US persons to `us-foreign-trust-reporting` and `us-estate-gift-706-709`; refer anyone giving up US citizenship or long-term residence.
- Refuse a structure whose stated purpose is to hide assets from a register, a tax authority or an heir.
