# Pillar Two GLOBE Minimum Tax

## What this file is

Read `cross-border-workflow-base` first. This Guide supplies a jurisdictional computation and review method for Pillar Two / GloBE, not a substitute for an implementing country's law, return or payment rules. The OECD Model Rules are a common approach; the EU Directive also covers large-scale domestic groups. Apply the law and agreed guidance effective for the actual fiscal year, including relief elections and their conditions. [S01](<https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2021/12/tax-challenges-arising-from-digitalisation-of-the-economy-global-anti-base-erosion-model-rules-pillar-two_eed81a23/782bac33-en.pdf>), [S02](<https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2026/05/tax-challenges-arising-from-the-digitalisation-of-the-economy-consolidated-commentary-to-the-global-anti-base-erosion-model-rules-2026_24289ddf/4377e89f-en.pdf>), [S05](<https://publications.europa.eu/resource/celex/32022L2523>)

**Currency: 16 November 2026.** The base Model Rules' operative Chapters 1–10 were read in full. The EU Directive's operative Articles 1–59 were read in full and reconciled to the published instrument. Selected 2026 Commentary sections, the September guidance, the May clarification, the updated Central Record and the source scopes below supply the current overlays. ## Quick reference

The ordinary revenue threshold is EUR 750 million in at least 2 of the preceding 4 fiscal years; the minimum rate is 15%. For fiscal years beginning in calendar 2026, the OECD SBIE schedule uses 9.4% eligible payroll and 7.4% eligible tangible assets, subject to the actual eligibility and national period rules. Base GIR filing is 15 months after year-end, or 18 months for the relevant Transition Year; local registration, domestic returns and payment are separate. [S01](<https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2021/12/tax-challenges-arising-from-digitalisation-of-the-economy-global-anti-base-erosion-model-rules-pillar-two_eed81a23/782bac33-en.pdf>) Arts 1.1, 5, 8.1, 9.2, 9.4; [S05](<https://publications.europa.eu/resource/celex/32022L2523>) Arts 2, 26–28, 44, 48, 51; [S12](<https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/minstg/__62.html>).

## Scope test and exclusions

**[T1] Revenue test.** The base OECD test is consolidated revenue of at least EUR 750 million in at least 2 of the 4 fiscal years immediately preceding the tested fiscal year. Use UPE consolidated revenue, not a segment or the tested year's revenue. A shorter or longer preceding fiscal year requires proportionate adjustment of the threshold for that year. Do not apply the old instruction to annualise the tested year's revenue using `365 / days`. Merger/demerger rules can change which revenue histories count. [S01](<https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2021/12/tax-challenges-arising-from-digitalisation-of-the-economy-global-anti-base-erosion-model-rules-pillar-two_eed81a23/782bac33-en.pdf>) Arts 1.1, 6.1; [S05](<https://publications.europa.eu/resource/celex/32022L2523>) Arts 2, 33

The group includes entities consolidated line by line and entities omitted solely for size/materiality or being held for sale. Each PE is a separate CE; Excluded Entities are not CEs. The OECD group must be multinational, including a main entity with a foreign PE. The EU Directive also brings large-scale domestic groups within scope and includes excluded-entity revenue in the scope threshold. Determine CE location under the applicable rules, including treaty residence, PE type, flow-through status and dual-location cases; place of incorporation alone does not settle every case. [S01](<https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2021/12/tax-challenges-arising-from-digitalisation-of-the-economy-global-anti-base-erosion-model-rules-pillar-two_eed81a23/782bac33-en.pdf>) Arts 1.2–1.3, 10.3; [S05](<https://publications.europa.eu/resource/celex/32022L2523>) Arts 2–4

Excluded entities include governmental entities, international organisations, non-profits, pension funds, investment funds that are UPEs and real-estate investment vehicles that are UPEs. The extension to owned entities is conditional: at least 95% of value owned by qualifying excluded entities, directly or through a chain of them, plus the specified asset-holding/investment or ancillary-activity condition; or at least 85% of value so owned where substantially all income consists of Excluded Dividends or Excluded Equity Gains or Losses. Pension Services Entities are not qualifying owners for these 95%/85% extensions. A 5-year election not to treat an otherwise qualifying owned entity as excluded must be checked. Exclusion of a parent does not automatically exclude every subsidiary. [S01](<https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2021/12/tax-challenges-arising-from-digitalisation-of-the-economy-global-anti-base-erosion-model-rules-pillar-two_eed81a23/782bac33-en.pdf>) Art 1.5; [S05](<https://publications.europa.eu/resource/celex/32022L2523>) Art 2(3)–(4)

## The method, step by step

1. Resolve scope, location, exclusions, ownership changes and each relevant fiscal year. If outside the applicable scope, stop this GloBE liability calculation and document the result; a separate below-threshold or scope notification is required only where actual local rules require it. Record national charging-law and safe-harbour versions before calculating. [S01](<https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2021/12/tax-challenges-arising-from-digitalisation-of-the-economy-global-anti-base-erosion-model-rules-pillar-two_eed81a23/782bac33-en.pdf>) Arts 1, 6, 10
2. Build the CE register and separate computation pools for stateless entities, minority-owned subgroups/standalone entities, JVs and investment entities where required. Ordinary jurisdictional blending is not a licence to blend every entity in the same country. ### Adjusted Covered Taxes and deferred tax

Covered Taxes include defined income/profit taxes, certain distribution-system taxes, taxes in lieu of a generally applicable corporate income tax and defined equity/retained-earnings taxes. Exclude qualified IIR, UTPR and QDMTT taxes, disqualified refundable imputation taxes and taxes paid by an insurance company in respect of returns to policyholders. “Minimum tax” in a tax's name is not enough to classify it. [S01](<https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2021/12/tax-challenges-arising-from-digitalisation-of-the-economy-global-anti-base-erosion-model-rules-pillar-two_eed81a23/782bac33-en.pdf>) Art 4.2; [S05](<https://publications.europa.eu/resource/celex/32022L2523>) Art 20

Start with accrued current Covered Taxes and make the specified additions/reductions; uncertain-tax accruals, later payments, refunds/credits, tax on excluded income, current tax not expected to be paid within 3 years and relevant OCI/equity tax require their own treatment. Include no tax more than once. Allocate PE, tax-transparent, CFC, hybrid and distribution taxes under the precise Article 4.3 rules, including the passive-income cap; a tax paid by an owner is not automatically a CE-level tax. [S01](<https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2021/12/tax-challenges-arising-from-digitalisation-of-the-economy-global-anti-base-erosion-model-rules-pillar-two_eed81a23/782bac33-en.pdf>) Arts 4.1–4.3; [S05](<https://publications.europa.eu/resource/celex/32022L2523>) Arts 21, 24

The Total Deferred Tax Adjustment Amount generally uses the booked tax rate if below 15% and otherwise recasts at 15%, with exclusions for tax on excluded items, disallowed/unclaimed accruals, valuation/recognition adjustments, rate remeasurement and tax-credit generation/use. A DTA attributable to a GloBE Loss can be recast to 15% when its conditions are met; do not apply a blanket lower-of rule to erase that exception. [S01](<https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2021/12/tax-challenges-arising-from-digitalisation-of-the-economy-global-anti-base-erosion-model-rules-pillar-two_eed81a23/782bac33-en.pdf>) Art 4.4; [S05](<https://publications.europa.eu/resource/celex/32022L2523>) Art 22

**DTL recapture.** Track claimed DTL categories and reversals. Except for Recapture Exception Accruals, a DTL not reversed by the end of the 5th subsequent fiscal year is removed from Adjusted Covered Taxes in its original inclusion year and that year's ETR/top-up tax is recomputed. It is not simply added to current-year Covered Taxes. The Commentary clarifies that “payment” in these recapture provisions means the accounting reversal of the DTL. A later reversal has its prescribed corresponding treatment. [S01](<https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2021/12/tax-challenges-arising-from-digitalisation-of-the-economy-global-anti-base-erosion-model-rules-pillar-two_eed81a23/782bac33-en.pdf>) Arts 4.4.2, 4.4.4; [S02](<https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2026/05/tax-challenges-arising-from-the-digitalisation-of-the-economy-consolidated-commentary-to-the-global-anti-base-erosion-model-rules-2026_24289ddf/4377e89f-en.pdf>) Art 4.4.4 paras 89–90.5

Recapture exceptions are defined categories: tangible-asset cost recovery; government licences/arrangements to use immovable property or exploit natural resources involving significant tangible investment; R&D; decommissioning/remediation; unrealised net gains from fair-value accounting; net FX gains; insurance reserves and deferred acquisition costs; specified reinvested tangible-property gains; and associated accounting-principle changes. There is no blanket exception for every lease DTL. Tracking aggregation and mixed exception/non-exception accounts have further conditions; do not claim an exclusion merely from an account label. [S01](<https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2021/12/tax-challenges-arising-from-digitalisation-of-the-economy-global-anti-base-erosion-model-rules-pillar-two_eed81a23/782bac33-en.pdf>) Art 4.4.5; [S02](<https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2026/05/tax-challenges-arising-from-the-digitalisation-of-the-economy-consolidated-commentary-to-the-global-anti-base-erosion-model-rules-2026_24289ddf/4377e89f-en.pdf>) Art 4.4.4 paras 90–90.8; [S05](<https://publications.europa.eu/resource/celex/32022L2523>) Art 22(8)

Have the practitioner review the choice. A jurisdictional GloBE Loss election creates a GloBE Loss DTA at 15% instead of the Article 4.4 approach, with use limited by available DTA and subsequent positive income. It is made with the first relevant GIR and is revocable, but revocation reduces the remaining election DTA to zero; it is unavailable to an Eligible Distribution Tax System jurisdiction. Do not claim an irreversible lock-in or that “most groups prefer” one method without evidence. [S01](<https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2021/12/tax-challenges-arising-from-digitalisation-of-the-economy-global-anti-base-erosion-model-rules-pillar-two_eed81a23/782bac33-en.pdf>) Art 4.5; [S05](<https://publications.europa.eu/resource/celex/32022L2523>) Art 23

### Jurisdictional ETR, excess profit and SBIE

For an ordinary positive-income jurisdictional pool:

`SBIE = payroll carve-out rate × eligible payroll + tangible-asset carve-out rate × eligible average carrying value`

`ETR = Adjusted Covered Taxes / Net GloBE Income`

`Top-up Tax Percentage = max(0, 15% − ETR)`

`Excess Profit = max(0, Net GloBE Income − SBIE)`

`Jurisdictional Top-up Tax = max(0, Top-up Tax Percentage × Excess Profit + Additional Current Top-up Tax − QDMTT payable)`

These are base calculation relationships, not a replacement for special pools, safe harbours or local charging rules. The EU formula images were visually read; its Article 27 also governs entity allocation and the treatment where no current-year net income exists. [S01](<https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2021/12/tax-challenges-arising-from-digitalisation-of-the-economy-global-anti-base-erosion-model-rules-pillar-two_eed81a23/782bac33-en.pdf>) Arts 5.1–5.4; [S05](<https://publications.europa.eu/resource/celex/32022L2523>) Arts 26–29

Eligible payroll includes qualifying employee remuneration, benefits, payroll employment taxes and employer social-security contributions and independent contractors participating in ordinary operations under the group's direction and control. Exclude payroll capitalised in eligible tangible assets and payroll attributable to excluded shipping income. Determine actual location under the applicable guidance; an employee count alone does not supply payroll cost. [S01](<https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2021/12/tax-challenges-arising-from-digitalisation-of-the-economy-global-anti-base-erosion-model-rules-pillar-two_eed81a23/782bac33-en.pdf>) Arts 5.3.3, 10.1; [S05](<https://publications.europa.eu/resource/celex/32022L2523>) Art 28

Eligible tangible assets include qualifying property, plant and equipment; natural resources; a lessee's right-of-use of tangible assets; and specified government licences/arrangements for immovable property or natural resources involving significant tangible investment. Use the average beginning/end net carrying value, with depreciation, amortisation, depletion and capitalised payroll as specified. Inventory does not become eligible tangible property merely by being located in the jurisdiction. Exclude property held for sale, lease or investment and assets linked to excluded shipping income; apply PE and flow-through allocation rules. An election may omit SBIE, but omitting a supported exclusion needs an explicit recorded choice. [S01](<https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2021/12/tax-challenges-arising-from-digitalisation-of-the-economy-global-anti-base-erosion-model-rules-pillar-two_eed81a23/782bac33-en.pdf>) Art 5.3; [S05](<https://publications.europa.eu/resource/celex/32022L2523>) Art 28

The ordinary de minimis election uses the current and preceding 2 fiscal years, excluding years with no relevant CEs in the jurisdiction as the rule specifies. Stateless CEs and investment entities are outside this election's ordinary computation. Do not substitute single-year CbCR thresholds for this test. [S01](<https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2021/12/tax-challenges-arising-from-digitalisation-of-the-economy-global-anti-base-erosion-model-rules-pillar-two_eed81a23/782bac33-en.pdf>) Art 5.5; [S05](<https://publications.europa.eu/resource/celex/32022L2523>) Art 30

## Rule order, safe harbours and 2026 overlays

**QDMTT credit is not QDMTT safe harbour.** Deduct eligible QDMTT payable in the jurisdictional calculation as prescribed, not simply cash tax paid. Test qualified status, effective year, entity/pool coverage and whether the liability is challenged or not assessable. A non-qualified domestic levy needs its own Covered Tax classification; it is not automatically creditable merely because paid. [S01](<https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2021/12/tax-challenges-arising-from-digitalisation-of-the-economy-global-anti-base-erosion-model-rules-pillar-two_eed81a23/782bac33-en.pdf>) Art 5.2.3, 10.1; [S02](<https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2026/05/tax-challenges-arising-from-the-digitalisation-of-the-economy-consolidated-commentary-to-the-global-anti-base-erosion-model-rules-2026_24289ddf/4377e89f-en.pdf>) Annex A3 paras 1–13; [S05](<https://publications.europa.eu/resource/celex/32022L2523>) Art 11

For the QDMTT safe harbour, document the separate accounting, consistency and administration standards, recognised effective dates, the election for each separate subgroup/standalone calculation and all switch-off conditions. Restrictions involving flow-throughs, investment entities, initial international activity, JV liability collection, securitisations or disallowed transition DTAs can switch off relief for all or a subset of entities and require the credit method. A Central Record “Yes” is not entity-specific clearance. The EU has its own Article 11/32 conditions; a QDMTT unpaid for 4 fiscal years can be brought back into the GloBE charge under Article 11(3). [S02](<https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2026/05/tax-challenges-arising-from-the-digitalisation-of-the-economy-consolidated-commentary-to-the-global-anti-base-erosion-model-rules-2026_24289ddf/4377e89f-en.pdf>) Annex A3; [S05](<https://publications.europa.eu/resource/celex/32022L2523>) Arts 11, 32; [S09](<https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/topics/policy-sub-issues/global-minimum-tax/updated-central-record-for-purposes-of-the-global-minimum-tax.pdf>)

**IIR.** Apply the top-down ownership chain, qualifying parent-rule exceptions and inclusion ratios, including the Partially-Owned Parent Entity rule where more than 20% of profit interests are held outside the group and the related offsets. “The UPE pays all top-up tax” is not universally correct. [S01](<https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2021/12/tax-challenges-arising-from-digitalisation-of-the-economy-global-anti-base-erosion-model-rules-pillar-two_eed81a23/782bac33-en.pdf>) Arts 2.1–2.3; [S05](<https://publications.europa.eu/resource/celex/32022L2523>) Arts 5–10

**UTPR.** Determine the residual amount after qualified IIR treatment and allocate under the prescribed 50% employee / 50% tangible-asset formula across eligible UTPR jurisdictions, with PE/flow-through, investment-entity and prior-uncollected-allocation rules. The domestic mechanism can deny deductions or make an equivalent adjustment; it need not be a tax return payment equal to an entity's own low-tax profit. [S01](<https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2021/12/tax-challenges-arising-from-digitalisation-of-the-economy-global-anti-base-erosion-model-rules-pillar-two_eed81a23/782bac33-en.pdf>) Arts 2.4–2.6; [S05](<https://publications.europa.eu/resource/celex/32022L2523>) Arts 12–14

**Initial international phase relief.** Model Article 9.3 is distinct from the nominal-rate safe harbour: broadly no more than 6 jurisdictions and no more than EUR 50 million net tangible assets outside the reference jurisdiction, with the reference-jurisdiction and 5-year conditions. EU Article 49 also addresses large-scale domestic groups. Do not cite a supposed Article 9.3.5 for a 20% UPE-rate exemption. ### Transitional CbCR safe harbour

The 2026 package extends the window to fiscal years beginning on or before 31 December 2027, excluding a year ending after 30 June 2029. This supersedes the former pre-1-January-2027 / pre-1-July-2028 limits. Meet at least one of the current-year de minimis, simplified ETR or routine-profit tests above. Relief deems relevant GloBE top-up tax zero; it does not discharge group-wide GIR duties or automatically disapply a national QDMTT. Use a Qualified CbC Report from Qualified Financial Statements, consistent data for each entity/PE and generally the same type of statements within a tested jurisdiction, subject to the actual NMCE/PE exceptions. Apply the stated PPA/goodwill, tax, held-for-sale, net-unrealised-fair-value-loss and special-entity rules, including separate JV pools, stateless/multi-parented restrictions and Article 7.3 interactions. Simplified Covered Taxes exclude non-Covered Taxes and uncertain-tax positions, with specified transition-DTA reversals/limits. Do not cherry-pick cash tax, book PBT or the most favourable data source. [S02](<https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2026/05/tax-challenges-arising-from-the-digitalisation-of-the-economy-consolidated-commentary-to-the-global-anti-base-erosion-model-rules-2026_24289ddf/4377e89f-en.pdf>) Annex A1 paras 6–23, 30–90

Track “once out, always out” for each jurisdiction: failure to apply the safe harbour in an in-scope year generally bars later use there, with the stated exception where the group had no CEs in that jurisdiction previously. Audit disqualification also affects later years. Adjust deduction/non-inclusion, duplicate-loss and duplicate-tax-recognition hybrid-arbitrage arrangements entered into after 15 December 2022, including the defined amendments/transfers/accounting changes and actual exceptions; superior-law jurisdictions may use the stated 18 December 2023 alternative. ### Permanent and other safe harbours

- **NMCE simplified calculations:** annual entity-level election for an entity omitted from line-by-line consolidation solely for size/materiality, with externally audited qualifying consolidated statements; above EUR 50 million revenue its CbCR financial accounts require an acceptable/authorised standard. Income and revenue both use relevant CbCR Total Revenue; taxes use Income Tax Accrued (Current Year), not deferred tax. Combine with the other tested-jurisdiction data and satisfy the permanent routine-profit, de minimis or at-least-15% ETR test. This framework does not generally wipe out Additional Current Top-up Tax. [S02](<https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2026/05/tax-challenges-arising-from-the-digitalisation-of-the-economy-consolidated-commentary-to-the-global-anti-base-erosion-model-rules-2026_24289ddf/4377e89f-en.pdf>) Annex A2 Sections 1–2
- **New Simplified ETR Safe Harbour:** the agreed main applicability date is fiscal years commencing on or after 31 December 2026; optional earlier use from 31 December 2025 has the QDMTT-safe-harbour / sole-taxing-jurisdiction / all-taxing-jurisdictions adoption-and-election conditions. The test is Simplified ETR at least 15% or a Simplified Loss, using its own income/tax adjustments, eligible tested pools, first-election/re-entry and integrity rules, not raw book tax divided by profit. Confirm domestic availability before use. The detailed computation is not interchangeable with transitional CbCR. [S02](<https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2026/05/tax-challenges-arising-from-the-digitalisation-of-the-economy-consolidated-commentary-to-the-global-anti-base-erosion-model-rules-2026_24289ddf/4377e89f-en.pdf>) Annex A2 Section 4 Box 1.2 paras 1–74, Box 7.4
- **Substance-based Tax Incentive Safe Harbour:** available under the agreed framework for fiscal years commencing on or after 1 January 2026 on election. Only qualifying generally available expenditure-based or tangible-production-volume incentives qualify; income/revenue-based holidays do not qualify merely because a zone has substance. The increase to Adjusted Covered Taxes is limited to QTIs used and the Substance Cap; the standard cap is 5.5% of the greater of eligible payroll or eligible-asset depreciation/depletion, with a 5-year 1% qualifying carrying-value alternative and revocation restrictions. Determine all eligibility, QRTC/MTTC election, valuation and local-adoption conditions before computing relief. [S02](<https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2026/05/tax-challenges-arising-from-the-digitalisation-of-the-economy-consolidated-commentary-to-the-global-anti-base-erosion-model-rules-2026_24289ddf/4377e89f-en.pdf>) Annex A2 Section 3
- **Transitional UTPR safe harbour:** covers only UTPR on the UPE jurisdiction where the defined nominal corporate income tax rate is at least 20%. The fiscal year must begin on or before 31 December 2025 and end on or before 3 January 2027; the stated period limit and the 2026 clarification for genuine 52/53-week years must both be applied. The official example 28 December 2025–2 January 2027 qualifies. This is not a blanket exemption for fiscal years beginning in 2026 and is separate from Article 9.3. [S02](<https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2026/05/tax-challenges-arising-from-the-digitalisation-of-the-economy-consolidated-commentary-to-the-global-anti-base-erosion-model-rules-2026_24289ddf/4377e89f-en.pdf>) Annex A4; [S04](<https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/topics/policy-sub-issues/global-minimum-tax/administrative-guidance-on-application-of-transitional-utpr-safe-harbour-to-mne-groups.pdf>)
- **Side-by-Side (SbS) Safe Harbour:** on election, a group whose UPE is located in a jurisdiction with a Qualified SbS Regime for the fiscal year can have IIR/UTPR top-up tax deemed zero for its CEs and its relevant JV interests; another non-eligible group's interest is not exempt. ### September 2026 changes and reporting version gate

The September guidance excludes **explicitly conditional taxes** from Covered Taxes and from the Net Taxes Expense add-back. Test paragraph 23.1 for the CE, JV or JV Group member: a tax applies under the condition that a qualified IIR or UTPR applies in respect of it in another jurisdiction and it is not eligible for SbS, or the tax does not apply under the condition that such a foreign rule does not apply or it is eligible for SbS. A separate, severable conditional levy or surcharge is excluded while the generally applicable tax remains a Covered Tax; where the conditional feature meets paragraph 23.1 and is not separate and severable, the whole tax must be excluded, subject to paragraph 23.4’s historical exception below. A EUR 750 million scope threshold alone is not such a condition. The narrow historical exception requires fiscal years beginning before 1 January 2025, enactment of the conditional feature before 30 November 2024 and unconditional application for all fiscal years beginning from 1 January 2025. QDMTT qualification also has the stated restrictions. Further guidance mentioned for the end of 2026 is not treated as already issued. [S03](<https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/topics/policy-sub-issues/global-minimum-tax/administrative-guidance-on-the-global-anti-base-erosion-model-rules-pillar-two-september-2026.pdf>) Chapter 1, added paras 23.1–23.6, 118.5.1–118.5.2

For mismatched local QDMTT accounting periods, September guidance requires analysis of the CE period included in the group's consolidated statements, local law and every Required QDMTT Fiscal Period that begins or ends during the UPE fiscal year; acquisition/disposal periods have specific treatment. Ordinarily all such periods must qualify for the safe harbour. Annex A3 paragraph 21.3 permits zero jurisdictional top-up tax for a UPE fiscal year beginning on 31 December 2023 or in 2024 if the safe harbour applies to a Required QDMTT Fiscal Period beginning during that UPE year, even if not every day of the UPE year was covered. Paragraph 21.5 takes priority: a switch-off in any period beginning or ending in the UPE year, including that defined 2024 year, denies the safe harbour for that UPE year. Where the safe harbour does not apply, reduce jurisdictional top-up tax by the QDMTT payable attributed to the UPE year under Commentary paragraph 20.4 to Article 5.2.3; use the prescribed income-based allocation, or the time-based fallback only when the information condition is met. Do not assume the UPE's calendar period is always the QDMTT computation period. [S03](<https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/topics/policy-sub-issues/global-minimum-tax/administrative-guidance-on-the-global-anti-base-erosion-model-rules-pillar-two-september-2026.pdf>) Chapter 2, added paras 18.1–18.4, 21–21.6, 20.4–20.6

The September 2026 GIR revises the standard template for the SbS package, including the general-section election and affected sections. The OECD publication specifies use for fiscal years commencing on or after 31 December 2025; its new explanatory notes unrelated to SbS also clarify earlier-period GIRs as paragraph 36.1 states; obtain the applicable domestic schema, accepted version and actual exchange route for the filing year. An OECD XML exchange schema is not by itself authority to upload that version into every domestic portal. Retain the CE register, calculations, safe-harbour/election evidence and local-return reconciliation even where a reporting simplification applies. [S08](<https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2026/09/tax-challenges-arising-from-the-digitalisation-of-the-economy-globe-information-return-september-2026_16d0f09e/0f9da895-en.pdf>) Introduction paras 1–12, 36.1; back-cover version statement

Do not present 12.6% as a complete US effective tax rate or reuse the former 13.125%→15.75% comparison.
