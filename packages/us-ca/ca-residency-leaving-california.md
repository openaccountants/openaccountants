# Leaving California: residency, domicile and the part-year return, tax year 2026

> **Source-cited draft.** Built from R&TC 17014, 17016 and 17952.5, California Code of Regulations Title 18, Regulations 17014, 17951-1, 17951-2, 17951-3, 17951-5, 17951-6, 17952 and 17953 (official Westlaw CCR site, read through Firecrawl), FTB Pub. 1031 (2025), the 2025 Form 540NR booklet, the 2026 California Tax Rate Schedules and 4 U.S.C. 114 (govinfo), read on 2026-11-13. No accountant has reviewed it. All outputs must be reviewed by a qualified tax professional before filing.

**Answer first.** California taxes a resident on all income and a nonresident only on California-source income. A resident is anyone in California for other than a temporary or transitory purpose, or anyone domiciled in California who is away only temporarily (R&TC 17014(a)). Moving out ends residence when the absence stops being temporary; it ends domicile only when the person abandons any intention of returning **and** settles elsewhere to stay indefinitely. More than nine months in California in a year raises a rebuttable presumption of residence (R&TC 17016). A California domiciliary away for at least **546 consecutive days** under an employment contract is treated as a nonresident unless intangible income exceeds **$200,000** in a contract year or the main purpose is tax avoidance; return visits totalling 45 days or less a year are ignored (R&TC 17014(d)). In the year of the move, file Form 540NR: tax on total taxable income at California rates, times California taxable income over total taxable income.

## Scope and who this is for

For a person who lives in California and is moving to another state or country, and the adviser helping them. Out of scope: the full-year resident return (`ca-540-individual-return`), the figures (`ca-income-tax`), federal residency and multi-state allocation in general (`us-multi-state-residency-and-allocation`), a US citizen's federal position abroad (`us-citizen-moving-abroad-tax`), the federal residency tests for non-citizens (`us-tax-residency`), and the Florida side of a move there (`fl-moving-to-florida-domicile`).

## Ask the client first

1. **When did you decide not to come back, and when did you settle in the new place with the plan to stay indefinitely?** Regulation 17014(c) needs both.
2. **How many days were you in California this year, and in each year after the move?** Calendar or travel records (R&TC 17016, the six-month rule, the 45-day limit).
3. **Is the move under an employment contract, for how long, and how much intangible income (interest, dividends, gains) will you have each year?** The 546-day safe harbor.
4. **What happened to the California home: sold, rented, or kept for your own use?** Regulation 17014 examples and Regulation 17951-3.
5. **Any sale before the move with payments after it, a business sale with a non-compete, or pay after the move for work before it (bonus, stock options, RSUs, deferred pay, severance)?** Regulations 17952 and 17951-6; refer the compensation items.
6. **Is your spouse moving at the same time, and is there community property?**

## The method, step by step

1. **Domicile before and after.** Regulation 17014(c): the place of the true, fixed, permanent home, to which the person intends to return whenever absent. One domicile at a time. A Californian "loses his California domicile the moment he abandons any intention of returning to California and locates elsewhere with the intention of remaining there indefinitely". Both limbs.
2. **Residence in each part of the year.** R&TC 17014(a): resident if in California for other than a temporary or transitory purpose, or domiciled here and outside for a temporary or transitory purpose. Regulation 17014(a): a domiciliary "remains a resident unless he is outside of this State for other than temporary or transitory purposes"; a person resident by presence who leaves for other than a temporary purpose "thereupon ceases to be a resident".
3. **Test the employment-contract safe harbor** (R&TC 17014(d)) if the absence is under an employment-related contract. If it applies, the person is outside California for other than a temporary purpose for the whole period.
4. **Count days and test the presumption** (R&TC 17016). More than nine months in aggregate raises a presumption, rebuttable by evidence of a temporary purpose. Fewer days proves nothing on its own.
5. **Fix the date residence ended.** Status depends on conduct during the entire year and ordinarily cannot be settled until the year closes (Regulation 17014(e)).
6. **Source the income after that date** under Regulations 17951-1 to 17951-6, 17952 and 17953; test retirement income under R&TC 17952.5 and 4 U.S.C. 114.
7. **Prepare Form 540NR** for the move year: Schedule CA (540NR) columns A to D for total income as if resident all year, column E for income while resident plus California-source income while nonresident; line 36 California tax rate = tax on total taxable income (line 31) over total taxable income (line 19), rounded to four decimals; line 37 = California taxable income (line 35) times that rate; line 38 exemption credit percentage = line 35 over line 19 (not more than 1.0000).
8. **File with a statement where status is in doubt.** Regulation 17014(d)(2): a person presumed resident, or with any question about status, should file a return for the year with a signed statement and evidence, even if they believe they had no California-source income.

## Boundary and exception table

### Who is a resident (Regulation 17014)

| Item | Rule | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Temporary or transitory, 17014(b) | Passing through, a brief rest or vacation, completing a particular transaction or a short contract | [Regulation 17014](https://govt.westlaw.com/calregs/Document/IEB5956134C8311EC89E5000D3A7C4BC3?viewType=FullText&transitionType=Default&contextData=(sc.Default)) |
| Not temporary, 17014(b) | A long or indefinite stay for health, business needing a long or indefinite period, a job that may last permanently or indefinitely, retiring with no definite intention of leaving | same |
| Closest connection, 17014(b) | "the state with which a person has the closest connection during the taxable year is the state of his residence" | same |
| Keeping domicile, 17014(c) | Kept "as long as he has the definite intention of returning here regardless of the length of time" | same |
| Proof, 17014(d)(1) | Affidavits of friends and business associates about the reasons for being outside the state should be submitted; voting and filing as a resident elsewhere are relevant to domicile but "of little value in determining one's residence"; no weight to where charitable gifts go | same |
| Timing, 17014(e) | Status depends on conduct during the entire year; "it will not be possible, ordinarily, to determine his status until after the close of the year" | same |

**Facts the examples weigh** (Regulation 17014(b) examples 1 to 3): where the person spends time each year and for how many months; which homes they keep and use, and whether a California house is rented out or kept with a caretaker; where bank accounts are; social, club and business connections and business office space; where relatives are. In example 2 a Nevada declaration and bank accounts did not outweigh six or seven months a year in a kept California estate with California clubs and business: the person was a resident. The examples apply equally where the other place is a country.

**Domicile and residence differ.** A domiciliary absent for other than a temporary purpose (a long posting abroad) is not a resident while away, but stays domiciled in California; while domicile stays here, any later absence that is only temporary leaves the person a resident.

### Records that decide an audit

Keep, for the move year and after: a day-by-day calendar with travel records; the reason for the move and proof it is not temporary (job offer, lease or purchase, a business set up there); what happened to the California home; where bank accounts, clubs, business office space and family are, before and after; names of people who can give affidavits on why the person is outside California.

### Move checklist

1. Decide the intention, and record when both domicile limbs became true.
2. Make the new place the main home (example 2).
3. Decide the California home: kept and used is a connection; rented is California-source rent.
4. Move bank accounts, clubs and office space.
5. Plan visits back: after domicile moves, a visit is temporary under the six-month rule only if all four conditions hold; under the safe harbor, keep visits to 45 days or less a year.
6. Time sales: a sale before the move fixes the source in California for later installments; California real estate is California-source whenever sold.
7. Keep the records above; file with a signed statement if status is in doubt.

## Worked cases

### Case 1: the part-year return (computation)

Dana, single, left San Jose for a permanent job in Austin on May 31, 2026. Total 2026 taxable income as if resident all year (Form 540NR line 19) is $200,000. California taxable income (line 35: everything while resident plus California-source income after, here the rent on her San Jose condo) is $80,000. Tax on $200,000 from the 2026 Schedule X: $3,310.88 plus 9.3% of $124,803 = $14,917.56, entered as $14,918 on line 31. California tax rate (line 36): $14,918 / $200,000 = 0.0746. California tax before credits (line 37): $80,000 x 0.0746 = $5,968. Exemption credit percentage (line 38) 0.4000, so her $153 personal credit (2025 amount, pending the 2026 figure) becomes $61.20.

```oa-check
name: CA 2026 Form 540NR part-year tax, single
inputs:
  total_ti: 200000
  ca_ti: 80000
  credit: 153
steps:
  - total_tax: round(3310.88 + (total_ti - 75197) * 0.093, 0)
  - ca_rate: round(total_tax / total_ti, 4)
  - ca_tax: ca_ti * ca_rate
  - credit_pct: min(ca_ti / total_ti, 1)
  - prorated_credit: credit * credit_pct
expected:
  total_tax: 14918
  ca_rate: 0.0746
  ca_tax: 5968
  credit_pct: 0.4
  prorated_credit: 61.20
```

### Case 2: does the 546-day safe harbor apply? (decision)

Sam, domiciled in San Diego, takes a two-year contract in Germany from February 1, 2026 to January 31, 2028 (729 days). He comes home for a 40-day visit in 2026 and expects $150,000 of interest and dividends a year. Days 729 is at least 546; visits 40 do not exceed 45; intangible income $150,000 does not exceed $200,000: the safe harbor applies and he is a nonresident for the contract period (a 1 below). If his investment income were $250,000 in any contract year, the safe harbor would fail (a 0) and his status would turn on the general closest-connection facts.

```oa-check
name: CA 546-day employment contract safe harbor test
inputs:
  days_away: 729
  visit_days: 40
  intangible_income: 150000
  intangible_income_alt: 250000
steps:
  - ok_days: clamp(days_away - 545, 0, 1)
  - ok_visits: clamp(46 - visit_days, 0, 1)
  - ok_income: clamp(200001 - intangible_income, 0, 1)
  - applies: ok_days * ok_visits * ok_income
  - ok_income_alt: clamp(200001 - intangible_income_alt, 0, 1)
  - applies_alt: ok_days * ok_visits * ok_income_alt
expected:
  applies: 1
  applies_alt: 0
```

## When to refuse or refer

- Refer equity compensation (options, RSUs), deferred bonuses and severance relating to work before the move and paid after it: the regulations read here do not print how California sources or accrues them.
- Refer spouses who move at different times or a spouse who stays, and community-property splits.
- Refer any case where a California home stays in regular use, California business activity continues, or long periods are spent here after the move (example 2).
- Refer an open FTB residency inquiry or audit, and any statute-of-limitations question.
- Refer retirement income received by someone no longer resident but still domiciled in California: 4 U.S.C. 114 protects only a person who is neither resident nor domiciled.
- Refuse to say that a declaration of domicile, a new driver's licence or voter registration alone ends California residence (Regulation 17014(d)(1) and example 2).
