# Italy cross-border tax: residence, moving in, leaving, non-residents and withholding (2026)

> **General reference only.** This skill is general tax reference material for AI-assisted work. It has not been reviewed for any specific person's facts. Do not rely on it to file, pay or take a tax position without review by a qualified Italian professional (commercialista).

This skill is the hub for what changes when a person or a company crosses Italy's border. It summarises the rules and links to the detailed skills in this pack rather than repeating them. The tax year is the calendar year. The authority is the Agenzia delle Entrate. Currency is the euro.

**Which law applies in 2026.** A new income tax code, the Testo unico delle imposte sui redditi approved by D.Lgs. 19 June 2026 n. 117, came into force on 4 July 2026 but applies only from 1 January 2027 (its art. 377, "Decorrenza"). For 2026 the old TUIR (D.P.R. 917/1986) still applies, so this skill cites the old article numbers, read on Normattiva as in force on 30 June 2026. From 2027 the article numbers change. Normattiva's "current" view may already show some old TUIR articles as repealed: that is the 2027 change, not a 2026 one.

## Who this applies to

### Individuals: the residence test (TUIR art. 2, from 2024)

D.Lgs. 209/2023 rewrote art. 2 comma 2 with effect from 1 January 2024. A person is Italian resident for income tax if, **for most of the tax year (more than half), counting part days as well**, they have any one of these in Italy:

- **residence** in the civil-law sense (Civil Code art. 43, the place of habitual abode);
- **domicile**, which now means "the place where the person's personal and family relations mainly develop" (no longer the centre of business and economic interests);
- **physical presence** in Italy.

Any one test is enough. A presence count above half the year, with part days counted, makes the person resident even with no home or family in Italy. For 2026 (365 days) that means 183 days or more.

**Registry presumption.** A person registered in the Italian population register (anagrafe) for most of the tax year is presumed resident, but this is now rebuttable ("salvo prova contraria"). Before 2024 registration was effectively conclusive.

**Citizens who move to a state not on the white list.** Italian citizens removed from the register who move to a state or territory not on the Ministry's white list are presumed still resident unless they prove otherwise (art. 2 comma 2-bis).

**No split year in domestic law.** Residence is decided for the whole tax year: a person is resident or non-resident for all of 2026. Someone who arrives on 1 July 2026 has fewer than 183 days and is non-resident for 2026 under domestic law (unless registered or domiciled in Italy for most of the year). Someone who leaves on 1 August has more than half the year in Italy and stays resident for the whole of 2026, taxed on worldwide income. Some treaties allow a split year; check the treaty.

### AIRE (registry of Italians resident abroad)

An Italian citizen who moves abroad must declare the move to the Italian consulate for the new area **within 90 days** of arrival (L. 470/1988 art. 6 comma 1). A change of address abroad is declared within 90 days as well (comma 3). The registry presumption above runs on the municipal population register, so the register entry matters. But AIRE registration alone does not make a person non-resident: the presence, domicile and civil-law residence tests still apply.

### Companies (TUIR art. 73 comma 3, from 2024)

A company or entity is Italian resident if, for most of the tax period, it has in Italy any of:

- its **registered office** (sede legale);
- its **place of effective management** (sede di direzione effettiva): the continuous and coordinated taking of strategic decisions about the company as a whole;
- its **day-to-day management** (gestione ordinaria in via principale): the continuous and coordinated carrying out of current management acts.

Foreign companies that control an Italian company are presumed resident (rebuttable) if they are themselves controlled from Italy or if most of their board are Italian residents (art. 73 comma 5-bis).

## The taxable base

**Residents: worldwide income.** A resident individual or company is taxed on income from anywhere. There is no remittance basis. The nearest thing is the art. 24-bis flat tax, which replaces tax on foreign income with a fixed yearly amount.

**Non-residents: Italian-source income only** (TUIR art. 23). The main items treated as produced in Italy:

- income from land and buildings in Italy;
- capital income paid by the Italian State, Italian residents or Italian permanent establishments (but **not** interest on bank and postal deposits and current accounts);
- employment income for work done in Italy;
- self-employment income from activities carried on in Italy;
- business income through an Italian permanent establishment;
- other income from activities in Italy or assets in Italy, and gains on shares in Italian resident companies (gains on listed shares are excluded);
- gains on shares in foreign companies whose value comes mainly (more than half, at any time in the 365 days before the sale) from Italian real estate (comma 1-bis; not for shares traded on regulated markets).

Pensions, end-of-service payments and royalties paid by Italian payers are Italian-source whatever the place of work (comma 2).

**How a non-resident is taxed** (TUIR art. 24). The normal IRPEF bands apply, but only a few deductions are allowed and **no family allowances** are given. A non-resident who earns at least **75% of total income in Italy** and lives in a state with adequate information exchange is taxed like a resident, with full allowances, provided they get no similar relief at home (art. 24 comma 3-bis).

**Inbound regimes in one paragraph each.** Full rules, codes and edge cases are in the `it-impatriati` skill.

- *Impatriati (D.Lgs. 209/2023 art. 5, arrivals from 2024).* Employment and professional income produced in Italy counts at 50% (40% with a minor child resident in Italy), up to EUR 600,000 a year, for the year of the move and the next four. Conditions: not resident in the three prior years (six or seven if staying with the same employer or group), commitment to stay at least four years (or the benefit is clawed back with interest), work done in Italy for most of the year, and high qualification under D.Lgs. 108/2012 and 206/2007 (or applied AI research). Business income and investment income are not covered.
- *Flat tax for new residents (art. 24-bis).* A fixed EUR 300,000 a year (moves from 1 January 2026; EUR 200,000 for moves after D.L. 113/2024 came into force on 10 August 2024; EUR 100,000 before that) replaces IRPEF on foreign income. Each family member added pays EUR 50,000 a year for moves from 1 January 2026 (EUR 25,000 before). The person must not have been resident in nine of the previous ten years. A favourable ruling (interpello) is needed first. It lasts up to 15 years. Gains on qualifying shareholdings in the first five years are still taxed normally.
- *Pensioners' option (art. 24-ter).* Foreign-pension holders who move to a town of up to 30,000 inhabitants in certain southern regions (or listed earthquake towns), and who were not resident in the five previous years and move from a country with an administrative cooperation agreement, can pay 7% on all foreign income for the year of the move and the next nine.

**Leaving: the exit tax** (TUIR art. 166). It applies to "soggetti che esercitano imprese commerciali": companies, and also individuals and partnerships that carry on a commercial business. The charge arises when such a taxpayer moves tax residence abroad, transfers assets to an exempt foreign branch, moves a whole Italian permanent establishment abroad, or is merged or demerged into a foreign company. The taxable amount is the difference between the market value and the tax cost of the assets and liabilities that do not stay in an Italian permanent establishment, valued at arm's length and including goodwill. Losses up to the last resident period reduce the gain; where no Italian permanent establishment remains, without the usual 80% limit (comma 6). Tax-suspended reserves are taxed too (comma 5).

**Individuals who are not in business.** Art. 166 does not reach an employee or a private investor. We found no other general exit charge on the shares or other assets of an individual who leaves Italy in the 2026 TUIR. The person simply becomes non-resident and is then taxed on Italian-source income only (including, under art. 23, gains on unlisted Italian shares, subject to any treaty).

**Non-resident returns.** A non-resident with Italian income that was not taxed at source by final withholding (for example rent, or employment income from a non-Italian payer) files Redditi PF on the same timetable. Final withholding (dividends, interest, fees, royalties) usually ends the obligation.

**Foreign assets (quadro RW).** Resident individuals, non-commercial entities and simple partnerships (not companies) report foreign investments, foreign financial assets and crypto-assets each year in the RW section of the return (D.L. 167/1990 art. 4). People under the art. 24-bis flat tax are exempt from this reporting.

**Withholding agents.** The Italian payer withholds at the moment of payment, pays the tax over and reports the payments in its annual withholding agent return. See `italy-payroll` for the payroll side.

**Exit tax instalments end early** if the taxpayer moves on from the EU or EEA state to a state outside the list, sells the assets whose value made up the gain, goes bankrupt or is wound up, or misses an instalment and does not put it right within 5 months; the remaining tax is then due at the next instalment date (art. 166 comma 12).

The impatriati regime has its own clawback: if the worker does not keep Italian residence for at least four years, the benefit is recovered with interest (D.Lgs. 209/2023 art. 5 comma 3). The flat tax ends if the yearly amount is not paid in full and on time (art. 24-bis comma 4).

## Cross-border points

**Treaty tie-breaker.** Where a person is resident in Italy under art. 2 and in another state under its law, the treaty's residence article decides (in the OECD model: permanent home, then centre of vital interests, then habitual abode, then nationality). The new domestic tests (part days count, domicile as personal and family relations) make dual residence more likely in the year of a move. Read the specific treaty.

**Foreign tax credit, not exemption** (TUIR art. 165). Italy relieves double tax by a credit. Foreign tax paid "a titolo definitivo" (finally, not as a refundable advance) is credited against Italian net tax, limited to the Italian tax on that foreign income: Italian tax times foreign income divided by total income (net of losses brought forward). The limit is applied country by country (comma 3). The credit is claimed in the return for the year the income belongs to, if the foreign tax is final before filing; otherwise in a later return (commi 4, 5 and 7). **No credit if the return is not filed or the foreign income is left out of it** (comma 8). For businesses, excess foreign tax can be carried back and forward for eight years (comma 6).

**EU social security.** For moves within the EU and EEA, Regulation (EC) 883/2004 decides which single country's social security applies, usually the country where the work is done; posted workers can stay in the home system with an A1 certificate. Italian social contributions are covered in `it-inps-contributions` and `italy-payroll`.

## Worked examples

### Example 1: an engineer moves to Milan under the impatriati regime

Facts. Anna, a software engineer with a qualifying degree, moves from London to Milan on 10 January 2026 to join an Italian company that is not part of her former employer's group. She was not Italian resident in 2023, 2024 or 2025. She has no children. Her 2026 salary is EUR 120,000, all for work done in Italy.

Residence. She is present and registered in Italy for most of 2026, so she is resident for the whole year under art. 2.

Regime. She meets the art. 5 conditions (three years abroad with a new employer, high qualification, work in Italy for most of the year, a commitment to stay four years). Her salary is below the EUR 600,000 cap, so 50% counts: EUR 60,000.

Tax (2026 bands, before deductions, tax credits and regional and municipal surcharges):
- first EUR 28,000 at 23% = EUR 6,440
- next EUR 22,000 at 33% = EUR 7,260
- last EUR 10,000 at 43% = EUR 4,300
- gross IRPEF = EUR 18,000

Without the regime, gross IRPEF on EUR 120,000 would be EUR 13,700 + 43% of EUR 70,000 = EUR 43,800. The regime saves EUR 25,800 of gross IRPEF in 2026. If she leaves Italy before four years, the saving is clawed back with interest.

```oa-check
name: Impatriato, salary EUR 120,000, 50% taxable (2026 IRPEF bands)
inputs:
  salary: 120000
  share_taxable: 0.50
steps:
  - taxable: salary * share_taxable
  - band_23: min(taxable, 28000) * 0.23
  - band_33: (min(taxable, 50000) - 28000) * 0.33
  - band_43: max(taxable - 50000, 0) * 0.43
  - irpef_regime: band_23 + band_33 + band_43
  - irpef_normal: 28000 * 0.23 + 22000 * 0.33 + (salary - 50000) * 0.43
  - saving: irpef_normal - irpef_regime
expected:
  taxable: 60000.00
  irpef_regime: 18000.00
  irpef_normal: 43800.00
  saving: 25800.00
```

### Example 2: an Italian company moves its tax residence to Germany

Facts. Alfa S.r.l., a calendar-year trading company, moves its place of effective management and registered office to Germany during 2026 and keeps no permanent establishment in Italy. At the end of its last Italian tax period, the market value of its assets and liabilities (including goodwill) is EUR 2,500,000 and their tax cost is EUR 1,300,000. It has EUR 200,000 of tax losses brought forward and no other income in the period.

Exit gain. EUR 2,500,000 - EUR 1,300,000 = EUR 1,200,000 (art. 166 comma 3(a)).

Losses. No Italian permanent establishment remains, so the EUR 200,000 of losses reduce the gain in full, without the 80% limit (comma 6). Taxable exit gain: EUR 1,000,000.

Tax. IRES at 24% = EUR 240,000.

Payment. Germany is an EU state, so Alfa can opt to pay in five equal annual instalments of EUR 48,000, with interest on the four later instalments (commi 9 and 11). The option covers the whole exit tax (comma 10). If Alfa later moves to a non-EU, non-listed state, the balance falls due at the next instalment date (comma 12). Tax-suspended reserves, IRAP and the interest amount are not computed here.

```oa-check
name: Company exit tax on move to Germany, IRES 24%, five instalments (2026)
inputs:
  market_value: 2500000
  tax_cost: 1300000
  losses_bf: 200000
  ires_rate: 0.24
  instalments: 5
steps:
  - gain: market_value - tax_cost
  - taxable_gain: gain - min(losses_bf, gain)
  - exit_tax: taxable_gain * ires_rate
  - instalment: exit_tax / instalments
expected:
  gain: 1200000.00
  taxable_gain: 1000000.00
  exit_tax: 240000.00
  instalment: 48000.00
```

A short outbound withholding check: an Italian company pays a non-resident consultant EUR 10,000 for work done in Italy. With no treaty relief claimed, it withholds 30%, EUR 3,000, as a final tax (D.P.R. 600/1973 art. 25). If the work is done abroad, there is no Italian withholding.

## Ask the client first

1. On which date did you arrive in or leave Italy, and how many days (counting part days) were you in Italy in 2026? Were you on the population register or AIRE, and for how long?
2. Where do your spouse or partner and children live? Under the 2024 rules, that decides domicile.
3. Are you an Italian citizen, and if you left, which country did you move to? (A move to a non-white-listed state keeps the presumption of residence.)
4. For a move in: in how many of the last ten years were you Italian resident, will you work for the same employer or group as before, and what is your qualification? This decides impatriati, flat tax or neither.
5. For a company or a business owner leaving: do you carry on a commercial business, will a permanent establishment stay in Italy, what are the market values and tax costs of the assets, and which state are you moving to (EU or listed EEA for instalments)?
6. What Italian income will you have after leaving (rent, salary, dividends, gains on Italian shares), and does a treaty apply?
