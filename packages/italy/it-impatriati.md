# IT Impatriati

## Two regimes: which applies

**Two regimes: which applies**

| Regime | Who it's for |
| --- | --- |
| Impatriati | Qualifying workers (employed/self-employed) moving to Italy |
| €100k/€200k flat tax | High-net-worth individuals moving to Italy (no work requirement) |

## Regime 1: Impatriati (D.Lgs 209/2023, from 2024)

### Eligibility (reformed rules from 2024)

- **Non-residency requirement before transfer** — Must not have been Italian tax resident in the 3 years preceding transfer (previously 2 years for most categories)  _(D.Lgs 209/2023)_
- **Commitment to remain resident** — Must commit to remain Italian tax resident for at least 4 years  _(D.Lgs 209/2023)_
- **Predominant work in Italy** — Must work predominantly in Italy (> 183 days of working days in Italy)  _(D.Lgs 209/2023)_
- **University degree requirement** — Must hold a university degree (new requirement from 2024)  _(D.Lgs 209/2023)_
- **No prior benefit** — Must not have already benefited from the impatriati regime previously  _(D.Lgs 209/2023)_

Pre-2024 rules (for those who started before 1 Jan 2024 under the old regime):
- 5-year non-residency gap was required for the old 70% exclusion version; the rules varied by category. Those grandfathered under old rules retain their benefit.

### Benefit

- **50% exclusion of employment/self-employment income** — 50% of employment or self-employment income earned in Italy is excluded from Italian taxable income.

**Effective rate examples**

| Income | Effective rate |
| --- | --- |
| Up to ~€28,000 (23% bracket) | ~11.5% effective on full income |
| Top rate (43% bracket, €50k+) | ~21.5% effective on full income |

- **Duration of impatriati benefit** — 5 years (the year of transfer + 4 following)
- **Extension of benefit** — Extension of 3 more years (8 total) if: Taxpayer purchases a residential property in Italy, OR Has one or more minor children

### What is excluded vs not

- **Included in 50% benefit** — Included in the 50% benefit: employment income (PAYE), self-employment income
- **Not included in 50% benefit** — NOT included: investment income (dividends, capital gains), rental income (these are taxed at normal Italian rates or flat imputed rates)

## Regime 2: €100,000 Flat Tax (Res Non-Dom — Art. 24-bis TUIR)

### Who it's for

- **Who the €100k flat tax is for** — High-net-worth individuals who transfer tax residence to Italy and have not been Italian tax residents for 9 of the previous 10 years.  _(TUIR Art. 24-bis)_

### How it works

- **€100,000 annual flat tax on foreign income** — Pay a €100,000 annual flat tax on ALL foreign-source income and gains (regardless of amount)  _(TUIR Art. 24-bis)_
- **Italian-source income taxation** — Italian-source income taxed at normal Italian progressive rates  _(TUIR Art. 24-bis)_
- **Flat tax for additional family member** — €25,000 EUR (flat tax for each additional family member who also opts in)  _(TUIR Art. 24-bis)_

### Duration

- **Duration of €100k flat tax regime** — 15 years (renewable annually by paying the flat tax). Can exit at any time.  _(TUIR Art. 24-bis)_

### What it covers

Foreign dividends, interest, capital gains, rental income, employment income from abroad — all covered by the €100k payment. No per-income-item analysis needed for foreign income.

## Italy progressive income tax rates (for reference)

**Italy progressive income tax rates (for reference)**

| Taxable income | Rate |
| --- | --- |
| Up to €28,000 | 23% |
| €28,001 – €50,000 | 35% |
| Above €50,000 | 43% |

> Have a qualified Italian commercialista review before relying on either regime.
