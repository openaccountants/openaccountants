# Forming a business in New York, 2026: LLC or corporation, publication, biennial statement and the tax registrations that follow

> **Source-cited draft.** Built from the Department of State fee schedules, biennial statement and beneficial owner disclosure pages, Limited Liability Company Law 206 and 417, the instructions for Forms CT-6 and CT-3-S, FinCEN's beneficial ownership page and the New York City Department of Finance GCT and commercial rent tax pages, read on 2026-11-13. No accountant or lawyer has reviewed it. All outputs must be reviewed by a qualified professional before filing.

**Answer first.** A New York LLC is formed by filing articles of organization with the Department of State (**USD 200**). Within **120 days** it must publish a notice once a week for **six successive weeks** in **two newspapers** (one daily, one weekly) designated by the county clerk of the county named in the articles, then file a certificate of publication (**USD 50**); if it does not, its authority to carry on business in New York is **suspended** until it complies. A New York business corporation files a certificate of incorporation (**USD 125** plus the tax on shares) and has **no** publication requirement. Both file a **USD 9** biennial statement every two years. A federal S corporation is a New York S corporation only if it files **Form CT-6** (or is mandated), and New York City does not recognize S status at all. From 1 January 2026, only LLCs formed under the law of a **foreign country** and authorized in New York report beneficial owners to the Department of State; US companies are exempt from federal BOI reporting.

## Scope and who this is for

In scope: forming a domestic LLC or business corporation in New York, qualifying an out-of-state LLC or corporation to do business in New York (fees only), the LLC publication requirement, the operating agreement, the biennial statement, the New York S election, the first tax registrations, and the beneficial ownership rules in force in 2026.

## Ask the client first

1. **LLC or corporation, and why?** Investors who want a Delaware C corporation, a single owner who wants pass-through treatment, or a plan to elect S status.
2. **Which county will the articles name as the office?** It decides which newspapers the county clerk designates for an LLC's publication, and so the cost.
3. **Formed in New York, in another state, or in a foreign country?** An out-of-state entity doing business in New York applies for authority; a foreign-country LLC also has New York beneficial owner reporting.
4. **Will it operate in New York City?** City taxes (UBT, GCT for S corporations, business corporation tax, commercial rent tax) are separate.
5. **For a corporation, is a New York S election wanted for the first year?** Form CT-6 is due on or before the 15th day of the third month after the certificate of incorporation takes effect.
6. **Will it hire staff or make taxable sales?** Payroll and sales tax registrations start before the first payroll or sale.

### Forming an LLC

1. **Choose the name and county.** The articles name the county in New York where the LLC's office is to be located. That county's clerk designates the newspapers for publication; Manhattan, Brooklyn and other large-county newspapers cost far more than upstate ones. Ask for quotes before filing: the cost is a newspaper charge, not a state fee.
2. **File the articles of organization** with the Department of State (online through NY Business Express or by mail), USD 200, plus any expedited handling fee.
3. **Adopt a written operating agreement.** The members "shall adopt a written operating agreement" covering the business, the conduct of its affairs and the members' and managers' rights; it may be entered into before, at the time of, or within 90 days after filing the articles, and is not filed with the state ([LLC Law 417](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/LLC/417)). A single-member LLC needs one too.
4. **Publish within 120 days** after the initial articles take effect: a copy of the articles or a notice of their substance, once in each week for six successive weeks, in two newspapers of the office county (one printed weekly, one daily) designated by the county clerk. The notice includes the LLC's name, the filing date of the articles (and formation date if different), the county of its office and the other items LLC Law 206(a) lists ([LLC Law 206](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/LLC/206)).
5. **File the certificate of publication** with the newspapers' affidavits of publication attached, USD 50 ([Department of State, fee schedules](https://dos.ny.gov/fee-schedules)).
6. **If step 4 or 5 is missed,** the LLC's authority to carry on, conduct or transact business in New York is suspended at the end of the 120 days. The suspension does not void its contracts and does not make members liable for its obligations, and it is annulled once proof of publication in substantial compliance (other than the 120 days) is filed ([LLC Law 206](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/LLC/206)).
7. **A foreign (out-of-state) LLC** doing business in New York files an application for authority (USD 250) and also publishes and files a certificate of publication (USD 50) ([Department of State, fee schedules](https://dos.ny.gov/fee-schedules)).

### Forming a business corporation

8. **File the certificate of incorporation** with the Department of State, USD 125 plus the tax on shares ([Department of State, fee schedules](https://dos.ny.gov/fee-schedules)). A corporation has no publication requirement: LLC Law 206 applies to LLCs only.
9. **A foreign (out-of-state) corporation** doing business in New York files an application for authority, USD 225.
10. **New York S election.** A federal S corporation is not a New York S corporation until it files **Form CT-6**: at any time during the preceding tax year or on or before the 15th day of the third month of the tax year to which it applies. A corporation organized in New York that wants S status for its first year files CT-6 by the 15th day of the third month after its certificate of incorporation takes effect; one organized elsewhere, by the 15th day of the third month after it began doing business in New York. An election is not effective until the next year if the corporation was not a federal S corporation on every earlier day of the year, or a shareholder who held stock before the election did not consent ([CT-6-I](https://www.tax.ny.gov/pdf/current_forms/ct/ct6i.pdf)).
11. **Mandated S.** An eligible federal S corporation that has not elected is deemed to have elected if its investment income (interest, dividends, royalties, annuities, rents and gains from dealings in property) is more than 50% of its federal gross income for the year ([CT-3-S-I](https://www.tax.ny.gov/pdf/current_forms/ct/ct3si.pdf)).
12. **A New York S corporation** files Form CT-3-S and pays the fixed dollar minimum tax under Article 9-A, which for S corporations runs from USD 25 up to USD 4,500 for New York receipts over USD 25,000,000 ([CT-3-S-I](https://www.tax.ny.gov/pdf/current_forms/ct/ct3si.pdf)). A federal S corporation that has not elected and is not mandated is taxed as a C corporation under Article 9-A: see `ny-corporate-franchise-article-9a`.

### After formation (both)

13. **Biennial statement every two years,** USD 9, online through the Department of State's e-Statement Filing Service with the exact entity name and DOS ID number; do not file before the calendar month in which it is due. A corporation's statement gives its chief executive officer's name and business address, its principal executive office, its address for service of process and the number of directors (and how many are women); an LLC's gives its address for service of process ([Department of State, biennial statements](https://dos.ny.gov/biennial-statements-business-corporations-and-limited-liability-companies); Business Corporation Law 408; LLC Law 301(e)).
14. **Tax registrations.** LLCs with New York source items pay the yearly IT-204-LL fee (`ny-llc-filing-fee-it-204-ll`); corporations file CT-3 or CT-3-S (`ny-corporate-franchise-article-9a`); employers register for withholding and unemployment insurance and obtain disability and PFL cover before the first payroll (`ny-payroll`); sellers obtain a Certificate of Authority at least 20 days before the first taxable sale (`ny-sales-tax`).
15. **Beneficial ownership.** Federal: US companies are exempt from BOI reporting under FinCEN's final rule (effective 14 August 2026), and US persons need not be reported; foreign companies registered to do business in a US state still report ([FinCEN, BOI](https://www.fincen.gov/boi)). New York: from 1 January 2026, non-exempt LLCs formed under the law of a **foreign country** and authorized to do business in New York file initial and annual beneficial owner disclosures with the Department of State, naming those with substantial control or at least 25% ownership (full legal name, date of birth, street address and an ID number); exempt LLCs file an attestation of exemption ([Department of State, beneficial owner disclosure](https://dos.ny.gov/beneficial-owner-disclosure)). A New York-formed or other US-formed LLC does not file.

### Red flags

| Flag | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |

| Federal S corporation in New York City | City GCT applies at entity level: model it before recommending S status |

## Worked cases

**Case 1, state fees in the first year: LLC against corporation.** A founder in Manhattan forms either a New York LLC or a New York corporation in March 2026. LLC: articles USD 200, certificate of publication USD 50, and in 2027 the IT-204-LL fee for 2026 of USD 25 (a disregarded LLC with New York business income): USD 275 in state fees, plus the newspapers' charges for six weeks in two Manhattan newspapers (quotes needed). Corporation: certificate of incorporation USD 125 plus the tax on shares, and no publication. The corporation then owes at least the Article 9-A fixed dollar minimum each year (USD 25 for New York receipts of USD 100,000 or less: see `ny-corporate-franchise-article-9a`). The biennial statement (USD 9) falls in 2028 for both.

```oa-check
  - corporation_state_fees: certificate_of_incorporation + fixed_dollar_minimum_lowest

  - both_biennial_2028: biennial_statement * 2

  llc_state_fees: 275
  corporation_state_fees: 150
  difference_before_newspapers: 125
  both_biennial_2028: 18
```

**Case 2, a decision: is the LLC's publication on time?** Articles of organization took effect on 2 March 2026 (day 61 of the year). The 120-day period ends on day 181, 30 June 2026. The two newspapers ran the notice for six weeks ending 12 June 2026 (day 163) and the certificate of publication was filed on 8 July 2026 (day 189). Decision: publication ran in time, but proof was filed 8 days late, so the LLC's authority to do business was suspended from 1 July to 8 July 2026; the filing on 8 July annulled the suspension. Had the certificate been filed by 30 June, there would have been no suspension. Its contracts in that week are not void.

```oa-check
name: LLC publication deadline under LLC Law 206 (decision)
inputs:
  effective_day: 61
  last_publication_day: 163
  certificate_filed_day: 189
steps:
  - deadline_day: effective_day + 120
  - published_in_time: min(1, ceil(max(deadline_day - last_publication_day + 1, 0) / 1000))
  - days_late: max(certificate_filed_day - deadline_day, 0)
  - suspended: min(1, days_late)
expected:
  deadline_day: 181
  published_in_time: 1
  days_late: 8
  suspended: 1
```

## Filing and payment

- **Where:** Department of State, Division of Corporations: online through NY Business Express or the e-Statement Filing Service (biennial statements), or by mail to One Commerce Plaza, 99 Washington Avenue, Albany NY 12231-0001 (518-473-2492). Payment by money order payable to "Department of State", or MasterCard, Visa or American Express ([Department of State, fee schedules](https://dos.ny.gov/fee-schedules)).
- **Sequence for an LLC:** articles (day 0), operating agreement (by day 90), publication (six weeks, finished in time to file proof by day 120), certificate of publication (by day 120), first biennial statement (the calendar month of the second anniversary), IT-204-LL each year with New York source items (15 March for calendar years).
- **Sequence for a corporation:** certificate of incorporation, CT-6 by the 15th day of the third month if New York S status is wanted for the first year, CT-3 or CT-3-S each year (15 April for calendar years), first biennial statement in the second anniversary month.
- **Foreign-country LLCs:** initial and annual beneficial owner disclosures, or an attestation of exemption, with the Department of State ([Department of State, beneficial owner disclosure](https://dos.ny.gov/beneficial-owner-disclosure)).

## Completion checklist

- Entity type chosen with the tax result compared (Article 9-A, S status, city taxes).
- Office county chosen with newspaper quotes in hand (LLC).
- Articles or certificate filed and receipt kept; DOS ID number recorded.
- Written operating agreement adopted within 90 days (LLC).
- Publication completed and certificate of publication filed within 120 days (LLC, domestic or foreign).
- CT-6 filed on time if New York S status is wanted; mandated S tested.
- Biennial statement month diarized; registered address kept current.
- IT-204-LL, CT-3 or CT-3-S, payroll and sales tax registrations set up as needed.
- Beneficial ownership: none for a US-formed entity; Department of State disclosure for a foreign-country LLC.
