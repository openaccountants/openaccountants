# Sint Maarten: turnover tax v1.0

> **General reference only.** This skill is general tax/accounting reference material for AI-assisted workflows. It has not been reviewed for any specific person's facts, documents, elections, deadlines, residency, filing status, or local procedures. Do not rely on it to file, pay, amend, or take a tax position without review by a qualified professional in the relevant jurisdiction.

Tax year: 2026. Law: the Sint Maarten Turnover Tax Ordinance, consolidated text in force from 12 April 2023 to the present ([Turnover Tax Ordinance, CVDR697488](https://lokaleregelgeving.overheid.nl/CVDR697488)). Authority: Inspectorate of Taxes. Amounts in Cg. This English summary is not an official translation. Dutch Sint Maarten only.

## Who this applies to

- **Entrepreneurs:** anyone independently carrying on a business or profession, and anyone exploiting an asset to obtain lasting income from it (articles 1 and 2). Article 2(b) can also cover goods delivered and services enjoyed in Sint Maarten by a supplier from abroad; a foreign address alone does not settle it ([Turnover Tax Ordinance](https://lokaleregelgeving.overheid.nl/CVDR697488)).
- **Not a VAT:** tax is on business turnover realised in the levy area; there is no general input credit (article 6).
- Not covered: room tax, excises, import duties, profit and wage tax.

## The base

1. **Base:** the business turnover the entrepreneur realised in the levy area (article 6(1)). Amounts paid to a third party **in the customer's name and for the customer's account** are not turnover (article 6(2)); ordinary costs recharged to a client are ([Turnover Tax Ordinance](https://lokaleregelgeving.overheid.nl/CVDR697488)).
2. **Timing:** tax is due when the consideration is received, wholly or partly (article 10(1)). On request the Inspector can allow invoice-based timing (article 10(2)), with numbered, dated invoices carrying the transaction details (10(3)), issued within 15 days after the month of supply, keeping a copy (10(4)). Do not switch to invoice accounting just because the accounts use accruals (same text).
3. **Exemptions (article 8)**, each only to the extent the entrepreneur can show it (same text):
   - letting property fitted and intended for permanent living, by a locally established entrepreneur, to individuals living in Sint Maarten (8(1));
   - delivery of real property on which transfer tax was paid, under ministerial conditions (8(2));
   - sale of lottery tickets under the national lottery ordinance (8(3));
   - transport by aircraft or sea-going ships, not pleasure craft (8(4));
   - hospitals, medical laboratories, doctors, dentists, dental technicians, nurses, midwives, physiotherapists, chiropractors, speech therapists, dietitians, psychologists and podiatrists, under ministerial conditions (8(5));
   - fuel and services for sea-going ships and aircraft in international transport, and warships (8(6)); fuel to gas, power and water companies (8(7));
   - free-zone oil terminal and economic-zone exports and services, under conditions (8(8), 8(19));
   - prescription medicines (8(9)); self-employed bus and taxi drivers and school buses (8(10)); aid projects paid by the Netherlands, Curacao, Aruba or international organisations (8(11)); concession postal items (8(12)); port and airport services for goods entering and leaving (8(13)); non-commercial education and congresses at least 90% attended by non-residents (8(14)); medical aids (8(15));
   - hotel rooms and apartments, if room tax (logeergastenbelasting) was paid on the whole proceeds (8(16));
   - bread, sugar, salt, milk, eggs, butter, cooking oil, baby food, diapers, toilet paper, rice, beans, potatoes, grain, flour, chicken and fruit (8(17)); only these items, not all food;
   - casino games (8(18)); dealings in securities, banknotes and coins that are legal tender (8(20)); and the further paragraphs of article 8.
   The Inspectorate's code list for non-taxable turnover ([List of codes, November 2021](https://www.sintmaartengov.org/Documents/Forms%20for%20Businesses/List%20TURNOVERTAX%20ORDINANCE%20Codes%20for%20Non-taxable%20turnover%20November%202021.pdf)) is supporting guidance and does not replace the law; it also says to file monthly despite an exemption.
4. **Export of goods (article 9):** exempt if an export entrepreneur supplies goods to customers living or established outside the levy area and ships them, or has them shipped, to a destination outside it. **For this article, the French part of the island counts as inside the levy area** (9(2)), so a delivery to Saint-Martin is not an export. Evidence (9(3)): a copy of the invoice with goods, price and customer details; proof of payment; transport documents proving exit; and the destination's endorsed import document or an accepted confirmation ([Turnover Tax Ordinance, art. 9](https://lokaleregelgeving.overheid.nl/CVDR697488)).

## Deadlines and filing

- Monthly return (article 12); a natural person exploiting an asset can ask for an annual period ([Turnover Tax Ordinance](https://lokaleregelgeving.overheid.nl/CVDR697488)).
- File and pay within 15 days after the period ends, and file even when no tax is due ([General Tax Ordinance](https://lokaleregelgeving.overheid.nl/CVDR671742)). The Tax Administration calendar lists 16 November 2026 for the October 2026 return ([Sint Maarten Tax Administration](https://www.tax.sx/)).
- Pay by bank transfer to the Receiver's accounts listed by the Tax Administration (same site).

## Penalties and interest

The ordinance has a chapter on additional assessments (naheffing) and administrative penalties (chapters III and IV), and makes failing the article 12 conditions an offence punishable by a third-category fine ([Turnover Tax Ordinance](https://lokaleregelgeving.overheid.nl/CVDR697488)). The penalty amounts themselves were not confirmed for this skill.

## Cross-border points

- **Overseas suppliers:** article 11(2) can make the locally established business customer liable in the specified cases, if supplier and customer jointly ask in writing, or if the overseas supplier has not paid the tax on its return. This is not a general reverse charge on all imported services ([Turnover Tax Ordinance, arts. 5 and 11](https://lokaleregelgeving.overheid.nl/CVDR697488)).
- **French side:** inside the levy area for the export exemption (article 9(2)); the two tax systems otherwise stay separate.

## Two worked examples that recompute

**Case 1: receipts and a genuine disbursement.** A consultant receives Cg 22,000, of which Cg 2,000 is a documented payment made in the client's name and for its account (article 6(2)). Base 20,000; tax 5% = 1,000. Had the 2,000 been the consultant's own costs recharged, the base would be 22,000 and tax 1,100.

```oa-check
name: Sint Maarten turnover tax with a disbursement
inputs:
  received: 22000
  disbursement: 2000
  rate: 0.05
steps:
  - base: received - disbursement
  - tax: base * rate
  - tax_if_recharged_cost: received * rate
  - difference: tax_if_recharged_cost - tax
expected:
  base: 20000.00
  tax: 1000.00
  tax_if_recharged_cost: 1100.00
  difference: 100.00
```

**Case 2: decision, receipt basis or ask for invoice accounting.** A supplier invoices Cg 10,000 in March; the client pays 4,000 in March and 6,000 in April. Receipt basis (default): 200 due with the March return and 300 with April. Invoice basis (only with permission): 500 with March, so cash leaves earlier. A seller to a goods customer on the French side charges the 5% too: that is not an export under article 9(2).

```oa-check
name: Receipt basis against invoice basis
inputs:
  invoice: 10000
  paid_march: 4000
  paid_april: 6000
  rate: 0.05
steps:
  - receipt_march: paid_march * rate
  - receipt_april: paid_april * rate
  - invoice_march: invoice * rate
  - earlier_cash_under_invoice: invoice_march - receipt_march
expected:
  receipt_march: 200.00
  receipt_april: 300.00
  invoice_march: 500.00
  earlier_cash_under_invoice: 300.00
```

## Ask the client first

1. What is supplied, to whom, and where are the customers (Sint Maarten, French side, abroad)?
2. Receipt basis, or does the business hold an invoice-accounting permission?
3. Any exempt turnover (residential rent, hotel rooms under room tax, listed goods, medical)? Evidence for each.
4. Exports: invoices, payment proof, transport and destination documents?
5. Purchases from overseas suppliers, and any joint request under article 11(2)?
