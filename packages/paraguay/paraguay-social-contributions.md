# Paraguay IPS social security contributions, 2026 v0.2

> **General reference only.** This skill is general tax/accounting reference material for AI-assisted workflows. It has not been reviewed for any specific person's facts, documents, elections, deadlines, residency, filing status, or local procedures. Do not rely on it to file, pay, amend, or take a tax position without review by a qualified professional in the relevant jurisdiction.

Paraguay's compulsory social insurance (seguro social obligatorio) is run by the Instituto de Previsión Social (IPS). Its charter is Decreto-Ley N° 1860/1950, approved by Ley N° 375/1956; the contribution rates are in its art. 17 as replaced by art. 2 of Ley N° 98/1992. Domestic workers are covered by Ley N° 5407/2015, voluntary affiliation by Ley N° 4933/2013 and the sanctions by Ley N° 98/1992 and Ley N° 5655/2016. The laws are read in the texts published by the Biblioteca y Archivo Central del Congreso Nacional (BACN); the rates as IPS applies them are read on the IPS portal. Amounts are in guaraníes (G). Contributions are monthly; the tax year is the calendar year 2026.

The minimum wage (the contribution floor), gross-to-net pay, the aguinaldo and the family allowance are set out in [paraguay-payroll](paraguay-payroll.md). The employee's own income tax (IRP), from which the employee's IPS contribution is excluded, is in [paraguay-income-tax](paraguay-income-tax.md).

**Quick answer.** For an ordinary employee the employer withholds 9% of the salary, adds 16.5% of its own, and pays 25.5% of the salary to IPS each month through the REI system. The base is the whole monthly remuneration except the aguinaldo and the family allowance, and, for full-time workers, never less than the minimum wage; the part-time exception is below. Domestic workers are in the same general regime. Bank employees pay into the Caja Bancaria instead (11% employee, 17% bank). A self-employed person may join the IPS pension fund voluntarily at 13%.

## Who this applies to

- **Employees in the private sector (régimen general).** Every worker in a dependent relationship is insured; the employer withholds the worker's share and pays both shares to IPS (IPS, domestic and part-time employment page). An employer that fails to deduct the worker's share pays it itself (Decreto-Ley 1860/50 art. 68 as replaced by Ley 98).
- **Domestic workers.** Anyone hired for domestic work, in any arrangement, is in the general regime of the compulsory insurance (Ley 5407 art. 18). The earlier reduced domestic rates of art. 17 e) and h) (2.5% worker and 5.5% employer, on a deemed minimum) were repealed by Ley 5407 art. 26 d). Domestic work is for households only, not companies, and a spouse or partner may not be registered as the domestic worker (IPS page).
- **Part-time workers** (16 to 32 hours a week, written contract): insured for each job they hold, at the general rates (IPS page).
- **Private teachers and lecturers**: 9% worker and 14% employer IPS quota under Ley 4370 art. 5 as replaced by Ley 5555/2015, plus applicable additional employer levies. ANDE staff and pensioners have separate art. 17 rates (table).
- **Insured persons who stop working** may continue in the pension insurance only (continuidad en el beneficio), with no medical cover while they contribute (IPS contribution base page).
- **Bank employees** are members of the Caja de Jubilaciones y Pensiones de Empleados Bancarios for their pension, not of the IPS pension fund (Ley 2856/2006 arts. 7 and 9, art. 9 as last replaced by Ley 6303/2019).
- **Independent workers, employers, homemakers and domestic workers** may join the IPS pension fund voluntarily under Ley 4933/2013. This gives pensions only, not medical care (art. 1).

Out of scope: public servants under the Caja Fiscal, other special pension funds, foreign workers posted under a social security agreement, and the benefits themselves.

## The base

**Salary (salario).** IPS applies the definition of Decreto-Ley 1860/50 art. 76 a): the total remuneration the worker receives from employers in money, kind or regalías. It includes overtime, extra or piece work, commissions, salary supplements, bonuses (gratificaciones), dismissal indemnities, prizes, fees, profit shares and any other regular accessory pay. It excludes the aguinaldo. The family allowance (bonificación familiar) is also excluded (Ley 430/1973 art. 4 c), as IPS cites it; Labour Code art. 268) ([IPS, contribution base page](https://portal.ips.gov.py/sistemas/ipsportal/contenido.php?c=315)).

**Continuity after leaving work.** Ley 7446 art. 5 retains 12.5% of the last 36 contributed salaries, subject to the current minimum-wage floor. Missing months where there are fewer than 36 are filled using the corresponding minimum wages for the immediately preceding months. If the gap between leaving and grant of continuity exceeds 24 months, update the last-36-salary average by CPI; contributions under the provision are updated annually by CPI. Payments may be monthly or for elapsed quarters/years. These rules are separate from the voluntary elected-base arrangement.

**Floor.** The contribution base may not be below the legal minimum wage, even for apprentices (Decreto-Ley 1860/50 art. 20 as updated by Ley 427/1973, cited by IPS). The minimum wage changed on 1 July 2026: G 2,899,048 a month to 30 June and G 3,044,000 from 1 July (rates table). The daily, hourly, night and part-time minimums are in [paraguay-payroll](paraguay-payroll.md).

**Part-time workers.** The base is the pay actually received for each job. Where that pay is below the minimum wage, the employer must make up 100% of the amount that funds the sickness and maternity fund only; the pension fund is covered by what was actually paid in (IPS page).

**Domestic workers.** The base is the total pay actually received, from one employer or several (Ley 5407 art. 19). Domestic workers are entitled to the full minimum wage for unspecified activities, and those working shorter or broken hours may not be paid proportionally less (Ley 5407 art. 10 as replaced by Ley 6338/2019).

**No ceiling.** Art. 17 sets no maximum salary for the contribution, and none was found in the laws read.

**General-regime computation, each month and each worker.**

1. Take the month's remuneration under the salary definition; leave out the aguinaldo and the family allowance.
2. Raise it to the minimum wage if lower (full-time workers), or apply the part-time rule above.
3. Worker's share: 9% of the base, withheld from pay. The Labour Code allows deductions from pay for the compulsory insurance quotas (art. 240 c); the employer's share is never deducted from the worker.
4. Employer's share: 16.5% of the base, from its own funds.
5. Pay both to IPS together through the REI planilla.

## Deadlines and filing

1. **Register as an employer** with IPS (número patronal) and register each worker on hiring. Domestic employers use the Seguro Doméstico employer form and the REI PIN request; in REI the worker type is "Seguro Doméstico" or "Tiempo Parcial", and a part-time contract is uploaded with the registration ([IPS, domestic and part-time employment page](https://portal.ips.gov.py/sistemas/ipsportal/contenido.php?c=275)).
2. **Monthly planilla.** The employer declares each worker's pay in the IPS REI system (Recepción Electrónica de la Información) and pays the worker's and employer's shares together, through the IPS collection points (bocas de cobranza).
3. **Leavers.** When employment ends, the employer records the exit in REI with the reason under the Labour Code grounds, as required by Resolución C.A. N° 018-001/2020, in force under Resolución AOP N° 041/2020 (IPS page).
4. **Due date.** The IPS calendar lists domestic employers on the 10th; zones 0001-0002 on the 11th; zones 0051-9999 and agricultural employer codes 01/02 on the 14th; zones 0006-0008 on the 17th; and zones 0003-0005 on the 20th ([IPS calendar](https://portal.ips.gov.py/sistemas/ipsportal/contenido.php?c=353)). Match the employer category and zone rather than infer a due day from its final digit.

## Penalties and interest

- **Not registering a worker in time**: one minimum daily jornal per worker (art. 67).
- **Not deducting the worker's share**: the employer bears it and pays it (art. 68).
- **Deducting and not paying in time**: 2 to 10 times the amount withheld and not paid, plus the contributions themselves (art. 68).
- **Administrative infractions** (Ley 5655 art. 3.1): graded by the IPS board according to the benefit to the offender, the harm to the insured and repetition, and fined in días-multa within the ranges in the rates table; 75% of the fines go to the sickness and maternity fund.
- **Criminal evasion** (Ley 5655 art. 3.2): an employer that deducts the worker's share and does not pay it to IPS commits evasión de aportes. The court may waive punishment where the employer reports the amount and reasons in writing by the due date or immediately afterwards, and makes serious efforts to pay, and no punishment applies if the contributions are then paid within the period IPS sets.
- **Arrears.** IPS collects unpaid contributions, with surcharges and legal interest, by an executive court action on a debt certificate, and its claims rank after those of the Treasury and the municipalities (art. 66 as replaced by Ley 98). The surcharge and interest rates were not confirmed.

## Cross-border points

- **Non-resident workers in Paraguay** employed by a Paraguayan employer are insured like any worker. Pay to a non-resident may also bear the non-resident income tax (INR), which is outside this skill.
- **Bilateral and Mercosur social security agreements** that let a posted worker stay in the home system were not read; refer.
- **Income tax.** The employee's IPS contribution is excluded from IRP gross income ([paraguay-income-tax](paraguay-income-tax.md)); for the employer's IRE deduction see [paraguay-corporate-income-tax](paraguay-corporate-income-tax.md).

## Two worked examples that recompute

### Example 1: employee in December 2026, with overtime, family allowance and aguinaldo

Assume the art. 261-266 child, support and wage conditions for the family allowance are met, including that the higher-paid parent's wage does not exceed twice the minimum if both are employees. Ana works full time for a private company. For December 2026 she is paid a salary of G 4,000,000, overtime of G 600,000, the family allowance for two children of G 304,400 (5% of the G 3,044,000 minimum wage per child, Labour Code art. 261) and her aguinaldo of G 4,300,000.

- **Base.** Salary plus overtime, G 4,600,000. The family allowance and the aguinaldo are out. The base is above the minimum wage, so the floor does not bite.
- **Worker's share** 9%: G 414,000, withheld from her pay.
- **Employer's share** 16.5%: G 759,000.
- **Paid to IPS** for Ana: G 1,173,000 (25.5%).
- Had the aguinaldo and allowance been wrongly included, the base would have been G 9,204,400 and the payment G 2,347,122: G 1,174,122 too much.

```oa-check
name: PY IPS, December 2026 employee with overtime, allowance and aguinaldo
inputs:
  salary: 4000000
  overtime: 600000
  family_allowance: 304400
  aguinaldo: 4300000
  worker_rate: 0.09
  employer_rate: 0.165
steps:
  - base: salary + overtime
  - worker_share: base * worker_rate
  - employer_share: base * employer_rate
  - total_to_ips: worker_share + employer_share
  - wrong_base: base + family_allowance + aguinaldo
  - wrong_total: wrong_base * (worker_rate + employer_rate)
  - overpayment: wrong_total - total_to_ips
expected:
  base: 4600000
  worker_share: 414000
  employer_share: 759000
  total_to_ips: 1173000
  wrong_base: 9204400
  wrong_total: 2347122
  overpayment: 1174122
```

### Example 2: decision, a self-employed consultant choosing a voluntary base

Luis is an independent consultant, not an employee. He wants an IPS pension and joins the pension fund voluntarily under Ley 4933. He pays 13% on a base he chooses, not below one minimum wage, and may change the base each year (art. 3). At 60 with 25 years of contributions the pension is 100% of the average base of his last 120 months (art. 4 a). He compares two bases from July 2026, when the minimum wage for unspecified activities is G 3,044,000 (Resolución MTESS 670/2026 art. 1):

- **One minimum wage**: 13% of G 3,044,000 = G 395,720 a month.
- **Two minimum wages**: 13% of G 6,088,000 = G 791,440 a month, G 395,720 more.

The immediate cost comparison is G 395,720 more per month for the higher elected base; each extra G 1,000,000 costs G 130,000. The stated pension formula uses the last 120 months, but this does not establish a universal optimal contribution strategy: actual retirement timing, contribution history, future law and eligibility matter. Voluntary affiliation provides no health cover while contributing under art. 1; once retired, members have health benefits (IPS contribution-base page). Any other route to health cover before retirement needs a separate eligibility check.

```oa-check
name: PY IPS voluntary affiliation, base of one or two minimum wages (from July 2026)
inputs:
  minimum_wage: 3044000
  voluntary_rate: 0.13
steps:
  - contribution_one_mw: minimum_wage * voluntary_rate
  - contribution_two_mw: minimum_wage * 2 * voluntary_rate
  - extra_cost: contribution_two_mw - contribution_one_mw
  - cost_per_million: 1000000 * voluntary_rate
expected:
  contribution_one_mw: 395720
  contribution_two_mw: 791440
  extra_cost: 395720
  cost_per_million: 130000
```

## Ask the client first

1. Is the person an employee, a domestic worker, a part-time worker (hours a week), a bank employee, a teacher, an ANDE employee, or self-employed?
2. The month's pay broken down: salary, overtime, commissions, bonuses, pay in kind, family allowance and aguinaldo.
3. Does the full-time pay reach the minimum wage for that month (before or from 1 July 2026)?
4. Is the employer registered with IPS (número patronal) and the worker registered in REI, and since when?
5. Are any months unpaid, and was the worker's share deducted from pay in those months?
