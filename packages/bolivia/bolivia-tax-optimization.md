# Bolivia tax optimisation for 2026: the simplified regime (RTS) and general-regime reliefs v0.2

> **General reference only.** This skill is general tax/accounting reference material for AI-assisted workflows. It has not been reviewed for any specific person's facts, documents, elections, deadlines, residency, filing status, or local procedures. Do not rely on it to file, pay, amend, or take a tax position without review by a qualified professional in the relevant jurisdiction.

The simplified regime is Decreto Supremo N° 24484 of 29 January 1997 (Régimen Tributario Simplificado, RTS), with the amounts set by DS 3698 of 25 October 2018 and DS 27924 of 20 December 2004. It is read in the SIN's consolidated edition "Ley N° 843 y Decretos Reglamentarios, Texto Ordenado, actualizado al 30/09/2026", which reproduces it in its annex of other rules. All amounts are in bolivianos (Bs). Tax year 2026 (calendar year for the RTS).

This skill owns the RTS. For the general regime it names each relief and links to the skill that owns its figures: [bolivia-corporate-income-tax](bolivia-corporate-income-tax.md), [bolivia-iva](bolivia-iva.md), [bolivia-income-tax](bolivia-income-tax.md), [bolivia-cross-border](bolivia-cross-border.md). Aggressive positions are never advised.

## Who this applies to

- **RTS: individuals only**, who habitually work as (DS 24484 arts. 2 to 6):
  - **comerciantes minoristas:** buying and selling goods or providing services in public markets, fairs, kiosks, small shops and street stalls;
  - **vivanderos:** selling meals, drinks and food in kiosks and small premises;
  - **artesanos:** practising a manual art or trade on their own account, in a workshop or at home, paid by clients.
- **Excluded from the RTS even if they meet the limits** (art. 7): sellers of electronic devices and household appliances; anyone registered for the ICE (excise tax); anyone who opts for the general regime; taxpayers in the Sistema Tributario Integrado (STI, transport owners) or the agricultural regime; commission agents, who pay the IUE. People with capital of Bs 1 to Bs 12,000 are also outside the RTS (art. 18).
- **Everyone else** (companies, professionals, larger traders) is in the general regime: IVA, IT, IUE or RC-IVA. Its reliefs are below.

**When to refer.** Refer for: transport owners (STI, DS 23027) and farmers (Régimen Agropecuario Unificado); recategorisation or a change of regime ordered by the SIN; any restructuring, holding company or transfer abroad (see [bolivia-transfer-pricing](bolivia-transfer-pricing.md)); and every question whose honest answer is "it depends on facts we cannot see".

## Rates, bands and thresholds

Quick answer: a small retailer, food seller or artisan with capital from Bs 12,001 to Bs 60,000 and sales up to Bs 184,000 a year can pay one fixed bimonthly amount, from Bs 47 to Bs 350, instead of IVA, IT and IUE. In exchange they cannot issue invoices.

## The base

**The RTS base is capital, not profit or sales.** Capital is the working capital in the business plus its fixed assets (DS 24484 art. 3):

- **Retailers and food sellers:** goods for sale, drinks, materials and supplies used, plus furniture, crockery and utensils.
- **Artisans:** furniture, tools and small machines, plus materials, work in progress and finished goods.
- Own and borrowed capital are counted alike. New fixed assets are valued at cost on purchase invoices in the taxpayer's name; used assets at market value less depreciation for the years used (furniture 10% a year, small machines 12.5%, tools 25%).

**Steps for an RTS taxpayer:**

1. Check the activity is one of the three, and none of the exclusions applies (art. 7).
2. Check the three limits: capital Bs 12,001 to Bs 60,000; sales up to Bs 184,000 a year; unit prices under the cap.
3. Find the category for the declared capital in the art. 17 table, and register with the SIN for a NIT in that category (art. 9). The SIN can check and correct the declared capital (art. 10).
4. Pay the bimonthly amount by the 10th of the month after each two-month period (art. 19). It replaces IVA, IUE and IT (art. 1). A business starting in the first month of a period pays for that period; one starting in the second month pays from the next period (art. 20).
5. **Do not issue invoices** (art. 25). Do **demand invoices in your name and NIT** from general-regime suppliers and keep them at the place of business; goods held without a purchase invoice are presumed to be fraud, and imported goods without an invoice or import document are presumed to be contraband (art. 26).
6. Show the registration certificate and the receipt for the last bimonthly payment where customers can see them (art. 27).
7. **At 31 December:** if capital moved to another category, declare the new category (art. 11). If any requirement is no longer met, tell the SIN; from 1 January the taxpayer is in the general regime for IVA, IUE and IT (art. 13).

**The trade-off.** The RTS is cheap and simple, but RTS sellers cannot give invoices. A customer who needs an invoice (a company claiming IVA credit or an IUE expense, or an employee claiming RC-IVA credit on Form 110) will buy elsewhere. A person who meets the RTS limits may choose the general regime when registering (art. 8).

**General-regime reliefs.** These are lawful, and each has its own conditions in the skill that owns it:

**Red lines.** Never use invoices for purchases that did not happen or were not for the business. Never register in the RTS, or in a lower category, against the facts. Never split payments to dodge the Bs 50,000 bank-document rule. Each is an audit focus and can cost the deduction, the credit and a sanction (DS 24484 arts. 14, 15 and 26; Ley 2492, see [bolivia-corporate-income-tax](bolivia-corporate-income-tax.md)).

## Deadlines and filing

| What | When | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| Register for a NIT in the right RTS category | Before starting, in the form and time the SIN sets | DS 24484 art. 9 |
| Bimonthly RTS payment | Day 10 of March, May, July, September, November and January | art. 19 |
| Declare a change of category | At the end of the year, as the SIN sets | art. 11 |
| Report that a requirement is no longer met; move to the general regime | At 31 December; general regime from 1 January | art. 13 |
| Choose the general regime instead of the RTS | When registering | art. 8 |

Late RTS payments are updated and carry interest and fines under the Código Tributario (art. 22).

## Penalties and interest

- **Wrong category or regime not reported:** sanctioned under the Código Tributario (DS 24484 art. 14). The SIN can move the taxpayer up a category or into the general regime and charge the difference between what was paid and what was due, plus the sanction (art. 15).
- **Issuing invoices while in the RTS:** sanctioned; the invoices give no IVA credit and no expense for IVA, RC-IVA or IUE (art. 25).
- **Goods without purchase invoices:** presumed fraud; imported goods without an invoice or import document: presumed contraband (art. 26).
- **Importing beyond the limits:** transfer to the general regime (art. 26).
- The decree cites articles of the Código Tributario then in force. Penalties under today's Código (Ley 2492) are in [bolivia-corporate-income-tax](bolivia-corporate-income-tax.md); refer for any sanction case.

## Cross-border points

- **RTS taxpayers cannot import appliances, electronics or ICE goods.** Their other imports are capped in total and per unit (art. 26).
- **Paying abroad** (royalties, services, dividends): IUE-BE at 12.5%, with treaty and reinvestment reliefs ([bolivia-cross-border](bolivia-cross-border.md)).
- **Related parties abroad:** prices must be at arm's length ([bolivia-transfer-pricing](bolivia-transfer-pricing.md)).

## Two worked examples that recompute

### Example 1: which RTS category, and what it costs in 2026

Doña Rosa runs a food stall (vivandera). Her stock and supplies are worth Bs 14,000, and her used kiosk furniture cost Bs 20,000 four years ago. Its market value is Bs 16,000. Her unit prices are under Bs 148 and her sales about Bs 120,000 a year.

- Furniture: market value Bs 16,000 less depreciation 10% x 4 years = 40%, so Bs 6,400. Net value Bs 9,600. The decree values used assets as "the difference between a) and b)": market value less accumulated depreciation. This example applies the percentage to the market value; see "Not yet confirmed".
- Capital: 14,000 + 9,600 = Bs 23,600. That is category 4 (Bs 23,501 to 29,500): Bs 158 every two months, Bs 948 for 2026.
- The edge: had the furniture been bought new this year for Bs 20,000 (with invoices), capital would be Bs 34,000, category 5: Bs 200 every two months, Bs 1,200 a year.

```oa-check
name: Vivandera, RTS category from declared capital, 2026
inputs:
  stock: 14000
  furniture_market: 16000
  years_used: 4
  furniture_new: 20000
steps:
  - depreciation: furniture_market * 0.10 * years_used
  - furniture_net: furniture_market - depreciation
  - capital: stock + furniture_net
  - annual_cat4: 158 * 6
  - capital_new_furniture: stock + furniture_new
  - annual_cat5: 200 * 6
expected:
  depreciation: 6400
  furniture_net: 9600
  capital: 23600
  annual_cat4: 948
  capital_new_furniture: 34000
  annual_cat5: 1200
```

### Example 2: should a market retailer stay in the RTS or choose the general regime? Worked both ways

Don Mario sells clothes in a market. Capital Bs 45,000 (category 6), sales Bs 150,000 a year, all purchases from general-regime suppliers with invoices: Bs 120,000. He meets every RTS limit.

- **RTS:** Bs 350 x 6 = Bs 2,100 a year, nothing else for IVA, IT or IUE.
- **General regime** (rates and method in [bolivia-iva](bolivia-iva.md)): IVA debit 13% of Bs 150,000 = Bs 19,500, less credit 13% of Bs 120,000 = Bs 15,600: IVA Bs 3,900. IT 3% of sales: Bs 4,500. IUE on his profit is credited against the next year's IT, so take it as adding no cash if it does not exceed the IT ([bolivia-corporate-income-tax](bolivia-corporate-income-tax.md)). Cash cost at least Bs 8,400, plus monthly returns.
- **What decides it** is whether his customers need invoices. If most are businesses that need an invoice, the general regime may keep sales the RTS would lose. Otherwise the RTS costs Bs 6,300 less a year.
- The edge: if sales rise to Bs 190,000, above Bs 184,000, the RTS is closed to him from 1 January of the next year, whatever he prefers (arts. 13 and 18).

```oa-check
name: Market retailer, RTS category 6 vs general regime cash cost (2026)
inputs:
  sales: 150000
  purchases: 120000
  rts_bimonthly: 350
steps:
  - rts_annual: rts_bimonthly * 6
  - iva_debit: sales * 0.13
  - iva_credit: purchases * 0.13
  - iva_net: iva_debit - iva_credit
  - it: sales * 0.03
  - general_cash: iva_net + it
  - rts_saving: general_cash - rts_annual
expected:
  rts_annual: 2100
  iva_debit: 19500
  iva_credit: 15600
  iva_net: 3900
  it: 4500
  general_cash: 8400
  rts_saving: 6300
```

## Ask the client first

1. What exactly the business does (retail in a market or stall, food sales, artisan work, services, appliances or electronics?) and whether it is an individual or a company.
2. Capital in the business, at cost or market value with the asset ages, and annual sales.
3. The highest unit price charged.
4. Whether customers need invoices (companies, employees claiming Form 110 credit).
5. Whether the business imports, and what.
6. For companies and professionals: assets bought in 2026, unused losses, payments abroad, profit distributions, and how purchases are paid (bank document for Bs 50,000 or more).
