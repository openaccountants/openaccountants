# Hiring your children and reimbursing owners through an accountable plan, tax year 2026

> **Source-cited draft.** Built from IRS Pub. 15 (2026), the IRS family employees page, 26 U.S.C. 1(g), 63(c)(5), 267, 280A and 3121(b)(3), Treas. Reg. 1.62-2, 1.162-7, 31.3121(b)(3)-1, 31.3121(d)-1 and 301.7701-2, Rev. Proc. 2025-32, Rev. Proc. 2019-46, Rev. Proc. 2019-48, Notice 2026-10, the IRS standard mileage rates page and Public Law 119-21 section 70110, read on 2026-11-13. No accountant has reviewed it. All outputs must be reviewed by a qualified tax professional before filing.

**Answer first.** A child **under 18** working in a parent's **sole proprietorship**, a partnership whose **only partners are the child's parents**, or a parent's **disregarded single-member LLC** pays no social security or Medicare tax on wages, and no FUTA applies **under 21**. A **corporation or S corporation never qualifies**, even if the parent owns 100%. Income tax rules apply at any age: a dependent child's standard deduction for 2026 is the greater of **USD 1,350** or earned income plus **USD 450**, capped at **USD 16,100**, so a reasonable wage up to USD 16,100 bears no federal income tax; the kiddie tax never reaches wages. Separately, an **accountable plan** (business connection, substantiation, return of excess) lets a business reimburse an employee, including an S corporation owner-employee, tax-free; 2026 car use is deemed substantiated at **72.5 cents** a mile to 30 June and **76 cents** from 1 July.

## Scope and who this is for

In scope: a business owner (and the adviser) who wants to pay their own child a wage for real work in the business, or to reimburse owners and employees for business costs through an accountable plan, for tax year 2026.

## Ask the client first

1. **What legal entity employs the child, and how is it taxed:** sole proprietorship, disregarded single-member LLC, partnership (who are ALL the partners?), or a corporation including an LLC taxed as an S or C corporation?
2. **The child's date of birth**, and when in 2026 the child turns 18 or 21.
3. **The work:** what the child actually does, hours, rate, what the business would pay an unrelated person for the same work, and whether there are timesheets.
4. **The child's other income** (interest, dividends, capital gains) and who claims the child as a dependent.
5. **For reimbursements:** is the person on payroll as an employee, is there a written plan, are expenses logged and submitted within 60 days and any excess returned within 120 days?
6. **For travel and home office:** what percentage of the corporation does the person own (directly and through family), and is any home office for the employer's convenience?

### Part A, hiring your own child

1. **Identify the legal employer.** The business entity pays the child, not the parent personally. The payroll tax result turns on this (IRS [Family employees](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/family-help); [26 C.F.R. 31.3121(b)(3)-1](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-26/section-31.3121%28b%29%283%29-1)).
2. **Confirm the child's age for each payment** and that the work is in the parent's trade or business. Social security and Medicare exemption ends at 18; FUTA at 21 ([26 U.S.C. 3121(b)(3)(A)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title26/html/USCODE-2024-title26-subtitleC-chap21-subchapC-sec3121.htm)).
3. **Set a wage that is reasonable for work the child actually does.** The deduction is "a reasonable allowance for salaries or other compensation for personal services actually rendered", tested on "whether they are reasonable and are in fact payments purely for services", against what "would ordinarily be paid for like services by like enterprises under like circumstances" ([26 C.F.R. 1.162-7](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-26/section-1.162-7)).
4. **Run the child through payroll like any employee:** name and SSN, income tax withholding under the normal rules, Form W-2 (Pub. 15 (2026), sections 3 and 4).
5. **Check the child's own income tax:** the dependent standard deduction ([26 U.S.C. 63(c)(5)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title26/html/USCODE-2024-title26-subtitleA-chap1-subchapB-partI-sec63.htm)) and the kiddie tax, which does not reach wages ([26 U.S.C. 1(g)(4)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title26/html/USCODE-2024-title26-subtitleA-chap1-subchapA-partI-sec1.htm)).

### Part B, accountable plans

6. **Put a written accountable plan in place** meeting all three tests of [26 C.F.R. 1.62-2](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-26/section-1.62-2): business connection, substantiation within a reasonable period, return of excess within a reasonable period.
7. **Reimburse car use** at no more than the business standard mileage rate for the date the miles were driven ([Rev. Proc. 2019-46](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/rp-19-46.pdf)), and **travel** at no more than the federal per diem rate or at actual cost with receipts ([Rev. Proc. 2019-48](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/rp-19-48.pdf)).
8. **Keep plan payments off Form W-2.** Anything paid outside the plan, or above the deemed-substantiated rate, goes through payroll as wages (Treas. Reg. 1.62-2(c)(4), (c)(5) and (h)).

## Boundary and exception table

### Who gets the child payroll tax exemption

The exemption depends on WHO employs the child, not on whether the parent owns the business (IRS Family employees page).

| Employer | Child's pay | IRS wording |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Parent's sole proprietorship, or a partnership in which each partner is a parent of the child | Income tax withholding at any age; no social security or Medicare under 18; no FUTA under 21 | "Payments for the services of a child under the age 18 are not subject to social security and Medicare taxes." |
| Same, child aged 21 or older | FUTA applies | "If the child is 21 years or older, then payments for the services of a child are subject to FUTA taxes." |
| A corporation (C or S), a partnership unless each partner is a parent, or an estate | Income tax withholding, social security, Medicare and FUTA all apply at any age | "subject to income tax withholding, social security taxes, Medicare taxes and FUTA taxes regardless of age" |
| Parent's single-member LLC disregarded for income tax | Treated as a corporation for employment taxes generally (Treas. Reg. 301.7701-2(c)(2)(iv)(B)), but for this family exemption the owner is treated as the employer, so it can qualify; applies to wages paid on or after 1 November 2011 (Treas. Reg. 31.3121(b)(3)-1(d)) | |

- **Turning 18 mid-year.** The exemption is lost for service performed from the 18th birthday, not retroactively for the year: the statute exempts "service performed by a child under the age of 18" and the regulation says the exception "continues only during the time that" the child is under the limit. Which pay date carries wages for service in the birthday month is not settled by these pages; ask the accountant.
- **The regulation's old age.** Treas. Reg. 31.3121(b)(3)-1(a)(3) still prints "under the age of 21". The statute as amended, and Pub. 15 (2026), say 18 for work in the parent's trade or business; the statute governs. Use the regulation for who the employer is (paragraphs (c) and (d)), not for the age.
- **A corporation never qualifies,** including an S corporation, a C corporation, and an LLC that elected either, even at 100% parent ownership: "Services performed in the employ of a corporation are not within the exception" (Treas. Reg. 31.3121(b)(3)-1(d)); Pub. 15 lists "A corporation, even if it is controlled by the child's parent".
- **A partnership qualifies only if EVERY partner is a parent of the child.** A parent and a grandparent, a sibling or an unrelated partner do not (Treas. Reg. 31.3121(b)(3)-1(c)).
- **Income tax withholding still applies.** A child with no income tax liability last year and none expected this year may claim exemption from withholding on Form W-4; the wages "are still subject to social security and Medicare taxes" where those apply (Pub. 15 (2026), section 9).
- **Domestic work in the parent's private home** (not the business) follows a different rule: no social security or Medicare until 21. Refer household employment questions.

### The child's own income tax

- The child's standard deduction is the lesser of USD 16,100 and the greater of USD 1,350 or wages plus USD 450 (section 63(c)(5) only limits the basic standard deduction; it never raises it above the single amount). So wages up to USD 16,100 bear no federal income tax, and wages above are taxed on the excess.
- The kiddie tax taxes "net unearned income", the part of AGI "which is not attributable to earned income" (section 1(g)(4)(A)). Wages for work actually done are earned income, so they are outside it at any age (IRS [Topic no. 553](https://www.irs.gov/taxtopics/tc553)). If the child also has unearned income, the deduction is still built from earned income only and the unearned income may fall under the kiddie tax; work the full return.
- A child with earned income can contribute to an IRA or Roth IRA, limited by that income and the annual limit: see `us-self-employed-retirement`.

### Reasonable pay for real work

- The work must be real and done by the child: tasks the child can do at that age, for the business, not household chores.
- The rate should match what the business would pay an unrelated person of similar ability.
- Keep records made at the time: timesheets or an hours log, a list of duties, pay stubs, payment into an account in the child's name. Pay above the reasonable amount is not deductible as wages.
- Pay through payroll and issue Form W-2; the employer must get "each employee's name and SSN and to enter them on Form W-2" (Pub. 15 (2026), section 4).

### Accountable plan: the three requirements (all three)

| Requirement | What it means | Regulation wording |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Business connection, paragraph (d) | Pays only for deductible business expenses the employee pays or incurs performing services as an employee. Payment regardless of whether the employee has expenses fails | "If a payor arranges to pay an amount to an employee regardless of whether the employee incurs" |
| 2. Substantiation, paragraph (e) | Each expense substantiated to the payor within a reasonable period; for travel, car use and other section 274(d) items: amount, time, place or use, business purpose | "requires each business expense to be substantiated to the payor" |
| 3. Return of excess, paragraph (f) | Employee returns within a reasonable period any amount beyond substantiated expenses | "requires the employee to return to the payor within a reasonable period of time" |
| Fixed date safe harbour, paragraph (g)(2)(i) | Advance within 30 days; substantiation within 60 days after the expense; excess returned within 120 days after the expense | "An advance made within 30 days of when an expense is paid or incurred" |
| Periodic statement safe harbour, paragraph (g)(2)(ii) | Statements at least quarterly; amounts substantiated or returned within 120 days of the statement are timely | "no less frequently than quarterly" |

Outside the safe harbours the reasonable period "will depend on the facts and circumstances" (paragraph (g)(1)). If wages and a reimbursement are paid together, the reimbursement must be paid or identified separately (paragraph (d)(1)). A pattern of abuse turns every payment under the arrangement into a nonaccountable payment (paragraph (k)).

The plan covers expenses an EMPLOYEE pays "in connection with the performance of services as an employee of the employer" (paragraph (d)(1)). "Generally, an officer of a corporation is an employee of the corporation" ([26 C.F.R. 31.3121(d)-1(b)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-26/section-31.3121%28d%29-1)); an officer who performs no or only minor services and is unpaid is not. So an S corporation shareholder who works as an officer and is on payroll can be reimbursed under the corporation's plan. A sole proprietor's own costs and a partner's costs are outside this skill.

### What happens if the plan fails

- If any requirement is not met, everything paid under the arrangement is nonaccountable: wages on Form W-2, subject to income tax withholding, social security, Medicare and FUTA (paragraphs (c)(3) and (c)(5)).
- The employee cannot fix it afterwards: an employee "cannot compel the payor to treat the payments as paid under an accountable plan by voluntarily substantiating the expenses and returning any excess" (paragraph (c)(3)(i)).
- If the plan meets all three tests but an employee keeps an excess beyond a reasonable period, only the excess becomes wages (paragraph (c)(2)(ii)).
- No deduction makes up for it: miscellaneous itemized deductions, where unreimbursed employee expenses sat, are suspended permanently, because Public Law 119-21 section 70110 struck "and before January 1, 2026" from section 67(g) ([Public Law 119-21](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/PLAW-119publ21/html/PLAW-119publ21.htm)). Educator expenses are the exception that section adds back. For an S corporation shareholder-employee an unreimbursed expense is generally lost.

### S corporation owner-employee: the usual items

Business miles in the owner's car, travel away from home, business costs on a personal card, and the home office, the item most often argued about:

- An employee's home office counts only if, among the other section 280A(c)(1) conditions, the exclusive use is "for the convenience of his employer" ([26 U.S.C. 280A(c)(1)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title26/html/USCODE-2024-title26-subtitleA-chap1-subchapB-partIX-sec280A.htm)).
- If that test fails, the cost is not the employee's business expense, so it fails the business connection test and a reimbursement is wages.
- How to allocate home costs for a reimbursement is not settled by the pages above; refer the computation.

### Car use

A mileage allowance in place of actual car costs is deemed substantiated up to "the business standard mileage rate multiplied by the number of substantiated business miles", if time, place (or use) and business purpose of each trip are substantiated (Rev. Proc. 2019-46, sections 7.01 and 7.02). Any amount above the rate "is treated as paid under a nonaccountable plan and is subject to withholding and payment of employment taxes" (section 8.01). There are two rates in 2026, so split miles by the date driven. A fixed and variable rate (FAVR) allowance cannot go to a "control employee" under 26 C.F.R. 1.61-21(f)(5) and (6) and needs at least five covered employees (section 6.05). A flat monthly car allowance paid whether or not business miles are driven fails the business connection test and is wages.

### Travel: per diem, and the trap for owners of more than 10 percent

- A per diem for lodging, meals and incidentals is deemed substantiated up to "the federal per diem rate" for the locality and day, if time, place and business purpose are substantiated (Rev. Proc. 2019-48, sections 4.01 and 7.01). The rates are published by the General Services Administration and not reproduced here.
- The full per diem method (section 4.01) and the high-low method (section 5) "do not apply if a payor and an employee are related within the meaning of § 267(b)", with the ownership test set at "10 percent" (section 6.07). An S corporation shareholder-employee owning, directly or through family attribution under section 267(c), more than 10 percent in value of the stock is reimbursed for lodging at actual cost with receipts; a child or parent of such an owner on the payroll is related in the same way ([26 U.S.C. 267](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title26/html/USCODE-2024-title26-subtitleA-chap1-subchapB-partIX-sec267.htm)).
- Section 6.07 does not name the meals and incidental expenses only per diem (section 4.02), which requires that "the payor pays the employee for actual expenses for lodging based on receipts submitted to the payor". The revenue procedure does not say in words that section 4.02 is open to a related employee; treat it as available only on the accountant's confirmation.

### Defaults when a fact is missing

| Missing fact | Default, and flag it |
| --- | --- |
| Entity type of the employer | Treat as a corporation (all payroll taxes apply) until confirmed |
| Child's date of birth | No exemption until the age is documented for each pay date |
| Timesheets | No wage deduction position until hours and duties are recorded |
| Written accountable plan | Treat reimbursements as wages until a plan exists before the payments |
| Mileage dates | Use 72.5 cents for all miles until the log shows the July to December miles |
| Owner's percentage | Treat as over 10%: lodging at actual cost |

### Red flags to raise

| Flag | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| Child paid by an S corporation with no payroll taxes | The exemption does not exist for corporations |
| Child's wage set at exactly USD 16,100 with no record of hours | Looks set by the deduction, not the work |
| Flat monthly allowance to an owner | Wages, not an accountable plan |
| Reimbursements decided after year end | Fails the reasonable period; cannot be backfilled |
| Owner reimbursed on per diem for lodging | Owner of more than 10% must use actual cost |

## Worked cases

**Case 1, same child, two employers.** A 15-year-old works weekends in a parent's business, keeps a timesheet, and is paid a reasonable USD 12,000 for 2026 through payroll; no other income; claimed as a dependent. Case 1A, the parent's sole proprietorship: no social security or Medicare (under 18) and no FUTA (under 21). Case 1B, the parent's S corporation: USD 744 social security (6.2%) plus USD 174 Medicare (1.45%) = USD 918 withheld, the employer pays the same again, USD 1,836 in total, and FUTA applies. Income tax is the same in both: the child's standard deduction is the lesser of USD 16,100 and the greater of USD 1,350 or USD 12,450 (wages plus USD 450), so USD 12,450, and taxable income is nil. At wages of USD 17,000 instead, the deduction is capped at USD 16,100 and USD 900 is taxable. The business deducts the wages (and in 1B the employer's share) if the pay is reasonable; in 1A the deduction also cuts the parent's self-employment income.

```oa-check
name: Child's wages, sole proprietorship against S corporation (2026)
inputs:
  wages: 12000
  higher_wages: 17000
  single_standard: 16100
steps:
  - ss_withheld: wages * 0.062
  - medicare_withheld: wages * 0.0145
  - employee_total: ss_withheld + medicare_withheld
  - fica_total_s_corp: employee_total * 2
  - child_deduction: min(single_standard, max(1350, wages + 450))
  - child_taxable: max(wages - child_deduction, 0)
  - higher_taxable: max(higher_wages - min(single_standard, max(1350, higher_wages + 450)), 0)
expected:
  ss_withheld: 744
  medicare_withheld: 174
  employee_total: 918
  fica_total_s_corp: 1836
  child_deduction: 12450
  child_taxable: 0
  higher_taxable: 900
```

**Case 2, a decision: what mileage rate to reimburse.** An S corporation's shareholder-employee drives 1,000 business miles in March 2026 and 1,000 in September 2026, logs each trip and submits the log within 60 days. Reimbursed at the right rates: USD 725 for March (72.5 cents) plus USD 760 for September (76 cents), USD 1,485, all under the accountable plan and off Form W-2. Paid at 72.5 cents throughout (USD 1,450), the September miles are under-reimbursed by USD 35, which the owner cannot deduct. Paid at 76 cents throughout (USD 1,520), the USD 35 excess on the March miles is wages. With no log, the whole allowance is wages. Reimburse each half-year at its own rate.

```oa-check
name: Mileage reimbursement across the two 2026 rates
inputs:
  miles_h1: 1000
  miles_h2: 1000
steps:
  - h1: miles_h1 * 0.725
  - h2: miles_h2 * 0.76
  - correct_total: h1 + h2
  - all_at_h1_rate: (miles_h1 + miles_h2) * 0.725
  - shortfall_if_h1_rate: correct_total - all_at_h1_rate
  - all_at_h2_rate: (miles_h1 + miles_h2) * 0.76
  - wages_if_h2_rate: all_at_h2_rate - correct_total
expected:
  h1: 725
  h2: 760
  correct_total: 1485
  all_at_h1_rate: 1450
  shortfall_if_h1_rate: 35
  all_at_h2_rate: 1520
  wages_if_h2_rate: 35
```

## Filing and payment

The child's wages go through the business's payroll: income tax withheld, Form 941 each quarter, Form 940 if FUTA applies, and a 2026 Form W-2 to the child and the SSA by **1 February 2027** (31 January 2027 is a Sunday); see `us-form-941-940-payroll` for deposits and due dates. In the exempt case, boxes 3 and 5 of Form W-2 are left blank for social security and Medicare wages. Accountable plan payments are not reported on Form W-2; amounts treated as wages are. The child files their own 2026 Form 1040 only if a filing requirement applies or to recover withheld tax.

## Completion checklist

- Employer entity and all partners identified; corporation cases run with full payroll tax.
- Child's age checked against each pay date; 18 and 21 birthdays noted.
- Wage supported by timesheets, duties and an unrelated-person rate; paid through payroll with a W-2.
- Child's standard deduction worked from earned income; unearned income checked for the kiddie tax.
- Written accountable plan in place before the payments; 60 and 120 day timings met.
- Mileage split between 72.5 and 76 cents; per diem not used for lodging for owners of more than 10%.
- Anything outside the plan run through payroll as wages.
