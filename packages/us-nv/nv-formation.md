# Nevada entity formation: LLC or corporation, fees, lists and business license (2026)

> **Source-cited draft.** Built from NRS chapters 76, 77, 78, 86, 112 and 166 (Nevada Legislature) and the Nevada Secretary of State's LLC and profit corporation fee schedules, expedite and copies schedules, State Business License FAQ and LLC formation page, read on 2026-11-14. No accountant has reviewed it. All outputs must be reviewed by a qualified professional before filing.

**Answer first.** A Nevada LLC costs **USD 425** in its first year at the Secretary of State: articles USD 75, initial list USD 150 and State Business License USD 200. Each year after, the annual list (USD 150) and the license renewal (USD 200) are filed together: **USD 350**. A corporation pays a **USD 500** business license, not USD 200, and its articles and annual list fees scale with the value of its authorized shares. Shares with a par value below 0.1 cent are counted at 0.1 cent, so 10,000,000 shares at USD 0.0001 par stay in the lowest band (USD 75 articles, USD 150 list); the same shares with no par value are counted at USD 1 each and cost USD 5,325 to file and USD 5,350 a year.

## Scope and who this is for

This skill covers forming a Nevada LLC (NRS chapter 86) or corporation (NRS chapter 78) with the Nevada Secretary of State: the filing, the initial and annual list, the State Business License, the registered agent, default and reinstatement, and the Nevada rules people form here for (the charging order, series, spendthrift trusts). It is for founders and the accountants who advise them.

It links, and does not restate:

- Commerce Tax and the Modified Business Tax: `nv-commerce-and-mbt`; sales and use tax: `nv-sales-tax`; unemployment insurance: `nv-payroll`. Nevada has no individual income tax and no state income-tax withholding (pack README).
- Choice of state against Wyoming, Delaware or the home state, foreign registration in other states, series recognition elsewhere and beneficial ownership (BOI) reporting: `us-state-formation-matrix`; Wyoming: `wy-formation`; Delaware: `de-formation`.
- Federal classification: `us-form-8832-entity-classification`; S election: `us-s-corp-election-decision`; Form 5472: `us-form-5472-foreign-owned-us`; Schedule C: `us-schedule-c-and-se-computation`; crypto: `us-crypto-tax`.

Out of scope: gaming, cannabis, mining, banking, insurance and other regulated-industry licensing; securities law; trust drafting; estate-tax planning beyond the spendthrift-trust overview (refer, see below).

## Ask the client first

- Is the owner a Nevada resident? Forming in Nevada does not change where the owner lives for tax. A resident of another state is taxed there on the entity's income, and the entity usually must register where it actually does business (`us-state-formation-matrix`).
- What will the entity do, and where: Nevada operations, Nevada property, or a holding entity only?
- LLC or corporation? For a corporation: number of authorized shares, par value or no par, classes. These set the articles and every annual list fee.
- Member-managed or manager-managed? Who are the initial managers or members (they go on the articles and the list)?
- Series wanted? Asset protection the main goal (charging order, spendthrift trust)? Any known creditor, pending claim or threatened suit?
- Who will be the registered agent (commercial or a named individual), and at what price?
- Outside investors, preferred stock or venture finance planned? If so, refer (see below).
- Is any owner a non-US person (Form 5472, EIN route)?

## The method, step by step

1. **Choose the entity type and the state** from the answers above; record why. A non-resident owner forming here for tax reasons alone gets no saving.
2. **Check the name.** An LLC name must contain "Limited-Liability Company," "Limited Liability Company," "Limited Company," or "Limited" or "Ltd.," "L.L.C.," "L.C.," "LLC" or "LC" and be distinguishable on the Secretary of State's records (NRS 86.171). A corporation's name is checked under NRS 78.039. A name may be reserved (USD 25, NRS 86.561 and 78.785).
3. **Appoint a registered agent.** The filing names the commercial registered agent, or a noncommercial agent's name and address, with the agent's certificate of acceptance (NRS 77.310). Anyone acting as agent for 10 or more entities must register as a commercial registered agent (NRS 77.320).
4. **Set the corporation's share structure** before filing: the articles fee and every annual list fee follow the value of authorized shares (below).
5. **File the articles online on ORION** (orion.nv.gov, the Secretary of State's licensing portal) for immediate service, or by mail or in person. Online filing of articles is available only for a chapter 86 LLC; other LLC types file on paper. [LLC page](https://www.nvsos.gov/businesses/start-a-business/limited-liability-company)
   - LLC articles set out the name, the registered agent information, each organizer's name and address, the name and address of each initial manager (manager-managed) or initial member (member-managed), and a statement if the LLC may have series or is a restricted LLC (NRS 86.161).
   - Corporation articles set out the name, the registered agent, the authorized stock and the other matters in NRS 78.035. One person may be the sole director (NRS 78.115).
6. **File the initial list and the State Business License with the articles.** The statute requires the initial list "at the time of the filing" of the articles (NRS 86.263(1), 78.150(1)); the Secretary of State's FAQ says the initial list and license are due "no later than the last day of the month following the filing". They are filed together and cannot be separated. File them with the articles to remove the deadline risk. [SBL FAQ](https://www.nvsos.gov/licensing/state-business-license/state-business-license-faq)
7. **What happens next.** ORION filings are effective immediately; a 24-hour, 2-hour or 1-hour expedite applies to paper filings (below). The entity appears on the business entity search with its license expiry date. Order a certificate of good standing (existence) if a bank wants one.
8. **Complete the internal documents** (operating agreement or bylaws, consents, stock issuance), get an EIN and decide the federal classification.
9. **Diarise the annual list and license renewal**: due on or before the last day of the anniversary month of formation each year (NRS 86.263(2), 78.150(2)). The Secretary of State sends a notice 90 days before; not receiving it is no excuse.
10. **Register as a foreign entity** in any other state where the entity does business, and keep the Nevada filings going there too.

### The State Business License

"A person shall not conduct a business in this State unless and until the person obtains a state business license issued by the Secretary of State." An entity that files lists must get it "at the time of filing the initial or annual list" (NRS 76.100(1)). Any entity organized under Title 7 (an LLC or corporation formed in Nevada) is deemed to conduct a business in Nevada, as is one with an office, a registered agent or paid staff in Nevada (NRS 76.100(7)). If the entity has no place of business in Nevada, the registered agent's address is treated as its location. So a Nevada holding LLC with no Nevada activity still owes the license each year.

- **Who is exempt.** Nonprofits under NRS chapter 82 do not need one; certain chapter 81 nonprofits file a Declaration of Eligibility for Exemption with each list. [SBL FAQ](https://www.nvsos.gov/licensing/state-business-license/state-business-license-faq)
- **One license per entity.** An LLC or corporation is a separate "person" from its owner and needs its own license even if the owner already holds one as a sole proprietor. A single-member LLC is required to have one. [SBL FAQ](https://www.nvsos.gov/licensing/state-business-license/state-business-license-faq)
- **Local licenses are separate.** The state license is "in addition to any license to conduct business that must be obtained from the local jurisdiction" (NRS 76.100(6)).
- **No refund.** No part of the fee is refunded; an entity that stops must file dissolution to stop the next fee. [SBL FAQ](https://www.nvsos.gov/licensing/state-business-license/state-business-license-faq)

### Default, revocation and reinstatement

An entity that does not file its list and pay the fee on time is in default and owes the USD 75 penalty (NRS 86.272, 78.170). The Secretary of State notifies the registered agent. "On the first day of the first anniversary of the month following the month in which the filing was required, the charter of the company is revoked and its right to transact business is forfeited" (NRS 86.274(2), 78.175(2)). To reinstate, file the missing list and the NRS 77.310 agent information, a declaration that reinstatement is authorized, and pay every missed list fee and penalty for each year or part year plus USD 300 (NRS 86.276, 78.180). The license fees and USD 100 penalties are owed too (NRS 76.110). [SBL FAQ](https://www.nvsos.gov/licensing/state-business-license/state-business-license-faq)

### The charging order (NRS 86.401)

A judgment creditor of a member may get a court order charging the member's interest; "the judgment creditor has only the rights of an assignee". The charging order "Provides the exclusive remedy by which a judgment creditor of a member or an assignee of a member may satisfy a judgment out of the member's interest of the judgment debtor, whether the limited-liability company has one member or more than one member. No other remedy, including, without limitation, foreclosure on the member's interest ... is available". [NRS 86.401](https://www.leg.state.nv.us/NRS/NRS-086.html)

What it does not do:

- protect the LLC's own assets from the LLC's own creditors;
- stop a court disregarding the LLC as an alter ego where funds are commingled, formalities ignored or the LLC used for fraud;
- undo a fraudulent transfer: a transfer made "With actual intent to hinder, delay or defraud any creditor" is voidable whether the claim arose before or after it (NRS 112.180, below);
- bind the IRS, whose federal lien and levy reach the interest regardless of state law;
- override a written agreement between the member and a creditor (NRS 86.401(2)(c)).

Operating agreement points that support the order: say distributions are at the manager's discretion, not mandatory; restrict transfers of interests; keep separate bank accounts and books; document every contribution and distribution.

### Series (NRS 86.296)

The articles or operating agreement may authorize one or more series of members. A series may be created "without the filing of articles of organization", has its own property, purpose and contracts, and may sue and be sued in its own name. Its debts are enforceable against its assets only if "Separate and distinct records are maintained for the series" with its assets accounted for separately, and the articles or operating agreement says the liabilities are limited to that series (NRS 86.296(3)). The articles must state if the LLC may have series (NRS 86.161(1)(e)). Series pay no separate Nevada filing fee: the LLC files one list and holds one license. Whether other states, banks and courts respect a series, and its federal classification, are covered in `us-state-formation-matrix`. [NRS 86.296](https://www.leg.state.nv.us/NRS/NRS-086.html)

### Nevada spendthrift trusts (NRS chapter 166)

Nevada allows a self-settled spendthrift trust (often called a domestic asset protection trust). The points that interact with a Nevada LLC:

- **Settlor as beneficiary.** The writing must be irrevocable, must not require any distribution to the settlor, and must not have been "intended to hinder, delay or defraud known creditors" (NRS 166.040(1)(b)). The settlor may still veto distributions, hold a special power of appointment that cannot benefit the settlor or the settlor's creditors, and hold certain listed interests (NRS 166.040(2)).
- **Nevada trustee.** If the settlor is a beneficiary, at least one trustee must be a Nevada-resident individual, or a trust company or bank with a Nevada office (NRS 166.015(2)).
- **Time limit for creditors.** A creditor at the time of transfer must sue within 2 years after the transfer, or 6 months after discovering it (or when it should have been discovered), "whichever is later". A later creditor must sue within 2 years after the transfer (NRS 166.170(1)). A recorded deed or UCC filing counts as discovery (166.170(2)). Each transfer starts its own clock.
- **What a creditor must prove.** Clear and convincing evidence that the transfer was fraudulent under NRS chapter 112, or that it breaches a legal obligation under a contract or a valid court order enforceable by that creditor (NRS 166.170(3)). The statute names no other categories of exception creditors.
- **Badges of fraud** a court may weigh (NRS 112.180(2)): transfer to an insider; the debtor kept possession or control; concealment; the debtor had been sued or threatened with suit; substantially all assets transferred; absconding; removal or concealment of assets; value received not reasonably equivalent; insolvency at or shortly after the transfer; transfer shortly before or after a substantial debt. [NRS 112](https://www.leg.state.nv.us/NRS/NRS-112.html)

Fund such a trust only when there is no known creditor or pending claim, keep a dated solvency record, leave enough assets outside the trust, and have counsel draft it. Federal income and gift tax treatment (grantor or non-grantor, completed or incomplete gift) is out of scope: refer.

### Privacy on the public record

The articles name the organizers and the initial managers (manager-managed) or initial members (member-managed) (NRS 86.161), and every list names the managers or managing members (NRS 86.263) or the corporation's officers and directors (NRS 78.150), with addresses. A manager-managed LLC whose manager is another entity or a non-member keeps the members off the list, but the members are still known to the IRS (EIN responsible party), to banks, and to any court with a subpoena. Listing someone "with the fraudulent intent of concealing the identity" of the person really in control is an offence (NRS 86.263(3), 78.150(3)).

| --- | --- | --- |

### Red flags to raise with the client

- The State Business License was not paid at formation, or a corporation paid USD 200 instead of USD 500.
- No-par stock or a very large share value on a corporation.
- The entity shows "default" on the Secretary of State's search.
- The owner lives elsewhere and expects a Nevada tax saving.
- Assets moved into an LLC or trust after a claim arose or a suit was threatened.
- Personal and LLC money mixed; personal spending from LLC accounts (for crypto, seed phrases held personally rather than by the LLC).
- Venture finance planned for a Nevada corporation.

### Planning patterns kept from the old version (no figures)

- **Real estate in series.** One property per series, deeds and leases in the series' name, a bank account, books and insurance per series. Strict separation is the price of the shield.
- **Stacking.** An LLC interest held by a Nevada spendthrift trust combines the charging order and the trust's protection; the trust's 2-year clock starts when the LLC interest is transferred to it, not when the LLC was formed.
- **Operating business separate from holding assets.** Keep an operating entity with tort exposure outside the trust; a licensed professional cannot shield their own malpractice through a self-settled trust.
- **Crypto in an LLC.** New wallets or accounts in the LLC's name, a written contribution record valued at the transfer date, and custody of keys by the LLC. The federal tax treatment does not change (`us-crypto-tax`).

**Case 2, decision: LLC or corporation, and par or no par, over three years.** A founder compares three-year Secretary of State costs (no expedite, no agent). LLC: USD 425 in year 1, then USD 350 a year: USD 1,125. Corporation with 10,000,000 shares at USD 0.0001 par: share value USD 10,000 (par counted at USD 0.001), so articles USD 75, initial list USD 150, license USD 500 in year 1 (USD 725), then annual list USD 150 plus license USD 500 a year: USD 2,025. The same corporation with no-par shares: share value USD 10,000,000; articles USD 375 + 18 x 275 = USD 5,325 (18 further blocks of USD 500,000), initial list USD 150, license USD 500; annual list USD 400 + 18 x 275 = USD 5,350 plus USD 500 a year: USD 17,675. The LLC is cheapest; if a corporation is needed, a par value below USD 0.001 saves USD 15,650 over three years against no par.

## When to refuse or refer

- No business purpose: the owner is not a Nevada resident, has no Nevada activity and no asset-protection reason, and wants Nevada to avoid tax owed elsewhere. Explain the home-state rules; do not build the structure.
- A named creditor whose claim has arisen or is reasonably foreseeable, or a pending or threatened suit: moving assets into an LLC or trust now risks a fraudulent transfer (NRS 112.180). Do not facilitate it; refer to counsel.
- Stated intent to evade tax owed to the IRS or another state: refuse (Circular 230 standards).
- Regulated industries (gaming, cannabis, banking, insurance) without specialist counsel: refer.
- Spendthrift trust drafting, ING or NING trusts, and gift or estate tax structuring: refer to estate counsel; never supply trust documents.
- Venture finance, preferred stock or option plans: refer to startup counsel; Delaware is usually the vehicle (`de-formation`).
