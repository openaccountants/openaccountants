# US state estate and inheritance tax matrix, tax year 2026

> **Source-cited draft.** Built from each state's own revenue page or statute, read on 2026-11-13. No accountant has reviewed it. It is not a Circular 230 written opinion. Do not file a state estate or inheritance tax return from this skill without a qualified professional in that state.

**Answer first.** For a death in 2026, an estate tax (a tax on the estate) is imposed by Connecticut, the District of Columbia, Hawaii, Illinois, Maine, Maryland, Massachusetts, Minnesota, New York, Oregon, Rhode Island, Vermont, and Washington. An inheritance tax (a tax on what a beneficiary receives) is imposed by Kentucky, Maryland, Nebraska, New Jersey, and Pennsylvania. Maryland imposes both. Iowa imposes neither on a death on or after 1 January 2025. New Jersey imposes no estate tax on a death on or after 1 January 2018 and still imposes the inheritance tax. A state that is not in the table below is not certified, by this skill, to be free of a death tax.

The federal basic exclusion amount, Form 706, and portability of the federal exclusion are in `us-estate-gift-706-709`. This skill does not restate those federal figures. Where a state page says the state tax uses the federal basic exclusion amount, use that state's page for the state result and the federal skill for the federal figure.

## Scope

In scope: for a death in calendar year 2026, whether the opened state pages impose an estate tax or an inheritance tax, the exemption or filing threshold those pages state, and the rate those pages state. Resident and nonresident scope is included only where the same page states it.

Out of scope:

| Topic | Where it goes |
| --- | --- |
| Federal estate, gift, and GST tax, Form 706 and Form 709 | `us-estate-gift-706-709` |
| Fiduciary income tax of the estate, Form 1041 | `us-form-1041-trust-and-estate-income` |
| Income tax of the heirs | the state income tax skill |
| A state not named in the table | not covered; open that state's revenue site before saying it has no death tax |

## Ask the client first

1. **Date of death** and the state of domicile on that date. Washington uses one exclusion through 30 June 2026 and another from 1 July 2026. Several exemptions apply only to deaths in 2026.
2. **Where the real property and tangible personal property sit.** A nonresident return is often required only for property in the taxing state. Intangibles (brokerage accounts, bank deposits) usually follow domicile. Do not assume that from this list; read the row.
3. **Federal gross estate, allowable deductions, and adjusted taxable gifts,** including the state's own add-back window (New York, three years with exceptions; Minnesota, three years; Maine, one year; Connecticut, Connecticut taxable gifts since 1 January 2005).
4. **Who receives the property,** and how much each person receives. Inheritance tax (Kentucky, Maryland, Nebraska, New Jersey, Pennsylvania) depends on the relationship and on each beneficiary's share, not on one estate-wide exemption.
5. **Elections:** a state-only QTIP, Maryland deceased-spousal unused exclusion, Hawaii portability, a farm or small-business deduction (Minnesota, Maryland agricultural property, Oregon natural resource property, Washington qualified family-owned business interest).
6. **Whether any other state has already taxed the same property.** Maine allows a credit for a death tax another jurisdiction actually paid on property also taxed by Maine. Do not net two state death taxes against each other unless that state's page says so.

## The method, step by step

1. **Fix the date of death and the domicile.** Read only the row for that year. A 2025 instruction that prints a different exclusion is not the 2026 figure.
2. **List every state that can tax the transfer.** Domicile, plus any state where the decedent owned real or tangible property, plus any state whose row says it also taxes intangibles of a resident. If the property is in a state that is not in the table, stop and open that state's site. This skill does not fill the gap.
3. **Choose estate tax or inheritance tax.** An estate tax is computed once, on the estate, and is paid by the estate. An inheritance tax is computed on each beneficiary's share, at the rate for that relationship. Maryland can be both: run the estate-tax row and the inheritance-tax row.
4. **Apply that row's exemption to the base the row names.** Some states subtract an exclusion and tax the excess (Connecticut, Maine, Minnesota's line 6a, Vermont's statement of the base). Oregon's 2026 table is applied to the taxable estate and starts at USD 1,000,000. Illinois describes a USD 4,000,000 threshold that is not a credit. New York allows a credit only while the New York taxable estate is not greater than 105 percent of the basic exclusion amount.
5. **Apply the rate the row cites, then any cap, apportionment, or credit on that same page.** Connecticut's tax is 12 percent of the excess and the aggregate Connecticut gift and estate tax is capped. A nonresident computation follows the fraction that state's page gives. Do not import another state's brackets.
6. **File the form named in the row, by the deadline that row states.** Where this skill does not state a month count, the deadline was not on the page that was read. Do not borrow "nine months" from a neighbouring state.
7. **Hand the federal return to `us-estate-gift-706-709`.** Several states require a federal Form 706, or a pro forma Form 706, even when the federal exclusion means no federal tax is due. The federal figures stay in that skill.

## Worked cases

**A decision: Connecticut estate over the 2026 exemption, and the same estate under it.** A person domiciled in Connecticut dies in 2026. There are no Connecticut taxable gifts on or after 1 January 2005, and no Connecticut gift tax was paid in the three years before death. Federal deductions are zero, so the Connecticut taxable estate equals the federal gross estate. The federal basic exclusion amount is not recomputed here; Connecticut's own page sets the 2026 Connecticut exemption at USD 15,000,000 and states the tax above that line as 12 percent of the excess over the federal basic exclusion amount. For this case the page's USD 15,000,000 exemption is the line that is tested.

Version A, estate USD 16,000,000. The Connecticut taxable estate is over USD 15,000,000, so Form CT-706/709 is filed with the Department of Revenue Services and with the Probate Court. The tax is 12 percent of USD 1,000,000, which is USD 120,000. The USD 15,000,000 cap on aggregate Connecticut gift and estate tax does not reduce it: there is no prior Connecticut gift tax, and USD 120,000 is under the cap.

Version B, estate USD 14,000,000. The Connecticut taxable estate is not over USD 15,000,000. The tax is none. File Form CT-706 NT with the Probate Court only, unless a Connecticut QTIP election is made, in which case Form CT-706/709 is required even though no tax is due.

The figure that decides the two versions is whether the Connecticut taxable estate is over USD 15,000,000. Domicile still has to be Connecticut. If the decedent was not domiciled in Connecticut, this computation does not apply; use the nonresident fraction in the CT-706/709 instructions instead of this resident result.

```oa-check
name: Connecticut estate tax, over and under the 2026 exemption
inputs:
  estate_over: 16000000
  estate_under: 14000000
  exemption: 15000000
  rate: 0.12
  cap: 15000000
steps:
  - excess_over: max(estate_over - exemption, 0)
  - tax_over: min(excess_over * rate, cap)
  - excess_under: max(estate_under - exemption, 0)
  - tax_under: min(excess_under * rate, cap)
expected:
  excess_over: 1000000.00
  tax_over: 120000.00
  excess_under: 0.00
  tax_under: 0.00
```

## Filing and payment

File the state form named in the row, with that state's office, by the deadline that row states. Where a month count is in the table, it was on the page that was read: District of Columbia, 10 months; Illinois, Maine, Minnesota, and New York, nine months; Oregon, twelve months; Pennsylvania inheritance tax, delinquent nine months after death, with a 5 percent discount if paid within three months. Connecticut's page states which form goes to the Department of Revenue Services and which form goes only to the Probate Court. It does not, on the text that was read, state a month count, so none is given here. An extension to file is not an extension to pay in Maine or in Oregon.

## Completion checklist

- Date of death is in 2026, and the Washington half-year is the right one if Washington is involved.
- Domicile and the location of real and tangible property are both written down.
- The exemption used is the one in this skill's row, not a 2025 instruction and not the federal exclusion, unless the state row itself says to use the federal amount.
- Inheritance tax is computed per beneficiary, not as one estate tax.
- Kentucky Class A, and any rate this skill leaves unstated, is not filled in from memory.
- Federal Form 706 is prepared, or a pro forma is attached, where the state row requires it. The federal computation is the other skill's.
