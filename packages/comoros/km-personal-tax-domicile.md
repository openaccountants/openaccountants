# Comoros: personal tax domicile and moves

## Who this applies to

Individuals who need to know whether they are fiscally domiciled in the Union of the Comoros for income tax on individuals (IRPP), and individuals moving to or leaving Comoros. Tax year 2026.

The IRPP is due by every individual fiscally domiciled in Comoros, subject to international conventions and the exemptions of article 47. A person whose fiscal domicile is outside Comoros is liable on Comorian-source income. [2023 code, art. 45](https://justice.gouv.km/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/comores-cgi-2023.pdf).

This is a source-cited domicile topic, not full personal-tax or comprehensive country coverage. Historic personal-tax bands are deliberately not certified as 2026 rates here. No independent accountant review has occurred.

## The base

### The new 2026 definition (art. 50 bis)

Article 14 of [Finance Law 2026](https://finances.gouv.km/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Decret-portant-promulgation-du-loi-des-finances-2026.pdf) inserts CGI article **50 bis**, after article 50 (place of assessment). It says an individual is fiscally domiciled in Comoros when **either** of these conditions is met ("l'une des conditions suivantes"):

- A permanent home is available in Comoros ("foyer permanent d'habitation").
- The centre of the individual's economic interests is in Comoros.

The two conditions are alternatives, not a requirement to meet both. The enacted wording on PDF page 14 contains no 183-day threshold. This provision therefore does not support a claim that staying fewer than 183 days automatically prevents Comorian tax domicile. [2026 law, new article 50 bis, page 14](https://finances.gouv.km/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Decret-portant-promulgation-du-loi-des-finances-2026.pdf).

### Relationship with the older code needs reconciliation

The [official 2023 code, article 45](https://justice.gouv.km/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/comores-cgi-2023.pdf) used a broader set of criteria: home or principal stay, local professional activity unless merely ancillary, and centre of economic interests. It also addressed certain state officials serving abroad. The 2026 law **adds article 50 bis**; the text inspected does not expressly rewrite article 45 in the same passage.

Consequently, this skill does not assert that every older criterion has been repealed, or that a person failing the two new criteria is necessarily nonresident. A legible consolidated text, intervening amendments and authoritative interpretation are needed for that boundary. This is a specific unresolved legal interaction, not a reason to substitute a mainland French residence rule. In practice: if a person meets either art. 50 bis test, treat them as domiciled; if they meet neither but meet an art. 45(1) test (for example a non-ancillary professional activity in Comoros), flag the point rather than conclude either way.

Practical evidence includes the availability and use of a permanent home, business and investment locations, and the economic facts supporting the claimed centre of interests. Travel records can help establish facts, but they do not create a statutory day-count exemption absent a rule providing one.

### Residence, source and income categories are separate questions

Article 44 of the 2023 code defines global net income as the sum of net category income: wages, salaries, pensions and life annuities; securities income; property income; artisanal, agricultural, industrial and commercial profits; and non-commercial professional profits. Article 45 subjects fiscally domiciled individuals to IRPP, and nonresidents to Comorian-source income, subject to its treaty and exemption qualifications. Article 46 taxes partners of general partnerships and similar entities on their share of profits. Article 48 applies the rules to foreign nationals resident in Comoros, subject to reciprocity treaties. Article 49 lists exempt income. These are the baseline provisions to reconcile with the 2026 definition, not proof that every receipt is taxable at one rate. [2023 code, articles 44 to 49](https://justice.gouv.km/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/comores-cgi-2023.pdf).

Determine the income category and source separately from domicile.

### Arrival (art. 123)

Article 123 of the 2023 code starts income brought into charge by the move to Comoros from the date domicile is established. It extends the same rule to a person who had no habitual residence in Comoros and acquires one. It does not say all income for the entire arrival year is automatically included. [2023 code, article 123](https://justice.gouv.km/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/comores-cgi-2023.pdf).

### Departure (art. 124)

A person domiciled in Comoros who moves their domicile abroad is taxed on income available in the departure year up to the departure date, profits made since the end of the last taxed period, and all income acquired but not yet available before departure. That income is taxed under the rules in force on 1 January of the departure year. The same rules apply on abandoning every residence in Comoros. [2023 code, article 124](https://justice.gouv.km/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/comores-cgi-2023.pdf).

## Deadlines and filing

1. **Annual return:** by 31 March each year, at the tax centre of the place of assessment, on the administration's form, covering income of the previous year. A person whose only income is wages, pensions, life annuities or securities income on which tax was withheld at source does not file (art. 100).
2. **Departure, provisional declaration:** at least **thirty days before the expected departure**, absent force majeure (art. 124). It may be completed until the end of the first three months of the year after departure. If no corrective declaration is filed in time, the provisional declaration is treated as confirmed by the taxpayer.
3. **Departure, payment:** income tax is payable in full by every taxpayer leaving the country during the year (art. 125).
4. **Departure, clearance document:** the 2023 code (art. 125) says emigration services must not authorise departure unless the taxpayer shows a quitus fiscal from the tax service. The 2026 addition to LPF L.4 ter lists the attestation de regularite fiscale as required by emigration services before the boarding authorisation of a fiscally domiciled person leaving permanently. Hold both: obtain whichever document the emigration service asks for, and do not assume a quitus and an attestation are interchangeable (see [km-registration-and-tax-clearance](km-registration-and-tax-clearance.md) on the unresolved substitution sentence). Do not assume an expired certificate remains usable simply because tax was previously paid. [2026 law, page 13](https://finances.gouv.km/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Decret-portant-promulgation-du-loi-des-finances-2026.pdf).

Place of assessment (art. 50): a single residence in Comoros, at that residence; several, at the principal residence. Persons domiciled abroad who owe IRPP and have no residence in Comoros are assessed at the place of their main interests in Comoros; state officials serving abroad at the seat of their administering service.

## Penalties and interest

Late filing and late payment interest, reassessment surcharges and assessment after formal notice are general procedure rules kept in [km-electronic-filing-and-tax-procedure](km-electronic-filing-and-tax-procedure.md). For departures, the specific consequence in art. 124 is that a provisional declaration not corrected within the first three months of the following year is treated as confirmed. No departure-specific fine was found in the provisions read.

### Amendment check, 16 November 2026

The Amending Finance Law 2024 (art. 10) lists articles 31, 43, 42, L21, L114, L101, L42 and L46 as changed, and also inserts article 42 bis. Articles 44 to 50, 100, 123, 124 and 125 are not on that list. The Finance Law 2025 tax text (art. 20 only, an import fee) and the Finance Law 2026 do not amend them either; the 2026 law adds art. 50 bis. So the baseline above stands against those three laws. The Finance Law 2024 and the Amending Finance Law 2025 are not on an official host found, and were not read.

## Cross-border points

- A domestic residence finding does not itself decide the outcome under a particular treaty. No treaty tie-breaker or treaty exemption has been verified in this skill.
- Article 45(4): individuals of any nationality, domiciled in Comoros or not, are liable on income whose taxation a double tax convention allocates to Comoros.
- A non-domiciled person is liable on Comorian-source income only (art. 45(3)).
- There is no 183-day rule in art. 45 or art. 50 bis; day counts are evidence, not a test.
- This guide concerns the Union of the Comoros, not Mayotte.

## Two worked examples that recompute

Article 124 needs the provisional declaration at least 30 days before departure. A person leaving on day 200 of the year files by day 170.

A decision, worked both ways, on the art. 47 exemption: a domiciled individual with taxable income of KMF 140,000 has nothing above the KMF 150,000 limit and is exempt. With KMF 160,000 the income exceeds the limit by KMF 10,000, so IRPP applies to the income under the current scale, which this skill does not certify. The exemption is a threshold, not an allowance deducted from income.

```oa-check
  - excess_high: max(income_high - limit, 0)

  excess_low: 0
  excess_high: 10000
```

## Ask the client first

- Is a permanent home available to the person in Comoros, and where is the centre of their economic interests (business, investments, main income)?
- Do they work in Comoros, and is that activity their main or an ancillary one?
- Are they a state official serving abroad, and are they taxed in the host country?
- Are they arriving or leaving, on what date, and is the departure permanent?
- What income do they have, from where, and was tax withheld at source?
- Does a double tax convention with the other country apply?
