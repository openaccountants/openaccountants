# Formalizing small informal businesses in Colombia (2026) v1.1

> **General reference only.** This skill is general tax/accounting reference material for AI-assisted workflows. It has not been reviewed for any specific person's facts, documents, elections, deadlines, residency, filing status, or local procedures. Do not rely on it to file, pay, amend, or take a tax position without review by a qualified professional in the relevant jurisdiction.

**Tax year.** 2026. One UVT is **COP 52,374** for 2026 (DIAN Resolución 000238 of 15 December 2025). The Régimen Simple rules are in the Estatuto Tributario (ET), Libro Octavo, arts. 903 to 916, as introduced by Ley 2010 de 2019 and amended by Ley 2155 de 2021 and Ley 2277 de 2022, in the consolidated text on the Senate's official site.

## Scope

**Topic:** the path for taking an informal business (a street stall, an unregistered point of sale, and similar) into a formal structure with the DIAN (tax authority) and the Chamber of Commerce.

**Audience:** microentrepreneurs and the accountants who advise them.

Related skills: [colombia-income-tax](colombia-income-tax.md) (the ordinary regime for an individual), [co-corporate-income-tax](co-corporate-income-tax.md) (the ordinary regime for a company, and a worked Simple-or-ordinary comparison), [co-vat-return](co-vat-return.md) (IVA and the 3,500 UVT no-responsable tests).

## Ask the client first

1. Who owns the business: one person, or several partners? Are all owners individuals resident in Colombia?
2. How many informal points of sale or brands are there, and do they really belong to one owner?
3. What does the business actually do (to pick the CIIU code and the SIMPLE rate group)?
4. Gross income in the previous year, from all points together, and whether any income is passive (rent, interest, dividends).
5. Does the owner already have a RUT? If so, today's date matters for the SIMPLE deadline.
6. What assets, cash, stock and debts exist today, for the opening balance?

## The method, step by step

### 1. Choosing the legal structure

- **Sole proprietor (persona natural comerciante):** the simplest option for small, single-owner businesses (for example a food stall). No need to incorporate a company.
- **Legal entity (SAS or other):** makes sense when there are several partners, when the owner wants to separate personal and business assets, or when the business plans to grow and hire staff.
- **Consolidate several informal points under one structure:** if the business runs several informal "points" or "brands" but really has a single owner, it is usually best to consolidate everything under one structure before applying for the RUT. For the SIMPLE limit, an owner's other businesses are counted together in any case (art. 905(3) to (5)).

### 2. Registering the RUT with the DIAN

- Apply for or update the RUT through the DIAN's online services.
- Select the economic activity code (CIIU) that matches what the business actually does (for example food retail or restaurants), not a generic one. The CIIU also decides the SIMPLE rate group.
- Decide whether the business will sit in the **ordinary regime** or opt into the **Régimen Simple de Tributación (SIMPLE)**.
- **SIMPLE opt-in deadline (art. 909):** a taxpayer already in the RUT opts in by updating the RUT **by the last business day of February** of the year it wants to be covered. A business registering in the RUT for the first time can opt in at any time, by marking SIMPLE on its first RUT form. Leaving SIMPLE for the ordinary regime for the next year is done before the last business day of January of that year.

### 3. Ordinary regime or Régimen Simple (SIMPLE)

- **What SIMPLE bundles (art. 903):** an optional regime with one annual return and bimonthly advance payments. It replaces income tax and integrates the national consumption tax and the consolidated municipal industry and commerce tax (ICA), which includes the signage tax (avisos y tableros) and the fire-department surcharge. Employer pension contributions are credited against it.
- **Who can opt in (art. 905):** an individual running a business, or a company whose shareholders are all individuals resident in Colombia; gross income in the previous year **under 100,000 UVT** (COP 5,237,400,000 at the 2026 UVT); the owner's other SIMPLE businesses, and companies where they hold more than 10% or that they manage, are counted together; and the business must have the RUT, electronic signature and electronic invoicing in place. A new business qualifies if its income in the year stays under the limit.
- **Who cannot (art. 906), main cases:** foreign companies and non-residents; anyone whose work is in substance an employment relationship; subsidiaries and branches; companies that hold shares in other companies; financial entities; microcredit, factoring and financial advice; electricity, car and fuel import businesses; and businesses whose passive income (asset management, leasing of assets and similar) is 20% or more of gross income.
- **Tax compliance:** being up to date with national and local tax obligations is a practical precondition for registering; the law above sets the formal conditions.
- **Rates for 2026 (art. 908):** a consolidated rate on gross income, set by activity group and the year's gross income bracket (under 6,000 UVT, 6,000 to 15,000, 15,000 to 30,000, 30,000 to 100,000), as in the table under Boundary and exception table. The rates run from 1.2% to 14.5%, with 1.62% for recycling and waste activities (CIIU 4665, 3830, 3811). Food and drink outlets add the 8% consumption tax to their SIMPLE rate (art. 908 par. 1).
- **When SIMPLE is a good fit:** the business has few deductible costs and expenses to formally document, the typical profile of a business coming out of informality that does not yet keep detailed cost accounting. SIMPLE taxes gross income, the ordinary regime taxes profit; see the comparison in [co-corporate-income-tax](co-corporate-income-tax.md).

> UVT thresholds and rates are checked against the UVT for the year and arts. 903 to 916 before advising on a specific case.

### 4. Commercial registration

- Sole proprietors and companies must register with the Chamber of Commerce for their area and renew that registration every year.
- This step is separate from the RUT and is usually done in parallel.

### 5. Checklist for the opening balance sheet of a business coming from informality

This is where most time is lost if it is not organised from the start. Handle each informal point of sale or unit separately before consolidating:

- [ ] **Cash and digital balances:** cash on hand, plus balances in digital wallets such as Nequi or Daviplata at the cut-off date.
- [ ] **Inventory:** physical count of goods or supplies, valued at replacement cost.
- [ ] **Informally acquired fixed assets** (equipment, furniture, vehicles): value them at used-market price, not at the original invoice price if there is no invoice.
- [ ] **Informal accounts receivable:** credit sales or amounts still owed to the business.
- [ ] **Liabilities:** debts to suppliers, informal or family loans, other pending obligations.
- [ ] **Opening equity:** assets minus liabilities; this is the business's formal accounting starting point.

### 6. Other formalisation steps

- **Electronic invoicing:** a SIMPLE taxpayer must adopt electronic invoicing **within two months** of registering in the RUT, and must ask its suppliers for invoices (art. 915 and parágrafo).
- **IVA:** a SIMPLE taxpayer that only runs small shops, minimarkets or hairdressers is not responsable de IVA, nor is an individual SIMPLE taxpayer with gross income under 3,500 UVT (art. 437 par. 4); the others are responsables and file one annual IVA return, paying IVA through the SIMPLE electronic receipt (art. 915). See [co-vat-return](co-vat-return.md).
- **Social security:** set up the owner's social security as self-employed and that of any employees. SIMPLE taxpayers must pay pension contributions and are exempt from parafiscal contributions on the terms of art. 903 par. 3.
- **Business bank account:** open or formalise a business bank account separate from personal accounts.

SIMPLE consolidated rates on gross income for 2026 (art. 908):

|---|---|---|---|---|
| (1) Small shops, minimarkets, micromarkets, hairdressers | 1.2% | 2.8% | 4.4% | 5.6% |

## Worked cases

**A food stall opting into SIMPLE.** Doña Rosa sells breakfasts from one stall and two weekend points, all hers. Together they took COP 150,000,000 in 2025, so she is well under 100,000 UVT and, consolidated, under the 6,000 UVT bracket. She registers in the RUT for the first time in March 2026 and marks SIMPLE on the form (allowed at any time for a first registration, art. 909). Her group is food and drink outlets: 3.1% plus the 8% consumption tax (art. 908 par. 1), so 11.1% of gross income. On COP 150,000,000 for 2026 that is COP 16,650,000, paid in bimonthly advances and settled in the annual return; it already includes her ICA. She must invoice electronically by May 2026 (two months after registering, art. 915).

## When to refuse or refer

- The owner is not resident, or the business is a branch or subsidiary: SIMPLE is not available (art. 906); refer to the ordinary-regime skills.
- The "business" is in substance one client paying for personal work under subordination: art. 906(3) and (4) exclude it; refer to a contador público.
- Passive income of 20% or more, financial or excluded activities (art. 906(8)): no SIMPLE.
- Tax debts with DIAN or the municipality, or past informal income the owner wants regularised: refer to a contador público before registering.

## Filing and payment

- RUT: register or update through the DIAN's online services; mark SIMPLE on a first registration, or update by the last business day of February (art. 909).
- SIMPLE: bimonthly advance through the SIMPLE electronic receipt, filed even with nothing to pay, and one annual consolidated return with payment, on the dates the Government sets (art. 910). The return states the municipalities where income arose, for the ICA share.
- Chamber of Commerce: register, then renew every year.

## Completion checklist

- [ ] Legal form chosen and all informal points consolidated under one owner or company.
- [ ] RUT with the right CIIU code; SIMPLE marked or updated before the February deadline.
- [ ] Eligibility checked against arts. 905 and 906, with income consolidated.
- [ ] Electronic invoicing live within two months.
- [ ] Chamber of Commerce registration done.
- [ ] Opening balance prepared point by point, then consolidated.
- [ ] Social security and a business bank account set up.
