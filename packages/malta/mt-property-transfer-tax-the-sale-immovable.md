# Malta property transfer tax and duty on the sale of immovable property, 2026

## Scope

A sale of immovable property situated in Malta carries two separate charges. The **seller** pays the final tax under article 5A of the Income Tax Act, Cap. 123, normally on the **transfer value**, not on the gain, and payable even at a loss. The **buyer** pays duty under the Duty on Documents and Transfers Act, Cap. 364. MTCA: "The seller must pay capital transfer taxes on the transfer of any property located in Malta, whilst stamp duty is paid by the buyer." Both are collected by the **notary** on the deed, with a provisional stage at the promise of sale (*konvenju*).

Figures are for deeds in **2026**. The article 5A rates apply to transfers made on or after 1 January 2015 and are unchanged for 2026.

Covered: individuals and companies, residents and non-residents, selling Malta property bought, inherited or received by donation. This file absorbed `how-to-submit-capital-gain-taxes-in-malta` on 14 November 2026 (the accountant's order of work around the deed), and the article 5A detail previously repeated in [mt-capital-gains](mt-capital-gains.md), which now links here.

Not covered (refer or use the named skill): the transfer causa mortis and its declaration (only the later sale is covered); share transfers ([selling-a-company-in-malta](selling-a-company-in-malta.md)); article 5 gains on foreign property, shares and other assets ([mt-capital-gains](mt-capital-gains.md)); the assignment of a right under a promise of sale, which "is not regulated by the property transfers tax regime" (MTCA FAQ Q17); projects, emphyteusis and usufruct splits, partitions, special designated areas and AIP permits.

Built from the law: not yet approved by an accountant. Working paper for the accountant supporting the client around the deed; the notary is the collector.

## Ask the client first

1. **When and how was the property acquired?** Bought, inherited (date of death: before 25 November 1992, or after 24 November 1992) or donated (more than five years before this sale?). Get the deed; never accept an oral acquisition value.
2. **Own home?** Owned and occupied as own residence for at least three consecutive years right before the sale, sold within twelve months of moving out, declared as main residence? If held under three years: did the purchase deed declare it the sole ordinary residence, and does the seller own any other residential property?
3. **Works and projects.** In the last five years, did the seller or a related person carry out works needing a development permission, or did a related person own it as part of a project?
4. **Promise of sale.** Is there a konvenju, and was any promise of sale on this property notified to the Commissioner before 17 November 2014 (including one later cancelled or expired)?
5. **Special property.** Urban conservation area or scheduled, restored under a permit; leased to a tenant on a Housing Authority scheme benefit for three years or more, and is the buyer that tenant?
6. **Residence of the seller and the price.** Malta resident? Consideration, market value, and brokerage paid by the seller with invoices. For the buyer: sole ordinary residence, first purchase of any residence?

## The method, step by step

Do these in order. A wrong order changes the rate, not only the arithmetic.

1. **Acquisition date and means first** (purchase, inheritance, donation). This fact set, not the price, picks the rate. For a donation five years or less before the transfer, use the date the donor acquired it (art. 5A(5)(b), third proviso). A company that took the property under an intra-group exemption uses the date the group last acquired it outside that exemption (art. 5A(5)(e) and (f) provisos).
2. **Exemptions** (art. 5A(4)), then **exclusions** (art. 5A(3)). If excluded, the transfer leaves article 5A: 7% provisional tax on the consideration within fifteen days (ITMA art. 43(1)(b)) and the gain goes in the return under article 5 ([mt-capital-gains](mt-capital-gains.md)).
3. **Transfer value**: the higher of market value and the consideration (art. 5A(6)(a)), less brokerage paid by the transferor where the Tax on Property Transfers Rules' verification conditions are met (MTCA FAQ Q18).
4. **Pick the rate** from the table below. Order: inheritance and donation rows (5A(5)(b), (c)); the pre-2004 10% (5A(5)(f)), which overrides (a), (d) and (e) but not (b); the restored-property 5% (5A(5)(h)), which overrides (a), (e) and (f); the 2%, 5% and Housing Authority rows; otherwise 8%.
5. **Inherited after 24 November 1992, or donated more than five years before**: the default is 12% of the excess of transfer value over acquisition value. The transferor may elect, by a declaration recorded in the deed, that this "paragraph (b) shall not apply", and then pays the transfer-value rate. Model both in writing; the election cannot be undone after the deed. (MTCA FAQ Q13 says the seller "can elect either"; the statute makes 12% of the gain the default.)
6. **Buyer's duty**: 5% of the higher of consideration and value, with the reliefs below.
7. **Hand the notary** a computation and the evidence before the deed date, and reconcile it to the notary's own figure.

### Exemptions: no article 5A tax (art. 5A(4))

- **Own residence (5A(4)(c)).** A dwelling house, not part of a project, owned **and** occupied as own residence "for a period of at least three consecutive years immediately preceding the date of transfer", disposed of within twelve months of vacating, and declared as main residence by election. "Own residence" includes garden or grounds and a garage attached or of not more than seventy square metres within five hundred metres, on the same deed (art. 5(5)(c)). Absences abroad for work, study, illness or care count if the premises are not used otherwise; periods of a spouse or direct ascendant from whom it was inherited count; an undivided share qualifies proportionately; a part used exclusively for business within two years is excluded proportionately.
- **Family donations (5A(4)(a)):** to a spouse, descendant or ascendant in the direct line or their spouse, or with no descendants to a brother or sister or their descendant, or to an approved philanthropic institution.
- **Separation and divorce (5A(4)(d), (e)); intra-group transfers and business incorporation (5A(4)(f), (g))**, with a clawback at 8% (10% if acquired before 2004) where a company leaves the group within six years (art. 5A(12A)); **trusts, exempt persons and 95% shareholder liquidation transfers (5A(4)(h) to (j))**: refer.

### Exclusions: outside article 5A (art. 5A(3)), 7% provisional tax applies

- Non-resident taxed on the gain abroad, with a certificate from the home tax authority, not owned or controlled by or acting for Malta residents (5A(3)(h)).
- One co-owner of a declared sole residence transferring to the other, on election (5A(3)(d)); Government acquisitions (5A(3)(e)); business-asset replacement within one year, on election (5A(3)(g)); project transfers by a company whose listed bonds funded the project, on election (5A(3)(j)).
- Judicial sale by auction or court winding up (5A(3)(f)), except a judicial auction of property inherited on or after 25 November 1992, which stays in 5A at 7%.
- The general twelve-year election (5A(3)(b)) is closed: it needed a promise of sale notified before 17 November 2014 and a transfer before 1 January 2016.

### Defaults and traps

- Occupancy for the own-residence exemption uncertain: assume no exemption, compute the tax, and get the evidence before the deed.
- Acquisition value on an old deed missing or ambiguous: do not estimate; get the deed from the Public Registry.
- "No profit, no tax" is wrong under 5A. Applying 8% by default misses the 10%, 5% and 2% rows.
- The notary collects; a wrong figure can hold up the deed, and after publication the rate and election are fixed.
- The undervalue comparison is declared value against 85% of market value, not market against 115% of declared.

## Worked cases

All sellers are invented; rates are the 2026 law.

### Case 1: inherited house, keep 12% of the gain or elect the transfer-value rate (a decision)

A seller inherited a house from a parent who died in 2010; the value in the causa mortis declaration, as the acquisition value, is EUR 150,000. Sold in 2026 for EUR 300,000 (market value not higher), no brokerage, acquired after 2003, held over five years. Default: 12% of EUR 150,000 = EUR 18,000. Electing out: 8% of EUR 300,000 = EUR 24,000. Keep the default; it saves EUR 6,000. If the declared value had been EUR 60,000, the default would be EUR 28,800 and electing 8% would save EUR 4,800. The appreciation since the declared value decides it: the default wins while the gain is under two thirds of the transfer value (8 / 12).

```oa-check
name: Inherited 2010, sale EUR 300,000, 12 percent of gain versus 8 percent of transfer value, two declared values
inputs:
  transfer_value: 300000
  acq_high: 150000
  acq_low: 60000
steps:
  - default_high: pct(transfer_value - acq_high, 12)
  - default_low: pct(transfer_value - acq_low, 12)
  - elected: pct(transfer_value, 8)
  - default_saves_high: elected - default_high
  - election_saves_low: default_low - elected
expected:
  default_high: 18000.00
  default_low: 28800.00
  elected: 24000.00
  default_saves_high: 6000.00
  election_saves_low: 4800.00
```

### Case 2: investment flat with brokerage, and the buyer's duty

A seller bought a flat in 2010 and sells it in 2026 for EUR 300,000, paying a verified agency fee of EUR 9,000. Base EUR 291,000; 8% = EUR 23,280, withheld by the notary. Had it been sold within five years with no permit works, 5% = EUR 14,550. The buyer, buying a sole ordinary residence but not a first-time buyer, pays 3.5% on EUR 200,000 (EUR 7,000) and 5% on EUR 100,000 (EUR 5,000): EUR 12,000, of which EUR 3,000 (1%) was paid at the promise of sale.

```oa-check
name: Flat bought 2010, sold EUR 300,000, brokerage EUR 9,000, and buyer duty at sole residence rate (2026)
inputs:
  price: 300000
  brokerage: 9000
steps:
  - base: price - brokerage
  - tax_8: pct(base, 8)
  - tax_5: pct(base, 5)
  - duty: pct(min(price, 200000), 3.5) + pct(max(price - 200000, 0), 5)
  - provisional_duty: pct(price, 1)
  - duty_on_deed: duty - provisional_duty
expected:
  base: 291000.00
  tax_8: 23280.00
  tax_5: 14550.00
  duty: 12000.00
  provisional_duty: 3000.00
  duty_on_deed: 9000.00
```

### Further cases (prose)

- **Pre-2004 purchase.** Bought 1998, sold 2026 for EUR 400,000: 10% = EUR 40,000.
- **Restored property under 5A(5)(d), 2026 deed.** EUR 300,000 at 10% = EUR 30,000. The 7% figure (EUR 21,000) applied only to qualifying 2015 deeds.
- **Undervalue test.** Declared EUR 100,000 against market EUR 116,000: 85% of market is EUR 98,600, so no order on that ground. Against market EUR 120,000, 85% is EUR 102,000 and the declaration falls EUR 2,000 short.
- **MTCA's duty example.** Market price EUR 200,000, contract EUR 180,000: final duty 5% of EUR 200,000 = EUR 10,000; provisional 1% = EUR 2,000.

## When to refuse or refer

- Refuse to give a seller's rate until the acquisition date and means, project status, permit works in five years, own-residence facts and the 2014 promise-of-sale question are known.
- Refer: projects and developers; emphyteusis, usufruct and bare-ownership splits; partitions; non-residents opting out under 5A(3)(h); special designated areas and AIP permits; promises of sale notified before 17 November 2014; intra-group, trust and liquidation exemptions.
- Refer the buyer-side reliefs not computed here: the transfer causa mortis and its 3.5% band on an inherited dwelling (EUR 35,000 to EUR 400,000, L.N. 306 of 2025, from 28 October 2025); first-time buyer claims with an unclear ownership history, or before 28 October 2025; the reliefs in S.L. 123.203 and S.L. 364.19 (vacant, old, UCA and traditional-style property), extended by L.N. 284 of 2025 to transfers up to 31 December 2026, whose conditions are not set out here; the second-time buyer refund (S.L. 364.17).
- A valuation dispute beyond the 15% tolerance goes through objection and appeal.

## Filing and payment

1. **Engage the notary**, usually chosen by the buyer, for searches, deeds and provisional duty (MTCA Buying Property; Selling Property).
2. **Promise of sale**: presented to the Property Tax Directorate within 21 days of signing, with the buyer's 1% provisional duty; notice on form DDT3. A receipt confirms the notary submitted the promise and the duty was paid.
3. **Before the deed**: fix the seller's rate, exemption or exclusion and any deed election; deliver the computation and evidence to the notary.
4. **On the deed**: the notary submits, through the Notaries Online Portal, the DDT1 with the transfer details, a copy of the contract, site plans, the Public Registry note, the buyer's duty, the seller's tax and, for residential property, Schedule 8 (physical attributes) (MTCA Promise of Sale; Selling Property). The seller's 5A tax is due within fifteen working days.
5. **After**: receipts normally issue within 3 weeks; an internal board decides whether an architect values the property. A claim beyond the 15% tolerance adds duty plus 20% additional duty for the buyer, and the additional duty only for the seller.
6. **Reporting**: 5A tax is final; the transferor does not declare that income in a return (ITMA art. 43(4)(a)). A transfer outside 5A goes in the return with the 7% provisional tax credited. Every owner keeps an account of proceeds and expenditure on 5A transfers (art. 5A(10)(c)); a company allocates the profits to its final tax account (art. 5A(10)(d)).

## Completion checklist

- [ ] Acquisition deed seen; date and means recorded.
- [ ] Exemption and exclusions tested; rate row chosen with the reason.
- [ ] Transfer value = higher of market value and consideration, less verified brokerage.
- [ ] Inheritance or donation: both routes modelled; election instruction in writing.
- [ ] Buyer's duty and reliefs computed; provisional duty credited.
- [ ] Figure reconciled to the notary's before the deed.
