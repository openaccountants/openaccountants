# Form 1040-X amended returns and capital loss carryforward corrections in United States

Form 1040-X, Amended U.S. Individual Income Tax Return, is used to correct a previously filed Form 1040 series return or to change amounts previously adjusted by the IRS. It is filed by the individual taxpayer (or their authorised representative) after the original return has already been filed, and it is filed year by year: one Form 1040-X for each tax year being corrected. A capital loss carryforward correction typically touches more than one year, because the amount carried into a later year depends on the net loss and the deduction allowed in the earlier year. Figures are for tax year 2026. Where a source page states its own year for a figure, the note in the table says so and that year governs that row.

## Who this is for

This Guide is for individuals amending a Form 1040, 1040-SR, 1040-NR or 1040-SS (and, for periods before 2023, Form 1040-PR), and in particular for those whose Schedule D or Form 8949 figures, allowed capital loss deduction, or capital loss carryforward were wrong on a return already filed.

It is not for taxpayers whose returns the IRS has already corrected without an amendment: the IRS may correct certain errors on a return and may accept returns without certain required forms or schedules, and in those instances there is no need to amend; an amended return is filed if there is a change in filing status, income, deductions, credits or tax liability. It is not for corporate, partnership, estate or trust filers, who use their own amendment forms, and it does not cover state amendments — a change made on a federal return may affect state tax liability, and the state tax agency should be contacted for how to correct a state return.

## The method, step by step

1. Confirm an amendment is actually needed. Per [Topic no. 308, Amended returns](https://www.irs.gov/taxtopics/tc308), the IRS may correct certain errors and may accept returns without certain required forms or schedules, in which case no amendment is required; file if there is a change in filing status, income, deductions, credits or tax liability. The IRS Interactive Tax Assistant carries a "Should I file an amended return?" tool, referenced on [When and how to amend a tax return](https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/when-and-how-to-amend-a-tax-return).
2. Fix the earliest wrong year first. A capital loss carryforward figure for any year is built from the prior year's Schedule D result, so the chain is rebuilt from the first incorrect year forward. Pull the filed Schedule D (Form 1040) and Form 8949 for each year in the chain, and pull an IRS transcript for each year so that column A of Form 1040-X matches the IRS's records, whether column A is "as originally filed" or "as previously adjusted" — [Topic no. 308](https://www.irs.gov/taxtopics/tc308) states that Form 1040-X is also used "to change amounts previously adjusted by the IRS".
3. Check each affected year against the time limits in the first table before assuming a refund is available. The general 3-year / 2-year rule and the early-filing and extension rules are in the [Instructions for Form 1040-X](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1040x). [When and how to amend a tax return](https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/when-and-how-to-amend-a-tax-return) notes that special rules apply where there are net operating losses, foreign tax credits, bad debts or loss or credit carrybacks, and that taxpayers in disaster relief situations or combat zone service may have more time.
4. Recompute the capital gain and loss figures. Per [Topic no. 409, Capital gains and losses](https://www.irs.gov/taxtopics/tc409), most sales and other capital transactions are reported and the gain or loss calculated on Form 8949, Sales and Other Dispositions of Capital Assets, then summarised on Schedule D (Form 1040), with the allowed excess loss claimed on line 7a of Form 1040, 1040-SR or 1040-NR.
5. Recompute the carryforward itself using the Capital Loss Carryover Worksheet, which [Topic no. 409](https://www.irs.gov/taxtopics/tc409) locates in Publication 550, Investment Income and Expenses, or in the Instructions for Schedule D (Form 1040). Do this year by year, feeding each corrected year's result into the next.
6. Prepare Form 1040-X for each corrected year, following the [Instructions for Form 1040-X](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1040x), and attach the corrected Form 8949, Schedule D and every other form and schedule required as if it were the original submission — the [amended return FAQ](https://www.irs.gov/filing/amended-return-frequently-asked-questions) states that all necessary forms and schedules must be submitted even if some have no adjustments. [When and how to amend a tax return](https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/when-and-how-to-amend-a-tax-return) adds that a corrected Form 1040, 1040-SR or 1040-NR for the applicable tax year is submitted with the supporting documents and updated forms or schedules.
7. Choose the filing channel by year, using the limits in the third table above: the current or two prior tax periods may go electronically, and earlier years must go on paper, per the [amended return FAQ](https://www.irs.gov/filing/amended-return-frequently-asked-questions). The mailing address for paper filing is given in the [Instructions for Form 1040-X](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1040x).
8. Handle the money. If tax is owed and the year's filing due date has not yet passed, [Topic no. 308](https://www.irs.gov/taxtopics/tc308) says filing and paying by that due date, without regard to any extension, avoids penalties and interest, and the return supersedes the original; if the due date has passed, do not enter interest or penalties on Form 1040-X. For refunds, [When and how to amend a tax return](https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/when-and-how-to-amend-a-tax-return) states that for tax years 2021 and later, direct deposit of refunds may be requested when filing electronically.
9. Track it. Use [Where's My Amended Return?](https://www.irs.gov/filing/wheres-my-amended-return) or the automated line in the third table, allowing the appearance and processing times shown there before contacting the IRS.
10. Deal with the state separately. [When and how to amend a tax return](https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/when-and-how-to-amend-a-tax-return) states that taxpayers should contact their state tax agency for guidance and should not attach state returns to the federal amended return.

## Ask the client first

- For each year in the carryforward chain: on what date was the original return filed, and on what date was the tax paid? Both dates drive the limits in the first table, and the later of the two controls.
- Was any of those returns filed early, or filed before the end of an extension? The two rules produce different start dates for the 3-year period.
- What was the filing status in each affected year? Married filing separately changes the annual excess-loss figure in the capital loss table.
- Do you hold the filed Schedule D and Form 8949 for every year from the first wrong year onward, and has the IRS already adjusted any of those years? Column A must match the IRS's records as originally filed or as previously adjusted.
- Does the correction arise from a bad debt or a worthless security, rather than an ordinary sale? That carries the separate, longer limit in the first table.
- Was the loss on personal-use property? Losses from the sale of personal-use property, such as a home or car, are not deductible, so no carryforward arises from them.

## When to refuse or refer

- Years where the limits in the first table have already run out, and the question is whether an out-of-time year's figures can still be corrected to fix a later year's carryforward. The IRS pages cited here set the refund claim limits but do not state a method for recomputing a carryforward through a year that is closed to refund claims; refer this.
- Loss or credit carrybacks, net operating losses and foreign tax credits. [When and how to amend a tax return](https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/when-and-how-to-amend-a-tax-return) says special rules apply to these, and this Guide does not set them out.
- Disaster relief, State-declared disasters, combat zone or contingency operation extensions, the lookback period extension, and qualified wildfire relief payments. Each is flagged in the [Instructions for Form 1040-X](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1040x) as having its own timing rule not covered here.
- The section 174A domestic research or experimental expenditures election by amended return, which has its own deadline in the first table and its own procedure in Rev. Proc. 2025-28.
- Injured spouse allocations on Form 8379, corrections to wages requiring Form W-2c and Form W-3c with the Social Security Administration, and reportable transaction disclosures on Form 8886 — all named in the [Instructions for Form 1040-X](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1040x) as separate filings.
- Amendments to business entity, estate or trust returns, and any state amended return.
- Basis, wash sale and holding period disputes that determine whether the carryforward was wrong in the first place; the pages cited here state where the figures go, not how contested basis is proved.
