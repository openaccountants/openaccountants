# Moving to Florida: making Florida your domicile, tax year 2026

> **Source-cited draft.** Built from the Florida Constitution Article VII section 5, Florida Statutes 193.155, 196.011, 196.015, 196.031, 196.061, 222.17, 320.01, 320.02, 320.38, 322.01, 322.03 and 322.031, Department of Revenue guides PT-112 and PT-113 and the Department's estate tax page, read on 2026-10-03. No accountant has reviewed it. All outputs must be reviewed by a qualified tax professional before filing.

**Answer first.** Florida does not tax the income of individuals who live there. Moving there does not by itself end tax in the state you leave: that state decides, under its own law. In Florida, a sworn declaration of domicile (F.S. 222.17) is evidence, not proof. If you own the home and live in it as your permanent residence on **1 January**, apply for the homestead exemption by **1 March** of that year: the first USD 25,000 of assessed value is exempt from all property tax and an additional exemption (base USD 25,000, indexed) applies to assessed value above USD 50,000 for non-school levies. From the next year the Save Our Homes cap limits each annual increase in assessed value to the lower of 3% and the CPI change.

## Scope and who this is for

Figures are for tax year 2026. This Guide is for a person moving their permanent home to Florida from another US state, usually a state with an income tax, and for their adviser. It covers what Florida itself offers and asks for: no state tax on an individual's income, the sworn declaration of domicile, the homestead exemption and the Save Our Homes assessment limit, and the driver licence and vehicle registration time limits. It does not decide whether you have left your old state. That question is decided by the old state under its own law, and the sibling Guides named below cover it. Official sources were read on 3 October 2026. This is a working paper for an adviser, not a filing.

## Ask the client first

- On what date did you start living in the Florida home, did you hold legal or beneficial title on 1 January, and has the deed been recorded?
- Do you or your spouse still receive or claim a property tax exemption or credit in another state that requires permanent residence there?
- Do you keep a home in the old state, and who lives in it?
- Did you take a job, start a business, or enrol children in a Florida public school, and on what date?
- Where are your bank accounts, your cars, your voter registration and the address on your last federal return?
- Do you own a business that pays Florida corporate income tax, or will it move with you?

### Steps

1. Settle the date the move happens and what moves on that date: the home you live in, family, work, bank accounts, cars. Florida's property appraiser weighs these same facts for homestead (step 5), so collect them once. See [Florida Statutes 196.015](http://www.leg.state.fl.us/statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0100-0199/0196/Sections/0196.015.html).
2. Confirm the Florida income tax position. Florida does not tax the income of a natural person who is a Florida resident beyond what the federal or another state's tax allows as a credit or deduction, and the Department of Revenue says plainly that Florida has no personal income tax. See [Florida Constitution, Article VII, section 5](https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Constitution) and the [Department of Revenue page](https://floridarevenue.com/taxes/compliance/Pages/violations.aspx).
3. Work out the exit from the old state first, using the old state's own rules: New York in `ny-residency-leaving-new-york`; California and every other state under that state's own residency rules, so refer the California question to an adviser who works with California law; and the general method for part-year returns and income allocation in `us-multi-state-residency-and-allocation`. Nothing Florida issues decides that question.
4. If the client wants a public record of the move, file the sworn declaration of domicile with the clerk of the circuit court in the Florida county where they live, under [Florida Statutes 222.17](http://www.leg.state.fl.us/statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0200-0299/0222/Sections/0222.17.html). Treat it as one piece of evidence, not as proof (see the section below).
5. If the client owns the Florida home and lives in it as a permanent residence on 1 January, apply for the homestead exemption with the county property appraiser by 1 March of that year on Form DR-501. See [Florida Statutes 196.011](http://www.leg.state.fl.us/statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0100-0199/0196/Sections/0196.011.html), [Florida Statutes 196.031](http://www.leg.state.fl.us/statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0100-0199/0196/Sections/0196.031.html) and the Department's [homestead guide PT-113](https://floridarevenue.com/property/Documents/pt113.pdf).
6. Before the homestead application, stop any residency-based property tax exemption or credit in the old state. Florida refuses homestead to a person receiving or claiming one (see the homestead section).
7. Get the Florida driver licence and register the cars in Florida within the statutory windows if the trigger in those laws applies (see the licence section). See [Florida Statutes 322.031](http://www.leg.state.fl.us/statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0300-0399/0322/Sections/0322.031.html) and [Florida Statutes 320.38](http://www.leg.state.fl.us/statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0300-0399/0320/Sections/0320.38.html).
8. Change the address with the IRS. The IRS says to use [Form 8822](https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs/about-form-8822) to notify it of a change to your home mailing address. The address on the federal return is one of the facts the Florida property appraiser may weigh.
9. Keep the file: dates, the recorded declaration, the homestead approval, licence and registration dates, school records, utility bills. The old state's auditor will ask for the same story.

### No Florida income tax on individuals

Article VII, section 5(a) of the Florida Constitution reads: "No tax upon estates or inheritances or upon the income of natural persons who are residents or citizens of the state shall be levied by the state, or under its authority, in excess of the aggregate of amounts which may be allowed to be credited upon or deducted from any similar tax levied by the United States or any state." The Department of Revenue states: "The State of Florida does not have a personal income tax."

Who this covers: natural persons who are residents or citizens of Florida. Who it does not cover: corporations and other non-natural persons. Section 5(b) allows a tax on their income, and Florida has a corporate income tax. A business owner whose company owes it should use `fl-corporate-income-tax`. This Guide does not cover it.

What this does not do: it does not stop another state taxing you. Income sourced in another state, and all income up to the date your old state accepts you have left, stay with that state's rules (step 3).

### The declaration of domicile (Florida Statutes 222.17)

What it says. A person who has established a domicile in Florida "may manifest and evidence the same" by filing a sworn statement with the clerk of the circuit court for the county where they reside. The statement says they reside in and maintain a place of abode in that county which they recognize and intend to maintain as their permanent home (subsection 1).

If they also keep a home in another state. Subsection 2 lets a person who has established a Florida domicile, but keeps another place of abode elsewhere, file a sworn statement that the Florida home is their "predominant and principal home" and that they intend to continue it permanently as such.

What the statement must contain (subsection 3): a declaration that the person is, at the time of signing, a bona fide resident of Florida; the place of residence in Florida; the city, county and state where they formerly resided; and any other places where they keep a place of abode.

How it is filed (subsection 5): signed under oath before an official authorized to take affidavits, then filed with the clerk of the circuit court, who records it. The clerk charges a service charge under section 28.24. This Guide does not state the amount; ask the clerk. The Department of Legal Affairs prescribes the form (subsection 6).

The reverse filing. Subsection 4 lets a person who is domiciled in another state, and has a Florida home or acts in Florida that might look like a move, file a sworn statement that their domicile stays in the other state. A seasonal resident who has not moved uses this, not the declaration of Florida domicile.

What it proves, and what it does not.
- It is evidence. The statute's own words are "manifest and evidence". It does not say that filing makes a person domiciled in Florida, and subsection 7 keeps every other way of proving domicile.
- Florida itself treats it as one factor. For homestead, a formal declaration of domicile recorded in the county is the first factor on the property appraiser's list, but the statute says "any one factor is not conclusive" ([196.015](http://www.leg.state.fl.us/statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0100-0199/0196/Sections/0196.015.html)).
- It does not bind the state you are leaving. Nothing in 222.17 speaks to another state's tax. Whether you are still a resident of New York, California or anywhere else for income tax is decided by that state under its own rules, on all the facts. For New York see `ny-residency-leaving-new-york`; for California, refer to an adviser who works with California's residency rules.

### Homestead exemption (Florida Statutes 196.031 and 196.011)

Who qualifies. A person who, on 1 January, has the legal title or beneficial title in equity to real property in Florida and who in good faith makes it their permanent residence, or the permanent residence of others legally or naturally dependent on them ([196.031(1)(a)](http://www.leg.state.fl.us/statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0100-0199/0196/Sections/0196.031.html)). Both conditions must hold on 1 January: title AND permanent residence. A buyer who closes or moves in after 1 January waits for the next 1 January. The deed must be recorded in the county before the exemption is granted.

Who does not qualify. "A person who is receiving or claiming the benefit of an ad valorem tax exemption or a tax credit in another state where permanent residency is required as a basis for the granting of that ad valorem tax exemption or tax credit is not entitled to the homestead exemption" (196.031(6)). The exception: an owner who keeps the Florida property as the permanent residence of a legally or naturally dependent person. Also out: a home that is no longer the owner's permanent home ([PT-113](https://floridarevenue.com/property/Documents/pt113.pdf)). Renting it out: under [Florida Statutes 196.061(1)](http://www.leg.state.fl.us/statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0100-0199/0196/Sections/0196.061.html), "The rental of all or substantially all of a dwelling previously claimed to be a homestead for tax purposes shall constitute the abandonment of such dwelling as a homestead, and the abandonment continues until the dwelling is physically occupied by the owner. However, such abandonment of the homestead after January 1 of any year does not affect the homestead exemption for tax purposes for that particular year unless the property is rented for more than 30 days per calendar year for 2 consecutive years." Chapter 2026-239 amended this section retroactively to 1 January 2026, so this is the 2026 wording; the 2026 roll is the first it applies to.

The amounts. The base amounts are in the table below. The first amount applies to all property taxes, including school district taxes. The additional amount applies only to assessed value above the threshold in the table, and only to levies other than school district levies. The additional amount is adjusted each 1 January for inflation when the consumer price index change is positive, so the 2026 additional amount can be higher than the base. No allowed page printed the 2026 indexed amount; the county property appraiser applies it.

The deadline. The application goes to the county property appraiser "on or before March 1 of each year". Missing it "shall constitute a waiver of the exemption privilege for that year", except under subsections 8 and 9 ([196.011(1)(a)](http://www.leg.state.fl.us/statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0100-0199/0196/Sections/0196.011.html)). The form needs the Social Security numbers of the applicant and spouse; a timely application without them is incomplete and may be completed by 1 April after the appraiser makes contact (196.011(1)(b)). After the first approval, the appraiser mails a renewal each year (196.011(7)).

A late applicant. Subsection 8: the value adjustment board grants the exemption if the applicant clearly documents that the late filing was due to postal error. Subsection 9: a qualified applicant who missed 1 March must apply on or before the 25th day after the appraiser mails the notices under section 194.011(1); the appraiser may grant it on evidence that the applicant could not apply in time or of extenuating circumstances, and if not, the applicant may petition the value adjustment board within the same period. Subsection 8 is mandatory once postal error is clearly documented ("shall grant"); subsection 9 is at the appraiser's or the board's discretion ("may grant"). Neither is a second deadline to plan around.

What the appraiser looks at. Intention to establish a permanent residence is a factual determination made in the first instance by the property appraiser. The relevant factors listed in [196.015](http://www.leg.state.fl.us/statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0100-0199/0196/Sections/0196.015.html), none conclusive on its own: a recorded declaration of domicile; where dependent children are registered for school; place of employment; the previous permanent residency outside Florida and the date it ended; Florida voter registration matching the homestead address; a Florida driver licence or ID card and evidence of giving up other states' licences; a Florida licence tag; the address on federal income tax returns; where bank statements and checking accounts are registered; and proof of paying utilities at the property. The statute's note says chapter 2026-239 amended section 196.015 retroactively to 1 January 2026, first applying to the 2026 roll, so this is the list in force for 2026.

### Save Our Homes assessment limit (Florida Statutes 193.155)

A home that receives the homestead exemption is assessed at just value as of 1 January of the year it first receives the exemption. From the following year it is reassessed each 1 January, and the change "shall not exceed the lower of" the two limits in the table below ([193.155(1)](http://www.leg.state.fl.us/statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0100-0199/0193/Sections/0193.155.html)). If the capped value would exceed just value, it is lowered to just value (193.155(2)).

| --- | --- | --- |
| Source | all limits below | http://www.leg.state.fl.us/statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0100-0199/0193/Sections/0193.155.html |
| Limit (a) | three percent of the prior year's assessed value | "Three percent of the assessed value of the property for the prior year" ([source](http://www.leg.state.fl.us/statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0100-0199/0193/Sections/0193.155.html)) |
| Limit (b) | the CPI change for the preceding calendar year | "The percentage change in the Consumer Price Index for All Urban Consumers" ([source](http://www.leg.state.fl.us/statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0100-0199/0193/Sections/0193.155.html)) |

What ends it. A change of ownership (any sale, foreclosure, or transfer of legal title or beneficial title in equity to any person) resets the home to just value on the following 1 January, except for the listed transfers such as between spouses and certain transfers on death (193.155(3)). Losing homestead also triggers reassessment ([PT-112](https://floridarevenue.com/property/Documents/pt112.pdf)).

Portability is for Florida-to-Florida moves only. PT-112 describes moving the accumulated benefit "from a previous Florida homestead to a new homestead in Florida". A person arriving from another state has no Florida benefit to carry. The cap starts building only from the first Florida homestead year.

### Driver licence and vehicle registration time limits

These limits are written for a nonresident who takes a job, engages in a trade, profession or occupation in Florida, or enrols children in Florida public schools. They are the two Florida time limits printed on an allowed page for the nonresident path; this Guide does not state any other.

- Driver licence: within 30 days after beginning the employment or education, if the person drives on Florida highways. The spouse and dependent children must also get a Florida licence within the same 30-day period before driving. Active duty military, commuters domiciled in another state with a valid licence (for the employment trigger only, 322.031(3)), and certain students are excepted ([322.031](http://www.leg.state.fl.us/statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0300-0399/0322/Sections/0322.031.html)).
- Vehicle registration: within 10 days after the start of the employment or education, if the vehicles will be driven on Florida roads. Certain students are excepted ([320.38](http://www.leg.state.fl.us/statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0300-0399/0320/Sections/0320.38.html)).

A retiree with no job in Florida and no children in Florida public schools is not caught by either of these two nonresident triggers. But the two chapters define who is a resident. [Florida Statutes 322.01(36)](http://www.leg.state.fl.us/statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0300-0399/0322/Sections/0322.01.html) reads: "'Resident' means a person who has his or her principal place of domicile in this state for a period of more than 6 consecutive months, has registered to vote, has made a statement of domicile pursuant to s. 222.17, or has filed for homestead tax exemption on property in this state." [320.01(34)](http://www.leg.state.fl.us/statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0300-0399/0320/Sections/0320.01.html) is to the same effect. So filing the declaration in step 4, or the homestead application in step 5, makes the mover a resident for licence and registration purposes, and the nonresident rules above no longer describe them. For a resident, [322.03(1)(a)](http://www.leg.state.fl.us/statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0300-0399/0322/Sections/0322.03.html) says "Except as otherwise authorized in this chapter, a person may not drive any motor vehicle upon a highway in this state unless such person has a valid driver license issued under this chapter", and [320.02(1)](http://www.leg.state.fl.us/statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0300-0399/0320/Sections/0320.02.html) says "Except as otherwise provided in this chapter, every owner or person in charge of a motor vehicle that is operated or driven on the roads of this state shall register the vehicle in this state." Neither section prints a day count for this path, and this Guide states none. Both the licence and the tag are also factors in 196.015.

Voter registration: Florida voter registration matching the homestead address is a homestead factor under 196.015. This Guide states no deadline for it, because no allowed page read for this Guide prints one.

### Estate tax

The Department of Revenue states: "A federal change eliminated Florida's estate tax on people who died after December 31, 2004." Since 1 July 2023, personal representatives do not have to file the Affidavit of No Florida Estate Tax Due (Form DR-312) or Form DR-313 ([Department of Revenue, Florida Estate Tax](https://floridarevenue.com/taxes/taxesfees/Pages/estate_tax.aspx)). Federal estate and gift tax still applies; see `us-estate-gift-706-709`. A state the decedent did not fully leave may still claim an estate tax; that is the old state's question.

## Boundary and exception table

| Situation | Treatment |
| --- | --- |
| Buyer closes or moves in after 1 January | No homestead until the next 1 January |
| Applicant still receives a residency-based property tax exemption or credit in another state | No Florida homestead (196.031(6)), unless the Florida home is the permanent residence of a dependent |
| Missed 1 March | Exemption waived for the year, except postal error (196.011(8), mandatory) or the late-filing route in 196.011(9) (discretionary) |
| Home rented out after 1 January | Homestead kept for that year unless rented more than 30 days per calendar year for 2 consecutive years (196.061(1), as amended by chapter 2026-239) |
| Seasonal resident who has not moved | Files the reverse statement under 222.17(4), not a declaration of Florida domicile |
| Retiree with no Florida job and no children in public school | Not caught by the 30-day and 10-day nonresident triggers, but becomes a resident for licence and registration once the declaration or homestead is filed (322.01, 320.01) |
| Mover from another state wanting to carry a capped value | No portability: it applies only from one Florida homestead to another (PT-112) |
| Sale or transfer of the homestead | Reset to just value on the next 1 January, except listed transfers such as between spouses (193.155(3)) |

## Worked cases

**Case 1, the homestead exemption at USD 65,000 of assessed value.** This is the Department's own example in PT-113, using the base amounts (the 2026 indexed additional amount is applied by the county and is not stated here).

```oa-check
name: Florida homestead exemption, PT-113 example
inputs:
  assessed_value: 65000
  first_exemption: 25000
  additional_base: 25000
  additional_threshold: 50000
steps:
  - exempt_all_levies: min(assessed_value, first_exemption)
  - exempt_non_school_extra: min(max(assessed_value - additional_threshold, 0), additional_base)
  - taxable_school: assessed_value - exempt_all_levies
  - taxable_non_school: assessed_value - exempt_all_levies - exempt_non_school_extra
expected:
  exempt_all_levies: 25000
  exempt_non_school_extra: 15000
  taxable_school: 40000
  taxable_non_school: 25000
```

The slice from USD 25,000 to USD 50,000 stays taxable for all levies, as PT-113 says.

**Case 2, a decision: apply for homestead now or wait?** A couple bought a Florida home in November 2025, recorded the deed and moved in before 1 January 2026; they dropped their old-state residency credit. The 2026 assessed (just) value is USD 400,000. If they file DR-501 by 1 March 2026, the 2026 value becomes the Save Our Homes base. Suppose the 2027 just value is USD 440,000 (10% up). The published CPI figure is not stated here; whenever it is above 3%, the 3% limit binds, and the case uses 3%. If they wait and apply in 2027, the cap would start only from 2027.

```oa-check
name: Save Our Homes cap, apply in 2026 against waiting
inputs:
  just_value_2026: 400000
  just_value_2027: 440000
  cap_rate: 0.03
steps:
  - capped_2027: min(just_value_2026 * (1 + cap_rate), just_value_2027)
  - assessed_if_wait: just_value_2027
  - value_sheltered_2027: assessed_if_wait - capped_2027
expected:
  capped_2027: 412000
  assessed_if_wait: 440000
  value_sheltered_2027: 28000
```

Applying in 2026 means the 2027 assessed value is capped at USD 412,000, USD 28,000 below just value, and the exemption itself applies a year earlier. Waiting gives up both. The decision turns on whether title and permanent residence truly held on 1 January 2026; if not, they cannot apply for 2026 anyway.

## When to refuse or refer

- Refuse to say that filing the declaration of domicile, or getting homestead, ends residency in the old state. Refer the old-state question to `ny-residency-leaving-new-york` for New York, to the general method in step 3 for other states, and California movers to an adviser who works with California's residency rules.
- Refer a homestead denial, a late application or a portability claim to the county property appraiser and, if needed, the value adjustment board.
- Refer non-tax homestead questions (creditor protection, wills, title held in a trust) to a Florida lawyer. This Guide covers the property tax exemption only.
- Refer business taxes to the Florida Guides for that tax, such as `fl-corporate-income-tax`. Do not answer sales tax, surtax or rental tax questions from this Guide.
- Refuse to state the 2026 inflation-adjusted additional homestead amount or a county millage; the county property appraiser holds those figures.

## Filing and payment

There is no Florida income tax return for an individual. The filings in a move are the declaration of domicile (optional, with the county clerk, fee set by the clerk under section 28.24), the homestead application on Form DR-501 to the county property appraiser, and the federal address change on IRS Form 8822. Property tax itself is billed by the county.

| When | Action | Law |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Before the move | Decide the move date; plan the exit from the old state with its own Guide | old state's rules |
| After establishing the Florida domicile | File the declaration of domicile with the county clerk (optional evidence) | 222.17 |
| Within 30 days after starting Florida work or enrolling children in public school | Florida driver licence for the driver, spouse and dependent children who drive | 322.031 |
| Within 10 days after starting Florida work or enrolling children in public school | Register vehicles in Florida | 320.38 |
| Before 1 January | Hold legal or beneficial title and live in the home as the permanent residence; record the deed before the appraiser grants the exemption; stop any old-state residency-based property tax benefit | 196.031 |
| 1 January | Title and permanent residence tested on this date | 196.031 |
| By 1 March | File Form DR-501 with the county property appraiser | 196.011 |
| Any time | File Form 8822 with the IRS for the new home address | IRS |

## Completion checklist

- Move date fixed and the old state's exit rules applied with that state's own Guide.
- Title and permanent residence both held on 1 January; deed recorded.
- Old-state residency-based property tax benefits stopped before the homestead application.
- DR-501 filed by 1 March, with both spouses' Social Security numbers.
- Declaration of domicile filed if wanted, treated as evidence only.
- Licence and registration done within 30 and 10 days where the nonresident trigger applies, or promptly once a resident.
- Form 8822 filed; file of evidence kept (dates, declaration, homestead approval, licence, school, utilities).
