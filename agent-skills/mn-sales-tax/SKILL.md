# Minnesota sales and use tax, 2026

> **General reference only.** Source-cited draft, not approved by an accountant. For US Treasury Circular 230 §10.37 purposes this is general reference material; a qualified professional must check the facts, current law and forms before filing or taking a position.

## Who this applies to

Minnesota retailers, remote sellers, marketplace providers and purchasers of untaxed taxable Minnesota-use goods/services. Load `us-sales-tax` for the general nexus/marketplace method; this skill supplies Minnesota rules. Minnesota participates in Streamlined Sales Tax, but state registration and local sourcing still matter. Currency: USD. This 2026 source-cited draft replaces the old blanket “all clothing exempt,” fixed local-rate maximum and “never compute” statements.

## The base

**Ordinary patterns.** General retail tangible property is taxable at state plus applicable local rates. Delivery/use destination governs the core sale; preserve proof of out-of-state delivery. Food/food ingredients are generally exempt, with statutory candy, soft drinks, prepared-food, alcohol and other exceptions. Prepared meals are not grocery-exempt merely because purchased to carry out. Prescription and qualifying human OTC drugs are exempt; grooming/hygiene products and dietary supplements are not all drugs. Resale requires a completed ST3 and actual resale facts, rather than a purchaser merely calling itself a business. [297A.67](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/297A.67); [Revenue nontaxable sales](https://www.revenue.state.mn.us/guide/nontaxable-sales-8).

**Clothing.** General-use apparel and ordinary footwear are exempt, including general-use clothing with protective features. Accessories, equipment, sports/recreational items unsuitable for general use and protective equipment unsuitable for general use can be taxable. Ordinary sneakers differ from cleated athletic shoes; a general-use jacket differs from a helmet. The previous “ALL clothing and footwear exempt” instruction is withdrawn. Separately billed alterations and purchases of sewing materials require the specific rule, not a guess based on the retailer's name. [Clothing fact sheet 105](https://www.revenue.state.mn.us/sites/default/files/2026-01/FS105.pdf); [297A.67 subd. 8](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/297A.67).

**Software/digital/services.** Prewritten/canned software is taxable whether downloaded or delivered on media; custom programming and properly separated qualifying modification services can be nontaxable. Remote database access/data processing can be nontaxable, but “SaaS” alone does not establish the facts. Determine whether there is software delivery/licensing, only remote access, a taxable digital product or a mixed transaction. The old absolute “never tax SaaS” instruction is withdrawn. Specified digital goods are not a general exemption for all electronic purchases. Ordinary professional service is generally outside sales tax unless a taxable-service rule applies or a taxable product/bundle is sold. [Computer software fact sheet 134](https://www.revenue.state.mn.us/sites/default/files/2023-01/FS134.pdf); [software rule 8130.9910](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/rules/8130.9910/).

Optional prewritten-software maintenance separately priced for upgrades versus support follows those components; a single optional charge combining upgrades and support is taxable on 20% of the price under the cited rule. Mandatory maintenance can be part of the taxable sale. Multistate concurrently used software/digital/taxable service can require a valid ST3 multiple-points-of-use claim and the purchaser's consistent, supported allocation/remittance. It is not a generic exemption for use partly outside Minnesota. [Fact sheet 134](https://www.revenue.state.mn.us/sites/default/files/2023-01/FS134.pdf); [Rule 8130.9910](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/rules/8130.9910/).

**Production equipment.** Qualifying capital equipment is an exemption, not merely the old mandatory-refund description. It must be used in Minnesota primarily in qualifying production, essential to the integrated production process, with statutory inclusions/exclusions. Administrative/security equipment, ordinary building materials and restaurant meal-preparation equipment do not automatically qualify. Retain the valid ST3 and production/use evidence; overpaid-tax refund procedure is separate. [297A.68 subd. 5](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/297A.68).

## Deadlines and filing

Register for a Minnesota taxID/sales-use account and the actual local tax types through Revenue e-Services. A Minnesota physical place, home-office worker, inventory, agent, own-vehicle delivery or other statutory presence can create collection without waiting for remote thresholds. Remote threshold counts use retail transactions, not item count; resale sales properly evidenced by ST3 are excluded from the small-seller test under Revenue's registration page. Marketplace-covered sales and the seller's remaining obligations must be reconciled, not double collected. [297A.66](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/297A.66); [Who needs to register](https://www.revenue.state.mn.us/guide/who-needs-register).

Once the remote test is met, start collection by the statutory first day of a calendar month no later than 60 days afterwards and continue for the prescribed minimum collection period. Cessation needs actual continued qualification and notification to the commissioner; one quiet month does not close the account. A seller making only exempt sales can have a use-tax registration obligation for its own taxable purchases. [297A.66 subd. 2](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/297A.66); [registration page](https://www.revenue.state.mn.us/guide/who-needs-register).

Use assigned monthly/quarterly/annual filing frequency. Report gross sales, exempt/deductible sales, state/local taxable bases and tax, consumer use tax and prior credits under the actual account; reconcile marketplace collections and all addresses. File required zero returns until status is changed/closed. Retain invoices, exemption certificates, shipping proof, rate lookup and e-Services acceptance/payment confirmation. The published 2026 calendar moves May's monthly due date toJune 22, August's toSeptember 21 and November's toDecember 21; December monthly/quarterly isJanuary 20,2027. Larger filers' June accelerated payment, electronic-payment requirements and cash/accrual election details require the specific current instructions, not this ordinary schedule. [Filing/recordkeeping](https://www.revenue.state.mn.us/so/node/9616); [actual due-date calendar](https://www.revenue.state.mn.us/sales-tax-return-filing-due-dates).

## Penalties and interest

Late filing/payment, missed use tax or invalid exemption can generate assessment, penalties and interest. Compute exact charges under the actual notice/current statute and payment dates; do not copy another tax's percentages. Local taxes can have different product/special-tax bases. Exact 2026 late-charge and accelerated-payment formulas remain unconfirmed below.

## Cross-border points

Map delivery/use destination and actual local jurisdiction at the transaction date, not ZIP alone or the seller's office. A nondelivery Minnesota sale can still involve customer receipt in Minnesota. Purchaser use tax generally complements sales tax, with proper credit for qualifying tax already paid. Physical nexus, rolling 12 month remote nexus and marketplace collection are separate tests. Special vehicles, lodging, meals/liquor, cannabis and other product taxes need their actual tax-specific rules. No statewide maximum-combined-rate estimate is used.

## Two worked examples that recompute

**General-use clothing decision.** A qualifying ordinary jacket is 100; a separate taxable helmet is 100. Assume no other exemption and isolate state tax only: jacket 0, helmet 6.875, rounded 6.88. Local tax is deliberately excluded from this isolated state illustration and must be added from the actual address. The customer is not exempt on the helmet merely because the store sells clothing.

```oa-check
name: Minnesota general-use clothing versus equipment
inputs:
  jacket: 100
  helmet: 100
  state_rate: 0.06875
steps:
  - jacket_tax: jacket * 0
  - helmet_tax: round(helmet * state_rate, 2)
  - state_total: jacket_tax + helmet_tax
expected:
  jacket_tax: 0
  helmet_tax: 6.88
  state_total: 6.88
```

**Optional software-maintenance allocation.** A1,000 optional prewritten-software maintenance charge combines upgrades/support without separate prices. Assuming the cited 20% taxable portion applies and isolating state tax, taxable base 200, state tax 13.75. If facts instead establish a support-only contract, no taxable upgrades, its state tax is 0. Do not apply the 20% rule to the software licence price or every SaaS invoice.

```oa-check
name: Minnesota optional software maintenance decision
inputs:
  price: 1000
  taxable_fraction: 0.2
  state_rate: 0.06875
steps:
  - mixed_base: price * taxable_fraction
  - mixed_state_tax: mixed_base * state_rate
  - support_only_tax: price * 0
expected:
  mixed_base: 200
  mixed_state_tax: 13.75
  support_only_tax: 0
```

## Ask the client first

1. What product/service/software rights are sold, including bundles, maintenance and general-use versus specialized clothing?
2. Delivery/receipt/use address and date, local product tax type and exemption/resale/MPU documents?
3. Prior 12 month retail sales and transaction counts, physical presence/agents/inventory and marketplace collection evidence?
4. TaxID/accounts, assigned frequency, book method, gross-to-taxable reconciliation, untaxed purchases and payment history?
5. Any special vehicles/lodging/cannabis/liquor or June accelerated-payment obligation?
