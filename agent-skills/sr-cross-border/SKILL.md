# Suriname cross-border income tax (2026) v1.0

> **General reference only.** This skill is general tax/accounting reference material for AI-assisted workflows. It has not been reviewed for any specific person's facts, documents, elections, deadlines, residency, filing status, or local procedures. Do not rely on it to file, pay, amend, or take a tax position without review by a qualified professional in the relevant jurisdiction.

This skill covers what Suriname taxes when income, people or companies cross its border, for the 2026 calendar year (a body's book year where it keeps one, IB art. 38). Suriname has one income tax for individuals and bodies, the Wet Inkomstenbelasting 1922 (IB), with a separate Wet Dividendbelasting 1973 and wage tax under the Wet Loonbelasting (LB). It has no separate withholding tax on interest, royalties or service fees. The authority is the Suriname Tax Administration (Belastingdienst Suriname). The currency is the Surinamese dollar (SRD).

**Which text of the law.** The IB as last amended by S.B. 2024 no. 3, the Wet Dividendbelasting as last amended by S.B. 1984 no. 109, and the LB as last amended by S.B. 2024 no. 2, as published by the Tax Administration and read on 11 November 2026. Treaty texts are those the treaty partner's government publishes: the Dutch government (wetten.overheid.nl, Verdragenbank, Tractatenblad) and the Indonesian Directorate General of Taxes (pajak.go.id). No treaty text is published on a Surinamese government site.

**One figure, one home.** Rates and bands for individuals are in [sr-income-tax](sr-income-tax.md); the 36% company rate, the participation exemption and the dividend tax mechanics for resident shareholders in [sr-corporate-income-tax](sr-corporate-income-tax.md); payroll in [sr-payroll-social](sr-payroll-social.md); VAT on imported services in [sr-vat-gst](sr-vat-gst.md). This skill links to them and adds only what is cross-border.

## Who this applies to

- **Resident individuals** (binnenlandse belastingplichtigen): natural persons who live in Suriname, judged on the facts (IB arts. 1(2)(a), 3(1)). Someone who returns to live in Suriname within one year of leaving, without living elsewhere in between, is treated as resident throughout (art. 3(2)). A Surinamese national abroad employed by a Surinamese public body, with their spouse and minor children living with them, is treated as resident (art. 3(3)). Residents are taxed on income from all sources (art. 4).
- **Resident bodies:** bodies established in Suriname (art. 1(2)(b)); a body incorporated under Surinamese law is always treated as established in Suriname (art. 3(4); Wet Dividendbelasting art. 1(2)).
- **Non-resident individuals and bodies** (buitenlandse belastingplichtigen) are taxed only on Surinamese-source income, binnenlands onzuiver inkomen (IB art. 1(3)). Non-resident bodies in scope: legal persons, partnerships limited by shares and other companies with share capital, associations without legal personality and special-purpose funds (art. 1(3)(b)).
- **Payers in Suriname** who pay dividends abroad (dividend tax), wages to non-residents (wage tax) or fees to non-resident artists and athletes.
- **Out of scope:** consular exemptions (art. 33), VAT, and residents' worldwide-income computations, which follow the domestic skills.

## The base

Work a cross-border case in this order.

1. **Residence.** Decide where the person lives or where the body is established (IB art. 3). A Surinamese-law company is always resident (art. 3(4)). A resident is taxed on everything under the domestic skills; stop here unless foreign tax relief is the question (step 7).
2. **Source.** For a non-resident, list only the items in IB art. 27(2). Interest on an unsecured loan, royalties, service fees and dividends from shareholdings are not on the list: Suriname does not tax them in the hands of a non-resident (dividends carry dividend tax at source instead, step 5). Interest on a debt secured by mortgage on Surinamese property is on the list (art. 27(2)(b)(1°)) and is taxed by assessment on a return, not by withholding.
3. **Permanent establishment.** A fixed place of business, or a permanent representative (art. 28(1)), makes business profit Surinamese. Construction and similar work counts only over 183 days in 12 months (art. 28(2)(b)); insurers, aerial mapping and resource surveys always count (art. 28(2)(a), (c)); so does licensed work on the continental shelf (art. 28(3)). International shipping and air transport by non-residents do not (art. 29).
4. **Non-resident's taxable amount.** Individuals: binnenlands onzuiver inkomen less Surinamese losses and less interest on mortgages on Surinamese property not linked to a source of income (art. 26(2)); no deduction for foreign tax (art. 26(4)); tax at the art. 34 bands (sr-income-tax). Bodies: binnenlands belastbaar bedrag on the same footing, with arts. 21, 23 and 25 applied to business profit (art. 30bis), at 36% (art. 36). A foreign insurer's taxable profit is 15% of its total income (art. 30).
5. **Dividends paid abroad.** The Surinamese company withholds 25% when the dividend is made available and files and pays within one month (Wet Dividendbelasting arts. 5, 6). If it bears the tax itself, the tax is 25% of the dividend times 100/75 (art. 5). Dividend tax covers profit distributions in any form, liquidation payments above paid-in capital, bonus shares not paid up, capital repaid while there are profits (unless the capital was formally reduced first), and profit-sharing bond interest (art. 3).
6. **Treaty cap.** If the recipient is resident in the Netherlands or Indonesia, the treaty caps Suriname's tax (step 2 decides whether there is any Surinamese tax to cap). The dividend tax act has no procedure for applying a lower rate at source; the texts read do not say whether the inspector allows it. Under the Netherlands treaty, tax levied above the treaty rate can be reclaimed within five years (Protocol VII). For wages, the employer may stop withholding under a treaty only on the inspector's statement (LB art. 19(3)).
7. **Residents with foreign income.** Relief for double tax comes only from a staatsbesluit or a treaty (IB art. 37(1), (2)); the act has no general unilateral credit. For a body, foreign profit tax is not deductible where a double-tax relief rule applies to it (art. 23(3°)). Under the Netherlands treaty, Suriname exempts items the Netherlands may tax by the proportional method of art. 24(2), and credits the lower of the Dutch tax and the Surinamese tax on dividends, interest, royalties and art. 18 income (art. 24(3)).

**Deduction limits that bite on payments abroad.** Directors' and supervisory-board profit shares are deductible only when paid to people who live in Suriname or are taxable in Suriname on them, and within the caps (IB art. 23(1°)(b)); interest and rent to related parties are deductible only so far as they are at arm's length (art. 23(2°)). Detail in sr-corporate-income-tax.

All income tax and wage tax returns are filed in the Tax Administration online portal (portaal.belastingdienst.sr/OPO) with an electronic signature (IB art. 42(1); LB art. 20(1)).

## Penalties and interest

- **Dividend tax not withheld or not paid** is collected by a top-up assessment within five years after the end of the calendar year, increased by 100%; 10% (at least SRD 0.05) where the shortfall is not due to intent or gross negligence (Wet Dividendbelasting arts. 10, 11). The assessment is payable within one month (art. 12). Liquidators of a company dissolved or moved abroad are jointly liable (art. 13).
- **Income tax and wage tax** penalties for non-residents are the same as for residents: see sr-income-tax and sr-payroll-social. A foreign employer's wage tax and fines are also owed by the head of its Surinamese permanent establishment, its resident permanent representative, or whoever runs the work in Suriname (LB art. 29(1)(a)).

## Cross-border points

- **Treaties in force with an official text found.** The Netherlands: agreement of 25 November 1975 (Trb. 1975, 134), in force 13 April 1977 for the Netherlands in Europe only, not for Aruba, Curacao, Sint Maarten or the Caribbean Netherlands (Verdragenbank 002112). Indonesia: agreement signed in Paramaribo on 14 October 2003, effective date 1 January 2014 per the Indonesian Directorate General of Taxes.
- **Signed, not in force.** Curacao: treaty of 1 July 2024 (Trb. 2024, 90; corrections Trb. 2025, 42). The Verdragenbank (014036) shows no entry-into-force date when read. Once in force it applies from 1 January of the next year (art. 30(2)); do not apply it to 2026 unless the client shows it entered into force in 2025.
- **No other treaty was found** on an official Surinamese or treaty-partner government site. Without a treaty, a non-resident shareholder bears the full 25% dividend tax.
- **Employment under the treaties.** Pay for work in Suriname by a Dutch or Indonesian resident is taxed only in the home State if the employee is in Suriname no more than 183 days in a 12-month period, the employer is not resident in Suriname, and no Surinamese permanent establishment bears the pay (Netherlands art. 16(2); Indonesia art. 15(2)). The employer still needs the inspector's statement to stop withholding (LB art. 19(3)).
- **Permanent establishment under the treaties.** The Netherlands treaty lets Suriname tax construction work over three months and services by employees over 183 days in 12 months (art. 5(2)(h), (j)); the Indonesia treaty, construction over six months (art. 5). A treaty only limits Surinamese tax: below the 183-day domestic test of IB art. 28(2)(b), a construction site is not deemed a permanent establishment under Surinamese law, whatever the treaty allows.
- **VAT** on imports and on services from abroad is in sr-vat-gst.

## Two worked examples that recompute

**Example 1: dividend of SRD 100,000 paid by a Surinamese NV abroad.** Domestic rule: 25% dividend tax, SRD 25,000, filed and paid within one month (Wet Dividendbelasting arts. 5, 6). If the NV declares the SRD 100,000 as net and bears the tax, the tax is 25% of 100,000 times 100/75 = SRD 33,333.33.

- Shareholder a Dutch BV holding 30% directly, whose dividend is exempt from Dutch corporate tax: Netherlands treaty art. 10(2)(a), 7.5% = SRD 7,500. If 25% was withheld, SRD 17,500 can be reclaimed within five years (Protocol VII).
- Shareholder a Dutch BV that owes Dutch corporate tax on the dividend: Protocol VI takes away the 7.5% rate; art. 10(2)(c) caps at 20% = SRD 20,000; reclaim SRD 5,000.
- Shareholder resident in Indonesia (beneficial owner): 15% = SRD 15,000; excess SRD 10,000 (no refund protocol was found in the Indonesia treaty text; ask the inspector).
- Shareholder resident anywhere else: SRD 25,000, final.

**Example 2 (decision): an Indonesian contractor builds a plant in Suriname. Finish in 150 days, or take 200?** Expected profit on the Surinamese work SRD 400,000; the contractor has no other fixed place of business or representative in Suriname. The figure that decides is 183 days of work within 12 months (IB art. 28(2)(b)).

- **150 days.** Not a deemed permanent establishment under Surinamese law, and under the treaty's six-month test (art. 5) Suriname could not tax it anyway. Surinamese income tax: nil.
- **200 days.** Over 183 days, so a deemed permanent establishment (art. 28(2)(b)); 200 days is also over six months, so the treaty does not stop Suriname taxing. Tax: 36% of SRD 400,000 = SRD 144,000 on the non-resident body's return (IB arts. 30bis, 36). The Indonesia treaty would allow an extra tax of up to 15% on a permanent establishment's after-tax profit (art. 10), but the Wet Inkomstenbelasting read levies no such tax, so none is computed.

Plan the schedule against the 183-day count, which includes work by successive contractors on the same project.

## Ask the client first

1. Where does the recipient live or where is it established, and since when? For a company, under which law was it incorporated?
2. What exactly is being paid: dividend, interest (secured by a Surinamese mortgage or not), royalty, service fee, wages, artist's fee? Only some are Surinamese-source.
3. For a dividend: what percentage of the capital does the recipient hold directly, is it the beneficial owner, and (for a Dutch company) is the dividend taxed in the Netherlands?
4. For work in Suriname: how many days within any 12 months, for which employer, and does the business have an office, site or representative in Suriname?
5. Is the recipient resident in the Netherlands (Europe), Indonesia or Curacao? Only the first two treaties were in force when read.
6. Does the employee or recipient hold a statement from the inspector applying a treaty?
