# Tax amnesty under the Finance Act, 2026: what to do and by when

> **General reference only.** This skill is general tax/accounting reference material for AI-assisted workflows. It has not been reviewed for any specific person's facts, documents, elections, deadlines, residency, filing status, or local procedures. Do not rely on it to file, pay, amend, or take a tax position without review by a qualified professional in the relevant jurisdiction.

The Finance Act, 2026 revived the amnesty in section 37E of the Tax Procedures Act by moving its dates forward. Section 37E requires the Commissioner to refrain from recovering qualifying penalties, interest and fines. Where principal remains unpaid, relief is conditional on an application, payment plan, full principal payment by 31 December 2026, no further tax debt, and a written undertaking to settle all outstanding tax. The statutory cutoff refers to interest, penalties or fines accrued up to 31 December 2025; do not assume every later charge on an older obligation qualifies. The window opened on 1 July 2026 and closes on 31 December 2026. Two routes exist. If you already cleared the principal, the waiver comes automatically. If principal is still outstanding, you pay it as a lump sum or under a payment plan that finishes by 31 December 2026.

## Scope

This applies to you if you carry penalties or interest on any tax head for periods up to 31 December 2025, whether you file as an individual or a company.

It does not apply to liabilities arising on or after 1 January 2026. Those stay payable in full, principal, penalties and interest.

Stop and call a tax professional if any of the following is true:

- Part of the debt sits under objection, appeal or active litigation. The amnesty does not settle a dispute. KRA’s [guidance](https://www.kra.go.ke/2026-tax-amnesty-programme-at-a-glance) directs active court/Tribunal disputes through ADR to settle principal before benefits unlock. Its FAQ separately tells taxpayers to leave disputed periods out of payment registration until resolved. Preserve all objection/appeal deadlines while resolving the debt.
- You are under an ongoing audit or hold an assessment you disagree with.
- The exposure involves customs, transfer pricing, or related-party transactions.
- You cannot tell from your ledger which periods the penalties belong to.

## Ask the client first

- The tax heads and periods where you carry penalties or interest.
- Confirmation that every return for those periods has been filed, nil returns included.
- Whether any part of the debt sits under objection, appeal or ADR.
- Whether you can pay the principal in one sum, or need instalments.
- Your iTax login, with your registration details current.

## The method, step by step

The authority is [TPA s37E](https://www.kra.go.ke/images/publications/Tax-Procedures-Act--2026.pdf), amended by [Finance Act 2026 ss1(c),45](https://www.kra.go.ke/images/publications/Finance-Act-2026.pdf).

1. Log into iTax and download your statement of account for every obligation you hold. Work from the ledger, not from memory. What you believe you owe and what KRA has recorded rarely match.

2. Split the ledger by period. Put everything that accrued up to 31 December 2025 on one side and anything arising on or after 1 January 2026 on the other. The amnesty reaches only the first group. Every figure after this step depends on getting the split right.

3. Reconcile both the underlying tax period and when penalties, interest and fines accrued. Section 37E(3)(a) limits the relief to qualifying charges accrued up to 31 December 2025. The old file automatically admitted charges raised in 2026 on older obligations; that cannot be assumed from this text.

4. Within the qualifying periods, separate the principal tax from the penalties and interest. The principal is what you must pay. The penalties and interest are what gets waived.

5. File outstanding returns and reconcile the liability. KRA’s [2026 amnesty guidance](https://www.kra.go.ke/2026-tax-amnesty-programme-at-a-glance) says late-filing penalties are waived automatically once missing returns are fully filed and no principal tax is due. Check the credited ledger and retain the certificate.

6. If you cleared the principal before 31 December 2025, stop here. The waiver is automatic and no application is required. Watch the ledger and confirm the penalties come off.

7. If principal remains, meet the statutory conditions. KRA’s [operational guidance](https://www.kra.go.ke/2026-tax-amnesty-programme-at-a-glance) describes immediate corresponding waiver after full lump-sum settlement and an iTax payment plan for instalments. Verify the ledger posting instead of assuming completion from a payment instruction alone.

8. For instalments, apply on iTax for a structured payment plan and schedule it so that all the principal clears by 31 December 2026. Build slack into the schedule. A plan that lands on the last week leaves you no room if a payment fails.

9. Reconcile before you treat the matter as closed. The qualifying principal you computed should tie to the ledger, the payment schedule should finish before 31 December 2026, and you should know what you still owe outside the amnesty.

10. Once the waiver posts, download and keep the ledger. That download is your evidence that the penalties came off.

### Traps

- **Ignoring the accrual cutoff.** Separate the underlying obligation from the date penalties or interest accrued; do not assume later accruals qualify.
- **Relying on an unreconciled ledger.** File and reconcile outstanding returns before treating a waiver as complete.
- **A payment plan that runs past 31 December 2026.** Section 37E(4) says the amount remaining unpaid then attracts interest and penalties for which no amnesty is granted. The relief is conditional, not granted up front.
- **Assuming the amnesty ends a dispute.** It does not. A disputed assessment stays disputed, and the ADR route runs on its own timetable.
- **Waiting for December.** Portal load and turnaround times at year end are the most common reason a taxpayer misses a window that was open for six months.
- **Mixing post-2025 debt into the plan.** Liabilities from 1 January 2026 onward carry their own penalties and interest and belong in a separate settlement.

## Boundary and exception table

| Situation | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Principal already paid before the statutory cutoff | Apply s37E(1), reconcile waiver against KRA ledger |
| Principal unpaid at the cutoff | Application, plan, undertaking, no further tax debt and final payment conditions all matter |
| Audit, objection, appeal, customs, transfer pricing or unclear periods | Preserve legal deadlines; refer before relying on relief |

## Worked cases

A hypothetical qualifying debt has KES 100,000 principal, KES 20,000 penalties and KES 10,000 interest accrued by the cutoff. Full qualifying relief leaves KES 100,000 principal to pay; it does not forgive principal.

```oa-check
name: Qualifying amnesty excludes principal
inputs:
  principal: 100000
  penalties: 20000
  interest: 10000
steps:
  - original: principal + penalties + interest
  - relief: penalties + interest
  - payment: original - relief
expected:
  original: 130000
  relief: 30000
  payment: 100000
```

A taxpayer who cannot pay KES 120,000 at once proposes 3 payments of KES 40,000 completed within the window. Paying only 2 leaves KES 40,000 principal and fails full settlement; do not mark the account cleared. This example assumes all other conditions are met and does not quantify restored charges.

```oa-check
name: Payment plan completion decision
inputs:
  principal: 120000
  instalment: 40000
  paid_count: 2
steps:
  - paid: instalment * paid_count
  - unpaid: principal - paid
  - full_plan: instalment * 3
expected:
  paid: 80000
  unpaid: 40000
  full_plan: 120000
```

## When to refuse or refer

Refer disputed assessments, mixed-period or customs exposure, further debt, uncertain accruals and inability to complete the plan. Do not stop an objection or appeal clock on the assumption that an amnesty application settles a dispute.

## Filing and payment

Use iTax (https://itax.kra.go.ke) to obtain statements and file missing returns. KRA’s [current page](https://www.kra.go.ke/2026-tax-amnesty-programme-at-a-glance) directs Payments > Apply Payment Plan (new), choose relevant amnesty periods, then Payments > Payments Registration, select tax head/sub-head/period and adjust to qualifying principal. The same page’s older FAQ also mentions an Amnesty application tab under Debt and Enforcement: use the route actually presented and seek KRA assistance if absent, rather than making duplicate applications. Match receipts to the ledger and download the amnesty certificate through Taxpayer Profile > Useful Links > Consult and Reprint Acknowledgement Receipts and Certificates > Debt and Enforcement > Amnesty Certificate. KRA excludes EACCMA customs duties, describes limited instalments within the window and warns that some withholding transactions, fines and missing PAYE credits require reconciliation; an old system-update target date is not proof those issues are resolved.

## Completion checklist

Confirm the accrual cutoff, filed returns, principal balance, written application/undertaking where required, no further debt, timely full payment, and the final ledger waiver.
