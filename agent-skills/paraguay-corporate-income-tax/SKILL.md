# Paraguay business income tax (IRE): 2026 rate, regimes, base, losses and filing v0.1

> **General reference only.** This skill is general tax/accounting reference material for AI-assisted workflows. It has not been reviewed for any specific person's facts, documents, elections, deadlines, residency, filing status, or local procedures. Do not rely on it to file, pay, amend, or take a tax position without review by a qualified professional in the relevant jurisdiction.

The law is Ley N° 6380/2019 "De Modernización y Simplificación del Sistema Tributario Nacional", Libro I, Título I (arts. 1 to 39), read in the text published by the Biblioteca y Archivo Central del Congreso Nacional (BACN). The regulation is the Anexo to Decreto N° 3182/2019, read in the copy published by the Dirección Nacional de Ingresos Tributarios (DNIT, which replaced the Subsecretaría de Estado de Tributación under Ley N° 7143/2023). Article 83 of that Anexo (advance payments) was replaced by Decreto N° 7402/2022. The DNIT list of tax laws was checked on 11 November 2026: the only later law that changes the text of Ley 6380 is Ley N° 7067/2023, which touches the IVA exemptions in art. 100 and nothing in the IRE. Ley N° 7548/2025 (new investment incentive regime) and Ley N° 7547/2025 (maquila) refer to Ley 6380 without amending the IRE articles. Amounts are in guaraníes (G). The fiscal year (ejercicio fiscal) is the calendar year (art. 7), so tax year 2026 is the year to 31 December 2026, with its return due in April 2027.

## Who this applies to

- **IRE taxpayers** (art. 2): sole-owner businesses (empresas unipersonales, including EIRL) and their undivided estates; sociedades simples, S.A., S.R.L., sociedades en comandita and de capital e industria; consortia for a public work (members jointly liable); associations, corporations, foundations, cooperatives and mutuals; transparent legal structures; public enterprises and mixed-economy companies; branches, agencies and permanent establishments of foreign persons; and any other private entity, with or without legal personality.
- **What is taxed** (arts. 1 and 6): Paraguayan-source income from every economic activity (primary, secondary, tertiary, including agriculture), plus income from assets and rights and any increase in net worth. Income taxed under the personal income tax (IRP) is excluded. Paraguayan source includes, among others, technical assistance and services performed in Paraguay, interest and returns on deposits abroad and dividends from foreign entities received by a resident investor, and derivatives where the investor is resident. Income from activities abroad not listed in art. 6 is also Paraguayan source **unless** the taxpayer paid a foreign income tax on it at a rate equal to or above 10% (art. 6, last paragraph).
- **A sole-owner business** (art. 3) is an organised, habitual unit of a person combining labour and capital, with capital predominant, in the 17 activities listed (agriculture and extraction, trade, renting, pawnbroking, repairs, transport, parking, security, shows, restaurants and hotels, travel agencies, funeral services, laundry, hairdressing, advertising, construction, carpentry/plumbing/electrics). Personal services without that structure belong to the IRP: see [paraguay-income-tax](paraguay-income-tax.md).
- **Transparent legal structures** (art. 4): trusts under Ley 921/1996, Fondos Patrimoniales de Inversión under Ley 5452/2015 and risk-sharing joint ventures are fiscally neutral; their income and costs are taxed in the hands of each beneficiary.
- **Residence** (art. 5): entities formed under Paraguayan law, and foreign entities that set up domicile in Paraguay from their registration in the Public Registry.
- **Exempt income** (art. 25): interest on public debt; education services by recognised entities; dividends received that bear IDU; cooperative surpluses from operations with members; international freight for exports by the carrier issuing the transport document; interest from Banco Central del Paraguay instruments; stock-exchange returns and gains on regulated securities; Fondo Patrimonial de Inversión returns. Exempt persons include foreign official bodies (reciprocity), religious entities (on worship income), non-profit public-benefit entities and political parties, unions and business associations (on their non-business income; the exemption is lost for the listed activities carried on permanently as a business), and social-security entities.

**When to refer.** Refer to a specialist for: banks and finance companies (Ley 861/1996 capital and provisions rules in art. 15.17), insurers, maquila (Ley 7547/2025), projects under the investment incentive regime of Ley 7548/2025, free-zone users, reorganisations, agricultural small-producer special regimes (Decreto 3276/2020 and Decreto 3182 arts. 93 to 98), and the transfer pricing study itself. Individuals: [paraguay-income-tax](paraguay-income-tax.md). IVA returns: [paraguay-iva](paraguay-iva.md). Employer contributions: [paraguay-social-contributions](paraguay-social-contributions.md).

## Rates, bands and thresholds

Quick answer: **10% of net income** in the general regime and in SIMPLE; RESIMPLE pays a fixed monthly amount instead. The regime is chosen by the gross income of the **previous** fiscal year.

## The base

**General regime (IRE RG): start from the accounting result.** The base is the accounting result of the year, adjusted as the law and Decreto 3182 require ([DNIT, IRE General guide](https://www.dnit.gov.py/documents/20123/216504/IRE+General+Cartilla+27.08.24.pdf/90fc2d63-0bc0-1aae-afe3-3c57113eb5b7)). Income, costs and expenses are taken on the accrual basis (art. 7). Gross income (renta bruta) is net sales less the cost of goods or services; costs must be documented and real, and an autofactura (self-issued invoice for a purchase from an informal seller) may not exceed market price (art. 8). Gross income also includes gains on fixed assets, exchange differences, insurance recoveries, gains on shares and on the transfer of a business, rents and royalties, recovered bad debts, debts forgiven, interest (never below the BCP average deposit rate on loans the company makes, except staff advances and cooperative or bank lending) and dividends received (art. 8). Unrealised gains from biological growth, the equity-method revaluation of shareholdings and technical revaluations of fixed assets are taxed only when realised (art. 7).

**Deductions (arts. 14 and 15).** An expense is deductible if it is needed to earn or keep the income, is a real outlay, is documented (with the withholding made where one is due) and, if supported by an autofactura, is not above market price. The listed deductions include taxes and social charges on the business; IVA that is not creditable; general business costs; salaries where social security (IPS) contributions are paid; the aguinaldo (13th salary); non-employee personal services where the provider is an IRP or INR payer; formation costs (up to 5 years); interest, rents and royalties where the creditor is taxed on them and the price is arm's length; extraordinary and theft losses not covered by insurance; bad debts; depreciation and amortisation of purchased intangibles; donations to the State and to recognised public-benefit entities; staff travel with supporting documents. The caps in the rates table then apply.

**Not deductible (art. 16).** The IRE itself; fines; profits set aside for capital or reserves; amortisation of self-generated goodwill; personal spending of owners, partners or their families and drawings on account of profit; IVA that is creditable or refundable; costs of earning untaxed, exempt or foreign-source income; anything that fails the art. 14 test. Costs that serve both taxed and untaxed income are prorated by gross income (art. 17).

**Depreciation (art. 11; Decreto 3182 arts. 30 and 31).** Straight-line on cost less the residual value, over the useful life the regulation sets. Depreciation starts, at the taxpayer's option, in the month or the fiscal year after the asset is brought into use. A used asset takes a life no longer than a new one's. Selected lives from Decreto 3182 art. 31 ([DNIT copy](https://www.dnit.gov.py/documents/47797/47809/Decreto+N%C2%B0+3182-19.pdf/ee6adbdb-f9c2-7a3f-877d-96a0bbbc0a3d)):

| Asset (Decreto 3182 art. 31) | Useful life | Residual value |
|---|---|---|
| Furniture and equipment in general | 5 years | 10% |
| Tools and equipment | 5 years | 10% |
| Machinery and implements | 10 years | 20% |
| Motor vehicles in general | 5 years | 20% |
| Motorcycles, tricycles and bicycles | 2 years | 10% |
| Public passenger transport and utility vehicles | 10 years | 20% |
| Buildings and improvements, urban and rural, excluding land | 30 years | 20% |
| Silos, warehouses and sheds | 20 years | 20% |
| Buildings on third-party property | 20 years, or the shorter contract term | 0% |
| Improvements on third-party property | 10 years, or the shorter contract term | 0% |
| Purchased intangibles (brands, patents, licences) | 5 years | n/a |
| Other assets not listed | 5 years | 20% |

**Inventories (art. 9).** At production or purchase cost; LIFO is not allowed; a change of method needs DNIT authorisation.

**Losses (art. 18).** A tax loss can be set off against the net income of the next five fiscal years, consecutive and uninterrupted from the year after the loss, and in each year only up to 20% of that year's net income. Any part not used within the five years is lost.

**SIMPLE (art. 26; Decreto 3182 arts. 76 to 79).** Calendar year, accrual basis. Gross annual billing is accrued income for the year excluding IVA, and for a SIMPLE payer that must keep full accounts it includes interest on savings accounts in those accounts. Net income is the lower of real income (income less documented, directly related outgoings, with equipment and premises works deducted in full when bought) and 30% of gross billing. A loss is not carried forward. A sole-owner business in SIMPLE is not an IDU taxpayer on its profits (art. 26, last paragraph).

**RESIMPLE (art. 27; Decreto 3182 art. 80).** No base computation: the fixed monthly amount for the prior-year income band, paid quarterly. A RESIMPLE payer is not an IVA taxpayer (art. 27), pays no advances and suffers no IRE withholdings (Decreto 3182 art. 80). It issues the "Boleta RESIMPLE" ([DNIT, RESIMPLE guide](https://www.dnit.gov.py/documents/20123/216504/IRE+Resimple+Cartilla+6.08.24.pdf/a0586c49-bc45-a6df-377d-562d771ddcf1)), which is deductible for a buyer only within the 1% cap. A business that files a nil annual return pays G 20,000 a month the next year (Decreto 3182 art. 80).

**Moving between regimes (art. 29; Decreto 3182 art. 82).** A business whose prior-year income exceeds its regime's ceiling moves up by operation of law and must update its RUC record; otherwise the DNIT changes it and fines it. A business that moved, or chose a regime, stays three fiscal years unless its income rises above that regime's ceiling. Real estate bought, sold or let by the owner of a sole-owner business in SIMPLE or RESIMPLE follows the IRP rules (arts. 30 and 31).

**Advance payments (law art. 24; Decreto 3182 art. 83 as replaced by Decreto 7402/2022).** IRE General and SIMPLE payers pay four advances a year, each 25% of the average IRE of the last three fiscal years, in the first, third, fifth and seventh months after the return deadline. If that average is G 10,000,000 or less, no advances are due. A business with fewer than three closed years uses the previous year's tax. The DNIT credits withholdings and perceptions suffered in the previous year against the advances, and an overpayment at year-end is set off against the next advances ([DNIT, IRE General guide](https://www.dnit.gov.py/documents/20123/216504/IRE+General+Cartilla+27.08.24.pdf/90fc2d63-0bc0-1aae-afe3-3c57113eb5b7)). After paying the first two, a taxpayer can ask to suspend the last two if they already cover the year's tax; if the final return then shows tax due, fines and surcharges run on the shortfall (Decreto 3182 art. 84).

## Deadlines and filing

Portal: the DNIT's Sistema Marangatu (marangatu.set.gov.py), logged in with the Clave de Acceso Confidencial de Usuario. Payment through banks and collection points authorised by the DNIT, electronic payment or card ([DNIT, IRE General guide](https://www.dnit.gov.py/documents/20123/216504/IRE+General+Cartilla+27.08.24.pdf/90fc2d63-0bc0-1aae-afe3-3c57113eb5b7)).

1. **Find the due day.** Every deadline falls on a fixed day of the due month set by the last digit of the RUC, ignoring the check digit (Resolución General 38/2020, DNIT deadlines page): 0 = 7th, 1 = 9th, 2 = 11th, 3 = 13th, 4 = 15th, 5 = 17th, 6 = 19th, 7 = 21st, 8 = 23rd, 9 = 25th. A non-working day moves the deadline to the next working day. A return filed on time but paid late attracts the late-payment fine and surcharge, not the late-filing (contravención) fine ([DNIT, deadlines under Ley 6380](https://www.dnit.gov.py/web/portal-institucional/w/vencimiento-ley-n-6380/19)).
2. **IRE General (Form 500, obligation 700).** Return and payment by the due day of the **fourth** month after year-end: April 2027 for the 2026 year (Decreto 3182 art. 75). The return is due even if there is no taxable income (art. 74). Year-ends other than 31 December are allowed only for sugar mills and agro-industrial cooperatives (30 April) and insurers, brewers and soft-drink makers (30 June), fixed for 5 years (Decreto 3182 art. 15).
3. **Financial statements (obligation 948).** IRE General payers file them by the due day of the fourth month after year-end on the informative-returns calendar (0 = 8th, 1 = 10th, through 9 = 26th). For the year closed 31 December 2025 the DNIT extended this to June 2026 (Resolución General DNIT N° 50/2026). From years closed 31 December 2025 the notes must reconcile accumulated profits and detail reserves (Resolución General DNIT N° 49/2026). Taxpayers required to have an external tax audit (auditoría externa impositiva) file the audit report on the dates the DNIT sets.
4. **IRE SIMPLE (Form 501, obligation 701).** Return and payment by the due day of the **third** month: March 2027 for 2026 (Decreto 3182 art. 76).
5. **IRE RESIMPLE (Form 502, obligation 702).** Annual informative return within the second month (February 2027 for 2026; the DNIT guide gives 26 February and auto-generates a return at the lowest band for non-filers on 27 February). The fixed amounts are paid in March, June, September and December of the year (Decreto 3182 art. 80).
6. **Advances.** Obligation 735 (General) and 736 (SIMPLE). For a December year-end the General advances fall in May, July, September and November, and the SIMPLE advances in April, June, August and October ([DNIT deadlines page](https://www.dnit.gov.py/web/portal-institucional/w/vencimiento-ley-n-6380/19)).
7. **Withholding agents** confirm the DNIT's pro-forma settlement of IRE, IRP, INR and IVA withholdings within the first six calendar days of the month and pay by the 7th (Form 525, obligation 725). After filing, keep the Marangatu acknowledgement; the payment is credited to the RUC current account.
8. **IDU.** The company withholds IDU when profits are put at the owners' disposal: when the shareholders' meeting decides the distribution (S.A.), on the date in the by-laws or, failing that, in the fourth month after year-end (S.R.L.), and in the fourth month after year-end for a sole-owner business unless the profit is reinvested (Ley 6380 art. 44).

## Penalties and interest

The penalties are in Ley N° 125/1991, Libro V, read in the copy of the law the DNIT publishes ([Ley 125/91, arts. 170 to 179](https://www.dnit.gov.py/documents/47797/47809/P+-+Ley+N%C2%BA+125-91.pdf/63cf3ff2-2b45-5920-cfc0-e562ff41ced9)). The four infractions are mora, contravención, omisión de pago and defraudación (art. 170); the mora charges add to any other fine (art. 178).

- **Late payment (mora, art. 171).** A fine on the tax not paid on time: 4% if up to one month late, 6% up to two months, 8% up to three, 10% up to four, 12% up to five, and 14% at five months or more, counted from the day after the due date. On top, a monthly surcharge or interest computed day by day until payment, at the rate the Executive fixes every four months (capped at the bank discount rate plus 50%). A return filed on time but paid late attracts only these mora charges, not the late-filing fine ([DNIT deadlines page](https://www.dnit.gov.py/web/portal-institucional/w/vencimiento-ley-n-6380/19)). Late advances carry the same mora (Decreto 3182 art. 84).
- **Late or missing return and other formal breaches (contravención, art. 176).** A fine between G 50,000 and G 1,000,000 in the 1991 text; both limits are updated each year by decree for consumer-price inflation (art. 179), so use the decree in force.
- **Underpayment without intent (omisión de pago, art. 177).** Up to 50% of the tax omitted.
- **Fraud (defraudación, arts. 172 to 175).** One to three times the tax evaded, graded by repetition, recidivism, the size of the loss and the taxpayer's conduct; a voluntary correction before any inspection mitigates. Intent is presumed, among others, where the books contradict the return, required books are missing or more than 90 days behind, or a withholding agent pays over withheld tax more than 30 days late (arts. 173 and 174).

## Cross-border points

- **Foreign-source income of a resident company** is taxed unless it bore a foreign income tax at 10% or more (art. 6). Foreign income tax on the same income is credited, up to the Paraguayan tax on that income, also under a tax treaty (art. 134). Costs of earning foreign-source income that is not taxed are not deductible (art. 16.7).
- **Payments to non-residents.** The payer withholds INR at 15% on the presumed net income of art. 75 and is jointly liable (art. 77). Examples: 30% of the gross for interest on non-related bank or multilateral loans, international transport and container hire; 70% for personal services; 100% for any amount paid to partners, a head office or related entities. The INR is final for the recipient. Expenses abroad are fully deductible for the payer only if INR was withheld; otherwise they fall under the 1% of gross income cap (art. 15.18).
- **Dividends and branch profits to non-residents** bear IDU at 15% (art. 43.2); the tax is final for the non-resident (art. 41). Presumed distributions include loans to owners (except by lenders within 2% of their loan book), a cash shortfall above 10% of the cash account and private use of company assets (art. 40).
- **Transfer pricing (arts. 35 to 39; Decreto 4644/2020).** Operations with related parties abroad, and domestic related-party operations where one side is exempt or out of scope, must be at arm's length. Operations with residents of low or nil tax jurisdictions, free-zone users and maquila companies are presumed related. A low or nil tax jurisdiction taxes the income at an effective or nominal rate below Paraguay's on similar operations **and** has no information-exchange agreement in force with Paraguay (Decreto 4644 art. 5 as replaced by Decreto 7402/2022). The comparable uncontrolled price method comes first; listed commodities exported are priced at the quoted market price on the shipment date (art. 38). A taxpayer above G 10,000,000,000 of prior-year gross income keeps a technical study by a professional on the DNIT register (art. 39).
- **Treaties.** Paraguay's double tax conventions override these rates where they apply; Ley N° 7271/2024 approved the convention with Spain. Check whether a convention is in force for the year before applying it.

## Worked examples

### Example 1: sole-owner business, SIMPLE or General (a decision)

Ana runs a hardware shop as an empresa unipersonal. Her 2025 accrued income, excluding IVA, was G 1,500,000,000, so she may choose SIMPLE for 2026 (the ceiling is G 2,000,000,000). In 2026 she bills G 1,500,000,000 excluding IVA. Her documented costs of goods and expenses are G 1,100,000,000, and in January she buys a machine for G 200,000,000.

- **SIMPLE.** Real income: 1,500,000,000 less 1,100,000,000 less the whole 200,000,000 machine = G 200,000,000. Presumed income: 30% of 1,500,000,000 = G 450,000,000. She is taxed on the lower, G 200,000,000: IRE **G 20,000,000**, due in March 2027.
- **General.** The machine is depreciated: 200,000,000 less 20% residual, over 10 years = G 16,000,000 a year. Net income 1,500,000,000 less 1,100,000,000 less 16,000,000 = G 384,000,000: IRE **G 38,400,000**, due in April 2027. On top, a sole-owner business in the General regime is an IDU taxpayer: profit not reinvested is treated as distributed in the fourth month and bears 8%.
- **The figure that decides it** is real margin against 30% of billing. In a variant year with no machine and costs of G 900,000,000, real income is G 600,000,000 but SIMPLE caps the base at G 450,000,000: IRE G 45,000,000 against G 60,000,000 in the General regime. SIMPLE never gives a higher base than the General regime for the same documented costs, because it takes the lower of the two; what it gives up is the loss carry-forward. Once chosen, she stays three years (art. 29).

### Example 2: S.A. in the General regime, related-party interest cap, loss cap and advances

Beta S.A. (calendar year) has 2026 net income of G 1,000,000,000 before deducting G 400,000,000 of arm's-length interest paid to its foreign parent. It has an unused 2023 tax loss of G 500,000,000. Its IRE for 2024 and 2025 was G 37,000,000 and G 51,000,000.

- **Interest cap (art. 15.23).** 30% of net income before the interest: 30% of 1,000,000,000 = G 300,000,000 deductible; G 100,000,000 is not. Net income after interest: G 700,000,000.
- **Loss (art. 18).** The 2023 loss is within its five years (2024 to 2028). The 2026 set-off is capped at 20% of G 700,000,000 = G 140,000,000; G 360,000,000 of the loss carries on to 2027 and 2028.
- **IRE.** Base G 560,000,000 at 10% = **G 56,000,000**, filed on Form 500 and paid in April 2027.
- **INR on the interest.** Interest to a related lender is not in the 30% bracket of art. 75.1(a), so the presumed net income is 100% (art. 75.6): Beta withheld 15% of G 400,000,000 = G 60,000,000 when it paid or credited the interest, whatever the deductible part.
- **Advances for 2027.** Average of 2024 to 2026 tax: (37,000,000 + 51,000,000 + 56,000,000) / 3 = G 48,000,000, above G 10,000,000. Four advances of G 12,000,000, in May, July, September and November 2027, less any 2026 withholdings and perceptions credited by the DNIT.
