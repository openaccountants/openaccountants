# Sri Lanka quarterly income tax instalments (year of assessment 2026/27)

Built from the law: not yet approved by an accountant. Tax year: year of assessment 2026/27, 1 April 2026 to 31 March 2027.

## Scope and who this is for

- **In:** any "instalment payer" under section 90(1) of the Inland Revenue Act No. 24 of 2017 (IRA): a person who derives or expects to derive assessable income in the year from a business, investment or other income, or from an employment where the employer is not required to withhold under section 83 or 83A. This covers resident and non-resident companies with Sri Lanka tax and individuals with business income not covered by APIT.
- **Out of the instalment base:** gains from the realisation of an investment asset (proviso to section 90(1)). Capital gains tax is paid separately: see `sri-lanka-capital-gains-tax`.
- **Not here:** the tax rates themselves (`sri-lanka-income-tax`, `sri-lanka-corporate-tax`), withholding by payers (`sri-lanka-withholding-tax`), APIT on salaries (`sri-lanka-payroll`).
- **What changed for 2026/27:** the Inland Revenue (Amendment) Act No. 11 of 2026 (certified 3 June 2026) replaced the estimate basis with a prior-year basis for years of assessment from 1 April 2026 (amended section 90(3), operative 1 April 2026), restricted the statement of estimated tax (section 91) and its revision (section 92) to years before 1 April 2026, added tax withheld in the year as a credit (item C(ba)), and allowed foreign tax credit to be taken into account (new section 90(6)).

## Ask the client first

1. Is the client an instalment payer: business, investment or other income, or employment income with no APIT withheld?
2. Tax payable on taxable income under section 2(1)(a) for the immediately preceding year of assessment (2025/26). Was there any taxable income that year?
3. Does the client expect 2026/27 taxable income lower than 2025/26? If so, an estimate under the Commissioner-General's procedure replaces the prior-year figure.
4. Instalments already paid this year, and tax withheld from receipts included in income (withholding certificates).
5. Any foreign income tax paid or reasonably expected to be paid in the year (section 80 credit, new section 90(6)).

### Steps

1. Confirm the client is an instalment payer (section 90(1)). Leave out investment-asset gains.
2. Take A: the section 2(1)(a) tax payable for 2025/26. If 2025/26 had no taxable income, or 2026/27 income is expected to be lower, use the estimated 2026/27 tax computed under the Commissioner-General's procedure, and keep the working.
3. Optionally reduce the tax for foreign tax credit under section 80, but only for foreign tax paid or reasonably expected to be paid in the period (section 90(6)).
4. Take C: instalments already paid this year before the due date, plus tax withheld under Division II before the due date from receipts included in income.
5. Divide (A minus C) by B, the instalments left including this one (4 at 15 August, 3 at 15 November, 2 at 15 February, 1 at 15 May).
6. Pay by the due date through the IRD e-Services portal or an authorised bank. Pay within 14 days to avoid the 10% penalty; interest at 1.5% a month runs from the due date regardless.
7. At year end, all instalments and withholding credits are set against the final tax in the return; any balance is paid with the return (`sri-lanka-income-tax`, `sri-lanka-corporate-tax`). Excess may be refunded under section 150.

## Boundary and exception table

| Situation | Treatment |
| --- | --- |
| Capital gain on an investment asset expected | Excluded from the instalment base (section 90(1) proviso) |
| Employee whose employer withholds APIT | Not an instalment payer for that employment income |
| No taxable income in 2025/26 | Use the 2026/27 estimate as A |
| 2026/27 income expected lower than 2025/26 | Estimate may be used as A, per the Commissioner-General's procedure |
| Instalment paid 10 days late | No 10% penalty (within 14 days); interest still runs from the due date |
| Statement of estimated tax (SET) for 2026/27 | Section 91 now applies only to years before 1 April 2026; do not prepare a 2026/27 SET under section 91 unless the IRD specifies a form |
| Foreign tax not yet paid and not expected in the year | Cannot be taken into account in the instalment |
| Overpaid instalments | Credited against the final liability; excess refunded or carried forward |

## Worked cases

### Case 1: company on the prior-year basis

Company, 2025/26 tax payable Rs. 1,200,000. No withholding credits. First instalment on 15 August 2026, 4 instalments left.

```oa-check
name: Company first instalment, prior-year basis, YA 2026/27
inputs:
  prior_year_tax: 1200000
  credits: 0
  remaining: 4
steps:
  - instalment: (prior_year_tax - credits) / remaining
expected:
  instalment: 300000
```

### Case 2: decision, prior-year basis or lower estimate

Same company, but trade has fallen and the estimated 2026/27 tax is Rs. 600,000. Tax of Rs. 50,000 was withheld from its interest before 15 August. The prior-year basis gives Rs. 287,500; the estimate gives Rs. 137,500. The estimate is allowed only because lower income is expected, and the working must follow the Commissioner-General's procedure. If the company used the estimate wrongly and owed the prior-year instalment, paying it more than 14 days late adds a 10% penalty on the unpaid amount.

```oa-check
name: Prior-year basis against lower estimate, first instalment
inputs:
  prior_year_tax: 1200000
  estimate: 600000
  withheld: 50000
  remaining: 4
steps:
  - prior_basis: (prior_year_tax - withheld) / remaining
  - estimate_basis: (estimate - withheld) / remaining
  - saving_now: prior_basis - estimate_basis
  - penalty_if_prior_unpaid: prior_basis * 0.10
expected:
  prior_basis: 287500
  estimate_basis: 137500
  saving_now: 150000
  penalty_if_prior_unpaid: 28750
```

## When to refuse or refer

- The client wants to use a lower estimate without a documented basis: refer; the estimate must follow the Commissioner-General's procedure.
- Instalment payer status is unclear (mixed employment and business income, Port City business of strategic importance): refer.
- Arrears, notices or Magistrate's Court recovery under the new section 163(4A): refer to a Sri Lankan tax professional.
- Never present this as a filed or final position.
