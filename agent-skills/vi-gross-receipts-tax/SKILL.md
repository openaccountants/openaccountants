# US Virgin Islands: gross receipts tax, 2026

> **Source-cited draft.** Built from the Virgin Islands Bureau of Internal Revenue (BIR) Form 720 V.I. (Rev. 08/2026) and Form 720-B (Rev. 10/2026) with their instructions, and the BIR forms directory, read on 2026-11-13. The underlying statute (Title 33 V.I.C. sections 42 and 43) was not read. No accountant has reviewed it. All outputs must be reviewed by a qualified tax professional before filing.

**Answer first.** The USVI charges a **5%** gross receipts tax on total receipts from business carried on in the USVI, with no deduction for any cost or expense. A business with annual receipts **above USD 225,000** files **Form 720 V.I. every month**, within 30 calendar days after the month ends. A business with **USD 225,000 or less** files **Form 720-B once a year**, due **30 January** after the year, and pays 5% only on receipts above **USD 9,000 in each month**. The exemption is applied month by month, never pooled for the year. Late payment costs 5% a month (up to 25%) plus 1% a month interest.

## Scope and who this is for

In scope: a sole proprietor, partnership or corporation with receipts from business in the USVI, for periods in 2026: which return, the monthly computation, the standard exemption, government up-front withholding, deadlines, and late-payment charges as the forms state them.

## Ask the client first

1. **Total gross receipts for each month of 2026**, cash or accrual (the form asks which), and the total for the year.
2. **Business type** (sole proprietor, partnership, corporation), EIN or SSN, and the principal business activity code from the back of the form.
3. **Does the owner run more than one business?** A sole proprietor files one consolidated return.
4. **Any exemption certificate or code** other than the standard exemption (EDC, enterprise zone and the others listed on the form).
5. **Up-front gross receipts tax withheld by the VI Government** on contracts, with the withholding documents.
6. **Returns and payments already made** for 2026, and any late months.

The BIR forms directory still lists older monthly forms, including a Rev. 10/2008 version at 4% for receipts over USD 120,000 and a "4.5%" Rev. 05/2011 version. They are not the current forms; use the Rev. 08/2026 Form 720 V.I. and the Rev. 10/2026 Form 720-B.

### Step 1: which return

1. Annual receipts more than USD 225,000: Form 720 V.I. for every month.
2. USD 225,000 or less: one Form 720-B for the year, with a row for each month. Businesses with several locations or divisions file one Form 720-B.

### Step 2: each month's tax

3. **Line 1, gross receipts** for the month: all receipts from business in the USVI, cash or accrued, with no reduction for cost of goods sold, services or any expense.
4. **Line 2, exemption.** For a business under USD 225,000 a year, the standard USD 9,000, but never more than the month's receipts. Other exemption codes need a certificate (see the refusal table). The front of Form 720 V.I. still labels line 2 "Standard $5,000 or $9,000", but its instructions give the USD 9,000 only to businesses under USD 225,000 a year: a monthly filer claims a standard exemption only with BIR confirmation, and the USD 5,000 figure is not explained anywhere on the form.
5. **Line 3, taxable receipts** = line 1 minus line 2. **Line 4, tax** = line 3 times 5%.
6. **Line 5,** subtract up-front gross receipts tax withheld by the VI Government; **line 6** is the adjusted tax due.
7. **Lines 7 and 8 if paid late:** penalty of 5% of line 6 for each month late, at most 25%, and interest of 1% of line 6 per month. **Line 9** subtracts credits from overpayments. **Line 10** is the total due.
8. **No tax for the period:** still file, with "0" or "NONE" in the total.

### Step 3: file and pay

9. Send the return and payment to the BIR (St. Thomas 00802 or St. Croix 00820) by the deadline: 30 calendar days after the month (postmarked or submitted), or 30 January for Form 720-B.

## Boundary and exception table

| Situation | Treatment |
| --- | --- |
| Annual receipts exactly USD 225,000 | Annual filer (Form 720-B "or less"); but the exemption wording on Form 720 V.I. and the Form 720-B instructions says "less than" or "under" USD 225,000, so whether the USD 9,000 exemption applies at exactly USD 225,000 is not settled: ask the BIR |
| A month with receipts below USD 9,000 | Exemption equals the receipts; tax zero; unused exemption does not carry to other months |
| Operating costs of the business | Never deducted |
| Sole proprietor with two businesses | One consolidated return; do not claim the exemption twice without authority |
| VI Government withheld tax up front on a contract | Deduct it on line 5 with the documents |
| Receipts crossed USD 225,000 during the year | Monthly filing applies; ask the BIR how to handle months already treated as annual |
| Medicare and Medicaid receipts | Separate Form 720MED; refer |

### Defaults when a fact is missing

| Missing fact | Default, and flag it |
| --- | --- |
| Annual receipts | Monthly filing until shown to be USD 225,000 or less |
| Exemption certificate other than standard | Standard exemption only, or none for a monthly filer |
| Withholding documents | Do not claim line 5 |
| Cash or accrual | Use the method of the books, and tick it on the form |

### Red flags to raise with the client

| Flag | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| Tax worked out on profit, not receipts | Understated tax |
| One annual USD 108,000 exemption claimed instead of USD 9,000 a month | Understated tax in high months |
| Missing zero returns for quiet months | A return is required for every period |
| Charging customers a separate "gross receipts tax" line | Authority to pass the tax on was not checked; refer |

## Worked cases

**Case 1, the monthly exemption on Form 720-B.** A business under USD 225,000 a year had receipts of USD 8,000 in each of eleven months and USD 20,000 in one month: USD 108,000 for the year. The eleven quiet months have no tax. The busy month has taxable receipts of USD 20,000 minus USD 9,000 = USD 11,000 and tax of USD 550. Pooling a USD 108,000 exemption for the year would wrongly give zero.

```oa-check
name: Monthly USD 9,000 exemption on Form 720-B (2026)
inputs:
  quiet_month: 8000
  quiet_months: 11
  busy_month: 20000
steps:
  - tax_quiet_each: max(quiet_month - min(quiet_month, 9000), 0) * 0.05
  - tax_busy: max(busy_month - 9000, 0) * 0.05
  - annual_receipts: quiet_month * quiet_months + busy_month
  - annual_tax: tax_quiet_each * quiet_months + tax_busy
  - wrong_pooled_tax: max(annual_receipts - 9000 * 12, 0) * 0.05
expected:
  tax_busy: 550.00
  annual_receipts: 108000
  annual_tax: 550.00
  wrong_pooled_tax: 0
```

**Case 2, a monthly filer, government withholding and a late payment.** A contractor with receipts well above USD 225,000 a year has USD 40,000 of receipts in a month, no exemption, and USD 500 withheld up front by the VI Government. Tax is USD 2,000; adjusted tax USD 1,500. Deducting USD 30,000 of costs to tax only USD 10,000 would be wrong. The payment is two months late: penalty 5% times 2 times USD 1,500 = USD 150, interest 1% times 2 times USD 1,500 = USD 30, total due USD 1,680. How partial months are counted was not confirmed (see below).

```oa-check
name: Monthly Form 720 V.I. with withholding, paid two months late (2026)
inputs:
  receipts: 40000
  exemption: 0
  withheld: 500
  months_late: 2
steps:
  - tax: (receipts - exemption) * 0.05
  - adjusted: tax - withheld
  - penalty: adjusted * min(0.05 * months_late, 0.25)
  - interest: adjusted * 0.01 * months_late
  - total_due: adjusted + penalty + interest
expected:
  tax: 2000.00
  adjusted: 1500.00
  penalty: 150.00
  interest: 30.00
  total_due: 1680.00
```

## Filing and payment

Monthly filers: Form 720 V.I. and payment within 30 calendar days after each month of 2026 (the December 2026 return by 30 January 2027). Annual filers: Form 720-B for 2026 and payment by **30 January 2027**. File a return even when no tax is due. Send to the BIR, St. Thomas, VI 00802, or St. Croix, VI 00820.

## Completion checklist

- Filing category decided on the year's receipts against USD 225,000.
- Every month computed separately, with the USD 9,000 exemption capped at the month's receipts.
- No expenses deducted.
- Government up-front withholding supported by documents.
- Zero returns filed for quiet periods; one consolidated return per sole proprietor.
