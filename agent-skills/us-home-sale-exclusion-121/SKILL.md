# US home sale exclusion under section 121, tax year 2026

> **Source-cited draft.** Built from 26 U.S.C. 121 and 1(h) and IRS Pub. 523 (2025), read on 2026-11-13. No accountant has reviewed it. All outputs must be reviewed by a qualified tax professional before filing.

## Scope and who this is for

In scope: an individual (or a married couple filing jointly) who sells, in 2026, a home that was their main home at some point, and wants to know how much of the gain is excluded, how much is taxed, and at what kind of rate. Covers the full exclusion, the joint-return rules, the surviving spouse rule, the partial exclusion, depreciation after 6 May 1997 and nonqualified use.

## Ask the client first

1. **Dates:** when the home was bought, when the client moved in, when they moved out (if before the sale), and the sale (closing) date. Use the date on Form 1099-S if there is one.
2. **Each spouse:** filing status for 2026, which spouse is on the title, and which spouse lived there. For a widow or widower: the date of the spouse's death.
3. **Earlier sales:** did either spouse exclude gain on another home sold in the 2 years before this sale?
4. **Other use:** was the home ever rented out, used for a business, or left empty, and when (any period after 2008 can count against the exclusion)?
5. **Depreciation:** was depreciation ever allowed or allowable (a rental, a home office on Form 8829)? Get the amounts by year.
6. **Basis and selling costs:** purchase price, closing costs at purchase, improvements, any casualty loss or energy credit that reduced basis, and the selling costs (commission, transfer fees).
7. **Reason for selling early:** if the 2-year tests are not met, was the main reason a new job at least 50 miles farther away, a health reason, or an unforeseen event?

### Step 1: the gain

1. **Amount realized** = sale price minus selling costs (commission, transfer taxes paid by the seller, legal fees).
2. **Adjusted basis** = purchase price plus purchase costs plus improvements, minus depreciation allowed or allowable, casualty losses deducted and any basis-reducing credits.
3. **Gain** = amount realized minus adjusted basis. A loss on a personal home is not deductible and needs no exclusion. Source: [IRS Pub. 523 (2025)](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/p523.pdf).

### Step 2: the tests

4. **Ownership:** owned for periods totalling 2 years or more in the 5 years ending on the sale date.
5. **Use:** lived in it as the main home for periods totalling 2 years (730 days) in the same 5 years. The two periods need not overlap; short absences such as holidays count as use.
6. **Once every 2 years:** no other home sale in the 2 years before this one on which the exclusion was used. Source: [26 U.S.C. 121(a), (b)(3)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title26/html/USCODE-2024-title26-subtitleA-chap1-subchapB-partIII-sec121.htm).
7. **Joint return:** USD 500,000 if either spouse meets the ownership test, both meet the use test and neither is barred by the 2-year rule. Otherwise each spouse's own limit is worked out as if unmarried and the two are added, with each spouse treated as owning for any period either spouse owned it. Source: [26 U.S.C. 121(b)(2)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title26/html/USCODE-2024-title26-subtitleA-chap1-subchapB-partIII-sec121.htm).
8. **Surviving spouse:** an unmarried widow or widower keeps the USD 500,000 limit if the sale is no later than 2 years after the death and the joint test was met just before the death; the deceased spouse's ownership and use count as theirs. Source: [26 U.S.C. 121(b)(4), (d)(2)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title26/html/USCODE-2024-title26-subtitleA-chap1-subchapB-partIII-sec121.htm).
9. **Divorce:** time a former spouse owned the home (transfer under section 1041) counts as ownership, and time the former spouse lived there under a divorce or separation instrument counts as use. Source: [26 U.S.C. 121(d)(3)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title26/html/USCODE-2024-title26-subtitleA-chap1-subchapB-partIII-sec121.htm).

### Step 3: the partial exclusion (tests failed)

10. If the tests in steps 4 to 6 fail, and the main reason for the sale was a change of place of employment, health, or an unforeseen circumstance, the limit is reduced, not lost: limit times (the shorter of the qualifying ownership-and-use time in the 5 years, or the time since the last excluded sale) divided by 2 years. Work it in days (divide by 730) or months (divide by 24). Source: [26 U.S.C. 121(c)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title26/html/USCODE-2024-title26-subtitleA-chap1-subchapB-partIII-sec121.htm).
11. Qualifying reasons, from Pub. 523: a new job location at least 50 miles farther from the home than the old one; a move for diagnosis or treatment, or to care for a sick family member, or on a doctor's advice; or an unforeseeable event such as destruction of the home, death, divorce, multiple births from one pregnancy, becoming eligible for unemployment, or no longer being able to pay basic living expenses. The same events for a spouse or co-owner count. Source: [IRS Pub. 523 (2025)](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/p523.pdf).

### Step 4: depreciation first, then nonqualified use, then the limit

12. **Depreciation after 6 May 1997** (from renting the home or a home office) is taken out of the gain first and is taxed; the exclusion never covers it. It is unrecaptured section 1250 gain, taxed at up to 25 percent. Source: [26 U.S.C. 121(d)(6)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title26/html/USCODE-2024-title26-subtitleA-chap1-subchapB-partIII-sec121.htm), [26 U.S.C. 1(h)(1)(E)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title26/html/USCODE-2024-title26-subtitleA-chap1-subchapA-partI-sec1.htm).
13. **Nonqualified use:** the rest of the gain is split in the ratio of nonqualified use (periods after 2008 when it was not the main home of the taxpayer or spouse) to total ownership. That share is taxed. Not counted as nonqualified use: time after the client last lived there within the 5-year window (moving out before selling), up to 10 years of qualified official extended duty, and up to 2 years of temporary absence for work, health or unforeseen reasons. Source: [26 U.S.C. 121(b)(5)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title26/html/USCODE-2024-title26-subtitleA-chap1-subchapB-partIII-sec121.htm).
14. **Exclusion** = the smaller of the remaining qualified gain and the limit (full or partial). Any gain above the limit is taxable long-term capital gain if the home was held more than a year.

### Step 5: reporting

15. If all the gain is excluded and no Form 1099-S was received, the sale is not reported. Otherwise report it on Form 8949 and Schedule D (Form 1040), showing the exclusion as an adjustment; depreciation gain goes through the unrecaptured section 1250 gain worksheet. Source: [IRS Pub. 523 (2025)](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/p523.pdf).
16. The taxable gain may also be net investment income for the 3.8% tax; the excluded part is not. Check the client's modified adjusted gross income in `us-federal-income-tax-return-1040`.

## Boundary and exception table

| Situation | Treatment |
| --- | --- |
| Lived there 2 of the last 5 years but rented it out before that, before 2009 | Pre-2009 rental is not nonqualified use; only depreciation after 6 May 1997 stays taxable |
| Moved out and rented the home, sold within 3 years of moving out | Still meets the 2-of-5 use test; the time after moving out is not nonqualified use; depreciation during the rental is taxed |
| Bought as a rental after 2008, moved in later | Rental years are nonqualified use: that share of the gain is taxed |
| Home office inside the living area | No split of the property; only the depreciation after 6 May 1997 is taxed |
| Separate business or rental unit (a duplex half, a detached unit) | Treat as two sales: the exclusion applies only to the home part (Pub. 523) |
| Spouse who did not live there, joint return | USD 500,000 fails; each spouse's own limit added (often USD 250,000 plus zero) |
| Second exclusion within 2 years | Not allowed, unless the partial exclusion applies, measured from the earlier sale |
| Home destroyed or condemned | Treated as a sale; section 1033 may defer the remaining gain |
| Cooperative apartment | Ownership test on the shares, use test on the apartment |
| Moved into care (physically or mentally unable to live alone) | Time in a licensed care facility can count as use if the client lived there 1 of the 5 years; refer for the detail (121(d)(7)) |
| Gain from a 1031 exchange property later used as a home | 5-year holding rule of 121(d)(10): refer |

### Defaults when a fact is missing

| Missing fact | Default, and flag it |
| --- | --- |
| Exact move-in or move-out date | Count only full months the client can show (driver's licence, voter card, tax returns) |
| Depreciation on an earlier rental | Treat depreciation allowable as taken, even if not claimed; ask for the rental returns |
| Reason for an early sale | No partial exclusion until the reason is documented |
| Spouse's use of the home | Assume single limits until confirmed |
| Improvements | Leave out of basis until receipts exist |

### Red flags to raise with the client

| Flag | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| Sale a few weeks or months short of 2 years | Waiting may save the whole tax (Case 1) |
| Any rental or business use after 2008 | Nonqualified use and depreciation reduce the exclusion |
| Gain above USD 250,000 single or USD 500,000 joint | Taxable gain, possibly the 3.8% tax and state tax |
| Form 1099-S received | The IRS expects to see the sale on the return |
| Another home sold in the last 2 years | Exclusion barred or reduced |

## Worked cases

**Case 1, a decision: sell now or wait to pass the 2-year test?** A single client bought and moved into a home on 1 March 2025. She has an offer now, in November 2026, after 20 months of ownership and use. Her gain is USD 180,000. Her other taxable income is USD 80,000, so the gain falls in the 15% long-term band (see the 2026 bands in `us-federal-income-tax-return-1040`).

Sell now with no qualifying reason: no exclusion, tax of USD 180,000 x 15% = USD 27,000 (before any 3.8% tax and state tax). Wait until on or after 1 March 2027: both tests are met, the full USD 250,000 limit covers the whole gain, and the federal tax is zero. If instead she must sell now because of a new job 60 miles farther away, the partial limit is USD 250,000 x 20 / 24 = USD 208,333.33, which still covers the gain. Recommend waiting about 4 months unless she has a qualifying reason or the price risk is larger than USD 27,000.

```oa-check
name: Sell now or wait for the 2-year test (2026)
inputs:
  gain: 180000
  months_owned_and_used: 20
  limit: 250000
  ltcg_rate: 0.15
steps:
  - tax_if_sold_now_no_reason: gain * ltcg_rate
  - partial_limit: limit * months_owned_and_used / 24
  - taxable_if_job_move: max(gain - partial_limit, 0)
  - taxable_if_wait: max(gain - limit, 0)
  - saving_by_waiting: tax_if_sold_now_no_reason - taxable_if_wait * ltcg_rate
expected:
  tax_if_sold_now_no_reason: 27000.00
  partial_limit: 208333.33
  taxable_if_job_move: 0.00
  taxable_if_wait: 0.00
  saving_by_waiting: 27000.00
```

**Case 2, rental years and depreciation, joint return.** A married couple bought a house on 1 January 2017 and rented it out for 2017 and 2018, taking USD 20,000 of depreciation. They lived in it as their main home from 1 January 2019 until they sold it on 31 December 2026. Total ownership: 10 years. Gain: USD 400,000.

Depreciation first: USD 20,000 is taxed as unrecaptured section 1250 gain (up to 25 percent). Remaining gain USD 380,000. Nonqualified use: 2 of 10 years (both after 2008), so USD 380,000 x 2 / 10 = USD 76,000 is taxed as long-term gain. Qualified gain USD 304,000 is under the USD 500,000 limit and is excluded. Taxable in all: USD 96,000, reported on Form 8949 and Schedule D.

```oa-check
name: Depreciation and nonqualified use, joint return (2026)
inputs:
  gain: 400000
  depreciation_after_may_1997: 20000
  years_nonqualified: 2
  years_owned: 10
  limit: 500000
steps:
  - remaining_gain: gain - depreciation_after_may_1997
  - nonqualified_gain: remaining_gain * years_nonqualified / years_owned
  - qualified_gain: remaining_gain - nonqualified_gain
  - excluded: min(qualified_gain, limit)
  - taxable_total: gain - excluded
expected:
  remaining_gain: 380000.00
  nonqualified_gain: 76000.00
  qualified_gain: 304000.00
  excluded: 304000.00
  taxable_total: 96000.00
```

## Filing and payment

A taxable gain is reported on the 2026 Form 1040 (Form 8949 and Schedule D), due **15 April 2027**. A large gain during 2026 may need an estimated tax payment: see `us-quarterly-estimated-tax`.

## Completion checklist

- Ownership and use periods dated from documents, in months or days.
- No excluded sale in the 2 years before.
- Joint limit checked spouse by spouse.
- Depreciation after 6 May 1997 taken out first and taxed.
- Nonqualified use after 2008 measured, with the exceptions applied.
- Partial exclusion only with a documented work, health or unforeseen reason.
- Form 1099-S received or not, and Form 8949 filed when needed.
