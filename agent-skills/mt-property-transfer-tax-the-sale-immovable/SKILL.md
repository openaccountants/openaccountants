# Property transfer tax and stamp duty on the sale of immovable property in Malta

## Property transfer tax and stamp duty on the sale of immovable property in Malta

A sale of immovable property situated in Malta attracts two separate charges: property transfer tax on the seller under the Income Tax Act, normally collected by the notary as a final withholding tax on the transfer value, and duty on documents and transfers on the buyer, administered by the Property Tax Directorate of the Malta Tax and Customs Administration. The Malta Tax and Customs Administration states that "The seller must pay capital transfer taxes on the transfer of any property located in Malta, whilst stamp duty is paid by the buyer." The charge arises on the deed of transfer, with a provisional stage at promise of sale. Figures are for tax year 2026. The official pages carrying these figures state their own effective dates rather than a tax year (for example, the 8% rate is stated as applying "With effect from 1st January, 2015"); where a page states its own period, that period is given in the tables below.

## Who this is for

This Guide is for a transferor selling immovable property situated in Malta by deed inter vivos, and for the transferee who pays the duty on that deed. It covers the final withholding tax rates the Malta Tax and Customs Administration publishes for such transfers, and the provisional and final duty steps the same authority publishes for the buyer.

A later sale of property that the seller inherited or received by donation is covered: its rates are in the tables below. It does not cover the transfer causa mortis itself, declarations causa mortis, share transfers, or the making of a donation, all of which the Malta Tax and Customs Administration deals with under separate pages and separate rules. It also does not cover the assignment of a right obtained under a promise of sale: the authority states that such an assignment "is not regulated by the property transfers tax regime".

## The method, step by step

- **Step 1 : Engage the notary** : Engage the notary. For a transfer of property to take place in Malta a notary must be engaged to process property searches and file the necessary deeds; the notary is usually chosen by the buyer and is also engaged to process provisional duty payments on behalf of the buyer. The seller may use an estate agent to find a prospective buyer, and the parties then delegate a notary.  _([Buying Property](https://mtca.gov.mt/personal-tax/property-taxes/buying-property); [Selling Property](https://mtca.gov.mt/personal-tax/property-taxes/selling-property))_
- **Step 2 : Register the promise of sale and pay provisional duty** : Register the promise of sale and pay provisional duty. Once the promise of sale is signed it is presented to the Property Tax Directorate within the period in the table above, and the purchaser pays the provisional duty at that stage. The notice is given on form DDT3, "Notice of Promise of Sale / Transfer of Immovable Property".  _([Buying Property](https://mtca.gov.mt/personal-tax/property-taxes/buying-property), [Promise of Sale](https://mtca.gov.mt/personal-tax/property-taxes/promise-of-sale); [Downloads](https://mtca.gov.mt/personal-tax/property-taxes/downloads))_
- **Step 3 : Obtain the provisional duty receipt** : Obtain the provisional duty receipt. A receipt for provisional stamp duty is issued to the taxpayer once payment is received by the Property Tax Directorate, confirming that the notary has submitted a copy of the promise of sale and that provisional duty has been paid by the buyer.  _([Buying Property](https://mtca.gov.mt/personal-tax/property-taxes/buying-property))_
- **Step 4 : Decide, before the deed, the seller's rate and any election** : Decide, before the deed, which article 5A rate or exemption applies, and whether a deed election is needed. The general election out of article 5A is closed for 2026 deeds. Only the specific exclusions in the table above remain. Where one applies, the seller pays 7% provisional tax within fifteen days and declares the gain in the tax return. For inherited or donated property, decide whether to keep the default 12% of the gain or elect the transfer-value rate. Every election is a declaration to the notary recorded in the deed.  _([ITA art. 5A(3), (5)(b)](https://legislation.mt/getpdf/69d8a9187da37f0580d5140c); [ITMA art. 43(1)(b)](https://legislation.mt/getpdf/69c65a5c7da36921dcc4f04b))_
- **Step 5 : Publish the deed and submit the DDT1** : Publish the deed and submit the DDT1. Upon signing of the contract the notary publishing the deed submits the DDT1 form at the Property Tax Directorate together with site-plans, a copy of the Public Registry note, the stamp duty payment due by the buyer, the capital gains tax payment due by the seller, and Schedule 8 for residential property only. Schedule 8 is listed as "DDT - Eight Schedule – Physical Attributes of Immovable Property".  _([General Information on Duty](https://mtca.gov.mt/personal-tax/property-taxes/general-information-on-duty); [Downloads](https://mtca.gov.mt/personal-tax/property-taxes/downloads))_
- **Step 6 : Await the receipts and any valuation** : Await the receipts and any valuation. Receipts are normally issued within the period in the table above; at this stage an internal departmental board decides whether an architect is sent to inspect the property to establish its market value, subject to the tolerance in the table above.  _([General Information on Duty](https://mtca.gov.mt/personal-tax/property-taxes/general-information-on-duty))_
- **Step 7 : Deal with any claim or assessment** : Deal with any claim or assessment. Where the architect adds value, the buyer's claim includes the duty due plus the additional duty (penalty), and the vendor is charged the additional duty (penalty) only; unpaid claims attract interest at the rate and from the point set out in the table above.  _([Buying Property](https://mtca.gov.mt/personal-tax/property-taxes/buying-property), [General Information on Duty](https://mtca.gov.mt/personal-tax/property-taxes/general-information-on-duty))_
- **Step 8 : Object in writing if the assessment is disputed** : Object in writing if the assessment is disputed, within the period in the table above, specifying the valid grounds; if no agreement upon objection is reached, the Commissioner issues a Refusal.  _([General Information on Duty](https://mtca.gov.mt/personal-tax/property-taxes/general-information-on-duty))_

## Ask the client first

- When exactly was the property acquired? Acquisition before 1 January 2004, and transfer within five years of acquisition, each carry a different rate in the tables above.
- How was it acquired: bought, inherited or donated? If inherited, what was the date of death (before 25 November 1992, or after 24 November 1992)? If donated, was the donation more than five years before this sale?
- Has the seller owned and lived in it as their only or main residence for at least three consecutive years right before the sale, and will the sale be within twelve months of moving out? This is the exemption.
- If bought within the last three years: did the purchase deed declare it as the sole ordinary residence, and does the seller own any other residential property? This is the 2% rate.
- In the last five years, did the seller or a related person carry out works that needed a development permission, or did a related person own it as part of a project? Either removes the 5% rate.
- Has the property been leased for three years or more to a tenant receiving a Housing Authority scheme benefit, and is the buyer that tenant?
- Was a notice of a promise of sale or transfer given to the Commissioner before 17 November 2014 in respect of this property, and was it later cancelled or did it expire? This determines whether the 12% row applies instead of the standard rate.
- Has the property been restored under a scheme for grade 1 or grade 2 scheduled property or property in an urban conservation area? Was the restoration permit applied for on or after 1 January 2015, and are the works certified as completed?
- Is the transferor non-resident in Malta and resident for tax purposes in another country, and does the transferor want to opt out and be taxed under article 5 (capital gains)?
- Does the property form part of a project, or is it situated in a special designated area? The 5% rate applies only to property not forming part of a project, whoever the transferor is.
- What brokerage fees did the transferor pay, and are they evidenced? They are deductible from the transfer value.

## When to refuse or refer

- The transfer causa mortis itself and the causa mortis declaration, including the duty on an inherited home; the Malta Tax and Customs Administration treats these under separate pages and this Guide does not cover them. The 3.5% band from EUR 35,000 to EUR 400,000 in [L.N. 306 of 2025](https://legislation.mt/eli/ln/2025/306/eng/pdf) applies to duty on an inherited dwelling house from 28 October 2025, not to a purchase. A later sale of inherited property is covered above.
- Share transfers, inter vivos or causa mortis, which the Property Tax Directorate handles separately.
- The assignment of a right obtained in terms of a promise of sale (konvenju), which the authority states is not regulated by the property transfers tax regime.
- First-time buyer claims where the buyer's earlier ownership history is unclear, and transfers before 28 October 2025 under the older provisos of S.L. 364.12 art. 3. MTCA's first-time buyer guideline still shows the older rules.
- Reduced duty for particular localities or property types, including the relief rules in S.L. 123.203 and S.L. 364.19, which [L.N. 284 of 2025](https://legislation.mt/eli/ln/2025/284/eng/pdf) extended to transfers up to 31 December 2026. Their conditions are not set out here.
- Cases where duty is claimed to be exempt or refunded under the second-time buyers scheme outside the period the guidelines themselves state.
- Determination of market value where the departmental architect's valuation exceeds the declared value beyond the stated tolerance; this requires the assessment and objection procedure, and a valuation the official page describes as remaining subjective.
