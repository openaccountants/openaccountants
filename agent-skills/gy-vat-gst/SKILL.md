# Guyana: value-added tax (2026) v1.0

> **General reference only.** This skill is general tax/accounting reference material for AI-assisted workflows. It has not been reviewed for any specific person's facts, documents, elections, deadlines, residency, filing status, or local procedures. Do not rely on it to file, pay, amend, or take a tax position without review by a qualified professional in the relevant jurisdiction.

**Which text of the law.** The Value-Added Tax Act, Chapter 81:05 (the Act), with the Value-Added Tax Regulations 2005, in the Guyana Revenue Authority (GRA) consolidation "revised March 27, 2026". It includes the Fiscal Enactments (Amendment) Act 2026 (Act No. 3 of 2026, Official Gazette (Extraordinary) of 17 February 2026), whose VAT changes came into operation on 16 February 2026 (s. 1(3)). The rate and the registration threshold are set by regulation (Act s. 9(1), Schedule IV para. 1), not in the Act itself.

## Who this applies to

- **Taxable persons**: anyone registered, or required to register (s. 10). VAT is charged on every taxable supply by a taxable person in Guyana and on every import of goods or services other than an exempt import (s. 9(1)).
- **Compulsory registration.** A person carrying on a taxable activity must apply within 15 days of (a) the end of any period of 12 months or fewer in which taxable supplies reached G$15,000,000, or (b) the start of a 12-month period in which they are reasonably expected to reach it (s. 11(1); Regulations reg. 3). Related persons' supplies can be counted together (s. 11(2)). There is no duty to register if the threshold was passed only because the business was closing or scaling down, or because capital goods were replaced (s. 11(4)).
- **Always register, whatever the turnover**: the State, its agencies and local authorities carrying on a taxable activity, auctioneers, and promoters, licensees and proprietors of public entertainment (s. 11(6) to (8)).
- **Voluntary registration** below the threshold: the business and location must be identifiable, the records of s. 60 must be kept, and the person must show an intention to make taxable supplies; for a new business, loans, projections, contracts or equipment purchases can show it (s. 11(5); reg. 4).
- **When registration takes effect**: for (a) above, from the start of the next tax period; for (b), from the start of the 12-month period; for voluntary registration, from the start of the tax period after the application (s. 12(4)). The GRA must decide within 10 days (s. 12(2), (5)). The certificate must be displayed at each place of business (s. 12(9)).
- **Recipients of imported services**: a resident who receives services from a non-resident (or from their own business abroad) and uses them in Guyana other than to make taxable supplies pays the VAT on the import (s. 2 "import", s. 9(2)(c)).
- **Out of scope**: excise tax, customs duty, and the detailed lists of zero-rated and exempt goods by tariff heading (see the GRA list in "Sources").

**What is zero-rated (Schedule I), in outline.** Exports of goods, including goods exported by the supplier and goods supplied outside Guyana (para. 2). Exports of services, including services physically rendered outside Guyana, and services to a non-resident who is outside Guyana when the services are supplied, except services connected with immovable property in Guyana and a few others (para. 3). Also, among others: raw and packaging materials for production (para. 4); the transfer of a business as a going concern between registered persons (para. 5(a)); listed medical supplies (para. 6); water and sewage services of the public utilities (para. 7); capital equipment for mining, forestry, agriculture, construction and manufacturing (para. 9); listed bread and other basic items (paras 13 and 14); private educational services (para. 16(a)); computers (para. 17); residential internet data (para. 18). The GRA keeps a full list by item.

**What is exempt (Schedule II), in outline.** Financial services to the extent set by regulation (reg. 11); international transport services; kerosene, LPG, butane, gasoline and diesel; accommodation in a residential dwelling and leases of land (para. 2); supplies by the State and budget agencies, and locally mined raw gold and diamonds (para. 2); funeral services (para. 3); listed motor vehicles (para. 4), including those added in 2026; and the other goods in paras 6 to 15.

## The base

**Output tax.** 14% of the value of each taxable supply: the consideration, or fair market value between related persons for less than market value (s. 16). For imported goods: customs value plus insurance and freight not in it, plus customs duty, excise and other charges except VAT (s. 20(1)). For imported services: the consideration, or market value between related persons (s. 20(2), (3)).

**Place of supply.** Goods: where delivered or made available, or where transport starts (s. 15(1)). Services: where the supplier's place of business is (s. 15(3)), except that intellectual property, consultancy, engineering, legal, architectural and accounting services, data processing, advertising and similar services are supplied where the recipient uses them (s. 15(4)).

**Input tax credit.** VAT on supplies and imports for use in a taxable activity, with a tax invoice (or customs entry for imports) held when the return is filed (s. 24(1), (2)). The Commissioner can allow a credit without a tax invoice if the person took all reasonable steps and the amount is right (s. 24(3)).

**Blocked inputs** (s. 25(2)): passenger vehicles (nine seats or fewer, including double cabs) unless the business deals in or hires them; entertainment (food, drink, tobacco, accommodation, amusement, hospitality) unless provided in the course of an entertainment business or to transport passengers; club, association and society subscriptions of a sporting, social or recreational kind.

**Partial exemption** (s. 25(3)). Input tax directly for taxable supplies is credited in full; directly for exempt supplies, not at all. Common input tax is credited by A x B / C, where B is taxable supplies and C all supplies of the current month. If B / C is more than 0.90, all common input tax is credited (s. 25(5)). The Commissioner can set another reasonable basis (s. 25(6)).

**Tax payable** for the month is output tax less input credit (s. 23(1)). An excess credit is carried forward (s. 35(2)) and can be claimed as a refund after six consecutive periods, or at once by a business at least half of whose taxable supplies are zero-rated (s. 35(3), (5)). The GRA must refund by the end of the second month after the claim (first month for zero-rated suppliers), or within 10 days after an audit (s. 35(4), (6)).

**Invoices.** A registered supplier issues a tax invoice with the Schedule III particulars for every taxable supply (s. 28(1)); for cash sales up to G$10,000 a sales invoice is enough (s. 28(2), reg. 5). Credit and debit notes correct the VAT on an invoice (s. 29).

## Deadlines and filing

| What | When | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| Apply to register | within 15 days of passing, or expecting to pass, G$15,000,000 | Act s. 11(1) |
| VAT return (G0002) for each month, even if nil, and the tax | 21st of the following month | Act s. 31(1), s. 42(1)(a) |
| Return and tax on imported services (G0001, VATIS) | 21st of the following month | Act s. 22(2) |
| VAT on imported goods | on entry, with customs | Act s. 22(1), (4) |

An extension of time to file can be asked for in writing; it does not move the payment date (s. 32).

**How to file.**
1. Log in to GRA eServices at https://eservices.gra.gov.gy with the TIN (an eServices account is needed first).
2. Choose "Submit Return", pick the VAT return (G0002) and the month.
3. Enter total taxable sales and output tax, zero-rated and exempt sales, and the input tax on purchases and imports, applying the blocks and the partial exemption rule above.
4. Upload the supporting documents and submit. The system shows the submission status and emails notices until assessment. A return filed electronically counts as filed when the GRA acknowledges receipt (s. 31A).
5. Pay the tax by the 21st. A paper G0002 can still be lodged at the Central Registration Unit, Camp Street, Georgetown, or an Integrated Regional Tax Office.
6. A recipient of imported services files G0001 (VAT on imported services) with the import declarations for the month by the same date (s. 22(2)).

## Penalties and interest

- **Late payment**: interest at 2% a month or part of a month, simple, from the due date (s. 27, Schedule IV para. 2). The Commissioner can remit it for good cause (s. 27(5)).
- **Late return**: the greater of G$1,000 a day (or part) and 10% of the tax payable for that period for each month (or part) the return is outstanding, capped at the tax payable for the return (s. 82(1), (2)).
- **Late import declaration or late VAT on an import**: the same formula, capped at the tax concerned; for unpaid import VAT it comes on top of interest (s. 81).
- **Failure to register**: twice the output tax payable from when registration was required until the application is filed (s. 80(1)).
- **Records not kept**: G$100 a day (s. 83(1)). False or misleading statements: a penalty the Commissioner determines (s. 84).
- **Criminal offences**, as the GRA summarises them: G$15,000 for knowingly or recklessly failing to file, and G$2,000 a day and up to 3 months' imprisonment for not filing after a written extension. The GRA can also seek a court order closing business premises for 3 to 30 days for repeated failures (s. 86).

## Cross-border points

- **Exports** of goods and of qualifying services are zero-rated (Schedule I paras 2 and 3); keep the export documents (s. 17(2)).
- **Imported goods**: VAT at 14% on the customs value plus duties, paid at entry (s. 20(1), s. 22(1)). An import is exempt if the same supply would be zero-rated or exempt in Guyana (s. 21).
- **Imported services**: the resident recipient self-charges 14% where the services are used in Guyana other than to make taxable supplies, files G0001 and pays by the 21st (s. 2, s. 9(2)(c), s. 22(2)). A business using the services wholly for taxable supplies is outside this charge.
- **Refunds to diplomats, international organisations and departing non-residents**: s. 37 and reg. 8.

## Two worked examples that recompute

### Example 1: a monthly return with a blocked input, and a late-filing variant

Coastline Traders is registered. In March 2026 it sells G$10,000,000 of standard-rated goods and exports G$2,000,000 of goods. Its purchases carry G$700,000 of VAT, of which G$300,000 is on a car for the manager (blocked, s. 25(2)(a)).

- Output tax: 14% of 10,000,000 = G$1,400,000. Exports: 0%.
- Input credit: 700,000 - 300,000 = G$400,000.
- Tax payable: G$1,000,000, with the G0002 return, by 21 April 2026.

**Late variant.** If the return is filed 20 days late, the penalty is the greater of 20 x G$1,000 = G$20,000 and 10% of G$1,000,000 for one month (or part) = G$100,000, so G$100,000 (under the G$1,000,000 cap). If the tax is also paid 20 days late, interest is 2% for one month or part: G$20,000.

  input_vat_total: 700000

  days_late: 20
  months_late: 1

  - output_tax: standard_sales * 0.14 + export_sales * 0
  - input_credit: input_vat_total - blocked_car_vat
  - tax_payable: output_tax - input_credit
  - late_penalty: min(max(days_late * 1000, tax_payable * 0.10 * months_late), tax_payable)
  - late_interest: tax_payable * 0.02 * months_late

  input_credit: 400000
  tax_payable: 1000000

### Example 2: partial exemption, and when the 90% rule switches it off

Riverview Properties is registered. In June 2026 it makes G$6,000,000 of taxable supplies (commercial rents) and G$4,000,000 of exempt supplies (residential lettings). Common overheads carry G$500,000 of VAT. Nothing is directly allocable.

- B / C = 6,000,000 / 10,000,000 = 0.60, not above 0.90.
- Credit: 500,000 x 0.60 = G$300,000; G$200,000 is a cost.
- Output tax: 14% of 6,000,000 = G$840,000. Tax payable: G$540,000.

**The other way.** If in July the residential lettings were only G$500,000 against G$6,000,000 of taxable supplies, B / C would be 6,000,000 / 6,500,000, about 0.923. That is above 0.90, so the whole G$500,000 is credited (s. 25(5)) and July's tax payable is 840,000 - 500,000 = G$340,000.

```oa-check
name: GY VAT 2026, partial exemption A x B/C and the 90% rule
inputs:
  taxable: 6000000
  exempt_june: 4000000
  exempt_july: 500000
  common_vat: 500000
steps:
  - ratio_june: taxable / (taxable + exempt_june)
  - credit_june: common_vat * ratio_june
  - output_tax: taxable * 0.14
  - payable_june: output_tax - credit_june
  - ratio_july: round(taxable / (taxable + exempt_july), 3)
  - credit_july: common_vat
  - payable_july: output_tax - credit_july
expected:
  ratio_june: 0.60
  credit_june: 300000
  output_tax: 840000
  payable_june: 540000
  ratio_july: 0.923
  credit_july: 500000
  payable_july: 340000
```

## Ask the client first

1. Is the business registered? If not, what were its taxable supplies over each of the last 12 months, and what does it expect in the next 12 (the G$15,000,000 test)?
2. What does it sell: standard-rated, zero-rated (exports, Schedule I items, the February 2026 additions) or exempt (financial services, residential rent, fuels, the vehicles exempt from February 2026)?
3. Does it make both taxable and exempt supplies (partial exemption), and what is the monthly split?
4. Does it import goods or buy services from abroad, and are those services used for taxable supplies?
5. Any blocked purchases (passenger vehicles, entertainment, club fees), and does it hold tax invoices for every input?
6. Has it carried excess credits forward, and for how many periods (refund claim after six, or at once if at least half its supplies are zero-rated)?
