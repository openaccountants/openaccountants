# Montserrat tax system reference

> **General reference only.** This skill is general tax/accounting reference material for AI-assisted workflows. It has not been reviewed for any specific person's facts, documents, elections, deadlines, residency, filing status, or local procedures. Do not rely on it to file, pay, amend, or take a tax position without review by a qualified professional in the relevant jurisdiction.

## Scope

Use this skill to identify the applicable Montserrat tax person, territorial branch, period, currency rule and authority before choosing a calculation or filing skill. It covers individuals, estates, trusts, companies, partnerships and other bodies, with separate attribution and representative rules, and it distinguishes Inland Revenue, Customs and Excise and social security administration. Establish the requested date and the person's legal form, residence, income source, capacity and activity first.

The statutes used are the revised editions showing the law at **1 January 2025**. This skill does not establish later changes or an actual liability, exemption, deduction, filing, payment or professional sign-off. Rates, returns and payments are in the other Montserrat skills:

## Ask the client first

1. What date, and which year of assessment, is the question about?
2. Is the taxpayer an individual, an estate, a trust, a partnership, a company (local, foreign or international business company) or another body?
3. For an individual: days physically present in Montserrat in the basic year, permanent abode, purpose of presence, ordinary residence and domicile.
4. Where does the income arise, and was foreign income received in Montserrat?
5. Who acts for the taxpayer: personal representative, trustee, partner, agent or manager of a non-resident?
6. Is the question about Inland Revenue tax, a Customs charge or social security?

## The method, step by step

### 1. Territorial reach

Income and Corporation Tax Act, Cap. 17.01, s 3 starts with the specified income of any person arising in Montserrat or elsewhere, whether received locally or not, subject to the Act. The sources include business or professional profits, employment, specified property benefits, investments, rents and other property income, other annual gains and pensions. Apply the following qualifications together with any applicable exemption or special provision. [Section 3](https://www.gov.ms/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/17.01-Income-and-Incorporation-Tax-Act.pdf#page=11)

- For foreign income that is earned income **or** arises to a person not ordinarily resident **or** not domiciled in Montserrat, the foreign-income proviso charges the amount received in Montserrat. Ordinary residence, domicile and statutory residence are separate questions.
- Where a trade, profession or vocation operates partly inside and partly outside Montserrat, the whole profits are treated as locally arising for a resident body of persons or a resident individual whose home is in Montserrat. The home condition belongs to the individual branch.
- A further proviso excludes foreign income for a person present only for a temporary purpose, without an intention to establish residence, who has not actually resided in Montserrat at one or more times for a period totalling 6 months in the basic year. Preserve that wording separately from the individual residence test of 183 days. The separate visitor exemption in s 6(1)(t) uses its own conditions and 'not exceeding six months' wording.
- For s 3 and its provisos, an international business company is deemed ordinarily resident and domiciled in Montserrat. The deeming is confined to that stated purpose. [Section 3 provisos](https://www.gov.ms/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/17.01-Income-and-Incorporation-Tax-Act.pdf#page=12) · [Section 6(1)(t)](https://www.gov.ms/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/17.01-Income-and-Incorporation-Tax-Act.pdf#page=16)

Foreign incorporation or non-residence does not by itself exclude local-source or local-business liability. Section 39(4) contains a separate remitted-profit branch for a non-resident company carrying on business locally. Determine its actual conditions in the cross-border analysis. Detailed exemptions, incentives, treaty entitlements and amounts require their respective provisions. [Section 39](https://www.gov.ms/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/17.01-Income-and-Incorporation-Tax-Act.pdf#page=40)

### 2. Tax residence

Cap. 17.01 s 2 defines residence in relation to a year of assessment. Its individual alternatives concern permanent abode and presence in the basic year, including the Comptroller's specified absence qualification; physical presence of at least 183 days in that basic year; or a period continuous with presence in a qualifying immediately preceding or succeeding basic year. An estate follows whether the deceased qualified as resident immediately before death. A trust or body of persons is resident if established in Montserrat. [Section 2](https://www.gov.ms/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/17.01-Income-and-Incorporation-Tax-Act.pdf#page=10)

A body of persons includes companies, so the establishment test must be considered for the relevant company. Establish the statutory facts instead of substituting a company-registration filing, registered-office address or an assumed general management-and-control test. The management-and-control wording in s 6(1)(n) serves its reciprocal shipping-income exemption. [Section 2 continuation](https://www.gov.ms/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/17.01-Income-and-Incorporation-Tax-Act.pdf#page=11) · [Section 6(1)(n)](https://www.gov.ms/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/17.01-Income-and-Incorporation-Tax-Act.pdf#page=15)

Tax Administration Act, Cap. 17.26, s 2 uses similar individual, estate and trust/body tests **in relation to a calendar year**. Preserve that temporal wording separately from the income-tax assessment/basic-year rules. [Administration s 2](https://www.gov.ms/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/17.26-Tax-Administration-Act.pdf#page=11)

### 3. Taxpayer types, attribution and representatives

Cap. 17.01 s 2 makes 'person' include a body of persons and excludes a body of persons from 'individual'. The definition covers bodies politic, corporate or collegiate, companies, fraternities, fellowships and societies, whether incorporated or not. 'Company' includes domestic and foreign incorporated or registered companies and international business companies. These are tax definitions; company-law registration is a separate classification. [Section 2](https://www.gov.ms/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/17.01-Income-and-Incorporation-Tax-Act.pdf#page=9)

Section 36 charges the chargeable income of a company, building society or body of persons at 30%, subject to the Act's deductions, exemptions and other applicable provisions. A residence definition alone does not place every trust or estate in that rate category. Classify income attribution and the person assessable before using the corporate calculation. [Section 36](https://www.gov.ms/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/17.01-Income-and-Incorporation-Tax-Act.pdf#page=39)

Section 21 assesses a married woman's income in her own name. Section 22 addresses specified deceased-person liabilities through personal representatives and the other listed recipients or controllers, with its estate-distribution proviso. Section 23 allocates partnership income to each partner's entitled share, which the partner returns. It separately requires partnership information and accounts from the precedent resident partner, another resident partner required by the Comptroller or, if none is resident, the resident attorney, agent, manager or factor. It does not impose an automatic company-rate charge on every partnership. [Sections 21 to 23](https://www.gov.ms/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/17.01-Income-and-Incorporation-Tax-Act.pdf#page=31)

Section 24(1) assesses the listed trustee, receiver, guardian, curator or committee on the represented-person basis, whilst preserving possible direct assessment of that person. Section 24(2) separately applies the s 36 rate where trust income arises to at least 2 beneficiaries **or** the whole income is not distributed. Sections 25 to 28 address assessment through non-resident representatives and intermediaries, their required acts, retention and indemnity. Sections 29 to 32 contain specific transferor-attribution and controlled-company direction branches. Apply their conditions; legal ownership alone does not settle who returns the income. [Sections 24 to 28](https://www.gov.ms/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/17.01-Income-and-Incorporation-Tax-Act.pdf#page=33) · [Sections 29 to 32](https://www.gov.ms/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/17.01-Income-and-Incorporation-Tax-Act.pdf#page=34)

Within Cap. 17.26's scope, ss 46 to 47 separately address representatives for individuals under disability, companies, partnerships, trusts, other bodies, government and non-residents. They distinguish designation, resident principal officers or agents, different trustee capacities, continuing bodies and representative duties. The ordinary limit to taxpayer assets possessed or controlled has specified personal-liability exceptions; separate shareholder and manager provisions also apply. Keep tax classification, attribution and filing/payment responsibility distinct and reconcile the applicable revenue law under s 4. [Sections 46 to 49](https://www.gov.ms/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/17.26-Tax-Administration-Act.pdf#page=35)

### 4. Currency

The 1 January 2025 Interpretation Act, ss 3 and 36 and its Dictionary, gives 'dollar' the Eastern Caribbean currency meaning unless a contrary intention appears. That is a statutory unit definition, not proof of a particular bank's accepted payment currency or an exchange rate. The ECCB Agreement's Article 17 denomination and its appointed-application chain are separate; the complete Gazette appointment chain has not been established here. [Interpretation Act, Dictionary](https://www.gov.ms/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/1.02-Interpretation-Act.pdf#page=30)

Cap. 17.26 s 54 expressly makes covered tax payable in Eastern Caribbean dollars. Apply it with that Act's s 4: the applicable revenue law prevails in an inconsistency, and the Act excludes collection and recovery provisions residing with the Comptroller of Customs and Excise, subject to its stated secrecy exception. Do not expand this payment rule into an unverified rule for every Customs or contribution remittance. No conversion method, exchange rate or USD equivalence is supplied. [Section 54](https://www.gov.ms/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/17.26-Tax-Administration-Act.pdf#page=40) · [Scope, s 4](https://www.gov.ms/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/17.26-Tax-Administration-Act.pdf#page=13)

### 5. Assessment periods

Cap. 17.01 s 2 defines the assessment year as the 12 months commencing on 1 January. Section 4 ordinarily assesses income of the 12-month basic year ending on the immediately preceding 31 December. A different trade accounting date requires the Comptroller's permission, continues on a corresponding basis in subsequent assessments and may require adjustment when changed. Section 4(3) preserves assessment on basic-year income even if no such income arises in the assessment year. [Sections 2 and 4](https://www.gov.ms/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/17.01-Income-and-Incorporation-Tax-Act.pdf#page=11) · [Section 4 continuation](https://www.gov.ms/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/17.01-Income-and-Incorporation-Tax-Act.pdf#page=13)

Annual income-tax assessment, an employer pay period, a contribution period and a transaction-based charge serve different purposes. Select the applicable regime's period and calendar; the income-tax basic year does not determine every return or remittance date. The detailed references retain their own period, procedure and later-law conditions.

### 6. Responsible authorities

The MCRS Enabling Act, Cap. 17.06, ss 2 to 5 establishes Montserrat Customs and Revenue Services as a Ministry of Finance division with Inland Revenue and Customs and Excise units. Its Director General administers and manages MCRS and reports to the Financial Secretary. Sections 6 to 7 distinguish Financial Secretary directions from written delegation to the relevant Comptroller. The definition of revenue laws is inclusive; its printed list is not an exhaustive inventory of charges. [Sections 2 to 7](https://www.gov.ms/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/17.06-Montserrat-Customs-and-Revenue-Services-Enabling-Act.pdf#page=7)

Cap. 17.26 s 2 identifies its Department as Inland Revenue, and s 5 assigns the Comptroller collection, accounting and administration for taxes within that Act's scope. Customs collection and recovery require their own applicable provisions. Cap. 17.06's definition refers to income-tax s 41 for the Inland Revenue Comptroller's appointment, whereas inspected Cap. 17.01 places appointment in s 40 and rulemaking in s 41. Preserve that cross-reference discrepancy when identifying the actual provision. [Administration ss 2 to 5](https://www.gov.ms/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/17.26-Tax-Administration-Act.pdf#page=11) · [Income-tax s 40](https://www.gov.ms/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/17.01-Income-and-Incorporation-Tax-Act.pdf#page=40)

Social security has a separate Board and Fund. Social Security Act, Cap. 18.09, s 4 vests the Fund in the Social Security Board and assigns its administration. Section 7 assigns the Director Fund management, including contribution collection and benefit payment. This institutional distinction supplies no contribution rate, rounding result or actual payment acceptance. [Sections 4 and 7](https://www.gov.ms/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/18.09-Social-Security-Act.pdf#page=11)

## Worked cases

### Case 1: a company's tax for the 2026 year of assessment

A Montserrat company has chargeable income of EC$200,000 for the basic year 2025 (the calendar year before the 2026 year of assessment, s 4). Section 36 charges a company, building society or body of persons at 30%: tax EC$60,000 (derived). Rate source: [Cap. 17.01, s 36](https://www.gov.ms/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/17.01-Income-and-Incorporation-Tax-Act.pdf#page=39). Deductions, exemptions and incentives come first and are in [ms-corporate-income-tax](ms-corporate-income-tax.md).

```oa-check
name: Montserrat company income tax, 2026 year of assessment
inputs:
  year_of_assessment: 2026
  chargeable_income: 200000
steps:
  - basic_year: year_of_assessment - 1
  - tax: chargeable_income * 0.30
expected:
  basic_year: 2025
  tax: 60000
```

### Case 2 (decision): a trustee who accumulates or distributes EC$50,000 of trust income

A trustee under a settlement has EC$50,000 of income and one beneficiary. If the trustee distributes the whole income to that one beneficiary, s 24(2) does not apply: the trustee is assessed under s 24(1) in like manner as the beneficiary would be, at the beneficiary's own rates and deductions (see [ms-income-tax-individuals](ms-income-tax-individuals.md)). If the trustee keeps any part of the income undistributed, or the income arises to two or more beneficiaries, s 24(2) charges the trust income at the s 36 rate of 30%: EC$15,000 (derived). Distributing the whole income can therefore lower the tax where the beneficiary's own rates are below 30%; check the trust deed allows it. Source: [Cap. 17.01, ss 24 and 36](https://www.gov.ms/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/17.01-Income-and-Incorporation-Tax-Act.pdf#page=33).

```oa-check
name: Montserrat trust income charged under s 24(2)
inputs:
  trust_income: 50000
  undistributed: 50000
steps:
  - s24_2_applies: min(1, undistributed / trust_income)
  - trustee_tax: trust_income * 0.30 * s24_2_applies
expected:
  s24_2_applies: 1
  trustee_tax: 15000
```

## When to refuse or refer

- Refuse to give a liability, exemption or filing result from this skill alone: route to the topic skill in the Scope table.
- Refer residence questions where the 183 days, permanent abode, temporary-purpose proviso or domicile facts are in doubt; do not substitute 183 days for every six-month rule.
- Refer trusts, estates, controlled companies and transferor attribution (ss 22 to 32) where the deed, the representative's capacity or the beneficiaries are unclear.
- Refer any Customs collection question to the Customs legislation: Cap. 17.26 does not apply to collection and recovery that rest with the Comptroller of Customs and Excise (s 4(3)).
- Refer any question that depends on law made after 1 January 2025.
