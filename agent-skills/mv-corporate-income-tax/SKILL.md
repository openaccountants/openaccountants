# Maldives corporate income tax

## Scope

Compute ordinary 2026 income tax for a Maldives company or another non-individual, non-bank entity. Establish residence, the accounting period, group membership and taxable income before applying a rate. A bank, insurer, international transport operator, specially exempt project or non-resident withholding-only recipient needs its specific rules as well as this starting point. Sole proprietors use [individual income tax](mv-income-tax.md); employment withholding and pensions use [payroll](mv-payroll-social.md); GST is separate.

This Guide is source-cited tier 2 material, without an accountant's approval. It does not establish that an entity has an exemption or treaty relief merely because the entity is incorporated abroad.

## Ask the client first

1. What is the legal entity, incorporation location, head office and place of control/management? Is there a Maldives permanent establishment or other Maldives-source income?
2. What are the accounting period, functional/presentation currencies and business activities? Is the entity a licensed commercial bank, insurer, international transport operator or specially exempt project?
3. Which entities form the group for consolidated-financial-statement purposes, and which are liable under the Act? Obtain the ownership/consolidation chart and prior returns.
4. Obtain accounts, tax depreciation/asset schedules, expense support, exempt income, tax losses, changes of ownership/business, related-party transactions and any interest limitation computation.
5. What payments were made or became payable to non-residents? Obtain agreements, beneficial recipient/residence evidence, withholding certificates, invoices and any treaty determination.
6. Obtain prior-year tax, estimated current tax, interim returns/payments, withholding credits, foreign-tax evidence and the MIRA tax-account reconciliation.

## The method, step by step

### 1. Fix residence, source and the rate category [https://www.mira.gov.mv/Legislations/View/Incometaxact](https://www.mira.gov.mv/Legislations/View/Incometaxact)

Under section 79(kk), incorporation in Maldives, a Maldives head office, or control and management in Maldives can establish company residence. A resident's income base includes domestic and foreign income under sections 2 and 10. A non-resident's base is Maldives-source income determined under section 11, including the relevant permanent-establishment branches. Do not treat a foreign bank account or customer as proof that income is outside Maldives.

| Taxpayer and conditions | Rate or band | Period | Official source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Non-individual, non-bank person outside a group allocation | First MVR 500,000 taxable income at 0%; excess at 15% | Full 2026 accounting period | [Income Tax Act section 9(a)-(b)](https://www.mira.gov.mv/Legislations/View/Incometaxact) |
| Entity in a group liable to the Act | Divide MVR 500,000 among the group's entities liable under the Act; 15% applies above the resulting band | 2026; determine actual group population | [Income Tax Act section 9(c)-(d), group definition section 79(k)](https://www.mira.gov.mv/Legislations/View/Incometaxact) |
| Commercial bank licensed under Maldives Banking Act 24/2010 | 25% of taxable income; no ordinary corporate zero-rate band | 2026; specialist bank computation | [Income Tax Act section 8](https://www.mira.gov.mv/Legislations/View/Incometaxact) |
| Accounting period shorter/longer than ordinary statutory period | Prorate a prescribed period amount under section 74 rather than giving an automatic full-year band | Applicable nonstandard period | [Income Tax Act section 74](https://www.mira.gov.mv/Legislations/View/Incometaxact) |

A non-resident permanent establishment is not automatically taxed at a flat corporate rate from the first rufiyaa. Determine the entity category, applicable band/allocation, source, deductions and treatment of income already subject to withholding. A partnership is separately taxable under section 37 and falls within the non-individual rate rule; general partners have the specified joint liability.

### 2. Reconcile accounts to taxable income [https://www.mira.gov.mv/Legislations/View/Incometaxact](https://www.mira.gov.mv/Legislations/View/Incometaxact)

Start with financial statements prepared under section 13 and Income Tax Regulation sections 57-60. The ordinary accounting period is the calendar year, subject to commencement, deregistration and winding-up rules. Accrual accounting is the starting point; cash-basis eligibility under Regulation section 59(e) requires its current income and non-current-asset conditions, not merely a bank statement.

Use a reconciliation with accounting profit, exempt income removed, disallowed charges added back, accounting depreciation reversed, statutory capital allowances/balancing adjustments, other deductions and loss relief. Sections 16-17 allow properly incurred income-producing expenditure and proportionate mixed expenses; Regulation section 67 requires a just and reasonable basis and retained calculations. Section 32 excludes private costs, income tax, fines, recoverable input GST and other listed items; capital cost is dealt with under its specific allowance rules rather than automatically expensed. Sections 19 and 31 permit the pension deduction and prohibit double deductions.

Identify the actual section 12 exemption before excluding a receipt. Resident-to-resident dividends may qualify under section 12(a); non-resident dividends are a different withholding question. Keep gross sales and deductible GST separate. Recovered expenses and previously relieved bad debts return to income under sections 14-15. A general provision for doubtful debts is not automatically the section 23 bad-debt deduction: test prior income, reasonable collection steps, irrecoverability, write-off and the exclusions.

Assets, leases and finance must use the applicable period's law. Capital allowances follow Act section 25 and Regulation Chapter 11, including use, class, cost and private-use restrictions. Operating-lease treatment for 2026 remains within Regulation section 72-1(a)-(d); the different straight-line rule in section 72-1(e)-(h) starts with 2027. Do not prematurely apply the future rule. Section 22's interest restriction and section 71's separate thin-capitalization provisions require testing the lender, taxpayer class and exclusions; related parties require arm's-length analysis under section 67. Refer a material financing or transfer-pricing computation not established by the records.

### 3. Test loss relief and foreign tax [https://www.mira.gov.mv/Legislations/View/Incometaxact](https://www.mira.gov.mv/Legislations/View/Incometaxact)

Business losses under section 33 can carry forward for up to five years, earliest losses first, limited by positive taxable income and the statutory continuity conditions. For an unlisted company, the same shareholder group must continuously own more than half the ordinary share capital and the same line of business must continue over the relevant period. Regulation section 72 limits the deduction to business income; do not offset a business loss indiscriminately against capital gains. Section 34 capital losses have a separate gains-only, five-year regime, read with Regulation section 95.

For a resident's foreign-source income also taxable in Maldives, section 72 provides foreign tax credit limited to the lesser of qualifying foreign income tax paid and Maldives tax on the net foreign-source income. Compute it separately by income type and country, retain evidence, apply the claim/time rules and the treaty provision where relevant. Do not deduct the same foreign tax as an expense and a credit or assume a non-resident can claim the resident credit.

### 4. Calculate tax and distinguish withholding [https://www.mira.gov.mv/Legislations/View/Incometaxact](https://www.mira.gov.mv/Legislations/View/Incometaxact)

Apply the correct band to taxable income after the permitted deductions and losses. Keep the tax charge, foreign credit, EWT/NWT/CGWT credits, interim payments and existing overpayments in separate controls. The same tax payment cannot be deducted twice.

The pack's non-resident payment classes are preserved below. These are domestic-law starting points for a person carrying on business in Maldives making a covered payment to a non-resident; check section 12 exemptions, section 55 exclusions and applicable treaty relief before deduction. Payment timing is the earlier of actual payment and becoming payable under section 80(b), not always the bank debit date.

| Payment class and conditions | Domestic withholding | Period | Official source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rent for Maldives immovable property; royalties; dividends; technical-service fees; commissions for services supplied in Maldives; Maldives public-entertainer/research-and-development payments; non-resident insurance premium | 10% of gross covered payment | 2026; statutory exceptions apply | [Income Tax Act section 55(a)](https://www.mira.gov.mv/Legislations/View/Incometaxact) |
| Interest | 10%, excluding interest paid/payable to a bank or non-banking institution approved for this purpose | 2026; verify lender qualification under Regulation sections 52 and 52-1 | [Income Tax Act section 55(a)(3)](https://www.mira.gov.mv/Legislations/View/Incometaxact) |
| Payment to a non-resident contractor within section 55(c)(3) | 10% of gross; apply contractor classification where the payment also falls in section 55(a) | 2026 | [Income Tax Act section 55(b)-(c)](https://www.mira.gov.mv/Legislations/View/Incometaxact) |
| Withholding return/payment | By the 15th of the following month for the relevant covered payment month | 2026; paid/payable rule applies | [Income Tax Act sections 58, 80](https://www.mira.gov.mv/Legislations/View/Incometaxact) |

Managerial, technical and consultancy services or similar non-employment services fall within the technical-fee definition in section 79(vv); a contract's label alone does not settle classification. Software royalty is limited by section 55(c)(2)'s copyright-rights conditions; a standard SaaS payment is not automatically a royalty. The bank commission, inter-carrier and reinsurance exclusions in section 55(d), payer exceptions in section 55(e), treaty and section 12 exemptions require separate checks. Unknown payee residence triggers the statutory non-resident presumption in section 55(c)(1) for its covered payments, rather than a universal assumption for all income-tax questions.

If the payer fails to deduct from a covered payment, section 56 and Regulation section 38 can require gross-up. For an amount received net, withholding is net × rate / (1 - rate), subject to the actual class and facts. Do not simply multiply the net payment by the headline percentage.

NWT is ordinarily final for the taxed income under section 61, subject to the election in section 50(c). For a PE, Act section 27 governs the election and income/expense treatment of withholding-taxed receipts; do not both exclude/deduct the same gross income and take the withholding credit. CGWT under section 50-1 and treaty relief are distinct branches requiring their own analysis, not an automatic addition to ordinary NWT.

## Boundary and exception table

| Trigger | Required decision |
|---|---|
| Company within a consolidated group | Identify liable entities before dividing the band; do not give every group member the full allowance. |
| No profit or low taxable income | Check return exemption separately from tax due; current/prior-year income, asset tests and mandatory-filing classes matter. |
| Bank or insurer | Use the specialist base and form, not only the ordinary-company rate table. |
| Non-resident PE or withholding-only income | Separate attributable/source income, final withholding and elective return/section 27 treatment. |
| Foreign income, associates or major borrowing | Test foreign-credit, controlled-entity, arm's-length and financing restrictions before finalizing. |
| Winding up, emigration, asset transfer or period change | Check special period/accelerated-return and withholding provisions; an ordinary calendar does not settle these cases. |

## Worked cases

### Case 1: standalone versus group entity

Assume a full-year non-bank company has MVR 600,000 taxable income after adjustments, no credits and no special regime. A standalone company has MVR 15,000 tax: (600,000 - 500,000) × 15%. If it belongs to a group of two entities both liable under the Act, its allocated band is MVR 250,000 and its tax is MVR 52,500. Group status changes this company's tax by MVR 37,500; it is not an elective choice to ignore a group. The other member must be computed separately. [Income Tax Act section 9](https://www.mira.gov.mv/Legislations/View/Incometaxact).

```oa-check
name: Standalone versus two liable group entities
inputs:
  taxable: 600000
  entities: 2
steps:
  - standalone: max(taxable - 500000, 0) * 0.15
  - group_band: 500000 / entities
  - group_tax: max(taxable - group_band, 0) * 0.15
  - difference: group_tax - standalone
expected:
  standalone: 15000
  group_band: 250000
  group_tax: 52500
  difference: 37500
```

### Case 2: gross fee versus guaranteed net fee

Assume a covered non-resident technical-service payment with no treaty/exemption. For a gross fee MVR 100,000, withhold MVR 10,000 and pay MVR 90,000 to the supplier. If the contract guarantees the supplier MVR 100,000 net and the payer bears tax, gross consideration is MVR 111,111.11 and withholding is MVR 11,111.11, subject to rounding. Establish the actual contractual obligation before choosing the computation. [Income Tax Act sections 55-56; Regulation section 38](https://www.mira.gov.mv/Legislations/View/Income-Tax-Regulation).

```oa-check
name: Gross and net non-resident technical fee
inputs:
  gross_fee: 100000
  net_guarantee: 100000
steps:
  - ordinary_wht: gross_fee * 0.1
  - ordinary_cash: gross_fee - ordinary_wht
  - grossed_fee: round(net_guarantee / 0.9, 2)
  - grossed_wht: grossed_fee - net_guarantee
expected:
  ordinary_wht: 10000
  ordinary_cash: 90000
  grossed_fee: 111111.11
  grossed_wht: 11111.11
```

## When to refuse or refer

Refer banks, insurers, international transportation, uncertain residence/PE, special-project exemption, a disputed group, treaty/beneficial-entitlement questions, controlled foreign entities and material financing/related-party issues unless the specific operative rules and facts have been established. Stop before filing where accounts, prior-year tax, loss continuity, foreign-credit evidence or payment classification are missing. A provisional estimate must identify the missing item and cannot be presented as an approved filing position.

## Filing and payment

1. Confirm MIRA registration and obligations under Act sections 64-65 and Regulation sections 3-8. A business registered through the Ministry is exempt from separately submitting MIRA 117 under Regulation section 3(b); verify the actual tax registration rather than creating duplicate records.
2. Determine whether a return is required under Act section 50 and Regulation section 101. The ordinary non-bank/non-individual exemption requires taxable income at or below MVR 500,000 and total income at or below MVR 2,000,000 in both the current and previous accounting periods, subject to proration and overriding mandatory-filing cases. A non-resident PE, insurer, specified transport operator, special-project exempt person or an entity meeting the non-current-asset tests may still have to file. Zero tax does not itself prove no filing.
3. Calculate interim payments under Act sections 43 and 49: the first liable year's instalments use half the estimated annual tax; later years ordinarily use half prior-year tax, adjusted for period length. A permitted lower estimate carries the statutory underestimation test. The no-interim branch requires the current/prior tax conditions or the relevant return exemption. Keep any withholding-credit allocation from being reused twice.
4. Subject to those exceptions, first interim return/payment is due 31 July of the tax year, second by 31 January following and final return/payment by 30 June following: Act section 42. For 2026 these are the ordinary statutory dates, subject to the actual holiday/extension rule and any notice. Use MIRA 603 for interim returns and [MIRA 604 v26.1](https://www.mira.gov.mv/Files/GetFile/08ee0850-211a-4b32-910a-a351dffb791b) for ordinary income tax; banks and insurers use their own forms under Regulation section 99.
5. In MIRAconnect select the actual taxpayer, period and currency. Complete the tax assessment and required schedules before the front-page computation. The published form identifies the total liability, foreign credit, EWT/NWT/CGWT credits, first/second interims, existing overpayments, final payment and amount paid. Enter the actual group population and residence, attach the required accounts and explanations, and reconcile to the working paper. Do not invent an authenticated portal box number.
6. Regulation section 102 requires financial statements and other documents, with its income/asset-based audit and cash-basis exceptions. Companies retain the directors' report obligation; group consolidated statements have their separate section 102-1 rules. The annual-income threshold alone does not settle audit exemption under the current consolidated regulation.
7. Submit and pay through the channels in Regulation section 103. Corporate income-tax/interim returns are online; limited individual/deemed-partnership exceptions do not automatically extend to companies. Currency follows sections 105-108: MVR presentation can be paid in MVR or USD, while USD presentation requires USD under the current rules. Retain currency-conversion workings, acknowledgement, payment reference/receipt and a reconciliation of the tax account.
8. For covered non-resident payments, file MIRA 602 for the payment month under Regulation section 98, including specified treaty-exempt payments; reconcile gross, withheld and net amounts, then pay by the separate withholding due date. Preserve certificates and do not label the annual income-tax return as a substitute.

Late filing/information offences, including failure to register, are governed by [Tax Administration Act section 65](https://www.mira.gov.mv/Legislations/View/tax-admin-act-consolidated): the civil formula includes 0.5% of tax payable and a daily fine not exceeding MVR 50. Late payment under section 66 is 0.05% per day on the outstanding amount. Identify the actual offence, base, period and MIRA determination; earlier BPT penalty caps are not automatically current income-tax caps. Refer enforcement/intentional inaccuracy and any requested mitigation separately.

## Completion checklist

- Residence, source, taxpayer category, period and group band established.
- Accounts-to-tax reconciliation, allowances, losses and supporting conditions complete.
- Foreign credits, withholding, interims and overpayments used once and matched to evidence.
- Every non-resident payment classified using contracts and paid/payable timing.
- Correct form, currency, attachments, authorised submission and separate payments completed.
- Acknowledgements, receipts, tax-account reconciliation and open referrals retained.
