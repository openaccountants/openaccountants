# Colombia: payroll for employers, 2026 v1.0

> **General reference only.** This skill is general tax/accounting reference material for AI-assisted workflows. It has not been reviewed for any specific person's facts, documents, elections, deadlines, residency, filing status, or local procedures. Do not rely on it to file, pay, amend, or take a tax position without review by a qualified professional in the relevant jurisdiction.

**Year.** Calendar 2026. Most figures hang on the monthly minimum wage (salario mínimo legal mensual vigente, SMMLV) of **COP 1,750,905** (Decreto 1469 of 29 December 2025) and on the 2026 UVT of **COP 52,374** (DIAN Resolución 000238 of 15 December 2025).

**Which law.** Ley 100 de 1993 (health and pensions), the Código Sustantivo del Trabajo (CST, wages and prestaciones), Ley 50 de 1990 art. 99 (cesantías funds), Ley 21 de 1982 and Ley 89 de 1988 (parafiscales), Decreto-Ley 1295 de 1994 and Decreto 1072 de 2015 (ARL), and the Estatuto Tributario (ET) for withholding and the exoneration, as consolidated on the Senate's official site and the official normogramas named on each row. The pension reform, Ley 2381 de 2024, is not in force in 2026: the Senate text notes that its articles found constitutional take effect on 1 April 2027 (Sentencia C-264-26). This skill applies Ley 100 de 1993.

## Who this applies to

- **Employers in the private sector** that hire under a contrato de trabajo (CST): companies, branches of foreign companies and individuals who employ staff.
- **Employees** for the deductions from their pay (health, pension, solidarity fund, withholding).
- **Contractors** paid for personal services without an employment contract are not employees, but the art. 383 table also applies to their monthly payments (art. 383 par. 2 ET) and the payer must check their PILA payment before deducting the fee (art. 108 par. 2 ET). Their own contributions are in [co-social-contributions](co-social-contributions.md).
- **Out of scope:** public-sector pay scales, teachers in the Magisterio fund, apprenticeship contracts (contrato de aprendizaje), domestic workers by the day, overtime and night or Sunday surcharges, working hours, and dismissal compensation (indemnización). Pensioners appear only where the withholding table touches them.

## The base

**What is salary.** Everything the worker receives in money or kind as direct pay for the service: fixed or variable pay, habitual bonuses, overtime, Sunday and holiday work, commissions (CST art. 127). Not salary: occasional gratuities, profit shares, tools and expenses to do the job, the prestaciones themselves, and benefits the parties expressly agree are not salary, such as meals, housing or extra-legal primas (CST art. 128). Those non-salary benefits still enter the health and pension IBC for whatever exceeds 40% of total pay (Ley 1393 art. 30).

**Contribution base (IBC).** The monthly salary under the CST (Ley 100 art. 18), never below 1 SMMLV and never above 25 SMMLV; 70% of a salario integral. Health uses the same base (Ley 100 art. 204 par. 1). ARL uses the pension base (Decreto-Ley 1295 art. 17). The parafiscales (caja, SENA, ICBF) are on the monthly payroll of salaries (nómina mensual de salarios; Ley 89 art. 1 par. 1 refers to Ley 21 de 1982 art. 17).

**The exoneration (art. 114-1 ET), step by step.**

1. Is the employer a company or legal person that files income tax, or an individual employing two or more workers? If not, there is no exoneration.
2. For each worker, are their monthly earnings under 10 SMMLV (COP 17,509,050)? If yes, the employer does not pay SENA 2%, ICBF 3% or the employer's 8.5% health share for that worker. The worker's 4% health and all pension, FSP, ARL and caja contributions stay.
3. Workers at 10 SMMLV or more: everything is paid (par. 1). Special-regime non-profits that must qualify under art. 19 do not get it (par. 2); income taxed at the art. 240 par. 8 rate does not get it (par. 6); free-zone users at the art. 240-1 rate and the other art. 240 parágrafo rates keep it (pars. 3, 4).
4. Related rule for the deduction: companies and legal persons that file need not show the parafiscal payments to deduct salaries of up to 10 SMMLV; above that, salaries are deductible only with parafiscales and Ley 100 contributions paid (art. 108 and par. 3 ET).

**AUDIT FLASH POINT.** The exoneration is per worker and per month. A raise, commission or bonus that takes a month's pay to 10 SMMLV or more switches on SENA, ICBF and employer health for that worker. Example 2 shows the cost of crossing the line.

**Withholding on wages (retención en la fuente), monthly.**

1. Take all taxable labour payments of the month (art. 385; DUR 1625 art. 1.2.4.1.6 par. 2), other than cesantías and their interest.
2. Subtract the ingresos no constitutivos: the employee's mandatory health and pension contributions, including the FSP (DUR art. 1.2.4.1.6(2), (3.1)); voluntary RAIS contributions up to 25% of income and 2,500 UVT a year (3.2).
3. Subtract the art. 387 deductions the worker certifies (dependants, prepaid medicine, home loan interest) and other exempt income.
4. Take the 25% exemption of art. 206(10) on what is left.
5. Check that deductions plus exempt income do not exceed 40% of step 1 minus step 2 (art. 388), nor 1,340 UVT a year (DUR art. 1.2.4.1.6 par. 3).
6. Convert the result to UVT, apply the art. 383 table, and convert the tax back to COP.

**Procedimiento 1 (art. 385)** applies the table each month to the month's payments. **Procedimiento 2 (art. 386)** fixes a percentage in June and December for the next six months: divide the taxable payments of the previous 12 months (without cesantías and interest) by 13, find the tax in the table, and divide that tax in UVT by the base in UVT (art. 383 par. 1). The percentage is then applied to each month's taxable pay. A new worker stays on the month-by-month calculation until the first semiannual calculation; with less than 12 months' service, divide by the months worked. The prima de servicios under procedure 1 is withheld on its own table bracket (art. 385). A worker may ask in writing for a higher rate, from the following month (art. 383 par. 3).

**Contractors.** For personal services without an employment contract, the art. 383 table applies to "pagos mensualizados": the contract value minus mandatory health and pension, divided by the months of the contract (art. 383 par. 2). They may take the 25% exemption if they declare in writing that they claim no costs (DUR art. 1.2.4.1.6(6)); the payer verifies their PILA payment (DUR art. 1.2.4.1.7).

**Prestaciones sociales.** Cesantías (one month of salary a year), interest on cesantías (12% a year), prima de servicios (30 days a year in two halves) and paid vacations (15 working days a year) are owed on top of salary. Under the optional monthly deposit, the base for cesantías is salary plus transport aid where due (Ley 50 art. 99 par. 1). A salario integral already includes the prestaciones except vacations (CST art. 132(2)).

The steps, in the order the employer meets them: affiliate each worker to an EPS (health), a pension fund, the employer's ARL and caja de compensación before work starts; run the monthly payroll and withhold; transmit the electronic payroll document to DIAN; file and pay PILA by the NIT business day; file and pay Form 350 by the NIT business day; pay the prima in June and December; deposit cesantías by 14 February. At year end, give each worker a certificate of income and withholding, which feeds their [annual return](colombia-income-tax.md).

## Penalties and interest

- **Late contributions.** Interest at the rate for late income tax (Ley 100 art. 23), that is, the consumer-credit usury rate minus 2 points, daily (ET arts. 634, 635; see [co-corporate-income-tax](co-corporate-income-tax.md)).
- **UGPP, not filing or under-paying (Ley 1607 de 2012, art. 179(1), as amended by Ley 1819 de 2016 art. 314).** After a UGPP request to file or correct: 5% of the unpaid contribution per month or part month, up to 100%. If not paid within the reply period: 10% per month, up to 200%, in the official assessment. No penalty if the employer files before the request.
- **UGPP, inaccuracy (art. 179(2)).** 35% of the difference if corrected after the request; 60% in the official assessment.
- **UGPP, information not supplied (art. 179(3), as amended by Ley 2010 de 2019 art. 121).** Up to 15,000 UVT (COP 785,610,000), reduced by 50% to 90% by income and by how fast the information is delivered.
- **Late cesantías deposit.** One day's salary per day late (Ley 50 art. 99(3)).
- **Lost deduction.** Salaries above 10 SMMLV are not deductible without the parafiscales and Ley 100 contributions paid (art. 108 ET).
- **Withholding returns.** The ET penalties for late filing (art. 641) and inaccuracy (art. 648) apply; see [co-corporate-income-tax](co-corporate-income-tax.md).

## Cross-border points

- **Foreign workers in Colombia under a Colombian contract** are on Colombian payroll like anyone else. Withholding uses the art. 383 table only for residents; payments to non-resident individuals follow the non-resident rules (35%, art. 247 ET). See [colombia-income-tax](colombia-income-tax.md).
- **Posted workers.** Whether a posted foreign worker can stay in a home-country system depends on a social security agreement; none is covered here (see "Not yet confirmed").
- **Payments abroad** for services by non-residents are not payroll; see the withholding of art. 408 ET in [co-corporate-income-tax](co-corporate-income-tax.md).

## Two worked examples that recompute

### Example 1: employee on COP 8,000,000 a month, exonerated employer

Laura works for a Bogotá SAS that files income tax. Her salary is COP 8,000,000 a month, ordinary (not integral). The SAS is ARL class I. She claims no dependants, prepaid medicine or home loan. Procedure 1.

- Employee contributions: health 4% = 320,000; pension 4% = 320,000; FSP 1% = 80,000 (8,000,000 is above 4 SMMLV of 7,003,620). Total COP 720,000.
- Withholding base: 8,000,000 minus 720,000 = 7,280,000. 25% exemption = 1,820,000 (well under the 40% cap of 2,912,000; over 12 months, 21,840,000 is under 790 UVT, COP 41,375,460). Taxable 5,460,000 = 104.25 UVT.
- Tax: (104.25 minus 95) x 19% = 1.7575 UVT = **COP 92,049** withheld.
- Net pay: 8,000,000 minus 720,000 minus 92,049 = **COP 7,187,951**.
- Employer: pension 12% = 960,000; ARL 0.522% = 41,760; caja 4% = 320,000; health, SENA and ICBF = 0 (exonerated, she earns under 10 SMMLV). Total **COP 1,321,760**.
- Monthly accruals: cesantías 666,667; interest on cesantías 80,000; prima 666,667.

```oa-check
name: CO payroll 2026, COP 8,000,000 salary, exonerated employer, procedure 1
inputs:
  uvt: 52374
  smmlv: 1750905
  salary: 8000000
  arl_rate: 0.00522
steps:
  - health_emp: salary * 0.04
  - pension_emp: salary * 0.04
  - fsp: salary * 0.01
  - employee_contrib: health_emp + pension_emp + fsp
  - net_income: salary - employee_contrib
  - exempt25: min(net_income * 0.25, 790 * uvt / 12)
  - taxable: net_income - exempt25
  - withholding: (taxable / uvt - 95) * 0.19 * uvt
  - net_pay: salary - employee_contrib - withholding
  - employer_pension: salary * 0.12
  - employer_arl: salary * arl_rate
  - employer_caja: salary * 0.04
  - employer_total: employer_pension + employer_arl + employer_caja
  - cesantias_month: salary / 12
  - intereses_month: cesantias_month * 0.12
  - prima_month: salary / 12
expected:
  employee_contrib: 720000
  net_income: 7280000
  exempt25: 1820000
  taxable: 5460000
  withholding: 92049.30
  net_pay: 7187950.70
  employer_total: 1321760
  cesantias_month: 666666.67
  intereses_month: 80000
  prima_month: 666666.67
```

The `790 * uvt / 12` step only checks that a twelfth of the annual cap is not reached; the law states the cap as annual.

### Example 2: a raise across 10 SMMLV (a decision)

A company that files income tax considers paying a manager COP 17,600,000 a month instead of COP 17,500,000. Ordinary salary, ARL class I. The line is 10 SMMLV = COP 17,509,050.

- **At 17,500,000 (under the line, exonerated):** pension 12% = 2,100,000; ARL 0.522% = 91,350; caja 4% = 700,000. Employer contributions **COP 2,891,350**.
- **At 17,600,000 (over the line):** pension 12% = 2,112,000; ARL = 91,872; caja = 704,000; plus health 8.5% = 1,496,000, SENA 2% = 352,000, ICBF 3% = 528,000. Employer contributions **COP 5,283,872**.
- **What decides it:** the extra COP 100,000 of salary costs the employer **COP 2,492,522** more a month. Unless the raise is large enough to justify losing the exoneration, keep monthly pay, including variable pay, under COP 17,509,050, or pay well above it.

```oa-check
name: CO payroll 2026, raise across the 10 SMMLV exoneration line
inputs:
  smmlv: 1750905
  salary_a: 17500000
  salary_b: 17600000
  arl_rate: 0.00522
steps:
  - line: smmlv * 10
  - cost_a: salary_a * 0.12 + salary_a * arl_rate + salary_a * 0.04
  - cost_b: salary_b * 0.12 + salary_b * arl_rate + salary_b * 0.04 + salary_b * (0.085 + 0.02 + 0.03)
  - extra_cost: (salary_b + cost_b) - (salary_a + cost_a)
expected:
  line: 17509050
  cost_a: 2891350
  cost_b: 5283872
  extra_cost: 2492522
```

## Ask the client first

1. Is the employer a company or legal person that files income tax, or an individual, and how many workers does it have (art. 114-1 exoneration)?
2. Each worker's monthly pay: fixed salary, variable pay, non-salary benefits agreed in writing, and whether the salary is integral.
3. The ARL risk class of each workplace or worker.
4. The worker's certified deductions for withholding: dependants, prepaid medicine, home loan interest, voluntary pension or AFC savings; and whether the employer uses procedure 1 or 2.
5. The employer's NIT (its last digits set the PILA and Form 350 dates) and whether it is already enabled for electronic payroll.
6. Whether any worker is a contractor rather than an employee, or a posted foreign worker covered by a social security agreement.
