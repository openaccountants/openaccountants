# Ecuador: company formation and entity choice (2026) v0.1

> **General reference only.** This skill is general tax/accounting reference material for AI-assisted workflows. It has not been reviewed for any specific person's facts, documents, elections, deadlines, residency, filing status, or local procedures. Do not rely on it to file, pay, amend, or take a tax position without review by a qualified professional in the relevant jurisdiction.

This skill sets up a company in Ecuador: which form to pick, what capital to pay in, and the steps from name reservation to RUC. Tax follows in the sibling skills: [ec-corporate-income-tax](ec-corporate-income-tax.md) (25% rate, dividends), [ecuador-iva](ecuador-iva.md), [ecuador-payroll](ecuador-payroll.md) and [ec-tax-overview](ec-tax-overview.md).

**Which texts.** The Ley de Compañías (Codificación, RO 312 of 5 November 1999), in the consolidated copy published on gob.ec with last reform Tercer Suplemento RO 347 of 10 December 2020 (the Ley Orgánica de Emprendimiento e Innovación of RO Supl. 151, 28 February 2020 added the S.A.S.); the SCVS procedure pages on the government portal gob.ec. Later reforms of the Ley de Compañías were not read (see Sources).

## Who this applies to

- **Founders** (individuals or companies, Ecuadorian or foreign) who want a commercial company in Ecuador. There are six kinds: the general partnership (compañía en nombre colectivo), the limited partnership (en comandita simple and por acciones), the compañía de responsabilidad limitada, the compañía anónima, the mixed-economy company and the sociedad por acciones simplificada (S.A.S.). All six are legal persons (Ley de Compañías art. 2).
- **Foreign companies** may be partners or shareholders only if their capital is represented by registered (nominative) shares or participations, never bearer (arts. 100, 145). Foreign company founders file a certificate of legal existence and a notarised, apostilled list of their partners down to the natural persons (SCVS electronic incorporation page).
- **Not allowed**: companies contrary to the Constitution or the law, without a real and lawful object, or without economic substance (art. 3). A compañía limitada may not do banking, insurance, capitalisation or savings (art. 94); an S.A.S. may not do financial, securities, insurance or other specially regulated activities (S.A.S. section).
- **Out of scope**: branches of foreign companies (art. 6 and following, not read), regulated sectors with special capital (prepaid medicine, air transport, private security; see the SCVS page), mixed-economy companies.

## The base

**Choosing the form.**

| | Compañía limitada | Sociedad anónima | S.A.S. |
|---|---|---|---|
| Founders | 2 to 15 (art. 92, 95) | may continue with a single shareholder (art. 147); the number needed at formation was not confirmed | one or more (S.A.S. section) |
| Liability | up to each partner's contribution (art. 92) | up to the shares held (art. 143) | up to the contributions; a shareholder may waive it in writing (S.A.S. section) |
| Capital at formation | fully subscribed, at least 50% of each participation paid, rest within 12 months (art. 102) | fully subscribed, at least a quarter paid (art. 147) | no minimum; cash paid within 24 months; in kind paid in full (S.A.S. section) |
| Units | participations, transferable under art. 113 | negotiable shares (art. 143) | shares of USD 1 or multiples; cannot be listed or traded on a stock exchange (S.A.S. section) |
| How formed | public deed registered in the Registro Mercantil (art. 96) | public deed registered in the Registro Mercantil (art. 146) | private document registered in the SCVS Registro de Sociedades (S.A.S. section) |
| Legal existence from | registration in the Registro Mercantil (art. 96) | registration; may operate only once it has its RUC (art. 146) | registration with the SCVS (S.A.S. section) |

For tax, all three are sociedades: the compañía limitada is a capital company for tax purposes (art. 97) and the corporate rules apply (see ec-corporate-income-tax).

**Capital paid in, step by step.** For a compañía limitada or anónima the founders declare under oath that they will deposit the cash capital in a bank; the SCVS checks it later through the opening balance sheet (arts. 103, 147). Contributions in kind are valued by the partners or by experts they appoint (art. 104).

**Electronic incorporation, step by step (compañía limitada or anónima).**

1. On www.supercias.gob.ec choose the Portal de Constitución Electrónica de Compañías, then Constituir una compañía, and log in.
2. Select the name reservation to use.
3. Fill in the Solicitud de Constitución de Compañías: company data, partners or shareholders, the table of subscription and payment of capital, legal representatives. Attach the documents the system asks for (colour scans, 200 dpi at most, 20 MB in total).
4. Pick the notary and check the cost (notary deed and two appointments, plus Registro Mercantil fees; the system shows them by capital and number of representatives).
5. Accept the terms and start; the system confirms with a procedure number and e-mails the amounts to pay at Banco del Pacífico.
6. After payment the notary reviews the file and books an appointment to sign the deed and appointments (up to three appointments, at most 5 business days apart). With an own minuta, the sponsoring lawyer brings the signed original.
7. The notary dematerialises the documents and sends them to the Registro Mercantil; once registered, the SRI issues the RUC and the SCVS confirms the incorporation by e-mail.

Electronic incorporation is only for compañías anónimas and limitadas whose capital is in cash or movable assets (not vehicles or real estate) and which are not in the securities market (SCVS page).

**S.A.S., step by step.** Reserve the name; download the SCVS model act (one founder) or contract (two or more) and the appointment of the legal representative; sign them (electronically for the electronic or semi-electronic routes); file them with the request, the list of shareholders and administrators and copies of the identity documents; the SCVS registers the S.A.S. and the appointment and sends the registration notes by e-mail (SCVS S.A.S. page).

## Cross-border points

- **Foreign shareholders**: allowed if their capital is in registered shares or participations (arts. 100, 145). A foreign company shareholder resident in a tax haven or preferential regime must attach its RUC document when incorporating electronically (SCVS page).
- **Beneficial owners**: the annual list sent to the SCVS includes legal owners and beneficial owners (art. 20(b)). Reporting the ownership chain also drives the corporate tax surcharge and the dividend rates: see ec-corporate-income-tax and ec-cross-border.
- **Galápagos**: companies domiciled there need a permanent-resident qualification and 51% of the investment from the permanent resident (SCVS page).

## Two worked examples that recompute

### Example 1: capital to pay in at formation, three forms

Three founders plan USD 10,000 of cash capital.

- **Compañía limitada**: at least 50% of each participation at formation = **USD 5,000**; USD 5,000 within 12 months (art. 102).
- **Sociedad anónima**: at least a quarter = **USD 2,500** once registered (art. 147).
- **S.A.S.**: no fixed minimum at formation; the statutes set the timing, with everything paid within 24 months (S.A.S. section). With shares of USD 1, the USD 10,000 is **10,000 shares**.
- **SCVS contribution**: if the company's real assets later reach USD 250,000, the contribution can be at most 1 per thousand = **USD 250** a year (art. 449).

**Which to pick.** One founder alone: the S.A.S., which the law lets one person form. Little cash at the start: the S.A.S., with up to 24 months to pay. Shares to be offered on a stock exchange later: not an S.A.S. (its shares cannot be listed). More than 15 partners: not a compañía limitada.

## Ask the client first

1. How many founders, individuals or companies, and are any foreign (registered shares only) or in a tax haven?
2. How much capital, in cash or in kind (real estate or vehicles rule out electronic incorporation)?
3. The business activity: is it regulated (finance, insurance, securities, prepaid medicine, transport, private security)?
4. Will the company ever list shares or need more than 15 partners?
5. Do all founders and representatives have an electronic signature (for the fully electronic routes)?
6. Where is the domicile (canton enabled for electronic incorporation; Galápagos rules)?
