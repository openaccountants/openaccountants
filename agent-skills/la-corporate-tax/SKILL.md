# Louisiana corporate income and franchise tax, 2026

> **Built from the law. Not yet approved.** Written by Codex; this version has not been reviewed by an accountant. General reference only. Under Circular 230 section 10.37, apply the cited authorities to the actual facts and obtain qualified professional review before filing or acting.

**Answer first:** Ordinary C-corporation Louisiana taxable income is taxed at 5.5%. Franchise tax is repealed for periods beginning January 1, 2026 or later; prior-period balances remain payable. Corporate income tax remains. [R.S.47:287.12](https://legis.la.gov/legis/Law.aspx?d=101674); [LDR corporate/franchise page](https://revenue.louisiana.gov/businesses/widely-used-tax-types/corporate-income-franchise-tax).

## Scope

Louisiana domestic and Louisiana-source foreign C corporations, including LLCs taxed as corporations. This is the sole state corporate/franchise owner. It covers ordinary income reconciliation and estimates; specialist entity elections, exempt industries, NOLs and complex apportionment require review. Federal corporate computations belong to `us-form-1120-c-corp`; federal depreciation to `us-section-179-and-bonus-depreciation`.

## Ask the client first

1. Entity formation, federal classification, Louisiana activity, accounting period, S election/PTE acceptance and related entities?
2. Separate-entity federal income before special deductions, Louisiana income-tax expense and complete state reconciliation/asset-basis schedules?
3. Louisiana/everywhere receipts, customer delivery/market facts, industry, allocable rents/royalties and direct/overhead expenses?
4. State NOL vintages, prior deductions/credits, supporting schedules, estimated payments and outstanding pre-2026 franchise periods?
5. Current LDR account/form, federal extension, payment record and IRS adjustments or amended returns?

## The method, step by step

1. **Classify and establish the period.** Domestic corporations and foreign corporations with Louisiana-source income have filing obligations; a loss/dormant year does not automatically close the account. Use a separate Louisiana entity computation even where the federal return is consolidated. For periods beginning in 2026 S corporations instead file an annual information return; optional nonresident composite filing and a separately accepted PTE election are distinct routes. Do not tax every S corporation at 5.5%. [R.S.47:287.732](https://legis.la.gov/legis/Law.aspx?d=101720); [CIT-620 instructions pp.1-2](https://dam.ldr.la.gov/taxforms/Cit620i-2025-revised%204-10-2026%20FINAL.pdf).
2. **Reconcile federal and state income.** Start from separate-entity federal net income before special deductions. Add back Louisiana income-tax deductions and other disallowed federal deductions. Federal NOL and dividends-received deductions are not simply imported: apply Louisiana's own adjustments. Reconcile permitted state exclusions and asset elections, keeping no double deduction. [R.S.47:287.73](https://legis.la.gov/legis/Law.aspx?d=101719); [R.S.47:287.71](https://legis.la.gov/legis/Law.aspx?d=101718). The corporate $20,000 subtraction is applied in the all-source state reconciliation, limited to positive modified income under the opened instructions; it does not create a loss or belong again after apportionment. [CIT-620 Schedule F line3f](https://dam.ldr.la.gov/taxforms/Cit620i-2025-revised%204-10-2026%20FINAL.pdf).
3. **Allocate and apportion.** Separate allocable classes and their expenses from apportionable income. Ordinary manufacturing/merchandising and other businesses within R.S.47:287.95(F) generally use the statutory single sales ratio; service-enterprise and special-industry rules are separate. Market-source services under (L), retain delivery/billing/commercial-domicile evidence as the applicable customer rule requires, and refer unusual allocations rather than using payroll presence as the sales factor. [R.S.47:287.95, including neighbouring special industries](https://legis.la.gov/legis/Law.aspx?d=101753). Louisiana net income = Louisiana net allocable income + apportionable income x properly determined factor. Apply the Louisiana NOL adjustment separately to obtain Louisiana taxable income. [R.S.47:287.69](https://legis.la.gov/legis/Law.aspx?d=101716).
4. **Calculate liability.** Ordinary corporate gross income tax = positive Louisiana taxable income x 0.055. Apply substantiated state credits and reconcile estimates/payment to the return. A PTE-election rate is a different computation, not an alternative for a C corporation. For 2026 periods do not create a franchise tax charge; separately settle the unrepealed prior periods. [R.S.47:287.12](https://legis.la.gov/legis/Law.aspx?d=101674); [LDR repeal guidance](https://revenue.louisiana.gov/businesses/widely-used-tax-types/corporate-income-franchise-tax).
5. **Check estimates and return readiness.** Expected income tax after credits of $1,000 or more requires estimates. Use the current CIT-620ES worksheet; ordinary calendar-year installments are April 15, June 15, September 15 and December 15, 2026. Change the remaining installments when the estimate changes; late estimates can incur the statutory underpayment charge. [2026 corporation estimate instructions](https://dam.ldr.la.gov/taxforms/CIT-620ESi-2026.pdf).

## Worked cases

### All-Louisiana C corporation

Assume $120,000 separate-entity federal net income before special deductions, including a $5,000 Louisiana income-tax deduction, and no other differences, allocation, NOLs or credits. Modified all-source income is $125,000; subtract $20,000 to get $105,000. All income is Louisiana, so tax is $5,775; four equal estimates are $1,443.75. Franchise tax for a period beginning 2026 is zero; that does not cancel an older balance.

```oa-check
name: LA C corporation reconciliation
inputs:
  federal_net: 120000
  state_tax_deduction: 5000
steps:
  - modified: federal_net + state_tax_deduction
  - la_taxable: max(0, modified - min(20000, modified))
  - income_tax: la_taxable * 0.055
  - installment: income_tax / 4
expected:
  la_taxable: 105000
  income_tax: 5775
  installment: 1443.75
```

### Apply the deduction before the agreed factor

Assume $220,000 modified all-source income, no allocable income/NOL, and an independently established ordinary sales factor of 40%. Deduct $20,000 first: $200,000 x 40% gives $80,000 Louisiana taxable income and $4,400 gross tax. A $1,000 valid credit leaves $3,400, or four $850 estimates. Deducting $20,000 again after the factor would understate tax.

```oa-check
name: LA corporate factor placement
inputs:
  modified: 220000
  factor: 0.40
  credit: 1000
steps:
  - apportioned: max(0, modified - 20000) * factor
  - gross: apportioned * 0.055
  - net: gross - credit
  - installment: net / 4
expected:
  apportioned: 80000
  gross: 4400
  net: 3400
  installment: 850
```

## When to refuse or refer

Refer S/composite/PTE elections, NOLs, oil/gas and other special industries, related-party addbacks, qualified full expensing, incentives, disputed nexus and alternative apportionment. Do not infer exemption from federal flow-through status, use the older S-exclusion rules for a 2026 period, or reuse a prior franchise base.

## Filing and payment

The general corporate return/payment rule is the fifteenth day of the fifth month after year end, next business day for weekends/holidays. LDR describes a six-month filing extension with timely federal extension, while payment remains due originally. Use LaTAP and retain the account acknowledgment. [LDR corporate filing guidance](https://revenue.louisiana.gov/businesses/widely-used-tax-types/corporate-income-franchise-tax).

The opened **2025-2026 filing cycle** CIT-620 instructions (revised April 2026) cover 2025 corporation income and limited 2026 S information periods. They map Schedule F federal/state reconciliation, Schedule B factor, Schedule D allocation/apportionment and Schedule H corporate tax, with no franchise schedule. They also identify the new annual S information/composite regime. Final calendar-2026 corporate forms/line numbers are a gap; do not file a 2025 income form as a 2026 C-corporation return. The older CIFT-620 name is retained only as a prior-form topic. [CIT-620 instructions](https://dam.ldr.la.gov/taxforms/Cit620i-2025-revised%204-10-2026%20FINAL.pdf).

## Completion checklist

Confirm period/entity and filing requirement, preserve separate-entity federal bridge, place the corporate subtraction correctly, substantiate factor/NOL/credits, remove only current-period franchise tax, and reconcile estimates and filing/payment dates to the current account.
