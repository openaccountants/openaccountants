# US entity formation by state: LLC and corporation, 2026

> **Source-cited draft.** Figures are taken only from the state's own fee schedule or statute, or from the state formation skills rewritten on 13 November 2026, read on 2026-11-13. Every other state's row links its business filing office and gives no figure. No accountant or lawyer has reviewed it. Entity choice needs a qualified professional.

**Answer first.** For a small business that operates in one state, form in that state: forming in Delaware, Wyoming or Nevada and then operating at home means paying both states, because the entity must also register as a foreign entity where it does business. Delaware is the default for a company that will raise venture capital. A California resident running an LLC from California owes the California USD 800 annual tax wherever the LLC was formed. US companies no longer file beneficial ownership reports with FinCEN.

## Scope and who this is for

In scope: forming an LLC or for-profit corporation in a US state or DC, its annual state filings and minimum taxes, and registering as a foreign entity in other states. Out of scope: nonprofits, benefit corporations, limited partnerships and LLLPs, statutory trusts, securities law on issuing equity, and the federal tax classification of the entity.

## Ask the client first

1. Where do the owners live, and where will the business actually operate (staff, office, inventory, real estate)?
2. Will it raise outside equity (angel or venture) in the next two years?
3. One owner or several? Any non-US owners?
4. Is the work a licensed profession (law, medicine, accounting, engineering, architecture)?
5. Does it hold real estate, and where?
6. Does it plan several separate ventures or properties under one umbrella (series LLC question)?
7. Is it already formed somewhere, and has it registered in the states where it operates?

## The method, step by step

1. **List the states where the business will be "doing business".** Employees working there, an office, shop or warehouse, real estate, and repeated in-person sales or services usually count. Internet sales into a state from outside usually do not, by themselves, require foreign registration (they may still create sales tax nexus). A bank account, one visit, defending a lawsuit or holding a trademark generally do not. Each state has its own statute: check it.
2. **Choose the formation state.**

| Situation | Usual choice |
| --- | --- |
| Venture-backed corporation seeking institutional capital | Delaware |
| Single-member LLC operating in one state | Home state |
| Multi-state operating business, no venture plans | State of the headquarters |
| Pure holding company with no operations anywhere | Wyoming (low cost) or Delaware (case law) |
| Real estate held in state X | State X |
| Licensed profession | Home state PLLC or PC, where the professional is licensed |

3. **Add up both states' costs** where the entity is formed in one state and operates in another: formation state fees and annual taxes, plus the foreign registration and annual filings at home, plus a registered agent in each.
4. **Register as a foreign entity** before starting to do business in each other state.
5. **Diary the annual filings** in every state (annual report, statement of information, minimum or franchise tax), and the formation-state anniversary dates.
6. **Check special rules:** New York LLC publication; California's USD 800 annual tax and Statement of Information; professional entity rules; series LLC recognition.

### Failing to register as a foreign entity

- The entity usually cannot bring a lawsuit in that state's courts until it registers and pays back fees and penalties; it can still be sued.
- Back fees, annual filings and minimum taxes for every year it was doing business there, with interest and penalties.
- California: an unregistered, suspended or forfeited entity doing business there faces a USD 2,000 penalty per taxable year after 60 days from notice and demand (`ca-formation`).

### New York LLC publication

A New York LLC must publish a notice in two newspapers designated by the county clerk of the county of its office, for six consecutive weeks, within 120 days of formation, then file a certificate of publication (USD 50). Until it does, its authority to carry on business in New York is suspended. Publication costs vary widely by county; the county is the one where the LLC's office is located. See `ny-formation`.

### Series LLCs

Several states have series LLC statutes (Delaware, Illinois, Texas, Nevada, Utah and others). A series may not be respected in a state without such a statute, bankruptcy treatment is untested, and banks often refuse accounts at series level. The federal classification of a series is still only proposed (Prop. Reg. 301.7701-1, 2010). Confirm the statute in each state before relying on it.

### Professional entities

Most states require licensed professionals to use a professional LLC or professional corporation, owned by licensees. California does not allow LLCs for most licensed professions; those professionals use a professional corporation. A professional entity generally cannot register in a state where its owners are not licensed, so a multi-state practice often needs an entity per state.

### Beneficial ownership information (Corporate Transparency Act)

FinCEN's final rule of 11 August 2026 made permanent the exemptions in the March 2025 interim final rule: **US companies are exempt from beneficial ownership reporting and no longer file BOI reports**. Foreign companies registered to do business in a US state remain reporting companies: read FinCEN's BOI page for their deadlines and what they report. The old file's position that reporting was stayed by an injunction is out of date.

## Boundary and exception table

| Situation | Treatment |
| --- | --- |
| Wyoming or Delaware LLC owned and run by a California resident from California | Doing business in California: register as a foreign LLC and pay the California USD 800 annual tax and LLC fee every year |
| Remote seller over a state's sales tax threshold | Sales tax registration; foreign registration is a separate question and often not required |
| One employee working from home in another state | Usually doing business there: register |
| Delaware LLC with no Delaware activity | Still owes the Delaware USD 400 annual tax by 1 June |
| Florida annual report filed after 1 May | Higher fee (LLC USD 538.75, profit corporation USD 550) |
| Texas entity with revenue below USD 2,650,000 | No franchise tax due, but the Public Information Report is still filed |
| New York LLC that never published | Authority to carry on business suspended until it publishes |
| Foreign-owned US LLC | Separate federal reporting (Form 5472): see `us-form-5472-foreign-owned-us` |

## Worked cases

**Case 1, the California trap.** A California resident formed a Wyoming LLC in 2023 and has run a consulting business from San Francisco ever since, never registering in California. Doing business in California from 2023 means the USD 800 annual tax for 2023, 2024, 2025 and 2026: USD 3,200. Add the foreign LLC registration (USD 70) and a Statement of Information (USD 20): USD 3,290, before the LLC fee on California income, late penalties, interest and the Wyoming fees paid in parallel.

```oa-check
name: California-resident owner of a Wyoming LLC, four years unregistered
inputs:
  years: 4
  annual_tax: 800
  foreign_registration: 70
  statement_of_information: 20
steps:
  - annual_tax_total: years * annual_tax
  - total: annual_tax_total + foreign_registration + statement_of_information
expected:
  annual_tax_total: 3200
  total: 3290
```

**Case 2, a decision: Texas founder, Wyoming LLC or Texas LLC?** A founder living and working in Austin compares two years of state filing costs (registered agent fees left out). Wyoming route: Wyoming articles USD 100, one Wyoming annual report at the USD 60 minimum, and Texas foreign registration USD 750, because the LLC is doing business in Texas: USD 910. Texas route: certificate of formation USD 300, and the Public Information Report has no fee: USD 300. The Texas LLC is USD 610 cheaper, and Texas franchise tax treats both the same. Choose Texas unless there is a reason outside cost.

```oa-check
name: Texas founder, two-year state filing cost, Wyoming against Texas LLC
inputs:
  wy_articles: 100
  wy_annual: 60
  tx_foreign: 750
  tx_formation: 300
steps:
  - wyoming_route: wy_articles + wy_annual + tx_foreign
  - texas_route: tx_formation
  - saving: wyoming_route - texas_route
expected:
  wyoming_route: 910
  texas_route: 300
  saving: 610
```

## Filing and payment

Formation documents and annual reports are filed with the state's business filing office (linked in the table); most file online. Minimum or franchise taxes may be paid to a tax department instead (California to the FTB, Texas to the Comptroller). Key dates confirmed: Delaware corporation annual report and franchise tax by 1 March, Delaware LLC tax by 1 June, Florida annual report by 1 May, Texas Public Information Report by 15 May.

## Completion checklist

- Every state where the entity does business listed, and foreign registration done before starting there.
- Formation state chosen with both states' costs added up.
- Annual filing and minimum tax dates diarised in each state.
- New York publication, California Statement of Information and USD 800, and Texas Public Information Report handled where relevant.
- Professional entity rules checked for licensed work.
- BOI: US company confirmed exempt; a foreign company checked against FinCEN's current rule.
