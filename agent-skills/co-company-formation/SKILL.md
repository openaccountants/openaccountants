# Colombia company formation and entity choice, 2026 v0.1

> **General reference only.** This skill is general tax/accounting reference material for AI-assisted workflows. It has not been reviewed for any specific person's facts, documents, elections, deadlines, residency, filing status, or local procedures. Do not rely on it to file, pay, amend, or take a tax position without review by a qualified professional in the relevant jurisdiction.

**Quick answer.** An ordinary Colombian SAS can have one or several natural or legal-person shareholders. It becomes a separate legal person when its formation document is registered in the commercial registry. A private document still needs prior authentication; assets requiring a public deed change the formation formality. Separate authorised, subscribed and paid capital, commercial registration, RUT/NIT, invoicing readiness and bank onboarding. SAS form alone does not select SIMPLE or establish VAT and withholding obligations.

This Guide covers ordinary private-sector formation and ongoing-compliance handoff in calendar 2026, with amounts in Colombian pesos (COP). It does not promise that incorporation and a bank account are completed in a fixed number of days. The statutory auditor test for a duty in 2026 normally uses the 2025 accounts and the 2025 minimum wage, not the new 2026 wage.

## Who this applies to

Founders comparing a Colombian subsidiary, an ordinary SAS, other company forms and a foreign-company branch. The detailed filing route below is the ordinary SAS route; alternative forms remain separate legal choices requiring their own formation rules.

| Route | Conditions and consequences | Official source |
|---|---|---|
| SAS | One or several natural/legal-person shareholders; contribution-limited liability subject to art. 42 fraud/prejudice exceptions; distinct legal person on registration | [Ley 1258 arts. 1-2,42](https://relatoria.colombiacompra.gov.co/normativa/ley-1258-de-2008/) |
| SAS governance | At least one legal representative; a natural or legal person can hold the role. A board is optional unless the articles require it | [Ley 1258 arts. 5(7),25-26](https://relatoria.colombiacompra.gov.co/normativa/ley-1258-de-2008/) |
| S.A. or Ltda. | Alternative corporate forms governed by their Commerce Code provisions; do not import SAS capital, authentication or auditor rules automatically | [Código de Comercio, arts. 353 onward and 373 onward](https://normativa.colpensiones.gov.co/compilacion/docs/codigo_comercio.htm) |
| Foreign-company branch | A branch of the foreign company, with its own Colombian registration and representation requirements, rather than a separate Colombian subsidiary | [Código de Comercio arts. 469-472](https://normativa.colpensiones.gov.co/compilacion/docs/codigo_comercio.htm) |

Foreign shareholders are not excluded by the ordinary SAS natural/legal-person founder rule. Supersociedades Oficio 220-213874/2017 confirms that general corporate law does not require the representative of a Colombian company to reside in Colombia; the governing body must ensure the duties can actually be performed. This administrative interpretation does not remove immigration, tax-residence, sectoral investment or exchange-control rules. No primary registration statistic is used to call SAS “by far the most common” vehicle.

Out of scope: regulated sectors, foreign-investment/exchange reporting, immigration, reorganisations, disputes and public securities offers; special public/private contributions; Escalera de la Formalidad program tax bands; department-specific fee quotes. The current SAS securities provision has been amended since the original law: do not repeat an old blanket prohibition as current guidance.

## The base

**Capital.** Record authorised capital, shares subscribed, paid amount, premium and payment terms separately (Ley 1258 art. 5(6)). Do not use authorised capital as though all of it had been subscribed or paid. Article 9's payment maximum does not set a universal initial contribution percentage. The absence of a general SAS minimum in those provisions is not a statement that a bank or regulated business has no funding requirements.

**Registration tax.** Ley 223 art. 229 uses the value of the act; for formation/increases, the total contribution includes the relevant social/subscribed capital and share premium. Where the act must be registered both in the Chamber and property registry, tax arises only at the property-registry instance. Real-estate base floors and public/private contribution rules require their own application under art. 229. Obtain the actual departmental ordinance and liquidation; the ranges above are not a universal final invoice.

Microenterprise treatment depends on the statutory classification. Article 230 para. 2 forbids surcharges/recargos for qualifying microenterprises. The law also contains departmental FONCOMDIGNIDAD financing provisions and separate program bands; identify those before quoting a non-micro total. Chamber registration/renewal duties, document authentication and professional/bank fees are separate from registration tax. No single all-in percentage was verified.

**Beneficial owners.** An ordinary domestic private SAS must assess and supply RUB information under current DIAN Res. 227 arts. 1.4.1.4-18. Identify natural persons with direct/indirect individual or joint ownership/voting rights or economic benefit at least 5%, and persons controlling by other means; use the representative/higher-management fallback only where the preceding criteria identify no person, not as a shortcut around due diligence. Update RUT with the Informante Beneficiarios Finales responsibility before the electronic RUB submission. Supply the required identity, residence, criterion, participation/benefit and effective-date data through DIAN's RUB system; retain evidence and the acknowledgement. Under art. 1.4.1.18, keep supplied-information and due-diligence supports throughout the identified person’s beneficial-owner status and for at least five years after the change that ends it; on liquidation the liquidator’s separate minimum five-year clock starts on 1 January of the following year. The table's two-month initial deadline concerns a newly formed ordinary entity; changes follow the specified quarterly review rule. Complex chains and exceptional entities require their actual criteria, not guessed ownership.

**Annual compliance.** Prepare the financial statements and approvals required by Ley 1258 arts. 28 and 37; a SAS auditor exemption does not mean no accounts. Article 28 requires profits to be supported by generally accepted accounting financial statements with the independent public accountant's opinion; art. 37 governs presentation/approval by the representative and assembly or sole shareholder. Test revisor fiscal duties each year, including special law. Route ordinary corporate income-tax obligations, regimes and filing dates to [co-corporate-income-tax](co-corporate-income-tax.md). IVA and withholding returns depend on actual responsibilities, not the SAS label: use [co-vat-return](co-vat-return.md), [colombia-payroll](colombia-payroll.md) and the corporate Guide. Exógena reporting is a separate current-resolution test under ET art. 631, not an invented universal threshold. Use [formalizing-small-informal-businesses-in-colombia](formalizing-small-informal-businesses-in-colombia.md) for the separate SIMPLE/formalisation decision.

## Deadlines and filing

1. **Choose and document the entity.** Identify shareholders, capacity, domicile, activity, assets and intended governance. For an ordinary SAS prepare the private formation document with Ley 1258 art. 5's name/S.A.S., identities, domicile, duration, activities, capital/share terms, administration and representative. If transferred assets require a public deed, formation must use that form and the corresponding asset registers.
2. **Authenticate before registration.** Article 5 paragraph 1 requires authentication by the signatories, directly or through an attorney. “Private document” does not mean “no authentication”. Confirm the actual Chamber's accepted in-person/virtual mechanism; do not assert a universal notary appointment or that no formal identity step is needed.
3. **Submit at the principal-domicile Chamber.** Apply through the competent Cámara de Comercio with the formation and appointment records. The Chamber checks legal conformity under art. 6 and can refuse incomplete documents. Pay the actual registry-tax liquidation and separate duties. Until registration, art. 7 exposes a sole founder personally or treats multiple associates as a de facto company; a saved application is not legal-person evidence.
4. **Use the current combined RUT route where applicable.** Under DIAN Res. 227/2025 arts. 1.1.1.1-5, provide RUT data in the RUES form administered by the Chamber. For this legal entity, the legal representative or duly authorised attorney can apply using its virtual or in-person mechanisms. Supply the required tax-registration documents and identity/authentication evidence; the Chamber sends information through DIAN interoperability. DIAN validates, formalises registration, assigns NIT and sends the record by the recorded email route. Do not require a separate DIAN visit as a universal second step.
5. **Verify the resulting records.** Obtain the Chamber certificate of existence/representation under art. 8, check the RUT's activity/responsibility/contact details and NIT, and retain the actual acknowledgements. RUT is the tax identification/classification registry; NIT is the identification number, not a separate company type. ET art. 555-2 requires RUT registration before economic activity begins. Document/invoicing authorisation is a further responsibility check.
6. **Arrange operating readiness.** Confirm invoicing requirements, bank onboarding, local/sector permissions, staff/social-security registration and foreign-investment requirements separately. No uniform five-day or five-to-ten-day end-to-end deadline was established. Sequence the actual approvals and identify who holds each acknowledgement rather than promise bank access from incorporation alone.
7. **Keep the entity current.** Schedule annual commercial renewal, financial statements/approvals, auditor reassessment, initial RUB reporting and quarterly change checks/updates, actual tax returns/withholding/IVA, exógena and corporate changes. Read the current year's DIAN calendar and responsibilities, rather than use a formation date as every future deadline. Preserve the capital-payment schedule and evidence of each instalment.

## Penalties and interest

Pre-registration exposure and fraud/prejudice liability are in Ley 1258 arts. 7 and 42. Incorrect/incomplete statutory documents can be refused under art. 6. For acts without a more specific registration period, Ley 223 art. 231 sets domestic/foreign-document timing and late-registration interest by reference to the ET mechanism; the actual case and current rate must be checked rather than inventing a uniform penalty. Tax late-registration/return/withholding sanctions belong to their owning current tax Guides. No paper should be backdated to imply earlier legal personality or compliance.

## Cross-border points

Distinguish the Colombian SAS from its foreign shareholder and a foreign branch. A representative's permitted foreign residence does not settle corporate effective-management tax residence, permanent establishment, signing powers, immigration or exchange reporting. Use [colombia-cross-border](colombia-cross-border.md) for those tax facts and [colombia-transfer-pricing](colombia-transfer-pricing.md) for controlled dealings. The general corporate-law interpretation is not a licence to ignore a sector's special local-presence rules.

## Two worked examples that recompute

### 1. Capital reconciliation, not a registration-tax quote

A sole shareholder authorises COP 100,000,000, subscribes COP 60,000,000 represented by 600 shares at COP 100,000, and pays COP 20,000,000. Unsubscribed authorised capital is COP 40,000,000; subscribed unpaid capital is COP 40,000,000. Record the actual payment term within art. 9's maximum. These are illustrative inputs, not a legal minimum. Do not compute registry tax on all COP 100 million automatically: first establish art. 229's relevant contribution/premium and the actual department/classification.

```oa-check
name: CO SAS illustrative authorised subscribed paid capital
inputs:
  authorised: 100000000
  subscribed: 60000000
  paid: 20000000
  subscribed_shares: 600
steps:
  - per_share: subscribed / subscribed_shares
  - unsubscribed: authorised - subscribed
  - unpaid: subscribed - paid
expected:
  per_share: 100000
  unsubscribed: 40000000
  unpaid: 40000000
```

### 2. Auditor boundary for a 2026 duty

Use the 2025 wage COP 1,423,500 and the prior-year accounts, as the statute and 2026 Supersociedades interpretation require. The asset boundary is COP 7,117,500,000 and income boundary COP 4,270,500,000. A SAS exactly at the asset boundary reaches it even if income is lower; one peso below both boundaries does not reach this threshold test, but another special-law duty can still apply. This is a legal-boundary comparison, not a recommendation to omit an auditor or manipulate accounts.

```oa-check
name: CO revisor fiscal 2026 prior year wage boundaries
inputs:
  prior_year_wage: 1423500
steps:
  - asset_boundary: prior_year_wage * 5000
  - income_boundary: prior_year_wage * 3000
  - assets_one_below: asset_boundary - 1
  - income_one_below: income_boundary - 1
expected:
  asset_boundary: 7117500000
  income_boundary: 4270500000
  assets_one_below: 7117499999
  income_one_below: 4270499999
```

## Ask the client first

1. Founder count, natural/legal-person status, residence/nationality and authority to sign.
2. Proposed form, business/sector, principal domicile, foreign assets and non-cash contributions.
3. Authorised/subscribed/paid capital, premium, shares and payment terms.
4. Representatives, governance, actual management location and signing arrangements.
5. Department, microenterprise/program status, Chamber and property-registration requirements.
6. Prior-year assets/income and wage, special auditor rules, registration evidence and tax responsibilities.
