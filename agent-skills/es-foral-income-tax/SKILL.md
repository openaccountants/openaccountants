# Spain: foral resident income tax for2026

## Who this applies to

An ordinary Spanish resident whose habitual residence falls in Navarra uses Navarra IRPF. A resident whose habitual residence falls in Bizkaia, Álava or Gipuzkoa uses that historical territory's IRPF. These are separate complete income-tax systems, not an additional autonomous-community percentage on top of the State scale. Apply the residence tests in order: greatest number of days during the tax period, counting temporary absences and the rebuttable habitual-home presumption; then principal centre of interests using the statutory income exclusions; then last declared IRPF residence. A Spanish resident spending no more than 183 days in Spain uses the principal economic-interest test. Keep the spouse/minor-child residence presumption and artificial residence-change rules in the applicable agreement. Mixed-territory joint returns follow the member with the largest taxable base calculated under that member's applicable law. Sources: Basque Economic Agreement arts 6 and 43 [Basque Economic Agreement, current consolidated law](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-2002-9969); Navarra Economic Agreement arts 8–9 [Navarra Economic Agreement, current consolidated law](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-1990-31117).

Payroll withholding jurisdiction is a separate decision from the employee's final annual IRPF residence. The Basque art 7 employment-service/assigned-work-centre rules expressly cover mixed working locations, telework, overseas work and ships; public pensions use the recipient's habitual residence; directors use the paying entity's fiscal domicile and the prescribed mixed-corporate allocation. Apply the public-State-employer exception rather than moving all State payroll to a province. Navarra arts 10–16 contain its separate withholding points of connection. A resident's final return credits the properly attributable withholding even where a different administration collected it. [Basque Economic Agreement, current consolidated law](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-2002-9969)[Navarra Economic Agreement, current consolidated law](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-1990-31117)

### General and savings baskets, reductions and pensions

Under arts 62–66 classify employment, ordinary business income and other general income separately from savings income. Only the housing categories identified by local art63(a) enter savings: Bizkaia art32.2–5, Álava art32.1–2 and Gipuzkoa art32.2–4. Other/seasonal housing and nonhousing rent enter general income. The specified financial-capital returns and disposal gains use art63(b)–(c). This differs from ordinary common-territory rental-income classification. Calculate the property result using the local art 32 before placing it in that basket. Do not insert the State rental reduction or its actual-cost method into the foral property computation. [Bizkaia NF13/2013, current2026 consolidation](https://www.bizkaia.eus/documents/880307/15187815/ca_13_2013.pdf/ce52e403-4fca-9ef7-bf85-2505ba29f921?t=1784098584068)[Álava NF33/2013, current2026 consolidation](https://web.araba.eus/documents/d/araba/indice_norma-foral_irpf_cas-4-pdf)[Gipuzkoa NF3/2014,2026 consolidation](https://www.gipuzkoa.eus/documents/2456431/8.0759126E7/NF%203-2014%20(2026-0).pdf/8ab5c95c-3f89-8fdf-5cb8-01950e3983cc) arts 30–32,62–66.

The savings-return and savings-disposal sub-baskets offset internally. A negative sub-basket carries to the same type of positive income for four following years; these provisions do **not** permit the common-territory 25% cross-offset between the two savings sub-baskets. Gipuzkoa has the specific ten-year qualifying innovative-company disposal-loss exception in art 66.1(b). Other non-business general gains/losses offset internally; a remaining negative gain balance offsets up to 25% of the qualifying positive general-income balance and then carries four years in statutory order. Economic-activity losses are kept separate and carry against positive business results for 30 years under art 65(b) and the incorporated corporate-tax conditions. Do not absorb them automatically against current salary. [Bizkaia NF13/2013, current2026 consolidation](https://www.bizkaia.eus/documents/880307/15187815/ca_13_2013.pdf/ce52e403-4fca-9ef7-bf85-2505ba29f921?t=1784098584068)[Álava NF33/2013, current2026 consolidation](https://web.araba.eus/documents/d/araba/indice_norma-foral_irpf_cas-4-pdf)[Gipuzkoa NF3/2014,2026 consolidation](https://www.gipuzkoa.eus/documents/2456431/8.0759126E7/NF%203-2014%20(2026-0).pdf/8ab5c95c-3f89-8fdf-5cb8-01950e3983cc) arts 65–66.

The unused art 69 compensatory-pension reduction can reduce savings under art 67.3. The general joint-return reduction is EUR 4,896 per return in all three territories for 2026, subject to the territorial family-unit definition and joint election. Navarra has a different joint-return method below. ### Basque business-asset gain: article 75.3 quota adjustment

Calculate the ordinary general-scale quota `Q` on the general liquidable base `B` before the article 77 minoración. For positive `B`, express `100 × Q / B` as a percentage with two decimals. If that general average rate does not exceed the **general corporate rate of 24%**, there is no article 75.3 reduction. If it exceeds 24%, reduce gross quota by `(general_average_percent − 24) / 100 × qualifying_business_asset_gain`. The gain must form part of **positive net economic-activity income**. Deduct business-asset losses already taken into account in that activity's net income from the business-asset gains for this measure. This does not reclassify the disposal into the savings basket, and the taxpayer's marginal IRPF rate is not the average rate. Sources: [Bizkaia art. 75.2–3](https://www.bizkaia.eus/documents/880307/15187815/ca_13_2013.pdf/ce52e403-4fca-9ef7-bf85-2505ba29f921?t=1784098584068), [Álava art. 75.2–3](https://web.araba.eus/documents/d/araba/indice_norma-foral_irpf_cas-4-pdf), [Gipuzkoa art. 75.2–3](https://www.gipuzkoa.eus/documents/2456431/8.0759126E7/NF%203-2014%20(2026-0).pdf/8ab5c95c-3f89-8fdf-5cb8-01950e3983cc); [general corporate rates and their primary statutes](es-foral-corporate-tax.md).

Álava and Gipuzkoa additionally reduce this gain measure by **prior-year negative business balances actually compensated**, as their article 75.3 expressly requires. Bizkaia's article 75.3 has the current business-asset loss adjustment but does not contain that additional prior-business-balance paragraph. Apply the actual territory's provision and use no negative gain measure to generate a quota increase. Then apply the article 77 minoración and the remaining annual settlement below. At general base 40,000, the supplied scale gives `Q = 9,220.80 + (40,000 − 36,160) × 35% = 10,564.80`; the general average is 26.41%. With 10,000 eligible net business-asset gain in positive business income, the reduction is 241.00. In Álava/Gipuzkoa, if 6,000 prior business negative balances are actually compensated and the same stated 40,000 liquidable base remains after all adjustments, the gain measure is 4,000 and the reduction is 96.40. With no qualifying positive business-asset gain, the reduction is zero. [Bizkaia](https://www.bizkaia.eus/documents/880307/15187815/ca_13_2013.pdf/ce52e403-4fca-9ef7-bf85-2505ba29f921?t=1784098584068), [Álava](https://web.araba.eus/documents/d/araba/indice_norma-foral_irpf_cas-4-pdf), [Gipuzkoa](https://www.gipuzkoa.eus/documents/2456431/8.0759126E7/NF%203-2014%20(2026-0).pdf/8ab5c95c-3f89-8fdf-5cb8-01950e3983cc) art. 75.3.

Subtract the EUR 1,615 art 77 minoración **from general-scale quota**, with a zero floor, then add savings-scale quota. Álava residents in qualifying rural nuclei/zones with population≤500 subtract an additional EUR 200 from general quota under art 77.2. The unused minoración does not reduce savings quota. Then apply qualifying personal/family/housing/other quota credits, with the art 78 zero floor, preserving eligible carryforwards. Finally subtract documented withholding, payments on account and instalments under Bizkaia/Álava art 95 or Gipuzkoa art 94. A negative differential produces the refund route; zero flooring liquid quota does not erase documented payments. [Bizkaia NF13/2013, current2026 consolidation](https://www.bizkaia.eus/documents/880307/15187815/ca_13_2013.pdf/ce52e403-4fca-9ef7-bf85-2505ba29f921?t=1784098584068)[Álava NF33/2013, current2026 consolidation](https://web.araba.eus/documents/d/araba/indice_norma-foral_irpf_cas-4-pdf)[Gipuzkoa NF3/2014,2026 consolidation](https://www.gipuzkoa.eus/documents/2456431/8.0759126E7/NF%203-2014%20(2026-0).pdf/8ab5c95c-3f89-8fdf-5cb8-01950e3983cc) arts 74–78,95/94.

Do not import a State deduction after already computing the territorial quota. ### C. Navarra: ordinary employee/self-employed return

### Net income, baskets and reductions

Use DFL 4/2008 as currently consolidated [Navarra DFL4/2008, current revision20April2026](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BON-n-2008-90013&p=20260420&tn=1). Its current art 55(4)–(7) old personal/family/care/political **base reductions are repealed**. The old italic paragraphs in the LexNavarra page are historical display text. DA 58 is also repealed from 2022. Current personal/family relief is a quota credit under 62.9. Do not claim both systems.

Employment income is gross taxable money/benefits less the **exclusive** art 18 expense list: compulsory social-security/pension charges, essential mandatory professional-college dues capped 500/year under regulation 9, and direct employment-dispute legal costs capped 300/year. Current art 18 has no union-dues expense and no State 2,000 ordinary employment expense. Union dues instead have the 62.6 quota credit. Apply only the actual current 17.2 irregular/pension reduction and 17.3 exclusions for the relevant benefit; do not treat a periodic pension as a universally 40%-reduced lump sum. [Navarra DFL4/2008, current revision20April2026](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BON-n-2008-90013&p=20260420&tn=1) arts 13–19.

Sole traders ordinarily use actual income less tax-deductible business expenses under 34–35. Simplified direct assessment applies, unless validly waived, where prior-year net turnover≤600,000 and the special method does not apply: table depreciation, no provisions/impairments, then deduct 5% of the positive net result. Special direct assessment covers qualifying agricultural/forestry/livestock/fishing turnover≤350,000 or specified recargo/simplified-VAT businesses≤250,000, with the linked-family/entity aggregation and VAT-election rules. Exclude provisions, impairment, depreciation and specified transport/agricultural-machine lease costs, then deduct 10% ordinarily,35% for qualifying agriculture/etc,45% road-freight IAE 722. This is a method on actual receipts/costs, not common-territory módulos. Apply 36(C)'s turnover definition, including grants/compensation where required, and the 2026 special-election rule to the actual election record. [Navarra DFL4/2008, current revision20April2026](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BON-n-2008-90013&p=20260420&tn=1) arts 34–36.

Separate taxable aid and subsidy income from the result used for the percentage allowance. Under art 36(D), integrate that income into net business income without the art 36(A.3) 5% or art 36(B.4) 10%/35%/45% reduction. The turnover definition in art 36(C), including grants, is a separate eligibility test. For example, a 50,000 qualifying pre-allowance result plus 10,000 taxable subsidy gives 57,500 under simplified assessment (50,000×95%+10,000), not 57,000. Under the ordinary 10% special-assessment allowance it gives 55,000, not 54,000. [Navarra DFL4/2008 art.36](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BON-n-2008-90013&p=20260420&tn=1).

General income art 53 comprises non-savings net income/imputations plus general non-disposal gains/losses. The latter negative balance offsets qualifying positive net income up to 25%, then carries four years. Savings art 54 has financial-capital and disposal-gain sub-baskets, including qualifying home-acquisition capital grants. Net internally, then cross-offset a negative sub-basket against a positive other sub-basket up to 25%; carry remainder four years in statutory order, oldest balances first. A general taxable-base loss carries four years under 56. Unlike the Basque method, ordinary Navarra savings does permit the art 54 cross-offset. [Navarra DFL4/2008, current revision20April2026](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BON-n-2008-90013&p=20260420&tn=1) arts 53–57.

Apply art 55 pension and qualifying compensatory-spouse/non-child-maintenance reductions to the general base. Savings taxable base equals savings taxable income under 57; do not apply the repealed family-reduction spillover. The pension reduction is limited by the lower of eligible net-work/business income 30% (50% if the participant is older than 50) and the monetary limit: ordinary 1,500, eligible employer/employee increment 8,500 using employer matching (≤500 employer×2.5;500.01–1,500:1,250+.25×[E−500];>1,500:E; ratio 1 for employee gross>60,000 from that employer), plus the specified 4,250 own-account qualified-plan route, with 8,500 combined increment ceiling. The special employer scheme covering members but not the remaining workers has a 4,250 additional limit. Collective-dependency employer premium has its separate 5,000 ceiling. Respect financial limits and the five-year eligible excess carry. Separate spouse contribution:1,000 if spouse has no net work/business income or less than 8,500. Do not use the State spouse 8,000 threshold. [Navarra DFL4/2008, current revision20April2026](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BON-n-2008-90013&p=20260420&tn=1) art 55.1.

### Basque disability-plan contributions

Each territory's art 72 covers a qualifying scheme for a person with physical/sensory disability at least 65%, mental disability at least 33%, or representative curatorship under the specified civil-law provisions. The disabled person's own annual reduction ceiling is 24,250. Each qualifying other contributor has an 8,000 ceiling, independent of that contributor's own ordinary pension-scheme allowance. Eligible others are direct/collateral relatives through fourth degree, spouse/registered partner, guardian/fosterer or representative curator under the local text. The disabled person must be the sole irrevocable beneficiary, with the statutory death-benefit exception.

Eligible excess arising from base insufficiency or the statutory other-contributor limit carries five years; excess over financial-law contribution limits does not. Early disposals use art 70.4's restoration rules through art 72.4. The contributions covered by art 72 are not subject to gifts tax.

The qualifying disability-annuity work-income exemption under art.9.25 is capped at three times SMI; it is distinct from Navarra’s three-times-IPREM rule below. Confirm the benefit and disability conditions before applying either indicator.

Control: own contribution 20,000 and two qualifying other contributors paying 8,000 each leave joint residual capacity 4,250, allocated 2,125 each, total 24,250. This is a limit on reduction, not permission to breach a scheme's financial contribution rules.

### Basque 2026 ordinary capital benefits and pre-2026 transition

Do not use a blanket old 60% integration for all 2026 pension capital. First distinguish the listed social-security/alternative-mutuality/collective-insurance exclusions from an eligible art 18(a) pension/EPSV benefit.

For benefits subject to current art 37(e), determine the **positive** difference between economic rights and contributions at the contingency/circumstance date. Divide by total economic rights to fix the yield proportion; that proportion stays fixed throughout payment, regardless of collection form. The attributable yield is movable-capital income in the savings basket under art 63(b). The remaining contribution component is work income. For a qualifying first capital benefit, art 19.2(b) integrates that remaining component at 70%, after more than two years from the first contribution; the two-year condition is waived for disability/dependency. Otherwise the capital work-income component is integrated at 100%. Aggregate capital amounts within the same tax period for the first-benefit test. Apply the statutory art 19 capital-integration ceiling of 300,000; excess is integrated at 100%. The listed social-security/alternative-mutuality exceptions instead follow the applicable art 19.2(a) treatment, and art 18(a)(6) collective-insurance benefits follow art 19.2(c).

Bizkaia DT 37, Álava DT 31 and Gipuzkoa DT 36 preserve the art 19.2(b) version in force on 31 December 2025 for the benefit portion attributable to contributions/employer contributions made **before 1 January 2026**. Use that historical portion/election only; for it the current art 37(e)/63(b) split is disapplied. The preserved qualifying first-capital integration is 60%. Document the local election procedure, first-benefit history, minimum contribution age, aggregation and cap; do not give a fresh 60% relief on the same contingency every year.

For annuity benefits, the yield exemption is **Bizkaia article 9.38, Álava article 9.44 and Gipuzkoa article 9.39**. Apply the named local benefit and contingency exclusions before computing the exemption. Bizkaia requires a lifelong annuity or a constant temporary annuity lasting at least 15 years. Álava/Gipuzkoa require a lifelong or at least 15-year temporary annuity and a constant amount. The statutes allow the stated technical or financial variations. The exemption concerns the article 37(e) **yield component**, not the entire pension or its contribution/work component. **Álava/Gipuzkoa cap the exempt yield at 40% of the total annuity benefit; Bizkaia article 9.38 contains no equivalent 40% cap.** Capital integration percentages do not apply to an ordinary periodic pension.

For example, assume an otherwise eligible constant annuity begun in 2026, annual benefit EUR 10,000 and an established fixed article 37(e) yield ratio of 60%. Yield is EUR 6,000 and the contribution/work component is EUR 4,000. Bizkaia exempts the EUR 6,000 yield. Álava/Gipuzkoa exempt `min(6,000, 10,000 × 40%) = EUR 4,000`, leaving EUR 2,000 of yield in the savings basket. The EUR 4,000 contribution/work component remains in its work-income computation in every province. At a 20% yield ratio, the EUR 2,000 yield is within the cap and can be fully exempt in all three provinces, assuming the other conditions.

For annuities whose collection began **before 1 January 2026**, B DA 45, A DA 44 and G DA 44 expressly apply the new yield split, annuity exemption and their yield-information additional provision. Establish the yield proportion at the **original contingency/circumstance date**, and measure the annuity's duration from its **original constitution date**. Do not recalculate the ratio at 1 January 2026 or restart a 15-year duration test then. A qualifying constant 20-year annuity constituted in 2018 retains that original 20-year duration for this test even though fewer than 15 payment years remain in 2026. This annuity bridge is separate from B DT 37/A DT 31/G DT 36's preserved capital-benefit election.

Where the first scheme contribution predates 1 January 2026 and the statutory provider-information condition applies, B DA 42/A DA 40/G DA 42 supply the yield measure instead of an invented current market-return calculation: **1% of the total benefit for each year of membership up to the original contingency, capped at 35%**; if the required membership information is also unavailable, use **25% of the total benefit**. Bizkaia describes the information not being made available to the tax authority; Álava/Gipuzkoa expressly require that the provider lacks the information and therefore does not supply it. Keep those conditions and the provider's reporting duties, rather than electing a fallback when actual information exists. At 20 established years, a EUR 10,000 benefit has EUR 2,000 statutory yield; at 40 established years the 35% ceiling gives EUR 3,500; unavailable required membership information gives EUR 2,500. The applicable annuity exemption and local cap are tested after establishing that yield. Sources: the current territorial article 9, article 37(e), and additional provisions identified here, linked below.

Controls assuming an already eligible first-capital benefit and an entirely pre-2026 attributable portion: benefit/economic rights 100,000, contributions 80,000 gives a fixed yield proportion 20%. Current-law route gives savings yield 20,000 and work component 80,000×70%=56,000. The valid preserved route instead gives 100,000×60%=60,000 work income and disapplies the yield split for that portion. Compare full territorial tax outcomes, not these income amounts alone.

### Navarra capital pension transition

Current art 17.2(b)'s 40% reduction is limited to the benefits expressly named there (art 14.2(a)1 and 2), and art 17.3 excludes annuity-form benefits. It does not create a blanket current 40% reduction for all private pension-plan withdrawals.

For an eligible plan/mutuality/PPA/occupational-insurance capital benefit with contributions through **31 December 2017**, DT 25 can preserve the old art 17.2(b) 40% reduction **only on that legacy-attributable portion**. The preserved provision requires two years from the first contribution and, for pension-plan/PPA benefits, reduces the amount on which the reduction is calculated by contributions made in the preceding year. Contributions from 1 January 2018 do not acquire that historic capital reduction. A payment must be in the contingency year or either of the next two tax years. Pre-2018 contingencies had an absolute last payment date 31 December 2020 and cannot newly qualify in 2026.

Therefore a 2026 payment can use this timing route for contingencies in 2024, 2025 or 2026; a 2023 contingency is outside that payment window. Confirm the actual contingency, legacy allocation and prior contribution history. Example with all other requirements met, no preceding-year contributions and legacy qualifying capital 10,000: reduction 4,000, taxable work income 6,000. If the contingency was in 2023, the DT 25 reduction on a 2026 payment is zero.

### Exemption with progression

Classify foreign income under the actual applicable treaty before applying exemption with progression. First identify the applicable treaty and its income-category provision. If the resulting exemption reserves the right to include that income for rate-setting, use the **territory's whole scale**, never the State half plus the local scale.

Gipuzkoa expressly supplies the calculation in DA 38: add exempt-with-progression income to the general or savings liquidable base according to its nature; compute that basket's local average rate on the augmented base; apply the resulting average rate only to the taxable base, excluding the exempt amount. Respect the applicable average-rate expression/rounding in arts 75.2 and 76.2, then continue the normal minoración/credits/settlement order. Do not insert a foreign-tax credit on an amount already exempt under that route.

For the other territories, anchor the exemption/progression right in the **actual treaty**, with the local law's treaty-priority provision and full scales. Spain–Germany art 22.1(b), for example, expressly permits Spain to take treaty-exempt income into account in calculating tax on remaining income; art 22.1(a) instead supplies the credit route for relevant taxable income. This treaty example does not establish that any particular foreign pension, salary or rent is exempt. Its actual category article decides that first. Exact conditional calculation is the same augmented-base average-rate mechanism; retain the treaty-category eligibility evidence with the calculation.

Control under Gipuzkoa's general scale: taxable liquidable base 30,000 and an already legally established exempt-with-progression general receipt 10,000. Gross schedule quota at 40,000 is 10,564.80; average is 26.412%, expressed as 26.41%. Apply that rate to taxable 30,000: 7,923.00. The exempt 10,000 is not itself taxed. General minoración 1,615 then gives 6,308 before other credits. Zero taxable base produces zero taxable-basket charge and avoids division by zero.

### Example 1: ordinary Basque employee and savings
