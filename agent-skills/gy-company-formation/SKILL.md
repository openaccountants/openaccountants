# Guyana: setting up a company (2026) v1.0

> **General reference only.** This skill is general tax/accounting reference material for AI-assisted workflows. It has not been reviewed for any specific person's facts, documents, elections, deadlines, residency, filing status, or local procedures. Do not rely on it to file, pay, amend, or take a tax position without review by a qualified professional in the relevant jurisdiction.

**Which text of the law.** The Companies Act, Chapter 89:01 (Act 29 of 1991, the Act), with the Companies Regulations (Reg. 2/1995 and 15/2008), as published by the Ministry of Legal Affairs in the Laws of Guyana revised edition "L.R.O. 1/2012" (Volume 16). That edition includes amendments to 1999 in the Act and to 2008 in the fees. Amendments after the 2012 revision were not read (see "Not yet confirmed"). Tax registration steps come from the Guyana Revenue Authority (GRA) pages, read on 11 November 2026.

## Who this applies to

- **Anyone forming a company under the Act.** One or more persons may incorporate a company (s. 4(1)). An individual under 18, one found to be of unsound mind by a tribunal in Guyana or elsewhere, or an undischarged bankrupt may not form or join in forming one (s. 4(2)).
- **Large unincorporated groups.** No association, partnership or other group of more than 20 persons may be formed to carry on a trade or business for gain unless it is incorporated under the Act or formed under another enactment (s. 3).
- **Foreign companies doing business in Guyana** must register as external companies before they begin or carry on an undertaking in Guyana (s. 312(1)); see "Cross-border points".
- **Sole traders and partnerships** are not companies. The GRA says partnerships generally do not need a TIN unless they register for VAT, and that a sole trader's individual TIN serves the business. Business-name registration for them is under a separate Act, not covered here (see "Not yet confirmed").
- **Out of scope**: continuing former-Act companies (Part IV Division B), amalgamations, winding up, public offers of shares, and licensed sectors (banking, insurance) that need a separate licence.

## The base

**Company or public company.** The Act has one kind of company. A "public company" is one whose shares or debentures are, were or are meant to be distributed to the public (s. 2). It needs at least two directors (s. 60), its directors must see that its secretary is qualified (s. 59(2)), and it must send its financial statements to the Registrar (s. 156). Every other company is what clients usually call a private company. Whether share transfers are restricted is a choice written into the articles (s. 5(1)(e)), not a separate company type.

**What the articles of incorporation contain** (s. 5(1)), in the prescribed form (Form 1):
- the proposed name, which must include "incorporated" or "inc." (s. 9); a former-Act company may keep "limited" or "ltd.";
- that the registered office is in Guyana;
- the classes and any maximum number of shares the company may issue, and the rights attached to each class;
- the minimum issue price of shares;
- any restriction on transferring shares (then each share certificate must say so, s. 5(3));
- the number, or minimum and maximum number, of directors;
- any restriction on the business the company may carry on.

**Share capital.** Shares have no nominal or par value (s. 25(2)). The company keeps a stated capital account for each class of shares (s. 31). The Act sets no minimum amount of capital.

**Directors and secretary.** At least one director (s. 60). The first directors and the secretary are named in the statement filed with the articles, with each person's signed consent; only people named there become the first directors and secretary (s. 67). The company keeps a register of directors and a register of its secretary (s. 189(5), (6)).

**Registered office and records.** The company must always have a registered office in Guyana (s. 187). It keeps its articles, by-laws, minutes and resolutions there, with a register of shareholders (s. 189).

## Deadlines and filing

**Steps to incorporate, in order.**
1. **Search and reserve the name.** Ask the Registrar of Companies for a name search (G$175) and, if wanted, reserve the name for 12 months (Form 18, G$800; s. 490). The name must not be the same as or similar to another person's name or business name if that is likely to confuse (s. 491).
2. **Prepare the articles of incorporation** (Form 1) with the contents above, in two signed copies, "duplicate originals" (s. 479(1)(a)).
3. **Prepare the documents that go with them** (s. 7): the statement of the first directors and secretary with their consents (Form 8, s. 67); the notice of the registered office address (Form 3, s. 188(1)).
4. **Attorney's declaration.** A statutory declaration by an attorney-at-law that no signatory is under 18, of unsound mind or bankrupt is conclusive of those facts (s. 4(3)). The Act does not make it compulsory for a local company, but it is the usual way to prove s. 4(2) is met.
5. **File with the Registrar and pay** G$60,000 (reg. 24(1)(a)(i)). The Registrar endorses both originals "registered" with the date and issues the certificate of incorporation in duplicate (s. 8(1), s. 479(1)(b)). The company exists from the date on the certificate (s. 8(2)).
6. **Hold the first directors' meeting** to make by-laws, adopt share certificate forms, authorise the issue of shares, appoint officers and an auditor, and set up banking (s. 63(1)). By-laws are made by the directors after incorporation; they are not filed to incorporate.
7. **Register with the GRA for a TIN.** Create a GRA eServices account (email and password), apply for the TIN online and upload the documents, or file the Taxpayer Registration Form at a GRA office. For a company the GRA asks for the completed form, a copy of the certificate of incorporation and valid identification of two directors; the same applies to non-resident companies. The GRA reviews the application, then asks a representative to visit an office with the originals, pay G$1,000 and collect the TIN certificate within one month. Someone who cannot attend names an authorised representative with a power of attorney.
8. **Register for VAT** if taxable supplies will reach G$15,000,000 in 12 months, or voluntarily (see [gy-vat-gst](gy-vat-gst.md)), and as an employer for PAYE and NIS before the first payroll (see [gy-payroll-social](gy-payroll-social.md)).

## Penalties and interest

- **Strike-off.** If a company fails to send a return, notice, document or fee the Act requires, the Registrar sends a notice; unless the default is remedied within 28 days, the company can be struck off the register and the strike-off published in the Gazette (s. 487(1)(a), (2), (4)). Liabilities continue after strike-off (s. 488).
- **False or misleading filings**: G$15,000 and six months' imprisonment, also for a director or officer who knowingly allowed it (s. 518).
- **Name offences**: G$15,000 for a company that breaks the "incorporated" naming rule (s. 516); G$3,000 for each individual trading under a name with "incorporated" or "inc." without being incorporated (s. 517).
- **Other contraventions** without reasonable cause where no other penalty is set: G$30,000 (s. 522). The court can also order compliance (s. 524).
- Tax penalties for late corporation tax, PAYE or VAT returns are in the tax skills.

## Cross-border points

- **External company.** Any incorporated or unincorporated body formed under the laws of a country other than Guyana (s. 310(1)) carries on an undertaking in Guyana if, among other things, it regularly transacts business from an office in Guyana, makes two or more contracts with Guyana residents to be performed substantially in Guyana, appoints an agent in Guyana for such contracts, or uses assets in Guyana for profit (s. 310(2)). A telephone listing in Guyana under its name is presumed to mean it carries on an undertaking (s. 310(3)).
- **Registration.** It must not begin or carry on business in Guyana until registered (s. 312(1)). Within one month after it starts business it files a statement in duplicate (Form 14) with its name, place and date of incorporation, constating documents, liability of members, the business it will carry on, its capital, the addresses of its head office and of its principal office in Guyana, and its directors (s. 316(1)). With it go a statutory declaration by two directors, a copy of its corporate instruments, an attorney-at-law's declaration of compliance, the fee and a power of attorney (s. 316(2)). Documents not in English need a notarially certified translation (s. 317).
- **Local attorney.** The power of attorney (Form 16) names a person resident in Guyana to accept service of process and notices for the company (s. 318(1)).
- **Annual accounts.** An external company makes out a balance sheet and profit and loss account every calendar year, as a public company would, and lodges them with the Registrar (s. 329(1)), with its annual return (Form 20).
- **Tax.** A branch pays corporation tax on its Guyana profits, and its profits are treated as distributed and bear 20% withholding tax unless reinvested; a subsidiary pays the same 20% on the dividends it pays out. See the worked case below and [gy-cross-border](gy-cross-border.md).

## Worked examples

### Example: subsidiary or branch for a foreign service company, and the set-up cost

A foreign consulting group will earn G$10,000,000 of chargeable profits a year in Guyana. It asks whether to incorporate a local subsidiary or register a branch (external company, capital under G$1,000,000), and what each costs to set up.

**Set-up fees.** Subsidiary: name search G$175, reservation G$800, certificate of incorporation G$60,000, TIN G$1,000, total G$61,975. Branch: registration G$80,000 (reg. 24(5)(a)), TIN G$1,000, total G$81,000.

**Tax each year, if all after-tax profit leaves Guyana.** Both pay corporation tax at 25% (a service company is non-commercial): G$2,500,000. The subsidiary's G$7,500,000 dividend bears 20% withholding tax, G$1,500,000 (ITA s. 39(1)(a)). The branch's G$7,500,000 of after-tax profit is deemed remitted and bears the same 20% (ITA s. 39(7) to (9)). Total G$4,000,000 either way.

**What decides it.** The tax is the same when all profit goes abroad. The branch avoids the 20% only on profit reinvested in Guyana to the Commissioner-General's satisfaction (s. 39(8)); the subsidiary avoids it by not paying a dividend. The subsidiary is cheaper to register by G$19,025 and limits the parent's liability to the Guyana company; the branch keeps one legal entity. A treaty may lower the 20% (see gy-cross-border).

## Ask the client first

1. Is the business new in Guyana, or a foreign company that will trade here (external company)?
2. Who are the founders and first directors: ages, any bankruptcy, and where they live?
3. Will shares or debentures ever be offered to the public (public company rules)?
4. What share classes, and should transfers be restricted?
5. Expected taxable supplies in the first 12 months (VAT at G$15,000,000) and whether it will employ staff.
6. Will profits be paid out to owners abroad, or kept in Guyana?
