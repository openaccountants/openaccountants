# New Zealand capital gains: when a gain is taxed, 2027 income year

> **Source-cited draft.** Built from Inland Revenue's property, share, cryptoasset and FIF pages, guide IR461 (April 2026) and the Budget 2026 FIF information sheet, read on 14 November 2026. No accountant has reviewed it. All outputs must be reviewed by a qualified tax professional before anyone relies on them.

## Scope

New Zealand has no general capital gains tax, but several rules tax a gain as ordinary income: the bright-line test for residential land, the intention rule and the dealer, developer and builder rules for land, the rules for shares and cryptoassets bought mainly to sell, and the foreign investment fund (FIF) rules for foreign shares. This Guide is for individuals. The year covered is the income year 1 April 2026 to 31 March 2027 (IRD's "2027 income year").

When a rule applies, the profit goes into the person's income tax return and is taxed at the ordinary rates. There is no separate capital gains rate. The return itself is in [nz-income-tax-ir3](nz-income-tax-ir3.md); cryptoassets in detail are in [new-zealand-crypto-tax](new-zealand-crypto-tax.md); residence is in [nz-tax-residency](nz-tax-residency.md).

**Quick answer.** Residential land sold on or after 1 July 2024 is taxed if the bright-line end date is within 2 years of the start date, unless the main home or another exclusion applies. Land bought with a purpose of resale, or by a dealer, developer or builder, is taxed whatever the holding period. Shares are taxed on sale if bought for the dominant or main purpose of sale. Foreign shares costing NZD 50,000 or more in total are taxed each year under the FIF rules, usually on 5% of opening market value.

## Ask the client first

1. On what date did title transfer to you, and on what date did you sign the binding sale and purchase agreement to sell? (Sets the bright-line period.)
2. Did you live in the property as your main home, for how much of the time, and over how much of the area?
3. When you bought it, was reselling one of your purposes? Are you, or anyone associated with you, a property dealer, developer or builder?
4. Why did you buy the shares or cryptoassets, and what records show it?
5. What did your foreign shares cost in total, and are any ASX-listed Australian companies?
6. Are you a New Zealand tax resident, and have you been at all times you held the asset?

## The method, step by step

1. **Identify the asset and its dates.** For land, find the title transfer date (bright-line start for a standard purchase, generally settlement) and the date of the binding sale and purchase agreement (bright-line end for a standard sale). Off-the-plan purchases and gifts have different rules: refer.
2. **Land taxed whatever the holding period.** Test the land against these first:
   - **Intention rule.** Profit is taxable if one of your purposes when buying was to resell, however long you keep it. It applies to commercial and residential property. It usually does not apply to your main home or a long-term rental bought as such.
   - **Dealers, developers and builders.** Property bought as part of a property or construction business is taxable whenever sold. Property bought outside the business is generally taxable if, when you bought it, you or an associated person was in the business of dealing, developing or subdividing and you sell within 10 years of buying. For builders, the rule applies if you or an associated person was in the building business when improvements began, and the 10 years run from completing the improvements. The 10-year rule does not apply to your main home, to property used in your business (but not as a rental), to an employee of such a business, or if you were no longer in the property business when you bought the property or bought it before going into business.
3. **Bright-line test.** If still untaxed, apply the bright-line period from the table below. For sales on or after 1 July 2024 the 2-year test is the only one: a property bought in 2022 and sold in 2026 is tested against 2 years, not 10. IRD: "you no longer have to separate out new builds from all other properties." For sales before 1 July 2024, which period applies depends on when the property was "acquired", generally the date of the binding purchase agreement; the period itself still starts at title transfer. The test also applies to New Zealand tax residents who buy and sell residential property overseas.
4. **Exclusions.** Main home (both limits in the table must be exceeded; at or under either limit, the whole profit is taxable). The exclusion does not apply if you have a regular pattern of buying and selling or building and selling your main home, or have already used it twice in the 2-year period immediately before the sale. A construction period can be ignored. Only actual use counts, not intention, and use by a family member alone does not count. Sales before 1 July 2024 use different main home criteria, depending on whether the home was acquired before, or on or after, 27 March 2021. Business premises used predominantly as such, and farmland, are excluded. The test does not apply to an executor or administrator of an estate or to a person who inherited the property.
5. **Shares.** An amount from selling shares is taxable when you bought the shares for the dominant or main purpose of selling them, have a share dealing business, or hold them as part of a profit-making scheme. You do not need to be in business: a one-off sale is taxed if the shares were bought mainly to sell. The taxable amount is the sale price less the cost. Keep records that show your purpose when buying. Dividends are always income, including foreign dividends.
6. **Cryptoassets.** Profits from selling or exchanging cryptoassets, including swapping one cryptoasset for another, are taxable if you acquired them for the purpose of disposal, trade in them, or use them in a profit-making scheme. Moving assets between your own wallets is not a disposal. Detail: [new-zealand-crypto-tax](new-zealand-crypto-tax.md).
7. **Foreign shares: FIF.** A FIF is a foreign company, foreign unit trust, foreign superannuation scheme or foreign life insurance policy. Total the cost of all attributing FIF interests and compare it with the de minimis. The de minimis is a cost test, not market value, and it must be met throughout the year: if total cost goes over NZD 50,000 on any day, all FIF interests are subject to the FIF rules, and the first NZD 50,000 is not exempt. Spouses or partners who hold interests jointly each apply the limit to their own share. Below it, the ordinary rules apply (dividends taxed, sale taxed only if bought mainly to sell). The exemption does not apply to anyone who has included FIF income or loss in their return for the year. Check the ASX exemption (Boundary table). Above the de minimis, FIF income is attributed each year, so there may be FIF income before any money is received.
8. **FIF method.** Choose from the 6 methods: fair dividend rate (FDR, the most common), comparative value, cost, deemed rate of return, revenue account method (RAM), or attributable FIF income. IR461: "From 1 April 2025, eligible individuals and family trusts may use the revenue account method", which taxes dividends and gains on a realisation basis (IR461 describes it as 0.7 times gains or losses on disposal, times the marginal tax rate, plus dividends). Eligibility is limited: do not suggest RAM without confirming the person is eligible. Foreign superannuation is taxed under the FIF rules only if it is a "FIF superannuation interest".
9. **Report.** Put any taxable profit in the income tax return, taxed at the rates in the table below. For a bright-line sale, complete the IR833 bright-line residential property sale information form too.

## Worked cases

**Case 1: sold within 2 years, is the gain taxed? (decision).** Mere took title to a rental flat on 1 March 2025 and signed a binding agreement to sell it on 1 October 2026, 579 days later. 579 is fewer than 731 days (2 years), so the bright-line test applies; she never lived there, so the main home exclusion fails. Profit after costs is NZD 60,000, and her other taxable income is NZD 70,000. The extra tax is 30% on the slice from 70,000 to 78,100 and 33% on the slice from 78,100 to 130,000. Had she signed the sale agreement after 1 March 2027, the bright-line test would not apply and, with no resale purpose, the gain would be untaxed.

```oa-check
name: NZ bright-line sale inside 2 years, extra tax
inputs:
  days_held: 579
  two_years_days: 731
  profit: 60000
  other_income: 70000
  band30_top: 78100
  rate30: 0.30
  rate33: 0.33
steps:
  - inside_period: min(max(two_years_days - days_held, 0), 1)
  - taxable_gain: profit * inside_period
  - total_income: other_income + taxable_gain
  - slice30: min(total_income, band30_top) - other_income
  - slice33: total_income - band30_top
  - extra_tax: round(slice30 * rate30 + slice33 * rate33, 2)
expected:
  inside_period: 1
  taxable_gain: 60000
  slice30: 8100
  slice33: 51900
  extra_tax: 19557.00
```

**Case 2: foreign shares, inside or outside FIF? (decision).** Tom's foreign shares (no ASX exemption) cost NZD 48,000 in total throughout the year: under the de minimis, so no FIF income, and only dividends are taxed. His sister's portfolio cost NZD 62,000 at one point in the year, so all her interests are inside the FIF rules. Her opening market value on 1 April 2026 is NZD 70,000 and she uses FDR: FIF income is 5% of 70,000 = NZD 3,500, taxed at her 33% marginal rate.

```oa-check
name: NZ FIF de minimis and fair dividend rate
inputs:
  cost_tom: 48000
  cost_sister: 62000
  de_minimis: 50000
  opening_value: 70000
  fdr: 0.05
  marginal_rate: 0.33
steps:
  - tom_in_fif: min(max(cost_tom - de_minimis, 0), 1)
  - sister_in_fif: min(max(cost_sister - de_minimis, 0), 1)
  - fif_income: opening_value * fdr * sister_in_fif
  - tax_on_fif: round(fif_income * marginal_rate, 2)
expected:
  tom_in_fif: 0
  sister_in_fif: 1
  fif_income: 3500
  tax_on_fif: 1155.00
```

## When to refuse or refer

- Refer property held in a trust, company or partnership, rollover relief transfers, off-the-plan purchases, subdivisions and gifts: the start and end dates and main home rules differ.
- Refer anyone associated with a property dealer, developer or builder.
- Refer FIF holdings above the de minimis, foreign superannuation, and any case that depends on whether the Budget 2026 FIF changes were enacted.
- Refer a person who changed residence while holding the asset, or who may be taxed in another country too (see [nz-tax-residency](nz-tax-residency.md)).
- Refuse to state that a gain is tax free without the purchase purpose and dates.

## Filing and payment

- Show the taxable profit in the Individual income tax return IR3 ([nz-income-tax-ir3](nz-income-tax-ir3.md)). For the 2027 income year the return period ends 31 March 2027.
- For a bright-line sale, complete the bright-line residential property sale information form IR833 and include the net profit in the return.
- FIF income is calculated per IR461 (IRD's FIF calculator is at ird.govt.nz/calculate-my-fif-income) and returned in the IR3.
- Tax on these gains is paid with the year's income tax; provisional tax may apply ([nz-provisional-tax](nz-provisional-tax.md)).
