# Suriname income tax on companies (2026) v1.0

> **General reference only.** This skill is general tax/accounting reference material for AI-assisted workflows. It has not been reviewed for any specific person's facts, documents, elections, deadlines, residency, filing status, or local procedures. Do not rely on it to file, pay, amend, or take a tax position without review by a qualified professional in the relevant jurisdiction.

Suriname has no separate corporate tax act. Companies and other bodies (lichamen) pay the same income tax as individuals, under the Wet Inkomstenbelasting 1922, at a flat rate on their taxable amount (IB art. 36(1)). This skill covers the 2026 book year: the calendar year 2026, or a book year that the body keeps instead (IB art. 38(2)).

**Which text of the law.** The Wet Inkomstenbelasting 1922 (G.B. 1921 no. 112, text G.B. 1960 no. 84, as last amended by S.B. 2024 no. 3) and the Wet Dividendbelasting 1973 (G.B. 1973 no. 8, as last amended by S.B. 1984 no. 109), both as published by the Suriname Tax Administration (Belastingdienst Suriname). No 2025 or 2026 amendment of either act was found on the Tax Administration's law pages or among the laws passed by De Nationale Assemblée (DNA) when read on 11 November 2026. The solidarity levy of 46% on taxable amounts above SRD 150,000 ran from 1 February 2021 to 1 January 2022 and no longer applies (IB art. 36(2)).

Linked skills: individuals and wage tax in [sr-income-tax](sr-income-tax.md); VAT in [sr-vat-gst](sr-vat-gst.md); employer withholding in [sr-payroll-social](sr-payroll-social.md); setting up an NV in [sr-company-formation](sr-company-formation.md).

## Who this applies to

- **Resident bodies (in Suriname gevestigd), taxed on their whole profit (IB art. 1(2)(b), art. 19):**
  1. naamloze vennootschappen (NV), limited partnerships with shares and other companies whose capital is divided into shares;
  2. co-operatives and associations on a co-operative basis;
  3. mutual insurers and associations acting as insurer or credit institution on a mutual basis;
  4. other private-law legal persons (for example foundations) and associations without legal personality, if and so far as they run a business other than solely for a general social interest;
  5. statutory bodies, if and so far as they run a business. These may be exempted by staatsbesluit (art. 1(4)).
  Bodies 1 to 3 are deemed to run their business with all their assets (art. 2(1)). An NV exists for tax as soon as a business is entered in the trade register as belonging to it (art. 2(2)). Where a body is established is judged on the facts (art. 3(1)).
- **Non-resident bodies (buitenlandse belastingplichtigen)** with Surinamese-source income: legal persons, limited partnerships with shares, other companies with share capital, associations without legal personality and special-purpose funds (IB art. 1(3)(b)). They are taxed on their binnenlands belastbaar bedrag (art. 30bis): mainly profit of a permanent establishment in Suriname, Surinamese real estate and mortgage-secured debts on it (art. 27).
- **Out of scope, refer to a specialist:** mining, oil and gas concessions and their contracts; bodies with a staatsbesluit exemption; banks and insurers (special reserves); the use of a foreign functional currency (art. 36(4), see below); mergers and conversions; transfer pricing documentation; any Pillar Two minimum tax (no Surinamese act found).

## The base

**1. Profit.** The belastbaar bedrag is the profit of the book year (or calendar year) less losses to be carried forward (IB art. 20(1)). Profit is worked out as for a business of an individual (art. 20(2), applying arts. 7, 7a, 10(1) and (2), and 11):
- by sound business practice (goed koopmansgebruik), on a consistent method that can change only for special reasons (art. 7(4));
- depreciation of business assets each year on the part of the unwritten-off cost that belongs to the year (art. 10(2)); spending on buying, building, improving or changing land, buildings, machinery and other business assets, and on taking over or extending the business, is capital, not a cost (art. 11(1)), except small items that sound practice treats as daily costs (art. 11(2));
- no deduction for reserves or for interest on own capital (art. 11(3)), except: an equalisation reserve for costs and for risks that are usually insured but that the body has not insured (art. 11(4)), and a pension reserve for a firm intention to give employees irrevocable pension rights (art. 21). Both need regular books with annual closings in Suriname (arts. 11(5), 21(4)); the pension reserve is released when the rights are granted or the intention lapses, and at the latest in the fourth book year after it was first formed (art. 21(3));
- costs of property used partly privately are not deductible for the private part (art. 7a(1)).

**2. Not deductible (IB art. 23):**
- any direct or indirect distribution of profit, under any name, owed or not, except payments to the State other than as shareholder, and profit shares to staff, directors and commissarissen for work in the business. Director and commissaris profit shares are deductible only up to the caps in the table: 2% per commissaris, 10% per director, 20% and SRD 12,000 for all of them together (art. 23(1°)). **AUDIT FLASH POINT:** a director's pay that depends on profit is a profit share subject to the SRD 12,000 cap; keep fixed salary and profit share apart in the books and contracts;
- interest and fees for the use of goods paid to persons or bodies in a special relationship with the taxpayer, unless the taxpayer shows the arrangement matches ordinary business financing between unrelated parties; even then, only the part not above an arm's-length amount is deductible (art. 23(2°)). **AUDIT FLASH POINT:** related-party loans and leases;
- the income tax itself, and foreign profit taxes where a double tax relief rule applies (art. 23(3°)).

**3. Co-operatives** may deduct profit shares paid to member businesses in proportion to their dealings with the co-operative (IB art. 24).

**4. Participation exemption (IB art. 25).** Left out of profit:
- dividends from resident bodies of art. 1(2)(b) (art. 25(1)(a));
- dividends from non-resident bodies that earned at least nine-tenths of their profit in Suriname in the year the profit relates to (art. 25(1)(b));
- dividends from non-resident bodies received on a shareholding that is part of the normal business; always so at 10% or more of nominal paid-up capital (art. 25(3)).
Conditions for all three (art. 25(4)): the payer could not deduct the distribution; it is not a return of capital that sound practice deducts from book value; together with similar receipts of the last five years it exceeds the related costs of this year and the last five years; and for foreign dividends, the payer is subject to a profit tax where it is established. Dividend tax withheld on exempt distributions is still credited (IB art. 52e(2)).

**5. Losses.** Set off start-up losses first, then losses of the seven previous years (art. 20(3)).

**6. Rate.** 36% of the belastbaar bedrag, on whole SRD (art. 36(1), (3)). Credit dividend tax withheld (IB art. 52e) and provisional payments (art. 52d).

**Functional currency.** On request, a body meeting conditions set by staatsbesluit may keep its books in a currency named by the Minister; amounts in the act are converted at the Central Bank's unified rate, and the return, assessment and payment are then in that currency (IB art. 36(4), (5), art. 42(3)(3°)). The staatsbesluit conditions were not found; refer.

**Bookkeeping.** NVs, co-operatives and mutual insurers keep books from which the yearly profit can be drawn up; other bodies keep books for their business (IB art. 42(3)(1°)(a), (b)). Books are kept in Dutch (art. 42(3)(2°)).

## Deadlines and filing

All returns are filed in the Tax Administration's online portal (portaal.belastingdienst.sr/OPO) with an electronic signature (IB art. 42(1)). From tax year 2025, paper returns from bodies are no longer accepted (Tax Administration news of 15 May 2026). For a calendar book year 2026:

| Step | Date for calendar 2026 | Law |
|---|---|---|
| Written, reasoned request to file the provisional return below the latest definitive tax (secinspectidb@belastingdienst.sr); attach the profit and loss account and balance sheet of the latest definitive return and show why 2026 profit will be lower. The inspector answers in two weeks; silence counts as yes | By 15 February 2026 (book year: within one and a half months of its start) | IB art. 42c(2), (3); Tax Administration notice on art. 42c |
| Provisional return (voorlopige aangifte), with a statement of last year's business income (art. 42b(5)); tax at least the latest definitive return's tax unless the request was granted | By 15 April 2026 (book year: within two and a half months of its start) | IB arts. 42a(1)(b), 42c(1) |
| Provisional tax in four equal instalments; the first with the return | 15 April, 15 July, 15 October, 31 December 2026 (book year: then every three months) | IB art. 42b(2) |
| Definitive return (definitieve aangifte) with a signed, dated balance sheet and profit and loss account showing gross profit and the costs set against it; tax due is paid with the return | Within six months of the end of the book year: 30 June 2027 | IB arts. 43(5), 44(2), 44(5) |
| Dividend tax: file and pay the tax withheld | Within one month after the dividend is made available | Wet Dividendbelasting art. 6(4) |
| Objection against an income tax assessment | Within 60 days of the date of the assessment notice | IB art. 54(1) |

**Portal steps (definitive return, Tax Administration manual for bodies, May 2026).** Log in to the portal; in the green bar choose IB, then Aangifte IB; choose Definitieve aangifte and the tax year; fill seven rubrics in this order: 1 Winst- en verliesrekening (turnover and purchases, staff costs and depreciation, other costs and exceptional items, financial income and charges), 2 Balans activa (cost, opening and closing book value of intangible, tangible and financial fixed assets, stock and work in progress, receivables and cash), 3 Balans passiva (equity, tax reserves and provisions, long and short-term debt; total assets must equal total liabilities at both dates or the portal will not continue), 4 Onttrekkingen en stortingen, 5 Fiscaal niet aftrekbare kosten en vrijstellingen (the art. 23 add-backs and art. 25 exemptions), 6 Overig, 7 Verliesverrekening. Enter 0 where nothing applies. The portal then shows the summary with the belastbaar bedrag and the amount to pay or to be refunded; click Aangifte IB indienen, then Voltooien. A confirmation email follows. A refund is given by assessment; if the inspector does not set it within two months, the inspector must tell the body why in writing (IB art. 44(3), (4)).

## Penalties and interest

- **Assessment with a 100% increase.** No provisional return or payment (IB arts. 42a, 42b, 42c), no definitive return (art. 44(1)), or no payment with the definitive return (art. 44(2)): the inspector sets an assessment and, after a reminder, increases the tax by 100%, or by 10% where the failure is not due to intent or gross negligence (IB art. 51(3), (5), (6)). Such an assessment is payable at once (art. 74(a)).
- **Corrections.** An incorrect definitive return may be corrected by assessment within two years of filing (art. 51(2)); a provisional return with income estimated too low within three months (art. 51(4)).
- **Interest.** Simple interest on late-paid tax and increase, from the day after the due date (IB art. 73(1)); 45% a year under S.B. 1995 no. 98 as noted in the published text.
- **Liability and fines.** Directors, managing partners and representatives in Suriname are jointly and severally liable for the body's tax unless they show they could not see to payment (IB art. 40(7)). A return without the required balance sheet and accounts, or unsigned, counts as not filed (art. 45(2)); failing to file or to attach them is punishable by a fine of up to SRD 500 (arts. 87, 88).
- **Dividend tax.** Tax not withheld or paid is collected by assessment increased by 100%, or 10% (at least SRD 0.05) where not due to intent or gross negligence (Wet Dividendbelasting art. 11); the assessment is payable within one month (art. 12).

## Cross-border points

- **Permanent establishment.** A non-resident body's business profit is taxed only so far as it belongs to a permanent establishment in Suriname, which includes a permanent representative (IB arts. 27(2)(c)(1°), 28(1)). It is always a permanent establishment for insurance activity; construction, assembly, excavation, dredging or clearing work for more than 183 days in twelve months (counting successive contractors on one project); aerial surveys and natural-resource inventories (art. 28(2)); and exploration or exploitation on the Surinamese continental shelf under licence (art. 28(3)). The whole profit on goods of Surinamese origin whose ownership passes in Suriname belongs to the permanent establishment (art. 28(4)).
- **International transport.** Sea and air transport between Suriname and abroad by a non-resident body is not a business in Suriname unless it is managed from Suriname (IB art. 29).
- **Dividends paid abroad.** A Surinamese company withholds 25% dividend tax on dividends to any shareholder, resident or not (Wet Dividendbelasting arts. 1, 5, 6); the art. 4 exemption covers only resident investment companies and resident corporate shareholders.
- **Interest and royalties paid abroad.** The published tax laws read for this skill contain no withholding tax on interest or royalties. A non-resident is taxed on Surinamese interest only where the debt is secured by a mortgage on Surinamese property (IB art. 27(2)(b)(1°)), by assessment.
- **Double tax relief** comes only from a staatsbesluit or treaty (IB art. 37). Foreign profit tax is not deductible from profit where a double tax relief rule applies to it (art. 23(3°)); where none applies, the act does not bar the deduction. Treaty rates are out of scope here.

## Two worked examples that recompute

**Example 1: resident NV, calendar 2026.** Accounting profit after all costs is SRD 2,000,000. The costs include a profit share (tantième) of SRD 150,000 to the one managing director and SRD 100,000 of interest to the parent company, of which the NV can show SRD 60,000 is arm's length. There is a 2020 loss of SRD 300,000 still open (within the seven years). Profit before the director's profit share is far above SRD 120,000, so the SRD 12,000 cap binds and SRD 138,000 is added back (art. 23(1°)). The SRD 40,000 of interest above arm's length is added back (art. 23(2°)). Profit: 2,000,000 + 138,000 + 40,000 = SRD 2,178,000. Less the 2020 loss: SRD 1,878,000. Tax at 36%: SRD 676,080.

```oa-check
name: Suriname NV, profit SRD 2,000,000 with add-backs and a loss (2026)
inputs:
  accounting_profit: 2000000
  director_profit_share: 150000
  related_interest: 100000
  arm_length_interest: 60000
  loss_2020: 300000
steps:
  - profit_before_shares: accounting_profit + director_profit_share
  - share_cap: min(profit_before_shares * 0.10, profit_before_shares * 0.20, 12000)
  - addback_share: max(director_profit_share - share_cap, 0)
  - addback_interest: max(related_interest - arm_length_interest, 0)
  - profit: accounting_profit + addback_share + addback_interest
  - taxable: max(profit - loss_2020, 0)
  - tax: floor(taxable) * 0.36
expected:
  addback_share: 138000
  addback_interest: 40000
  profit: 2178000
  taxable: 1878000
  tax: 676080.00
```

**Example 2 (decision): ask to pay lower provisional tax, or not?** The NV's definitive 2025 tax was SRD 360,000. A large contract ended, and it expects 2026 profit of SRD 500,000 (tax SRD 180,000). The figure that decides is the cash paid in 2026 against the risk of an underestimate.

- **No request.** The provisional tax must be at least the latest definitive tax (art. 42c(1)): SRD 360,000 in four instalments of SRD 90,000. The SRD 180,000 overpaid comes back only by refund assessment after the definitive return (due 30 June 2027).
- **Request by 15 February 2026, granted.** Provisional tax SRD 180,000 in four instalments of SRD 45,000: SRD 180,000 stays in the business during 2026. The inspector may still raise an assessment within three months if the estimate is too low (art. 51(4)).
- **Decision:** make the request if the profit fall can be shown with the latest accounts; it frees SRD 180,000 of cash. A request after 15 February is not dealt with (Tax Administration notice).


## Ask the client first

1. What kind of body is it (NV, foundation, co-operative, association, foreign company), and is it established in Suriname or only active here (permanent establishment, real estate)?
2. What is the book year, and does it differ from the calendar year? All deadlines run from it.
3. Does the body pay profit shares to directors or commissarissen, or interest and rent to related persons or companies? These are the main add-backs.
4. Are there losses from the first three book years or from the seven previous years, and have regular books with annual closings been kept in Suriname?
5. Does it receive dividends (from which companies, what percentage held, are they taxed where they are), or pay dividends (to whom, held since when)?
6. What was the latest definitive tax, and will 2026 profit be clearly lower? The art. 42c request must be made by 15 February.
