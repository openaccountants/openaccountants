# Paraguay: non-resident income tax (INR), dividends paid abroad (IDU) and treaties, 2026 v0.1

> **General reference only.** This skill is general tax/accounting reference material for AI-assisted workflows. It has not been reviewed for any specific person's facts, documents, elections, deadlines, residency, filing status, or local procedures. Do not rely on it to file, pay, amend, or take a tax position without review by a qualified professional in the relevant jurisdiction.

The law is Ley N° 6380/2019 "De Modernización y Simplificación del Sistema Tributario Nacional": Libro I, Título IV (arts. 71 to 79) for the non-resident income tax (INR) and Título II (arts. 40 to 46) for the dividend tax (IDU), read in the text published by the Biblioteca y Archivo Central del Congreso Nacional (BACN). The INR regulation is the Anexo to Decreto N° 3181/2019, in force from 1 January 2020 (Decreto 3181 art. 3), read in the copy published by the Dirección Nacional de Ingresos Tributarios (DNIT). Its start date for card processors withholding INR on digital services was put back to 1 January 2021 by Decreto N° 3667/2020 (art. 2), so that rule is in force for 2026. The INR has no annual return of its own: it is withheld payment by payment and the tax withheld is final (art. 77). Amounts are in guaraníes (G).

Detail lives in the sibling skills. The resident company's own tax (IRE), its deduction caps for payments abroad and the Ley 125/1991 penalties are in [paraguay-corporate-income-tax](paraguay-corporate-income-tax.md). A resident individual's tax (IRP) is in [paraguay-income-tax](paraguay-income-tax.md). IVA on services bought from abroad, including the digital services perception, is in [paraguay-iva](paraguay-iva.md). Related-party pricing is in [paraguay-transfer-pricing](paraguay-transfer-pricing.md).

**Quick answer.** A payment from Paraguay to a non-resident that is Paraguayan-source income bears INR at 15% of a presumed net income. The presumed share depends only on the type of payment: 30% (unrelated foreign bank interest, international freight, digital services, sale of movables), 30% (sale of real estate), 50% (rent of real estate), 70% (personal services) or 100% (payments to a shareholder, head office or related party except all preceding art. 75 categories, and other unlisted payments). That gives 4.5%, 7.5%, 10.5% or 15% of the gross. The Paraguayan payer withholds it and is jointly liable. Dividends and branch profits paid abroad bear IDU at 15% instead. The Spain treaty expressly covers INR and IDU. The table also records the text caps in the other five treaties; their application to the post-2019 taxes remains unconfirmed and must be established before using those caps.

## Who this applies to

**Residents and non-residents.**

- **Companies and other entities** are resident if incorporated under Paraguayan law, or if foreign and they establish their domicile in Paraguay, from their registration in the Public Registry (Ley 6380 art. 5).
- **Individuals** are resident for tax purposes if they hold permanent residence in Paraguay under Decreto 3181 Anexo art. 2. That decree refers to the former Ley N° 978/1996; Ley N° 6984/2022 art. 107 repealed it. Check the permanent-residence document under the current migration law; the historical statutory reference does not establish that an old document is sufficient.
- **INR taxpayers** are individuals, companies and other entities resident, domiciled or incorporated abroad that are not resident and have no permanent domicile in Paraguay, when they earn Paraguayan-source income from the taxable events of Títulos I (IRE) and III (IRP) of Libro I. Acting through an agent, attorney or representative in Paraguay does not change this (arts. 71 and 72).
- **A branch, agency or permanent establishment** of a foreign person that carries on taxable activities in Paraguay is itself an IRE taxpayer (art. 2.7) and is taxed in [paraguay-corporate-income-tax](paraguay-corporate-income-tax.md). What it remits to its head office bears IDU (arts. 40 and 43.2), not INR.

**Who acts.**

- **The Paraguayan payer.** Every IRE taxpayer listed in art. 2, including the State and the municipalities, withholds INR when it makes funds available, remits or pays income to a non-resident, and is jointly liable for the tax (art. 77).
- **A resident individual** in independent personal services withholds INR only when paying for services of a similar kind directly linked to the IRP-taxed income (art. 77, second paragraph; Decreto 3181 art. 9). Other resident individuals do not withhold.
- **Special agents** (Decreto 3181 art. 9): the notary on sales of real estate and registrable movables; the card processor or administrator on digital services; the Paraguayan company whose shares or quotas change hands between non-residents; the Paraguayan issuer of bonds placed abroad, on the interest; otherwise the notary, agent, attorney or representative who takes part in the operation.
- **The company paying dividends** withholds IDU from profits put at the disposal of its owners, resident or not (art. 41).

**Out of scope.** The IRE and IRP of residents, IVA, payroll and social security for workers posted to or from Paraguay, and the transfer pricing study. Free-zone users, maquila companies and projects under the investment incentive regime of Ley 7548/2025 have their own regimes: refer.

## The base

### What is Paraguayan-source income of a non-resident (art. 73)

Income from activities carried out, property situated or rights used economically in Paraguay. In addition:

1. interest on loans or other investments by the head office or other branches abroad, and royalties and technical assistance paid by a Paraguayan branch, agency or establishment of a foreign person;
2. interest or commissions on financing or loans, where the issuer or lender is incorporated or resident abroad;
3. interest, commissions, returns or capital gains from securities of Paraguayan companies;
4. capital gains on the sale or rent of property situated in Paraguay, assignments of rights, and the sale of securities, shares and quotas of Paraguayan companies;
5. insurance and reinsurance covering risks in Paraguay or persons resident there when the contract is made;
6. the assignment of use of property and rights used in Paraguay, even partly;
7. derivatives where the investing or receiving entity is in Paraguay;
8. international transport of goods or persons between Paraguay and another country;
9. services rendered from abroad by individuals, professional or not, linked to IRE-taxed income;
10. services rendered in Paraguay, wholly or partly, by individuals, whatever their link to IRE income;
11. services by non-resident companies and entities, from abroad or in Paraguay, linked to IRE-taxed income;
12. digital services used in Paraguay, including entertainment and gambling, whoever supplies them. A service counts as used in Paraguay when any one of these is in Paraguay: the IP address of the device or the SIM country code; the customer's billing address; the bank account used to pay; the billing address the bank holds; or the issuer of the card used;
13. any other amount made available, remitted or paid from Paraguay abroad, except amounts linked to imports of goods.

Repairs of aircraft, ships and land vehicles carried out abroad are not Paraguayan-source income (art. 73, last paragraph).

### When the tax arises (art. 74)

When the funds are made available, remitted or paid, whichever comes first. Set-off, novation, settlement and any other way of extinguishing the debt count as making funds available. For a sale of Paraguayan shares between non-residents the tax arises on the date of the operation or contract (art. 77, third paragraph).

### How the withholding is computed

1. Classify the payment under art. 75 and take the presumed net income share.
2. The base is the gross amount, excluding IVA where the operation is subject to IVA (Decreto 3181 art. 10).
3. Apply 15% (art. 76). The INR itself is not deductible in computing the withholding (art. 77).
4. **Gross-up.** The withholding may not be less than the rate applied to what the recipient actually received plus the withholding (Decreto 3181 art. 10, second paragraph). If the contract says the non-resident receives a net amount and the payer bears the tax, the base grows until the withholding equals the rate on the grossed-up amount.
5. Apply a treaty cap where one applies (see the treaty table and the facts to ask).
6. Issue the withholding voucher (comprobante de retención) through the DNIT's Tesakã software or other enabled means (Resolución General 33/2020 art. 3). It carries the non-resident's name, foreign identity or tax number, country of residence, concept, date the tax arose, net income, rate and tax (Decreto 3181 art. 11).

The INR withheld is the non-resident's final tax: it files no Paraguayan return for it (art. 77, last paragraph).

### Dividends and branch profits paid abroad (IDU)

- **Taxable event.** Profits, dividends or returns made available or paid to the owner, partners or shareholders of Paraguayan sole-owner businesses, S.A., S.R.L. and similar companies, and by permanent establishments of foreign entities, bear IDU (art. 40). A distribution is any sharing of profit in cash or in kind, except shares or quotas issued by capitalising profit. Distribution of excess legal reserve, voluntary reserves or capital from capitalised profits also counts, taking reserves first, then undistributed profits, then contributed capital (art. 40).
- **Presumed distributions** (art. 40): loans to owners (with the 2% exception for financial lenders), a cash shortfall above 10% of the cash account, and any withdrawal of funds or private use of company goods or services without consideration, including paying owners' or their families' personal expenses.
- **Base.** The net profits, dividends or returns made available or paid (art. 42).
- **Rate.** 15% for a non-resident recipient, including a foreign head office (art. 43.2). Reduced by 20% under art. 133 for qualifying agro-industrial investment.
- **Final tax.** For a non-resident the IDU is a single and final payment (art. 41, last paragraph).
- **When it arises** (art. 44): for an S.A. and other entities that hold shareholders' meetings, when the meeting decides the distribution, whenever it is paid; for an S.R.L. and others without a meeting, on the date in the by-laws, otherwise in the fourth month after year-end; for capital redemption, when the meeting or competent body decides it; for a sole-owner business, in the fourth month after year-end unless the profit was reinvested in the business.

## Deadlines and filing

Portal: the DNIT's Sistema Marangatu, logged in with the Clave de Acceso Confidencial de Usuario.

1. **Register the obligation.** A payer that withholds INR or IDU needs the obligation in its RUC: 725 for withholdings of income taxes (IRE, IRP, INR) and 726 for IDU withholding ([DNIT INR page](https://www.dnit.gov.py/web/portal-institucional/inr); [DNIT IDU page](https://www.dnit.gov.py/web/portal-institucional/idu)). The DNIT page points non-resident digital suppliers to RG 114/2022; its registration/payment procedure is not established here because that resolution has not been read.
2. **Withhold and issue the voucher** at the moment the tax arises (art. 74), through Tesakã (RG 33/2020 art. 3) or, for IDU, the virtual IDU withholding voucher in Marangatu (RG 31/2019).
3. **INR, monthly (Form 525, obligation 725).** The DNIT puts a pro-forma settlement of last month's withholdings in Marangatu. The withholding agent checks and confirms it within the first six calendar days of the month through "Formulario N° 525 Liquidación de Retenciones de los Impuestos a las Rentas Versión 1" (RG 33/2020 art. 3). If it does not confirm, the DNIT treats it as accepted and books it to the agent's current account as a final balance. Payment is due by the 7th of each month, electronically or with a payment slip from Marangatu; a non-working day moves it to the next working day ([DNIT INR guide, V 09/24](https://www.dnit.gov.py/documents/20123/218215/Cartilla+INR+23.09.2024.pdf/9715bb1b-7568-ae98-b3b2-4a72f521aae7)).
4. **IDU, monthly (Form 526).** The DNIT puts a pro-forma IDU settlement at the agent's disposal; the agent checks and confirms it within the first 12 calendar days of the month. The fiscal period is the month and year in which the tax arose, as shown on the voucher. Without confirmation it is treated as accepted, and the amount is paid by the 13th of each month, by electronic payment only; if the 13th is not a working day, the deadline moves to the next working day (RG 31/2019 arts. 8, 9 and 11).
5. **IDU informative return (obligation 954).** The company files the annual IDU informative return within the sixth month after year-end on the informative-returns calendar ([DNIT IDU guide](https://www.dnit.gov.py/documents/20123/302950/IDU+Cartilla+Informativa+03.09.24.pdf/25eeb741-b627-f2f0-f798-a9188a1a0f26)). The notes on profits and reserves required from years closed 31 December 2025 are in [paraguay-corporate-income-tax](paraguay-corporate-income-tax.md).
6. **Exempt payments.** A payer of income exempt under art. 79.3, 79.4 or 79.5 reports the payment through the same voucher with the full amount as net income and a rate of zero (Decreto 3181 art. 12).
7. **After filing.** Keep the Marangatu acknowledgement and the vouchers; give the non-resident its voucher, which is its proof of the final tax.

## Penalties and interest

- **Joint liability.** The withholding agent answers jointly with the non-resident for the INR (art. 77) and the company for the IDU (art. 41). Tax not withheld is collected from the payer.
- **Late payment, late filing, underpayment and fraud** follow Ley N° 125/1991 (mora, contravención, omisión de pago, defraudación). The rates and the presumption of fraud for a withholding agent that pays over withheld tax late are set out once, in [paraguay-corporate-income-tax](paraguay-corporate-income-tax.md#penalties-and-interest).
- **Foreign-expense deduction cap.** Necessary expenses abroad are limited to 1% of gross income unless the provider is subject to INR and withholding is made; with that condition met, art. 15.18 permits full deduction under this rule. This is a cap, not automatic denial of the entire expense, and does not remove withholding-agent liability.
- **Real estate.** Without proof of INR paid the Public Registry does not register the deed (art. 137).

## Cross-border points

- **Treaty relief at source.** Where a treaty applies, the cap in the treaty table limits Paraguay's tax on the gross amount. First establish that the tax and payment are covered, confirm beneficial ownership and check any permanent-establishment connection. RG 65/2020 arts. 7 and 8 require the local payer to obtain the foreign tax authority's residence certificate, containing its issue date, beneficiary name and identification number, and a declaration of treaty residence. It is valid for the foreign authority's stated term, or one year from issue if none is stated. Keep it for the limitation period. If it is not in Spanish, attach a translation by a Paraguayan registered public translator; a competent translator is permitted where no registered translator exists for that language. Keep the translation for the same period. A separate prior-registration or refund procedure remains unconfirmed.
- **Residents earning abroad.** A resident company's foreign-source income and its foreign tax credit (art. 6 last paragraph and art. 134, which also applies to income under the treaties in force) are in [paraguay-corporate-income-tax](paraguay-corporate-income-tax.md). A resident individual's foreign services income is in [paraguay-income-tax](paraguay-income-tax.md).
- **IVA.** Services from abroad used in Paraguay carry IVA as well as INR. The IVA rules, including the 10% digital services perception, are in [paraguay-iva](paraguay-iva.md).
- **Transfer pricing.** Payments to related parties abroad, and to residents of low or nil tax jurisdictions, must also meet the arm's length rules in [paraguay-transfer-pricing](paraguay-transfer-pricing.md).
- **Employees.** Pay to a non-resident for work done in Paraguay is personal services income (art. 73.10, 70% presumed net income). Treaty employment articles (for example Uruguay art. 14, with its 183-day test) can move the right to tax. Social security for posted workers is not in this skill.

## Two worked examples that recompute

### Example 1: a Paraguayan S.A. pays three foreign suppliers in March 2026

Alfa S.A. (IRE General) pays, in March 2026, to recipients resident in a state with no Paraguayan treaty:

- G 200,000,000 to a foreign consulting company for management advice linked to its IRE income (art. 73.11). The consulting company is not related. This is not one of the 30% items, so the presumed net income is 100% (art. 75.7): INR G 30,000,000.
- G 80,000,000 to a foreign individual engineer for services (art. 73.9). Personal services: 70% (art. 75.4) = G 56,000,000, at 15% = G 8,400,000.
- G 500,000,000 of interest to an unrelated foreign bank (art. 73.2). 30% (art. 75.1.a) = G 150,000,000, at 15% = G 22,500,000.

Total INR G 60,900,000, confirmed on Form 525 within the first six days of April 2026 and paid by 7 April 2026.

**Edge case, gross-up.** The consulting contract instead says the company receives G 200,000,000 net and Alfa bears the tax. The withholding must equal 15% of the amount received plus the withholding (Decreto 3181 art. 10): W = 15% x (200,000,000 + W), so W = 200,000,000 x 15 / 85 = G 35,294,118 (rounded), on a grossed-up base of G 235,294,118.

### Example 2: decision, distribute 2026 profit to a foreign parent or capitalise it

This is an illustration assuming the law and Spain treaty read on 11 November 2026 remain unchanged at the April 2027 distribution. Recheck the actual law and treaty conditions before that future decision; these are not verified 2027 liabilities.

Beta S.A. has 2026 profit after IRE of G 1,000,000,000. Its sole shareholder is a Spanish company that has held 100% of Beta directly for several years. The shareholders' meeting in April 2027 decides between:

- **Distribute.** IDU arises when the meeting decides the distribution (art. 44). The domestic rate is 15% (art. 43.2): G 150,000,000. The Spain convention covers the IDU (art. 2.3) and caps tax on dividends to a company holding at least 50% for 365 days at 5% (art. 10.2.a): G 50,000,000, if the treaty rate is applied. The parent receives G 950,000,000. If the shareholder were resident in a non-treaty state the tax would stay at G 150,000,000 and it would receive G 850,000,000.
- **Capitalise.** Profit taken to capital bears no IDU now (art. 42), provided the capital increase is formalised within 12 months of the meeting (art. 44). IDU comes back when that capital is redeemed, and the redemption is treated as coming from capitalised reserves and profits first (art. 40).

The figure that decides is the IDU paid now: G 50,000,000 (Spain, 5%) or G 150,000,000 (no treaty) against zero now if capitalised. The treaty saving is G 100,000,000. If the group needs the cash in Spain, distribution under the treaty costs 5%; if not, capitalising defers the tax but does not remove it. Do not lend the cash to the parent instead: a loan to a shareholder is a presumed distribution (art. 40.1).

```oa-check
name: PY IDU on 2026 profit to a Spanish parent, treaty against no treaty
inputs:
  profit: 1000000000
  idu_domestic_rate: 0.15
  spain_cap_rate: 0.05
steps:
  - idu_no_treaty: profit * idu_domestic_rate
  - idu_spain: profit * spain_cap_rate
  - net_no_treaty: profit - idu_no_treaty
  - net_spain: profit - idu_spain
  - treaty_saving: idu_no_treaty - idu_spain
  - idu_if_capitalised_now: profit * 0
expected:
  idu_no_treaty: 150000000
  idu_spain: 50000000
  net_no_treaty: 850000000
  net_spain: 950000000
  treaty_saving: 100000000
  idu_if_capitalised_now: 0
```

## Ask the client first

1. Is the recipient a non-resident (for an individual, does it hold Paraguayan permanent residence; for an entity, is it incorporated or registered in Paraguay)? Does it have a branch or permanent establishment in Paraguay?
2. What exactly is the payment for (service in Paraguay or from abroad, interest, royalty, rent, sale of real estate or shares, dividend, digital service), and when are the funds made available, remitted or paid?
3. Is the recipient related to the payer (shareholder, head office, group company), and does the contract make the payer bear the tax?
4. Where is the recipient resident, is it one of the six treaty states, and do you hold its residence certificate and evidence that it is the beneficial owner?
5. For dividends: the shareholder's residence and holding period, and whether the profit will be distributed, reserved or capitalised.
6. Is the payer a free-zone user, a maquila company or under a special investment regime?
