# Singapore tax planning v2.0: 2026 income and YA 2027

## Who this applies to

Use this for a Singapore individual, self-employed person or small company comparing lawful choices. The income period and Year of Assessment are different: an individual's calendar-year 2026 income is assessed in YA 2027. A company's basis period follows its financial year, so establish the relevant YA before using any exemption or rebate. YA 2026 filing information below is explicitly historical. Sources were read on 14 November 2026 within the scopes at the end.

Start with reconciled books and the taxpayer's legal identity. A sole proprietor's business belongs in that person's return; an incorporated company is a separate taxpayer. A company is not automatically cheaper and incorporation does not turn an employee's existing salary into company profit. Distinguish tax saving from a deduction, cash locked in retirement accounts, deferred tax, compliance costs and GST collected for IRAS. Do not present artificial income splitting as planning.

The tax authority is IRAS. The principal legislation is the Income Tax Act 1947 and the Goods and Services Tax Act. Legislative references below identify the provision discussed by the official guidance, not a claim that every current statutory section was accessible directly. The rules for cross-border status, gains, dividends and foreign income have exceptions; absence of a capital gains tax is not exemption for a trading receipt.

## The base

### The method, step by step

1. Identify taxpayer, residence, business structure, basis period, income sources, ownership, family facts, existing elections and any statutory restrictions. Reconcile receipts, invoices, stock, assets, loans, grants and private transactions. Use chargeable income rather than a bank balance.
2. Calculate a baseline with no optional planning change. Separate revenue, cost of sales, allowable revenue expenses, disallowed private/capital items, capital allowances, losses, donations and individual reliefs. A cash payment is not necessarily deductible; unpaid but incurred expenditure may belong in the period. Do not deduct an asset as an expense and again as capital allowances. [Official source](https://www.iras.gov.sg/taxes/individual-income-tax/self-employed-and-partnerships/business-expenses-and-deductions) [Official source](https://www.iras.gov.sg/taxes/corporate-income-tax/income-deductions-for-companies/claiming-allowances/capital-allowances) [Official source](https://www.iras.gov.sg/taxes/individual-income-tax/self-employed-and-partnerships/calculating-my-business-income/calculating-business-income)
3. Identify genuinely available choices in the modules below. For each choice, record eligibility evidence, dates, election deadline, tax effect, CPF/GST consequences, cash restrictions and compliance cost. Personal contributions are not a sole proprietor's business expense merely because paid from a business bank account.
4. Recompute each option on the same pre-remuneration business profit and same basis period. If owner salary is a company deduction, remove it from the company's profit before computing corporate tax and separately calculate the owner's salary tax and employer CPF where applicable. Do not charge corporation tax on the original profit and then deduct that salary a second time.
5. Apply resident bands or the correct non-resident rules, company exemptions, then any rebate actually belonging to that YA. Compute personal relief headroom before recommending a non-refundable contribution. Do not value a deduction at the top marginal rate if it crosses a band. [Official source](https://www.iras.gov.sg/taxes/individual-income-tax/basics-of-individual-income-tax/tax-residency-and-tax-rates/individual-income-tax-rates) [Official source](https://www.iras.gov.sg/taxes/individual-income-tax/basics-of-individual-income-tax/tax-reliefs-rebates-and-deductions/tax-reliefs)
6. Report the reconciled comparison, assumptions, cash paid or retained, blocked withdrawals, filing actions and unresolved eligibility. An option cannot be recommended solely because its arithmetic is lower. Preserve records and confirmation receipts for the actual filing and election.

### 1. Sole proprietor versus private company

A sole proprietor pays individual income tax on adjusted business profits together with other taxable income; a private company pays corporate income tax on its own profits. The company route has annual ACRA and IRAS obligations even if its tax is nil. Start with `singapore-formation` for formation and ongoing filings. Use the individual's actual compulsory CPF position rather than assuming a company removes it. [Official source](https://www.acra.gov.sg/register/business/choosing-business-structure/) [Official source](https://www.iras.gov.sg/taxes/corporate-income-tax/basics-of-corporate-income-tax/basic-guide-to-corporate-income-tax-for-companies)

SUTE requires a company incorporated in Singapore, Singapore tax residence for that YA, and no more than 20 shareholders throughout the relevant basis period with either all shareholders individuals or at least one individual directly holding at least 10% of ordinary shares. Investment holding companies and property developers are excluded. The first three consecutive YAs are not postponed by a loss or nil-income year. A non-qualifying company may instead qualify for PTE; never add both exemptions to the same income. At S$200,000 normal chargeable income, SUTE gives taxable S$75,000 and gross corporate tax S$12,750, an effective 6.375% before any particular YA's rebate. [Official source](https://www.iras.gov.sg/taxes/corporate-income-tax/basics-of-corporate-income-tax/corporate-income-tax-rate-rebates-and-tax-exemption-schemes)

A salary for actual work can be a company deduction when the ordinary deductibility conditions are met; it is personal employment income. Dividends are not a company deduction. One-tier dividends from Singapore-resident companies are generally exempt for the individual recipient; cooperative dividends and non-one-tier cases require separate treatment. Do not assume foreign residence or a trust has the same treatment. [Official source](https://www.iras.gov.sg/taxes/individual-income-tax/self-employed-and-partnerships/business-expenses-and-deductions) [Official source](https://www.iras.gov.sg/taxes/individual-income-tax/basics-of-individual-income-tax/what-is-taxable-what-is-not/dividends)

The former default that incorporation is beneficial above S$80,000 to S$100,000 profit is not an official threshold. Calculate the real comparison including remuneration, CPF, reliefs, exemptions, costs, extraction and commercial purpose. A lower company rate is not an automatic personal tax saving. Capital left in the company is not personal spending money.

### 2. Family structuring and personal reliefs

A family member's genuine business work and arm's-length remuneration require evidence of duties and payment; do not invent a salary or treat gifts as deductible wages. The old blanket claim that gifts to family can never attract attribution or anti-avoidance rules is not usable guidance. Record beneficial ownership, the source and purpose of transfers, related-party terms and commercial substance. Consider genuine spousal/dependant relief and eligible family CPF cash top-ups instead. ### 3. Self-employed deductions

Obtain bills, business purpose and a reasonable documented private/business allocation. The Section 14 approach is expenditure incurred wholly and exclusively in earning income, subject to specific exclusions; no deduction for drawings, income tax, capital costs or ordinary private car expenses simply because a business paid them. Home-office claims require proof of qualifying expenses and business use, not an automatic floor-area percentage or a new deduction for a domestic cost which fails the statutory test. The detailed old dedicated-workspace apportionment claim is held pending its relevant official conditions. [Official source](https://www.iras.gov.sg/taxes/individual-income-tax/self-employed-and-partnerships/business-expenses-and-deductions)

From YA 2027 the broad-based 20% fixed expense deduction ratio is an alternative for eligible self-employed business income with allowable expenses actually incurred and aggregate annual gross income not above S$50,000 per nature of business. Aggregate multiple businesses with the same nature; the same basis must be used within that nature. Different natures may use different eligible bases. Industry-specific FEDR income is excluded from this broad-based route. Actual-expense and FEDR deductions cannot both be claimed against the same income. Use `sg-income-tax` for the commission, delivery, private-hire/taxi and general sole-proprietor procedures. [Official source](https://www.iras.gov.sg/taxes/individual-income-tax/self-employed-and-partnerships/business-expenses-and-deductions/fixed-expense-deduction-ratio-for-self-employed-persons-and-platform-workers)

Renovation/refurbishment is now discussed under Section 14N, not the old Section 14Q label. Qualifying expenditure is limited to S$300,000 per fixed three-YA period, first YA 2025 to YA 2027. Three-year straight-line treatment exists, with an option to take qualifying expenditure in one year permanently available from YA 2025. Do not reset the cap for every project or claim the same cost under repairs/capital allowances as well. Costs must actually qualify; property purchase and structural/capital items are not all brought into this scheme by their invoice description. [Official source](https://www.iras.gov.sg/media/docs/default-source/e-tax/etaxguide_it_deduction_r_r_costs.pdf)

Qualifying IPC donations receive a 250% deduction, before personal reliefs. The official example expressly covers a 2026 donation in YA 2027. An ordinary charitable payment, foreign donation or transaction giving a material benefit is not automatically an IPC deduction; identify the recipient and scheme, and reconcile pre-filled information. There is no universal S$80,000 donation cap merely because personal reliefs have that cap. [Official source](https://www.iras.gov.sg/taxes/individual-income-tax/basics-of-individual-income-tax/tax-reliefs-rebates-and-deductions/donations)

Employee medical-expense deductions normally have a 1% remuneration limit. The higher 2% ceiling depends on qualifying portable medical arrangements, such as PMBS/TMIS or qualifying portable medical insurance, or qualifying additional MediSave contributions under the stated conditions. The former label 'PHPC programme' does not establish entitlement. Qualifying additional MediSave contributions are capped at S$2,730 per employee per year; the aggregate deduction ceiling still applies. Check excluded rider premiums and the IRAS remuneration denominator, rather than applying the percentage to turnover or only cash wages. GST input recovery for medical costs has a separate test. [Official source](https://www.iras.gov.sg/taxes/corporate-income-tax/income-deductions-for-companies/business-expenses/tax-treatment-of-business-expenses-%28m-r%29)

Keep staff training and R&D on the planning checklist, but apply the ordinary business deduction conditions and obtain the particular programme's official eligibility before using an enhanced deduction. Personal course fees are not reinstated as a business cost simply because course-fee relief has ended. The old unqualified references to Sections 14C/14E do not establish an enhanced R&D rate; those detailed conditions remain held rather than inventing a figure. [Official source](https://www.iras.gov.sg/taxes/individual-income-tax/self-employed-and-partnerships/business-expenses-and-deductions) [Official source](https://www.iras.gov.sg/taxes/individual-income-tax/basics-of-individual-income-tax/tax-reliefs-rebates-and-deductions/tax-reliefs/course-fees-relief)

### 4. Capital allowances and intellectual property

Accounting depreciation is not the tax deduction. Prepare an asset schedule with cost, use, purchase/obligation dates, grants, hire purchase, prior claims, disposal proceeds and private-use restrictions. Add back depreciation in the tax computation and claim only the qualifying allowance. An order or deposit on the last day of the year does not by itself establish entitlement. [Official source](https://www.iras.gov.sg/taxes/corporate-income-tax/income-deductions-for-companies/claiming-allowances/capital-allowances)

### 5. Current losses, carry-forward, carry-back and group relief

Keep current business losses, unabsorbed capital allowances and donations in separate schedules. A self-employed trade loss may interact with other income, but ring-fenced activities and the particular relief's conditions must be checked. A company's loss is not the shareholder's salary deduction. The old blanket 'all losses offset all income' is not authority to offset a rental deficit, passive investment loss or quarantined trade loss. Use the relevant IRAS loss computation and do not refund more than prior tax actually payable.

For companies, unutilised trade losses/allowances may be carried forward subject to the applicable shareholding test, and capital allowances additionally require continuity of the same trade/business. Do not apply a company shareholder test to an individual. The precise carry-forward testing dates, waiver process and ordering remain a source-confirmation item here; no transaction relying on a change in ownership is cleared by this guide. [Official source](https://www.iras.gov.sg/taxes/corporate-income-tax/income-deductions-for-companies/claiming-reliefs/loss-carry-back-relief)

Ordinary company carry-back is to the immediate preceding YA with a combined S$100,000 cap for current unutilised trade losses and capital allowances, excluding donations. Meet the shareholding test and, for capital allowances, same-business test; the election is irrevocable and made by the return due date. Use Form C and the required carry-back schedules, not Form C-S/C-S (Lite). Three-year carry-back was a specifically dated enhanced scheme for YA 2020/2021; it is not the current general rule. [Official source](https://www.iras.gov.sg/taxes/corporate-income-tax/income-deductions-for-companies/claiming-reliefs/loss-carry-back-relief)

Group relief is a separate transfer within an eligible Singapore group, not another name for carry-back. It covers current unutilised capital allowances, trade losses and donations in that order. Both companies must be Singapore-incorporated with the same financial year end. The 75% ordinary-capital test also requires the profits/assets test; the qualifying continuous period controls the amount. Common individual ownership alone does not qualify. Confirm exclusions and file GR-A/GR-B with Form C by the deadline; election is irrevocable. [Official source](https://www.iras.gov.sg/taxes/corporate-income-tax/income-deductions-for-companies/claiming-reliefs/group-relief)

## Penalties and interest

Lawful planning does not remove return, payment or record obligations. A failure to file, incorrect return, evasion and avoidance counteraction are different regimes; do not turn one maximum into a universal penalty quote. IRAS' individual pages distinguish incorrect-return and evasion penalties, filing enforcement and late payment. Personal late payment normally starts at 5%, with further monthly charges only after the specified notice/non-payment conditions; use the notice and current procedure. [Official source](https://www.iras.gov.sg/taxes/individual-income-tax/basics-of-individual-income-tax/receive-tax-bill-pay-tax-check-refunds/late-payment-or-non-payment-of-individual-income-tax) [Official source](https://www.iras.gov.sg/taxes/individual-income-tax/basics-of-individual-income-tax/consequences-of-non-compliance/errors-in-tax-returns) [Official source](https://www.iras.gov.sg/taxes/individual-income-tax/basics-of-individual-income-tax/understanding-my-income-tax-filing/late-filing-or-non-filing-of-individual-income-tax-returns-form-b1-b-p-m)

Section 33A's 50% avoidance surcharge is separate from an ordinary late-payment charge. For GST, use `sg-gst-return` for late-filing/payment/correction rules. The old generic 'S$10,000 plus 10% of unpaid GST' registration penalty has not been separately confirmed here and is not the amount to promise. Refer a historic default promptly, retain the timeline and test the actual voluntary-disclosure conditions; a genuine correction is not a guarantee of immunity. [Official source](https://www.iras.gov.sg/media/docs/default-source/e-tax/etaxguides_cit_the-general-anti-avoidance-provision-and-its-application.pdf) [Official source](https://www.iras.gov.sg/taxes/goods-services-tax-%28gst%29/gst-registration-deregistration/do-i-need-to-register-for-gst)

## Cross-border points

Establish residence for the actual period before using individual reliefs or the resident schedule. IRAS publishes the ordinary 183-day rule and specific three-year/two-calendar-year employee concessions; not every director, professional or short work engagement qualifies. Non-resident employment income is generally taxed at 15% or resident rates, whichever is higher, while other categories such as director fees/rental may be at 24%; non-resident professional income has its own gross/net rules. Do not treat all non-resident income as one rate. [Official source](https://www.iras.gov.sg/taxes/individual-income-tax/basics-of-individual-income-tax/tax-residency-and-tax-rates/working-out-my-tax-residency) [Official source](https://www.iras.gov.sg/taxes/individual-income-tax/basics-of-individual-income-tax/tax-residency-and-tax-rates/individual-income-tax-rates)

Foreign dividends/interest received by resident individuals can be exempt with partnership and business exceptions; Singapore-source employment or trade is not exempt just because the customer/bank is abroad. Treaty's residence, permanent establishment, withholding, foreign credits and another country's taxation require a separate supported analysis. An SRS foreigner contribution cap is not evidence of residence; SRS withdrawals may have withholding and final-assessment differences. Cross-border company extraction and foreign tax on dividends are not resolved by the domestic one-tier exemption. [Official source](https://www.iras.gov.sg/taxes/individual-income-tax/basics-of-individual-income-tax/what-is-taxable-what-is-not/dividends) [Official source](https://www.iras.gov.sg/taxes/individual-income-tax/basics-of-individual-income-tax/what-is-taxable-what-is-not/interest) [Official source](https://www.iras.gov.sg/taxes/individual-income-tax/basics-of-individual-income-tax/special-tax-schemes/tax-on-srs-withdrawals)

## Two worked examples that recompute

These are four retained planning decisions, now with consistent bases and explicit conditions. Gross tax is shown before any unpublished YA 2027 rebate. The examples do not choose an entity or lock cash away solely from the tax saving.

### Example 1: same S$200,000 profit, sole proprietor versus company with salary

Assume 2026 business profit before owner remuneration S$200,000. The individual is tax-resident; for this comparison compute personal tax before personal reliefs/compulsory employee contributions and assume no employer CPF applies (for example, an actual qualifying foreign employee). The company independently meets SUTE conditions in its first eligible YA. Salary of S$100,000 is genuine, incurred and deductible; no other adjustments, costs or foreign taxes are assumed. For a citizen/PR repeat the calculation with exact employee/employer CPF and all reliefs, not these assumptions.

Sole proprietor: chargeable income before reliefs S$200,000; gross tax S$21,150. Company: S$200,000 less salary S$100,000 leaves S$100,000 profit, S$75,000 SUTE exemption, S$25,000 taxed at 17% = S$4,250. Owner's S$100,000 salary has gross resident tax S$5,650 before reliefs. Combined S$9,900; difference S$11,250 before compliance cost and other missing effects. Remaining company cash from these profits is S$95,750, potentially a one-tier dividend under the assumed conditions. No YA 2026 rebate is carried into this YA 2027 comparison. [Official source](https://www.iras.gov.sg/taxes/corporate-income-tax/basics-of-corporate-income-tax/corporate-income-tax-rate-rebates-and-tax-exemption-schemes) [Official source](https://www.iras.gov.sg/taxes/individual-income-tax/basics-of-individual-income-tax/tax-residency-and-tax-rates/individual-income-tax-rates) [Official source](https://www.iras.gov.sg/taxes/individual-income-tax/basics-of-individual-income-tax/what-is-taxable-what-is-not/dividends)

```oa-check
name: Same pre-salary profit entity comparison without historic rebate
inputs:
  business_profit: 200000
  owner_salary: 100000
  sute_first_exemption: 0.75
  corporate_rate: 0.17
  individual_tax_to_160000: 13950
  individual_upper_rate: 0.18
  salary_tax_to_80000: 3350
  salary_upper_rate: 0.115
steps:
  - sole_tax: individual_tax_to_160000 + (business_profit - 160000) * individual_upper_rate
  - company_profit: business_profit - owner_salary
  - company_taxable: company_profit * (1 - sute_first_exemption)
  - company_tax: company_taxable * corporate_rate
  - salary_tax: salary_tax_to_80000 + (owner_salary - 80000) * salary_upper_rate
  - combined_tax: company_tax + salary_tax
  - difference: sole_tax - combined_tax
  - dividend_available: company_profit - company_tax
expected:
  sole_tax: 21150
  company_taxable: 25000
  company_tax: 4250
  salary_tax: 5650
  combined_tax: 9900
  difference: 11250
  dividend_available: 95750
```

### Example 2: employee, SRS and eligible cash top-ups

Assume a below-55 resident citizen/third-year-or-later PR with S$180,000 annual salary, verified employee compulsory CPF S$20,400, S$1,000 earned-income relief and no other personal relief. The actual OW/AW mix reaches the S$102,000 annual wage ceiling at a 20% employee share; a different wage mix may not. Baseline chargeable income is S$158,600, not S$179,000. Gross tax is S$7,950 + 15% × S$38,600 = S$13,740.

Assume accepted eligible SRS S$15,300 and eligible cash top-ups S$8,000 self plus S$8,000 parent, with recipient headroom, no excluded MRSS/MMSS-matched portion and all family eligibility satisfied. Total relief is S$52,700, below S$80,000. Revised chargeable income S$127,300; tax S$7,950 + 15% × S$7,300 = S$9,045. Saving S$4,695 for S$31,300 of additional restricted savings. This is not a S$31,300 cash refund; future SRS withdrawal taxation and liquidity matter. ### Example 3: S$10,000 qualifying IPC donation

The entire change is in the 22% band: revised CI S$320,000, gross tax falls from S$50,050 to S$44,550, saving S$5,500. Net cash cost is S$4,500. If the deduction crosses lower bands, the saving is smaller; eligibility and the actual donation still matter. ### Example 4: eligible one-year computer allowance versus spreading

Assume a SUTE-eligible company's normal chargeable income before this capital allowance is S$350,000, and S$150,000 of qualifying computers/prescribed automation equipment is eligible for the one-year allowance in this basis period. No depreciation double deduction, grant, disposal, loss or rebate applies. Before CA: exempt S$125,000, taxable S$225,000, gross tax S$38,250. One-year allowance reduces normal chargeable income to S$200,000; taxable S$75,000, tax S$12,750. Difference S$25,500 because this whole allowance removes income otherwise taxed at 17%.

If a valid three-year route were selected instead, first-year allowance S$50,000 leaves normal chargeable income S$300,000, taxable S$175,000 and first-year tax S$29,750. First-year tax is S$17,000 higher than the one-year option, with remaining allowances potentially available later subject to conditions. Other equipment does not qualify for one-year treatment just because the company prefers the cash result. A rebate, exemption-band crossing or loss can change the cash difference; a 50% rebate would not 'amplify' a saving. [Official source](https://www.iras.gov.sg/taxes/corporate-income-tax/income-deductions-for-companies/claiming-allowances/capital-allowances) [Official source](https://www.iras.gov.sg/taxes/corporate-income-tax/basics-of-corporate-income-tax/corporate-income-tax-rate-rebates-and-tax-exemption-schemes)

```oa-check
  equipment_cost: 150000
  sute_exemption: 125000
  corporate_rate: 0.17
steps:
  - tax_before: (income_before_ca - sute_exemption) * corporate_rate
  - income_one_year: income_before_ca - equipment_cost
  - tax_one_year: (income_one_year - sute_exemption) * corporate_rate
  - tax_saving_one_year: tax_before - tax_one_year
  - allowance_three_year: equipment_cost / 3
  - tax_three_year: (income_before_ca - allowance_three_year - sute_exemption) * corporate_rate
  - first_year_difference: tax_three_year - tax_one_year
expected:
  tax_before: 38250
  income_one_year: 200000
  tax_one_year: 12750
  tax_saving_one_year: 25500
  allowance_three_year: 50000
  tax_three_year: 29750
  first_year_difference: 17000
```

## Ask the client first

1. Who is the taxpayer, what is the exact basis period/YA, residence and citizenship/PR history, and which other countries can tax the income?
2. What are reconciled profits before owner remuneration, other income, actual OW/AW and compulsory CPF, and existing company exemption/election/loss histories?
3. Which dependants, shared claims, recipient account balances, matching-grant portions and already-used personal relief/contribution headroom apply?
4. What are the actual assets, grants, payment/obligation dates, related parties and business purposes behind each proposed deduction or structure?
5. What are taxable/exempt/overseas supplies, prospective contracts, customer types and recoverable input tax for the GST decision?
6. How much cash must remain accessible, what filing/operator deadlines remain, and what extra administration or cross-border cost changes the comparison?

Refuse or refer where books or eligibility evidence are missing, an avoidance purpose dominates, prior filings may be wrong, a foreign/treaty or related-party analysis is needed, or a proposed transaction depends on a figure held below. Give the missing evidence and a bounded calculation, not a fabricated tax saving. Complete only when the baseline, alternatives, dates, receipts/elections and cash consequences reconcile, with actual filing acknowledgements retained.
