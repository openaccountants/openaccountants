# US child and dependent care credit (Form 2441) and the dependent care exclusion, tax year 2026

> **Source-cited draft.** Built from Public Law 119-21 (the One Big Beautiful Bill Act) sections 70404 and 70405, 26 U.S.C. 21 and 129, IRS Pub. 15-B (2026) and the 2025 Instructions for Form 2441, read on 2026-11-13. No accountant has reviewed it. All outputs must be reviewed by a qualified tax professional before filing.

**Answer first.** For 2026 the credit is a percentage of up to **USD 3,000** of care expenses for one qualifying person or **USD 6,000** for two or more. The One Big Beautiful Bill Act raised the top rate to **50%**. It falls by 1 point for each USD 2,000 (or part of it) of AGI over USD 15,000, but not below 35%. It then falls again by 1 point for each USD 2,000 (USD 4,000 on a joint return) or part of it over USD 75,000 (USD 150,000 joint), but not below 20%. The credit is not refundable. An employer plan (dependent care FSA) can exclude up to **USD 7,500** (USD 3,750 married filing separately) from 2026 wages. Every dollar excluded comes off the USD 3,000 or USD 6,000 limit.

## Scope and who this is for

In scope: an individual or married couple claiming the section 21 credit on the 2026 Form 2441, and the section 129 exclusion of employer-provided dependent care benefits (Form W-2 box 10) reported in Part III of the same form.

## Ask the client first

1. **Filing status.** Married couples must file jointly to claim the credit, apart from the narrow exceptions for a spouse who lived apart for the last 6 months (Form 2441 instructions).
2. **Each qualifying person:** name, date of birth, SSN, and whether they were under 13 when the care was given, or a spouse or dependent physically or mentally unable to care for themselves who lived with the client more than half the year.
3. **Care expenses paid in 2026** for each person, and each care provider's name, address and TIN (SSN or EIN). The provider cannot be the client's spouse, the child's parent, a dependent, or the client's child under 19.
4. **Earned income for each spouse** in 2026, and months either spouse was a full-time student or disabled.
5. **Form W-2 box 10** for each spouse: employer dependent care benefits, including dependent care FSA amounts, and any amount forfeited.
6. **AGI for 2026** (Form 1040 line 11) or an estimate.

None of these amounts is indexed for inflation. Before 2026 the top rate was 35% and the exclusion USD 5,000 (USD 2,500 separate); a 2025 Form 2441 uses those figures.

### Part III first: employer benefits (Form 2441 lines 12 to 31)

1. **Total benefits** = Form W-2 box 10 for both spouses, plus any amount carried over from a 2025 grace period, minus amounts forfeited.
2. **Excludable amount** = the smallest of: the benefits, the qualified expenses actually incurred in 2026, the lower-earning spouse's earned income (the client's own for a single filer), and USD 7,500 (USD 3,750 married filing separately). Source: [26 U.S.C. 129(a)(2) and (b)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title26/html/USCODE-2024-title26-subtitleA-chap1-subchapB-partIII-sec129.htm), as amended by [Public Law 119-21, section 70404](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/PLAW-119publ21/html/PLAW-119publ21.htm).
3. **Benefits above the excludable amount** are taxable wages and go on Form 1040 line 1e.
4. **Reduce the credit limit.** The USD 3,000 or USD 6,000 limit is reduced, dollar for dollar, by the amount excluded. Source: [26 U.S.C. 21(c)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title26/html/USCODE-2024-title26-subtitleA-chap1-subchapA-partIV-subpartA-sec21.htm), last sentence. With two children and the full USD 7,500 excluded, nothing is left for the credit.

### Part II: the credit (lines 2 to 11)

5. **Qualified expenses** paid in 2026 for 2026 care, less any paid with excluded benefits. Expenses prepaid for 2027 wait for 2027; 2025 expenses paid in 2026 go through the prior-year worksheet in the Form 2441 instructions.
6. **Cap** at the reduced limit from step 4.
7. **Earned income limit.** The expenses used cannot exceed the client's earned income, or, if married, the lower-earning spouse's. A spouse who was a full-time student or disabled is treated as earning USD 250 a month (USD 500 with two or more qualifying persons) for each month of that status. Source: [26 U.S.C. 21(d)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title26/html/USCODE-2024-title26-subtitleA-chap1-subchapA-partIV-subpartA-sec21.htm).
8. **Credit rate.** Start at 50. Subtract 1 for each USD 2,000, or part of USD 2,000, of AGI above USD 15,000, stopping at 35. Then subtract 1 for each USD 2,000 (USD 4,000 joint), or part of it, of AGI above USD 75,000 (USD 150,000 joint), stopping at 20. "Or fraction thereof" means round up: AGI of USD 15,001 already loses a point.
9. **Credit** = the expenses from step 7 times the rate. It is non-refundable: limited to the tax on Form 1040 line 18 less the foreign tax credit and certain others (Form 2441 Credit Limit Worksheet). It goes to Schedule 3 line 2.

## Boundary and exception table

| Situation | Treatment |
| --- | --- |
| Child turned 13 during 2026 | Only expenses for care before the 13th birthday count |
| Married filing separately | No credit, unless the spouse-lived-apart exception applies; the exclusion is capped at USD 3,750 |
| Benefits forfeited from a dependent care FSA | Not income and not excludable; leave out of line 12 |
| Care by a relative who is not a dependent and is 19 or older | Qualifies; the relative's TIN is required |
| Overnight camp, school tuition for kindergarten and above, lessons | Not qualified expenses; day camp is qualified |
| Care outside the home for a disabled spouse or adult dependent | Qualifies only if the person spends at least 8 hours a day in the client's home |
| Provider refuses to give a TIN | Claim with a statement of due diligence (Form 2441 instructions, "Due diligence") |
| AGI of exactly USD 15,000 | Still 50%: only AGI above USD 15,000 reduces the rate |

### Defaults when a fact is missing

| Missing fact | Default, and flag it |
| --- | --- |
| Box 10 benefits on the W-2 | Assume zero; ask, because any amount reduces the credit limit |
| Spouse's earned income | No credit until known; it often sets the limit |
| Provider TIN | Do not file without it or the due-diligence statement |
| AGI | Compute the rate at the expected AGI and redo it when the return is final |

### Red flags to raise with the client

| Flag | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| Both a dependent care FSA and the credit claimed for the same dollars | Double benefit: the excluded amount must come off the limit |
| Expenses paid to the client's own child under 19 or to the child's other parent | Not qualified |
| Payments to a nanny of USD 3,000 or more in the year | Household employment taxes are likely due (Schedule H) |
| A 2025 return claimed 2026 care, or the reverse | Year mismatch; use the prior-year worksheet |

## Worked cases

**Case 1, a decision: dependent care FSA or the credit?** A married couple filing jointly have AGI of USD 160,000, two children aged 3 and 6, and USD 12,000 of daycare costs. Their employer offers a dependent care FSA of up to USD 7,500. Their marginal federal income tax rate is 22% and each spouse's wages are under the Social Security wage base, so excluded wages also save 7.65% of employee FICA (assumptions taken from the client's facts).

Rate: AGI exceeds USD 15,000 by USD 145,000, which takes the rate to the 35% floor. It exceeds USD 150,000 by USD 10,000, which is 2.5 steps of USD 4,000, rounded up to 3, so the rate is 32%. Without the FSA, the credit is USD 6,000 times 32% = USD 1,920. With the full FSA, the USD 6,000 limit falls by USD 7,500 to zero, so there is no credit, but USD 7,500 of wages escape income tax and FICA: USD 7,500 times 29.65% = USD 2,223.75. The FSA saves USD 303.75 more. Recommend the FSA, and tell the client the USD 7,500 must be elected in open enrollment and is lost if not used.

```oa-check
name: Dependent care FSA against the credit, joint AGI 160,000 (2026)
inputs:
  agi: 160000
  expense_limit: 6000
  fsa: 7500
  marginal_plus_fica: 0.2965
steps:
  - first_cut: min(ceil(max(agi - 15000, 0) / 2000), 15)
  - rate_after_first: 50 - first_cut
  - second_cut: ceil(max(agi - 150000, 0) / 4000)
  - rate: max(rate_after_first - second_cut, 20)
  - credit_without_fsa: expense_limit * rate / 100
  - limit_with_fsa: max(expense_limit - fsa, 0)
  - credit_with_fsa: limit_with_fsa * rate / 100
  - fsa_tax_saving: fsa * marginal_plus_fica
  - fsa_better_by: fsa_tax_saving + credit_with_fsa - credit_without_fsa
expected:
  rate: 32
  credit_without_fsa: 1920.00
  credit_with_fsa: 0.00
  fsa_tax_saving: 2223.75
  fsa_better_by: 303.75
```

**Case 2, a single parent, one child.** A head of household has AGI of USD 40,000 (all wages), one child aged 4 and USD 4,500 of daycare costs. No employer benefits. The expenses are capped at USD 3,000. AGI exceeds USD 15,000 by USD 25,000, which is 12.5 steps, rounded up to 13: the rate is 37%. AGI is under USD 75,000, so the second step does not apply. Credit: USD 3,000 times 37% = USD 1,110, limited to the income tax on the return.

```oa-check
name: Single parent, one child, AGI 40,000 (2026)
inputs:
  agi: 40000
  expenses: 4500
  earned_income: 40000
steps:
  - allowed: min(expenses, 3000, earned_income)
  - first_cut: min(ceil(max(agi - 15000, 0) / 2000), 15)
  - second_cut: ceil(max(agi - 75000, 0) / 2000)
  - rate: max(50 - first_cut - second_cut, 20)
  - credit: allowed * rate / 100
expected:
  allowed: 3000
  rate: 37
  credit: 1110.00
```

## Filing and payment

Form 2441 is attached to the 2026 Form 1040, due **15 April 2027**. Parts I and III are required whenever W-2 box 10 shows benefits, even if no credit is claimed. Keep the provider receipts and TINs; the IRS can deny the credit without them.

## Completion checklist

- Every qualifying person's age and SSN checked; care for a child counted only before the 13th birthday.
- Part III completed first, with the excluded amount taken off the USD 3,000 or USD 6,000 limit.
- Expenses capped at the lower earner's earned income, with deemed income for student or disabled months.
- Rate worked out with the 2026 rules (50% start, two phase-downs, round up each step), not the 2025 35% table.
- Credit limited to tax; provider names, addresses and TINs on line 1.
