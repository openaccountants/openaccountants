# Colombia: social contributions of the self-employed, 2026 v2.1

> **General reference only.** This skill is general tax/accounting reference material for AI-assisted workflows. It has not been reviewed for any specific person's facts, documents, elections, deadlines, residency, filing status, or local procedures. Do not rely on it to file, pay, amend, or take a tax position without review by a qualified professional in the relevant jurisdiction.

**Year.** Calendar 2026. The figures hang on the monthly minimum wage (SMMLV) of **COP 1,750,905** (Decreto 1469 of 29 December 2025, re-fixed in the same amount on a transitional basis by Decreto 0159 de 2026 after the Consejo de Estado suspended Decreto 1469).

**Which law.** Ley 2277 de 2022 art. 89 (the 40% base of independent workers, held constitutional in Sentencia C-019-24), Ley 100 de 1993 (pensions and health), Decreto 4982 de 2007 (pension rate), Ley 1562 de 2012 and Decreto 1072 de 2015 (ARL for contractors), and Decreto 780 de 2016 (DUR Salud: payment in arrears, the cost-presumption procedure, PILA dates and rounding). The pension reform, Ley 2381 de 2024, is not applied: the Senate text notes that its articles found constitutional take effect on 1 April 2027 (Sentencia C-264-26). Ley 1955 de 2019 art. 244, the earlier 40% rule, was declared unconstitutional with effect from 21 July 2022 and is replaced by Ley 2277 art. 89.

## Who this applies to

- **Independientes con contrato de prestación de servicios personales:** individuals paid under a contract for personal services (not a contrato de trabajo), with net monthly income of 1 SMMLV or more (Ley 2277 art. 89, third paragraph).
- **Independientes por cuenta propia** and **independientes con contratos diferentes a prestación de servicios personales** (who may subcontract, buy inputs or incur expenses), with net monthly income of 1 SMMLV or more (art. 89, first paragraph). Income tax filers whose main activity is rentista de capital fall in this second group (DUR 780 art. 3.2.7.2(b)).
- Health: independent workers with capacity to pay belong to the contributory regime (Ley 100 art. 157(A)(1)). Pension: contractors and independent workers are mandatory members (Ley 100 art. 15(1)).
- **Out of scope:** employees and employers (see [colombia-payroll](colombia-payroll.md)); independents below 1 SMMLV (subsidised regime and BEPS); special and exception regimes (teachers' fund, armed forces, Ecopetrol); voluntary caja de compensación contributions; posted workers and social security agreements.

## The base

**Step 1: classify the independent.** A contract for personal services (the person works personally, typically for one contracting entity) uses the contract value. Cuenta propia work, or contracts that involve subcontracting, buying inputs or expenses, use income less costs (Ley 2277 art. 89; DUR 780 art. 3.2.7.2).

**Step 2: monthly income.**

- *Contractor:* the contract value without IVA, spread over the months of the contract ("valor mensualizado"). No costs are deducted.
- *Cuenta propia and other contracts:* gross monthly income without IVA (accrued if the person must keep books, received if not; a person not obliged who keeps proper books may use either), minus costs that meet ET art. 107 and the support rules, not exceeding the costs in the income tax return for the year. Without supporting documents, the costs are the UGPP presumed costs for the activity (DUR 780 art. 3.2.7.5, as amended by Decreto 379 de 2026; Ley 2277 art. 89 par. 1). The UGPP may ask for the supports and, if there are none, applies its coefficient for the main activity reported in the income tax return (art. 3.2.7.5 par.).

**Step 3: the test.** If net monthly income is under 1 SMMLV, the art. 89 base does not apply. A person with income of 1 SMMLV or more is presumed obliged to join the contributory health regime (DUR 780 art. 3.2.7.4).

**Step 4: IBC.** At least 40% of the step 2 income, never under 1 SMMLV, never over 25 SMMLV, rounded up to the next peso. The 40% is a minimum: the independent may contribute on more.

**Step 5: contributions.** Health 12.5%, pension 16%, FSP 1% from 4 SMMLV plus the subsistence add-on from 16 SMMLV, and ARL at the class rate where ARL applies. Round each contribution up to the next COP 100.

**Several incomes at once.** Contributions are made on each income separately (Ley 2277 art. 89, fourth paragraph) and are added together for all purposes of Ley 100, never above the 25 SMMLV cap, with health on the same base (Ley 100 art. 18 par. 1). Where a person is both an employee and an independent, the employer pays on the salary (see [colombia-payroll](colombia-payroll.md)) and the person pays separately on the independent income. A contractor with several contracts is covered by one ARL for all of them (Decreto 1072 art. 2.2.4.2.2.9); once the 25 SMMLV cap is reached, ARL is paid starting with the highest-risk contract (art. 2.2.4.2.2.12).

**Pensioners.** The obligation to contribute to pension ends when the person meets the requirements for the minimum old-age pension or is pensioned for invalidity or early retirement (Ley 100 art. 17). Health is still due on the independent income (Ley 100 art. 157(A)(1)), and ARL where it applies.

**Income that changes month to month.** The base is the income of each month: contributions are paid month in arrears on the income received in the previous month (DUR 780 art. 2.2.1.1.1.7).

**AUDIT FLASH POINT.** Income tax returns and DIAN exogenous information can establish that an independent earns 1 SMMLV or more and must contribute (DUR 780 art. 3.2.7.4), and the UGPP may apply the cost presumption to audits of any tax year (Ley 2277 art. 89 par. 2). An independent who contributes on 1 SMMLV while the return shows much higher net income is the classic case. Penalties are in [colombia-payroll](colombia-payroll.md) (Ley 1607 art. 179).

The steps, in the order the independent meets them: affiliate to an EPS and a pension fund (and, for a contract over one month, give the contracting party your ARL, or let it affiliate you); each month, work out the previous month's net income, the IBC and each contribution; log in to a PILA operator, choose the independent planilla, enter the IBC per income and the ARL class; pay by the business day set by your ID digits; give the payment receipt to the contracting party, which checks it before paying and deducting your fee (see [colombia-payroll](colombia-payroll.md)). At year end, the contributions paid feed the [income tax return](colombia-income-tax.md).

## Penalties and interest

- **Late payment.** Interest at the rate for late income tax (Ley 100 art. 23); see [colombia-payroll](colombia-payroll.md).
- **Health cover suspended.** Not paying two consecutive periods suspends the independent's health affiliation and services (DUR 780 art. 2.1.9.3); no debt for contributions or interest accrues during the suspension (art. 2.1.9.4).
- **UGPP.** Failure to file, under-declaration and failure to supply information carry the Ley 1607 art. 179 penalties; the scale is in [colombia-payroll](colombia-payroll.md).

## Cross-border points

- The art. 89 base reads the independent's income without distinguishing where the client is; a Colombian independent billing foreign clients computes the IBC the same way. Whether a person living abroad or a foreigner working in Colombia under a home-country system must contribute depends on residence rules and social security agreements, which are not covered here.
- Tax on fees paid to or by non-residents is in [colombia-cross-border](colombia-cross-border.md).

## Two worked examples that recompute

### Example 1: contractor with a six-month contract, ARL class I

Andrés signs a six-month contract for personal services with a Medellín company for COP 54,000,000 plus IVA, so COP 9,000,000 a month. Office work, ARL class I. He is not a pensioner and has no other income.

- IBC: 40% x 9,000,000 = **COP 3,600,000** (above 1 SMMLV of 1,750,905; below 4 SMMLV of 7,003,620, so no FSP).
- Health 12.5% = 450,000. Pension 16% = 576,000. ARL 0.522% = 18,792, rounded up to 18,800.
- **Total COP 1,044,800** a month, paid through PILA in the following month by the business day set by his ID digits. The contract lasts more than one month, so ARL is mandatory and, at class I, he pays it.

```oa-check
name: CO independent 2026, contractor COP 9,000,000 a month, ARL class I
inputs:
  smmlv: 1750905
  monthly_contract: 9000000
  arl_rate: 0.00522
steps:
  - ibc: clamp(ceil(monthly_contract * 0.40), smmlv, smmlv * 25)
  - salud: ceil(ibc * 0.125 / 100) * 100
  - pension: ceil(ibc * 0.16 / 100) * 100
  - arl: ceil(ibc * arl_rate / 100) * 100
  - total: salud + pension + arl
expected:
  ibc: 3600000
  salud: 450000
  pension: 576000
  arl: 18800
  total: 1044800
```

### Example 2: cuenta propia, keep the cost invoices or not (a decision)

Marta runs a design studio on her own account. In a month she invoices COP 30,000,000 without IVA and has COP 10,000,000 of costs backed by electronic invoices that meet ET art. 107 and appear in her return. No ARL (voluntary for her). The question is whether supporting her costs is worth the paperwork.

- **With supported costs:** net 20,000,000; IBC 40% = **COP 8,000,000** (over 4 SMMLV, so FSP 1%; under 16 SMMLV). Health 1,000,000; pension 1,280,000; FSP 80,000. **Total COP 2,360,000.**
- **Contributing on gross income:** IBC 40% x 30,000,000 = **COP 12,000,000**. Health 1,500,000; pension 1,920,000; FSP 120,000. **Total COP 3,540,000.**
- **What decides it:** supported costs save **COP 1,180,000** a month. Without supports, the UGPP replaces her costs with its presumed coefficient for the activity, which may be lower than her real costs. Keep the e-invoices and make sure the costs match the income tax return.

```oa-check
name: CO independent 2026, cuenta propia with and without supported costs
inputs:
  smmlv: 1750905
  gross: 30000000
  costs: 10000000
steps:
  - ibc_net: clamp(ceil((gross - costs) * 0.40), smmlv, smmlv * 25)
  - total_net: ceil(ibc_net * 0.125 / 100) * 100 + ceil(ibc_net * 0.16 / 100) * 100 + ceil(ibc_net * 0.01 / 100) * 100
  - ibc_gross: clamp(ceil(gross * 0.40), smmlv, smmlv * 25)
  - total_gross: ceil(ibc_gross * 0.125 / 100) * 100 + ceil(ibc_gross * 0.16 / 100) * 100 + ceil(ibc_gross * 0.01 / 100) * 100
  - saving: total_gross - total_net
  - fsp_line: smmlv * 4
expected:
  ibc_net: 8000000
  total_net: 2360000
  ibc_gross: 12000000
  total_gross: 3540000
  saving: 1180000
  fsp_line: 7003620
```

### Edge cases (figures for 2026)

| Case | Result |
|---|---|
| Contractor at COP 2,000,000 a month | 40% = 800,000, below the floor: IBC COP 1,750,905 |
| Three contracts of 3, 5 and 2 million a month | contributions on each: IBC 1,200,000 + 2,000,000 + 800,000 = COP 4,000,000 in total. Whether each contract is lifted to the floor separately is not confirmed (see below); the combined IBC is above 1 SMMLV |
| Cuenta propia, net COP 100,000,000 a month | 40% = 40,000,000, under the 25 SMMLV cap of 43,772,625: IBC COP 40,000,000; FSP 1% plus 1% (over 20 SMMLV) = 2% |
| Employee on COP 3,000,000 who also bills COP 8,000,000 a month as a contractor | the employer pays on the salary; the independent IBC is 3,200,000; both count towards the cap and, read with Ley 100 art. 18 par. 1, towards the 4 SMMLV FSP test (6,200,000 in total, under 4 SMMLV here) |
| Pensioner billing COP 6,000,000 a month | IBC 2,400,000; health 300,000 and ARL if applicable; no pension (Ley 100 art. 17) |
| Contractor classed I but doing field work | the class is the higher of the entity's workplace and the activity (Decreto 1072 art. 2.2.4.2.2.11); the ARL may reclassify and the contracting party re-liquidates |
| PILA on 1 SMMLV, return shows COP 120,000,000 net for the year | about COP 10,000,000 a month; expected IBC COP 4,000,000; UGPP assessment of the difference plus interest and art. 179 penalties |

## Ask the client first

1. Is the income from a contract for personal services, own-account work, or a contract involving subcontracting or inputs? Contract value and duration, or monthly gross income and costs.
2. Does the person keep accounts, and are the costs supported by documents that meet ET art. 107 and match the income tax return?
3. Any other income at the same time: salary, other contracts, a pension? Is the person already a pensioner or entitled to the minimum pension?
4. For a contract over one month, the ARL risk class of the activity and of the contracting entity's workplace.
5. Health and pension fund (Colpensiones or a private AFP) and the last two digits of the identity document (PILA date).
