# Qualified Opportunity Zone investments: deferring and excluding capital gains, tax year 2026

> **Source-cited draft.** Built from Public Law 119-21 section 70421, 26 U.S.C. 1400Z-1 and 1400Z-2 (2024 edition, the pre-2027 text), Treas. Reg. 1.1400Z2(a)-1, (b)-1 and (c)-1, Rev. Proc. 2025-32, the IRS Opportunity Zones FAQ and Form 8997, read on 2026-11-13. No accountant has reviewed it. All outputs must be reviewed by a qualified tax professional before filing.

**Answer first.** Two sets of rules now run side by side, and **the date the money goes into the fund decides which applies**, not the date of the sale. **Original rules** (invested on or before **31 December 2026**): every deferred gain still held comes into income on the **2026 return**, with no sale and no cash; a 10 percent basis step-up counts only if the investment has reached 5 years by that date (plus 5 percent at 7 years). **New rules** (invested after **December 31, 2026**): each investment defers for 5 years from its own date, with a **10 percent** basis increase at 5 years (**30 percent** in a qualified rural opportunity fund); after 10 years an election sets basis to fair market value, capped at the value on the 30th anniversary. In both, only capital gain invested within 180 days of the sale qualifies, and the 10-year exclusion covers growth, not the deferred gain itself.

## Scope and who this is for

In scope: an investor with a large capital gain who is thinking about a qualified opportunity fund, and their adviser, for tax year 2026: the original-rules 2026 inclusion, the choice whether and when to invest, and the new rules for investments from 2027. Federal law only: sections 1400Z-1 and 1400Z-2 as amended by section 70421 of Public Law 119-21 (approved 4 July 2025). The result lands on Form 8949 (the deferral election and the inclusion) and Form 8997 (every year an investment is held).

## Ask the client first

1. **On what date did you sell, and on what date did (or will) the money go into the fund?** The second date decides which rules apply.
2. **Do you already hold a fund investment made before 2027?** If so, on what date was it made, and what is it likely to be worth on 31 December 2026?
3. **Can you pay the tax on the deferred gain from other money** when it falls due (the 2026 return for original-rules investments; the year that includes the 5-year date for new-rules investments)?
4. **Is the gain capital or section 1231 gain, and was the buyer related to you?**
5. **Will you need this money back within 10 years?**
6. **Is the fund (or the zone business it invests in) rural, and does it hold existing buildings it must substantially improve?**

### Step 1: which rules apply

The Act says its changes to section 1400Z-2(a), (b) and (c) "shall apply to amounts invested in qualified opportunity funds after December 31, 2026" (section 70421(c)(5)(A)). Three parts have their own start dates:

- The rural substantial improvement rule took effect on 4 July 2025, whichever rules the investor is under (section 70421(c)(5)(C)).
- The new acquisition date rules for property a fund or zone business buys apply to property acquired after 31 December 2026 (section 70421(c)(5)(B)).
- The new fund reporting and penalty rules apply to taxable years beginning after 4 July 2025 (section 70421(d)(5)). They bind funds, whichever rules their investors are under.

A gain from a sale in late 2026 whose 180-day window runs into 2027 can be invested on either side of the line: money in on or before 31 December 2026 is under the original rules; money in from 1 January 2027 is under the new rules by the Act's effective-date rule. But whether a deferral election can be made at all for a gain from a sale before 2027 is not settled: the regulations define a deferral election as one "made before January 1, 2027" ([Treas. Reg. 1.1400Z2(a)-1(b)(10)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-26/section-1.1400Z2%28a%29-1)) and have not been updated for the Act, and the pre-2027 statute bars an election "with respect to any sale or exchange after December 31, 2026" ([26 U.S.C. 1400Z-2(a)(2)(B)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title26/html/USCODE-2024-title26-subtitleA-chap1-subchapZ-sec1400Z-2.htm)). Refer that case rather than advise it.

### Step 2: getting in (both sets of rules)

1. Gross income does not include "so much of such gain as does not exceed the aggregate amount invested by the taxpayer in a qualified opportunity fund during the 180-day period beginning on the date of such sale or exchange", if the taxpayer elects ([26 U.S.C. 1400Z-2(a)(1)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title26/html/USCODE-2024-title26-subtitleA-chap1-subchapZ-sec1400Z-2.htm)). Only the part of the gain actually invested is deferred.
2. The gain must be treated as capital gain or be qualified section 1231 gain, must be gain that would otherwise be taxed, and must not come from a sale to a related person as the regulations define it. Ordinary income does not qualify. Gain from a section 1256 contract, and gain from a position that was part of a straddle, is also excluded, subject to exceptions ([Treas. Reg. 1.1400Z2(a)-1(b)(11)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-26/section-1.1400Z2%28a%29-1)).
3. The election is made on the return for the year the gain would otherwise be taxed, using Form 8949; a return already filed is amended to make it ([IRS Opportunity Zones FAQ, Q14 and Q16](https://www.irs.gov/credits-deductions/opportunity-zones-frequently-asked-questions)).
4. Under the new rules the sunset is gone: the election text now says only that "No election may be made under paragraph (1) with respect to a sale or exchange if an election previously made with respect to such sale or exchange is in effect" (section 70421(c)(1)).

### Step 3: original rules, the 2026 inclusion

The pre-2027 text of section 1400Z-2(b)(1) reads: "Gain to which subsection (a)(1)(B) applies shall be included in income in the taxable year which includes the earlier of" "(A) the date on which such investment is sold or exchanged, or (B) December 31, 2026" ([26 U.S.C. 1400Z-2(b)(1)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title26/html/USCODE-2024-title26-subtitleA-chap1-subchapZ-sec1400Z-2.htm)). The regulations say the same: included "in the taxable year that includes the earlier of" an inclusion event or 31 December 2026 ([Treas. Reg. 1.1400Z2(b)-1(b)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-26/section-1.1400Z2%28b%29-1)).

- A calendar-year investor who still holds an original-rules investment on 31 December 2026 reports the remaining deferred gain on the 2026 return, filed in 2027. No sale and no cash are needed; the tax must be paid from other money.
- A fiscal-year investor reports it in the taxable year that includes 31 December 2026.
- If an inclusion event (a sale, the fund liquidating) happens earlier, the gain is reported in that year instead ([IRS Opportunity Zones FAQ, Q30 and Q32](https://www.irs.gov/credits-deductions/opportunity-zones-frequently-asked-questions)).
- An investment made during 2026 under the original rules defers nothing past the 2026 return. Its only remaining benefit is the 10-year election.

**The amount** included on 31 December 2026 is the excess of the lesser of (a) the remaining deferred gain and (b) the fair market value of the investment on 31 December 2026, over the investor's basis in it on that date, counting only the basis rules in section 1400Z-2(b)(2)(B) ([Treas. Reg. 1.1400Z2(b)-1(e)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-26/section-1.1400Z2%28b%29-1)). Basis starts at zero. If the investment has fallen below the deferred gain, less is included.

**Which step-ups still count.** The 5-year and 7-year step-ups add together (the IRS describes the result: "If held for at least 7 years, the 10% exclusion becomes 15%"). Because the inclusion uses basis on 31 December 2026, a step-up counts only if the holding period reaches 5 years (or 7) by that date:

- An investment made in 2022 or later cannot reach 5 years by 31 December 2026: no step-up.
- An investment made in 2020 or later cannot reach 7 years by then: at most the 5-year step-up.
- For an investment made on the last day of 2019 or 2021, how the holding period is counted ([Treas. Reg. 1.1400Z2(b)-1(d)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-26/section-1.1400Z2%28b%29-1)) decides. Check it on the facts.

### Step 4: original rules, the 10-year election after 2026

After the 2026 inclusion, basis is increased by the gain included (section 1400Z-2(b)(2)(B)(ii)). For "any investment held by the taxpayer for at least 10 years and with respect to which the taxpayer makes an election", "the basis of such property shall be equal to the fair market value of such investment on the date that the investment is sold or exchanged" ([26 U.S.C. 1400Z-2(c)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title26/html/USCODE-2024-title26-subtitleA-chap1-subchapZ-sec1400Z-2.htm)). That removes tax on growth; it does not undo the 2026 inclusion. The election applies only to the part of an investment that came from deferred gain; other money in the same fund is a separate investment (section 1400Z-2(e)(1); FAQ Q25).

The regulations protect the election when a zone's designation ends, but that protection "does not apply to elections under section 1400Z-2(c) that are related to dispositions occurring after December 31, 2047" ([Treas. Reg. 1.1400Z2(c)-1(c)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-26/section-1.1400Z2%28c%29-1)). Plan an original-rules exit on or before 31 December 2047. The Act's 30-year rule does not apply to original-rules investments.

### Step 5: new rules, investments from 1 January 2027

- **Deferral of five years from each investment.** Deferred gain "shall be included in gross income in the taxable year which includes the earlier of" "(A) the date on which such investment is sold or exchanged, or (B) the date which is 5 years after the date the investment in the qualified opportunity fund was made" (new section 1400Z-2(b)(1)). Each investment has its own date. The amount is the lesser of the deferred gain or the fair market value on that date, minus basis (new section 1400Z-2(b)(2)(A)). Basis starts at zero.
- **Basis increase at five years.** "In the case of any investment held for at least 5 years, the basis of such investment shall be increased by an amount equal to 10 percent (30 percent in the case of any investment in a qualified rural opportunity fund) of the amount of gain deferred". The increase is "treated as occurring before the date described in paragraph (1)(B)", so it reduces the gain included at the 5-year date. There is no 7-year step-up under the new rules.
- **Qualified rural opportunity fund:** a fund that holds at least 90 percent of its assets in zone property whose use is substantially all in a zone "comprised entirely of a rural area". A "rural area" is any area other than "a city or town that has a population of greater than 50,000 inhabitants" and "any urbanized area contiguous and adjacent to" such a city or town.
- **Ten years, and the 30-year cap.** With the election, basis is "the fair market value of such investment on the date such investment is sold or exchanged" for "an investment sold before the date that is 30 years after the date of the investment", and "in any other case, the fair market value of such investment on the date that is 30 years after the date of the investment" (section 70421(c)(3)). Growth after the 30th anniversary is taxable on sale.

All quotes in this step: [Public Law 119-21, section 70421(c)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/PLAW-119publ21/html/PLAW-119publ21.htm).

### Step 6: the zones under the new rules

- New zones are chosen in rounds. The "decennial determination date" means "July 1, 2026, and" "each July 1 of the year that is 10 years after the preceding decennial determination date" (section 70421(a)(2)).
- A zone designated after 4 July 2025 runs from its "applicable start date", "the January 1 following the date on which such qualified opportunity zone was certified and designated", to "the day before the date that is 10 years after the applicable start date" (section 70421(b)(3)).
- Zones designated under the original law run "for the period beginning on the date of the designation and ending at the close of the 10th calendar year beginning on or after such date of designation" ([26 U.S.C. 1400Z-1(f)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title26/html/USCODE-2024-title26-subtitleA-chap1-subchapZ-sec1400Z-1.htm)). The first were designated on 9 April 2018 (FAQ Q3).
- For property acquired after 31 December 2026, zone business property must be bought after the applicable start date of its zone (section 70421(c)(4)(A) and (c)(5)(B)).
- Whether a given tract is in a zone in a given year comes from the Treasury list for that round. This skill does not carry the list.

### Step 7: check the fund (both sets of rules)

- A fund is a corporation or partnership organised to invest in qualified opportunity zone property that holds at least 90 percent of its assets in that property, measured as the average of the percentage "on the last day of the first 6-month period of the taxable year of the fund, and" "on the last day of the taxable year of the fund" ([26 U.S.C. 1400Z-2(d)(1)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title26/html/USCODE-2024-title26-subtitleA-chap1-subchapZ-sec1400Z-2.htm)).
- A fund that misses the test pays a monthly penalty on the shortfall at the underpayment rate unless the failure is due to reasonable cause (section 1400Z-2(f)). The investor does not pay it, but it reduces the fund.
- A fund self-certifies "by annually filing Form 8996 with its federal income tax return", which "must be filed timely, taking extensions into account" (FAQ Q38). Ask for the fund's Form 8996 history before investing.
- For tangible property, the IRS reads the two "substantially all" tests as at least 90 percent of the holding period and at least 70 percent of the use, in answers the IRS says "do not constitute legal authority and may not be relied upon as such" (FAQ Q52).
- **Substantial improvement of existing property.** Property a fund buys that is already in use counts only if, "during any 30-month period beginning after the date of acquisition of such property, additions to basis with respect to such property in the hands of the qualified opportunity fund exceed an amount equal to the adjusted basis of such property" at the start of that period (section 1400Z-2(d)(2)(D)(ii)). Equalling it fails. For property in a zone made up entirely of a rural area, the bar is 50 percent of adjusted basis, from 4 July 2025, under both sets of rules.

### Funds: new returns and penalties (taxable years beginning after 4 July 2025)

Every fund files an annual return under new section 6039K (assets, zone property, census tracts, employees, and each investor who disposed of an investment), gives a statement to each investor who disposed, and files electronically. Zone businesses must give their funds the information they need (section 6039L). A fund that fails to file "a complete and correct return" pays a daily penalty under new section 6726 (section 70421(d)). The penalty falls on the fund, not the investor.

| Investment made in 2022 or later, original rules | No step-up in the 2026 inclusion | [26 U.S.C. 1400Z-2(b)(2)(B)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title26/html/USCODE-2024-title26-subtitleA-chap1-subchapZ-sec1400Z-2.htm) |
| Mixed fund investment (deferred gain plus other money) | Two investments; only the deferred-gain part gets the 10-year election | [26 U.S.C. 1400Z-2(e)(1)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title26/html/USCODE-2024-title26-subtitleA-chap1-subchapZ-sec1400Z-2.htm) |
| Ordinary income, or gain from a sale to a related person | Not eligible | [Treas. Reg. 1.1400Z2(a)-1(b)(11)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-26/section-1.1400Z2%28a%29-1) |
| Foreign investor who will not waive treaty benefits on the later inclusion | "You may not elect to defer tax on an eligible gain by investing in a QOF" without the waiver | [Form 8997](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/f8997.pdf) |
| Original-rules exit after 31 December 2047 in a zone whose designation has ended | Regulatory protection of the election does not apply | [Treas. Reg. 1.1400Z2(c)-1(c)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-26/section-1.1400Z2%28c%29-1) |
| New-rules investment held past 30 years | Basis capped at fair market value on the 30th anniversary | [Public Law 119-21, section 70421(c)(3)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/PLAW-119publ21/html/PLAW-119publ21.htm) |

### Who should not do this

- Anyone who needs the money back within 10 years: the exclusion of growth needs the 10-year holding period and the election.
- An investor who cannot pay the deferred tax from other money (on the 2026 return for original-rules investments; in the year that includes the 5-year date for new-rules investments).
- Someone hoping to defer a 2026 gain by investing in 2026: under the original rules it is included on the same 2026 return (Case 3).
- An investor whose gain is ordinary income or comes from a sale to a related person.
- A foreign investor who will not waive treaty benefits on the later inclusion.
- Anyone choosing a fund that cannot show its Form 8996 filings and how it meets the 90 percent test.

### Defaults when a fact is missing

| Missing fact | Default, and flag it |
| --- | --- |
| Date the money went into the fund | No rules apply until documented; the date picks the set |
| Fair market value on 31 December 2026 | Assume at least the deferred gain (full inclusion); ask the fund for a valuation |
| Whether the fund is a qualified rural opportunity fund | Ordinary fund (10 percent) |
| Related-party status of the buyer | Ask; do not elect deferral until confirmed |
| Fund's Form 8996 history | Treat the fund as unverified; refer |

### Red flags to raise with the client

| Flag | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| Original-rules investment still held and no cash set aside | 2026 tax on the deferred gain with no sale proceeds |
| Fund cannot show Form 8996 filings or its 90 percent test | The fund may not qualify; the deferral can fail |
| Fund holds existing buildings | The 30-month substantial improvement test must be met |
| Sale near year end 2026 with the window into 2027 | Unsettled election question |
| Investor plans to exit before 10 years | Only deferral and step-ups; no exclusion of growth |

## Worked cases

All amounts are hypothetical. Each investor is an individual on a calendar year who makes the deferral election and invests the whole gain.

**Case 1, a decision: ordinary or rural fund under the new rules.** A capital gain of USD 1,000,000 from a sale in January 2027 is invested in a fund on 1 March 2027. On 1 March 2032 (5 years), the investment is worth USD 1,500,000, more than the deferred gain. Ordinary fund: the 5-year basis increase is 10% x USD 1,000,000 = USD 100,000, so USD 900,000 is included on the 2032 return. Qualified rural opportunity fund: 30% x USD 1,000,000 = USD 300,000, so USD 700,000 is included. The rural fund keeps an extra USD 200,000 of gain out of tax for good. Choose the rural fund if its expected after-fee return over 10 years is not more than that much below the ordinary fund's. Either way, holding past 1 March 2037 with the election sets basis to fair market value at sale for a sale before 1 March 2057; after that date, basis is the value on 1 March 2057.

```oa-check
name: New rules, ordinary against rural fund, 5-year inclusion (2032)
inputs:
  deferred_gain: 1000000
  fmv_at_5yr: 1500000
steps:
  - step_up_ordinary: deferred_gain * 0.10
  - step_up_rural: deferred_gain * 0.30
  - included_ordinary: min(deferred_gain, fmv_at_5yr) - step_up_ordinary
  - included_rural: min(deferred_gain, fmv_at_5yr) - step_up_rural
  - rural_advantage: included_ordinary - included_rural
expected:
  step_up_ordinary: 100000.00
  included_ordinary: 900000.00
  included_rural: 700000.00
  rural_advantage: 200000.00
```

**Case 2, original rules, invested in 2021.** A capital gain of USD 1,000,000 from a stock sale on 1 March 2021 was invested in a fund on 15 June 2021, inside 180 days. On 31 December 2026 the investment has been held more than 5 but less than 7 years and is worth USD 1,400,000. The 5-year step-up is 10% x USD 1,000,000 = USD 100,000 (no 7-year step-up). The 2026 inclusion is USD 1,000,000 minus USD 100,000 = USD 900,000, and basis afterwards is USD 1,000,000. Sold in 2033, after 10 years, for USD 2,000,000 with the 10-year election: basis becomes fair market value, so no gain (dispose on or before 31 December 2047). Without the election the 2033 sale would produce a USD 1,000,000 gain.

```oa-check
name: Original rules, 2021 investment, 2026 inclusion and 10-year sale
inputs:
  deferred_gain: 1000000
  fmv_31dec2026: 1400000
  sale_price_2033: 2000000
steps:
  - step_up: deferred_gain * 0.10
  - included_2026: min(deferred_gain, fmv_31dec2026) - step_up
  - basis_after: step_up + included_2026
  - gain_without_election: sale_price_2033 - basis_after
  - gain_with_election: sale_price_2033 - sale_price_2033
expected:
  step_up: 100000.00
  included_2026: 900000.00
  basis_after: 1000000.00
  gain_without_election: 1000000.00
  gain_with_election: 0.00
```

**Case 3, original rules, invested in 2026.** A capital gain of USD 500,000 from a sale on 2 March 2026 is invested on 1 May 2026, under the original rules. On 31 December 2026 the investment is worth USD 450,000; no step-ups (far short of 5 years). Included on the 2026 return: the lesser of USD 500,000 and USD 450,000, minus zero basis = USD 450,000. The gain is deferred only from the sale to the end of the same year; had the investment been worth at least USD 500,000, the whole USD 500,000 would be included.

## Filing and payment

- **Deferral election:** Form 8949 with the return for the year of sale (amend a filed return to make it) ([IRS Opportunity Zones FAQ](https://www.irs.gov/credits-deductions/opportunity-zones-frequently-asked-questions)).
- **Form 8997 every year** an investment is held at any point, attached to the timely filed return: Form 8997 is used "to inform the IRS of the QOF investments and deferred gains held at the beginning and end of the current tax year, as well as any capital gains deferred by investing in a QOF and QOF investments disposed of during the current tax year" ([About Form 8997](https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs/about-form-8997)).
- **The 2026 inclusion** (original rules) goes on the 2026 return with Form 8949 and Form 8997, due 15 April 2027; an extension moves filing (not payment) to 15 October 2027 ([IRS extensions page](https://www.irs.gov/filing/get-an-extension-to-file-your-tax-return)). Plan estimated tax for 2026 for the inclusion (`us-quarterly-estimated-tax`).
- **New-rules investments:** diary the date 5 years after each investment and compute the inclusion for the taxable year that includes it.
- **At sale after 10 years:** make the section 1400Z-2(c) election with the return for the year of sale.

## Completion checklist

- Date of sale, date of investment, and which rules apply recorded.
- Gain confirmed as capital or section 1231 gain, not from a related person, invested within 180 days.
- Fund's Form 8996 history and 90 percent test seen; rural status confirmed if the 30 percent increase is claimed.
- Original-rules investments still held: 2026 inclusion computed (lesser of deferred gain and value on 31 December 2026, minus basis), with only step-ups whose holding period is met; cash for the tax planned.
- New-rules investments: 5-year dates diarised; 10 or 30 percent increase applied.
- Form 8997 filed every year; 10-year election planned (by 31 December 2047 for original rules; 30-year cap for new rules).
- State treatment checked separately.
