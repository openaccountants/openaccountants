# Gibraltar: bookkeeping and tax records

## Scope

Section 63 applies to a person required to deliver a return under Income Tax Act section 28 or 29. Keep the records needed to make a full and complete return and preserve them for the statutory period. The duty is not limited to companies, and outsourcing bookkeeping does not change the person on whom this section imposes the duty. This skill addresses that general duty; it does not settle specialised payroll, customs, transaction-tax, regulated-sector or anti-money-laundering record rules. [Income Tax Act, section 63(1)](https://www.gibraltarlaws.gov.gi/uploads/legislations/income-tax/2010-21/2010-21(23-03-26).pdf).

**Quick reference:** preserve tax records for 6 years from the relevant return-period end (the statute prints the period in words); return notices can extend preservation. Company-law books have a separate 5-year rule, subject to section 243. Neither period is a general deletion permission. [Income Tax Act 63](https://www.gibraltarlaws.gov.gi/uploads/legislations/income-tax/2010-21/2010-21(23-03-26).pdf); [Companies Act 239](https://www.gibraltarlaws.gov.gi/uploads/legislations/companies/2014-19/2014-19(01-08-25).pdf).

| Period or penalty figure | Conditions and official source |
|---|---|
| Tax records: 6 years (in words on the page) | Required return under section 28 or 29; measure from the end of its period, with the separate notice/enquiry extensions in section 63(3)–(4). [Income Tax Act 63(1)–(4)](https://www.gibraltarlaws.gov.gi/uploads/legislations/income-tax/2010-21/2010-21(23-03-26).pdf) |
| Company books: 5 years | Every company, subject to section 243; this does not displace a longer applicable tax obligation. The Act gives the period but does not in this section specify the same clock as the tax rule. [Companies Act 239(1)](https://www.gibraltarlaws.gov.gi/uploads/legislations/companies/2014-19/2014-19(01-08-25).pdf) |
| Tax-record failure: up to level 5 | Maximum standard-scale level, not a fixed monetary bill; appeal can confirm or set aside the penalty. [Income Tax Act 63A(1)–(2)](https://www.gibraltarlaws.gov.gi/uploads/legislations/income-tax/2010-21/2010-21(23-03-26).pdf) |

## Ask the client first

1. Who must deliver the return, under which statutory route, and for what exact period-end?
2. Is the person also a company, and where are its books kept for directors' inspection?
3. What return notices, enquiries, requests and other preservation holds exist, including their dates and whether records are still held?
4. Which cash, bank, processor, sales, purchases, goods, payroll, assets and financing records support the return, and who can retrieve the underlying documents?
5. Are books in English, and are foreign-language documents or electronic substitutes involved?
6. Do regulated-sector, anti-money-laundering, payroll, customs, transaction-tax or foreign obligations require a separate specialist retention analysis?

## The method, step by step

### Identify the required return and evidence

Identify the person and period covered by Income Tax section 63(1) before setting a retention date. A bookkeeping provider's contract or a transfer to another provider does not change the person named in that statutory duty.

Section 63(5)–(8) covers the following evidence:

| Record | Statutory requirement |
|---|---|
| Cash book | Day-to-day entries of all cash received and paid. |
| Stock | Statements of annual stocktaking. |
| Goods sold and purchased | Sufficient detail to identify the goods, buyers and sellers. |
| Underlying transactions | Contracts, invoices and other documents significant to the trade, business, profession or vocation. |
| Supporting documents | Preserve the relevant accounts, books, deeds, contracts, vouchers and receipts. |
| Language | Books of account kept for this section must be in English. |

The examples do not limit section 63(1)'s broader requirement to support a complete return. A year-end total without its underlying evidence is not an adequate substitute for the prescribed transaction records. [Income Tax Act, section 63](https://www.gibraltarlaws.gov.gi/uploads/legislations/income-tax/2010-21/2010-21(23-03-26).pdf).

The language rule is expressly about books of account. This skill does not infer a universal certified-translation requirement for every foreign-language receipt, or permission to discard an original document after translating it. Establish any additional translation requirement for a particular request separately.

### Set the tax preservation clock and track notices

The ordinary tax retention period is **6 years from the end of the period for which the return is required**. It does not start on the purchase date, return filing date or accountant's handover date. [Income Tax Act, section 63(2)](https://www.gibraltarlaws.gov.gi/uploads/legislations/income-tax/2010-21/2010-21(23-03-26).pdf).

If notice requiring a return is given before that six-year period ends, retain records until the later date on which the enquiry is completed or, where there is no enquiry, the Commissioner's power to enquire ends. If such notice arrives after the six years and relevant records are still held, section 63(4) requires their preservation until the corresponding enquiry endpoint. Do not assume that the ordinary six-year date overrides a live statutory preservation duty. [Income Tax Act, section 63(3)–(4)](https://www.gibraltarlaws.gov.gi/uploads/legislations/income-tax/2010-21/2010-21(23-03-26).pdf).

Record each notice separately. For a pre-expiry notice, establish whether the later endpoint is completion of the enquiry or, if there is no enquiry, expiry of the Commissioner's enquiry power. A post-expiry notice engages section 63(4) for relevant records the person still possesses; do not treat that wording as a requirement to recreate every previously lawfully disposed-of record. The actual notice and other applicable powers still need review. [Income Tax Act 63(3)–(4)](https://www.gibraltarlaws.gov.gi/uploads/legislations/income-tax/2010-21/2010-21(23-03-26).pdf)

### Apply the separate company-books duty

Companies Act section 239 separately requires proper books for **5 years**, recording receipts and expenditure, sales and purchases of goods and services, and assets and liabilities. The books belong at the registered office or another place selected by the directors and must be available for their inspection. A company subject to the six-year tax rule cannot use the shorter company-law period to justify ending tax retention. [Companies Act, section 239](https://www.gibraltarlaws.gov.gi/uploads/legislations/companies/2014-19/2014-19(01-08-25).pdf).

Bookkeeping, preparing annual accounts and filing accounts are separate tasks. See the [company accounts and reporting skill](gi-company-accounts-and-reporting.md) for the accounting basis, signatures, reporting dates and unresolved audit-exemption cross-reference.

Section 239 expressly operates subject to section 243. That cross-reference concerns mainstream-company Non-IAS accounts, including clear accounts, a true and fair view, notes and the exceptional-departure conditions. It does not state a general permission to destroy supporting records. This bookkeeping skill does not reproduce the complete accounts/disclosure job. [Companies Act 239 and 243(1)–(7)](https://www.gibraltarlaws.gov.gi/uploads/legislations/companies/2014-19/2014-19(01-08-25).pdf)

### Reconcile and archive the supporting file

The following is a suggested working method for satisfying the evidence duties, not a statutory monthly filing requirement:

1. Reconcile each bank, cash and payment-processor account to the ledger. Identify outstanding deposits, unpresented payments and unexplained differences.
2. Match sales and purchases to invoices, credits and settlements. Track amounts still receivable or payable rather than treating every bank movement as income or expense.
3. Keep financing and owner transactions distinct from trading receipts. For an unexplained receipt, obtain evidence before choosing the accounting treatment.
4. Retain the year-end stocktake, quantity and valuation workings; reconcile movements and investigate differences.
5. Tie payroll, asset purchases and disposals to their supporting schedules. Prepare a separate bridge from accounting profit to the tax return rather than assuming all booked expenses are deductible.
6. Export the books and supporting index in a usable format, retain backups and confirm the director or responsible person can retrieve them after a provider change.

For each return period, maintain a retention register recording period-end, the six-year date, any notices and enquiry status, storage location, custodian and other retention requirements. Archive the submitted return with the working papers that explain its figures. Use the [corporate tax skill](gi-corporate-tax.md) for the distinct return and accounts-attachment rules.

Do not treat the suggested monthly control cycle as a tax filing frequency. Track the source of a balance difference, retain the evidence and approve the accounting entry only after the facts are established. This skill does not supply a universal chart of accounts.

## Boundary and exception table

| Situation | Treatment and boundary |
|---|---|
| Ordinary required-return record | Use the tax period-end clock and full evidence requirements; the purchase date and the filing date do not start that clock. [Income Tax Act 63(1)–(2)](https://www.gibraltarlaws.gov.gi/uploads/legislations/income-tax/2010-21/2010-21(23-03-26).pdf) |
| Return notice before the ordinary preservation period ends | Continue to the later statutory enquiry endpoint; determine which endpoint applies to the actual facts. [Income Tax Act 63(3)](https://www.gibraltarlaws.gov.gi/uploads/legislations/income-tax/2010-21/2010-21(23-03-26).pdf) |
| Return notice after that period, with relevant records still held | Preserve those relevant held records to the specified enquiry endpoint. This is distinct from the pre-expiry rule. [Income Tax Act 63(4)](https://www.gibraltarlaws.gov.gi/uploads/legislations/income-tax/2010-21/2010-21(23-03-26).pdf) |
| Company also subject to income tax record duties | Apply both duties; the shorter company-books period does not cancel the longer tax preservation requirement. [Companies Act 239](https://www.gibraltarlaws.gov.gi/uploads/legislations/companies/2014-19/2014-19(01-08-25).pdf); [Income Tax Act 63](https://www.gibraltarlaws.gov.gi/uploads/legislations/income-tax/2010-21/2010-21(23-03-26).pdf) |
| Books versus foreign-language supporting receipt | Books must be in English. No universal certified-translation requirement for every receipt, or permission to destroy the original after translation, is inferred. [Income Tax Act 63(7)–(8)](https://www.gibraltarlaws.gov.gi/uploads/legislations/income-tax/2010-21/2010-21(23-03-26).pdf) |
| Electronic original or specialist sector | Keep the question open until the applicable rule and actual medium/sector are established; this skill does not confer blanket substitution or deletion permission. |
| Annual accounts, return filing, or tax deductibility | Load the separate [accounts skill](gi-company-accounts-and-reporting.md) or [corporate-tax skill](gi-corporate-tax.md); bookkeeping evidence alone does not settle those jobs. |

## Worked cases

### Period-end preservation and a later enquiry

Example: for a required return covering the period ending **31 December 2025**, the ordinary six-year period runs through **31 December 2031**. Filing in September 2026 does not move that starting point. If section 63(3) applies and the enquiry completes on 15 March 2033, that later endpoint governs the records covered by the provision. This is a retention example, not an instruction to delete records on either date; check other applicable obligations and holds.

The ordinary tax endpoint is 31 December 2031, derived by adding 6 calendar years to the period-end. For this exact calendar interval, 2,191 days include the 2028 leap day. The later enquiry endpoint is 15 March 2033, which is 440 days after the ordinary endpoint. These day counts are derived case inputs for the calculation below, not a statutory fixed-day replacement for calendar years. The ordinal representation counts Gregorian days from 1 January in year 1; it is only a way to make the date comparison executable.

```oa-check
name: Gibraltar record-preservation endpoint with a later enquiry
inputs:
  period_end_year: 2025
  ordinary_calendar_years: 6
  period_end_ordinal: 739616
  six_calendar_year_interval_days: 2191
  applicable_enquiry_end_ordinal: 742247
steps:
  - ordinary_endpoint_year: period_end_year + ordinary_calendar_years
  - ordinary_endpoint_ordinal: period_end_ordinal + six_calendar_year_interval_days
  - preservation_endpoint_ordinal: max(ordinary_endpoint_ordinal, applicable_enquiry_end_ordinal)
  - extension_days: preservation_endpoint_ordinal - ordinary_endpoint_ordinal
expected:
  ordinary_endpoint_year: 2031
  ordinary_endpoint_ordinal: 741807
  preservation_endpoint_ordinal: 742247
  extension_days: 440
```

The no-later-enquiry branch has an endpoint ordinal of 741,807, representing 31 December 2031. This example assumes section 63(3) applies to the stated notice and enquiry, and no longer obligation or other hold. The case does not establish the start date for company-law books or permission to delete on either endpoint.

### Evidence-based bank reconciliation

Control example: if a bank balance is £12,400 and the ledger balance is £12,000, the **£400 difference** needs identified reconciling items and evidence. Posting £400 to miscellaneous income solely to make the balances agree is not a substantiated accounting conclusion.

Suppose the supporting bank statement and ledger establish a £100 deposit in transit and £500 of payments already recorded in the ledger but not yet presented to the bank. The adjusted bank balance is £12,400 + £100 − £500 = £12,000, matching the ledger. These are illustrative case inputs, not statutory limits or tax deductions. If the evidence does not establish those items, retain the unexplained £400 difference instead of inventing this reconciliation.

```oa-check
name: Gibraltar bank reconciliation with supported timing differences
inputs:
  bank_statement_balance: 12400
  ledger_balance: 12000
  deposit_in_transit: 100
  unpresented_payments: 500
steps:
  - original_difference: bank_statement_balance - ledger_balance
  - adjusted_bank_balance: bank_statement_balance + deposit_in_transit - unpresented_payments
  - unexplained_residual: adjusted_bank_balance - ledger_balance
expected:
  original_difference: 400
  adjusted_bank_balance: 12000
  unexplained_residual: 0
```

With neither reconciling item established, the residual remains £400 and the job is incomplete. The archive should retain the statement, ledger, individual timing-item evidence and their subsequent clearance, rather than only this arithmetic.

## When to refuse or refer

Section 63A provides a penalty up to level 5 on the standard scale for failure to comply, with appeal to the Magistrates' Court. This draft does not convert that level into a monetary figure without checking the current standard scale. [Income Tax Act, section 63A](https://www.gibraltarlaws.gov.gi/uploads/legislations/income-tax/2010-21/2010-21(23-03-26).pdf).

Do not give a current monetary equivalent for the penalty level without the applicable standard scale. Refer an actual notice, disputed penalty, appeal or enquiry-power expiry for the exact procedural analysis. Do not certify destruction, complete tax compliance, special-sector compliance or an accounting treatment based only on a matching total.

## Filing and payment

This is the evidence-preservation job, not a filing or payment instruction. Preserve the return and the papers supporting its figures; use the separate tax-return and annual-accounts skills for actual forms, attachments, deadlines and payment. Maintaining books or performing the suggested monthly reconciliation does not by itself file either return or accounts.

## Completion checklist

- The statutory person, return period-end and company status are recorded.
- Cash, annual stocktaking, goods/buyer/seller details, contracts, invoices and supporting documents are indexed and retrievable; books are in English.
- The ordinary tax endpoint is computed from the period-end, and each notice/enquiry and other hold is reviewed separately.
- Company books remain at the registered office or a place selected by the directors and available for their inspection.
- Bank, cash, processor, receivable/payable, stock, payroll, asset, financing and owner balances have evidence; unexplained differences remain visible.
- The submitted return, tax bridge, backup/export and provider-handover retrieval arrangements are retained.
- Electronic substitutes, translations, sector rules, monetary penalty scale and actual procedural questions remain unresolved unless separately established.
