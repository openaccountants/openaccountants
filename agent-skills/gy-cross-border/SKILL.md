# Guyana: cross-border income tax, withholding and treaties (2026) v1.0

> **General reference only.** This skill is general tax/accounting reference material for AI-assisted workflows. It has not been reviewed for any specific person's facts, documents, elections, deadlines, residency, filing status, or local procedures. Do not rely on it to file, pay, amend, or take a tax position without review by a qualified professional in the relevant jurisdiction.

**Which text of the law.** The Income Tax Act, Chapter 81:01 (ITA) and the Corporation Tax Act, Chapter 81:03 (CTA), in the Guyana Revenue Authority (GRA) consolidations "revised April 1, 2026". The ITA consolidation also reproduces, as subsidiary legislation under s. 89, the Double Taxation Relief Orders for Canada (Reg. 61/1987), the United Kingdom (Ord. 51/1992) and the United Arab Emirates (Ord. 29 of 2023), and the United States exchange of information agreement (Ord. 35/1992). The CARICOM agreement of 1994 is read from the text published by the CARICOM Secretariat, the depositary (Article 27). Withholding tax under the ITA binds every payer, companies included; CTA s. 15 applies ITA s. 89 and 90 to corporation tax.

**Quick reference (2026).**

| Payment to a non-resident | Domestic rate | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| Dividends and other distributions | 20% of the gross amount | ITA s. 39(1)(a) |
| Interest, rent, royalties, management charges, personal and technical services, commissions, fees, annuities (recipient not trading in Guyana) | 20% of the gross payment | ITA s. 39(1)(b), s. 40 |
| Branch profits remitted or deemed remitted | 20% of after-tax profits not reinvested | ITA s. 39(7) to (9) |
| Contract for goods, services or work (non-resident company) | 10% of each gross payment, a credit against its corporation tax | CTA s. 10B |
| Treaty rate | only if the payer satisfies the Commissioner-General that it applies; otherwise 20% | ITA s. 39(3) |

## Who this applies to

- **Payers in Guyana** (companies, individuals, the State) making a distribution to a non-resident, or a "payment" within ITA s. 40 to a non-resident not engaged in trade or business in Guyana. The payer accounts for the tax (s. 39(1), (2)).
- **Non-residents with Guyana income.** An individual is resident if they reside permanently (or intend to) apart from accepted temporary absences, or reside in Guyana more than 183 days in the year; a company is resident if its business is controlled and managed in Guyana (ITA s. 2). A non-resident is chargeable in the name of a trustee, agent, branch or manager in Guyana (s. 49(1), (2)). Pay for employment exercised in Guyana is Guyana income wherever it is paid (s. 6).
- **Branches of non-resident companies** carrying on business in Guyana: corporation tax on Guyana profits, plus the remittance tax (ITA s. 39(7)).
- **Anyone paying a non-resident company under a contract undertaking** (supply of goods or services, or work, including sub-contracts): CTA s. 10B.
- **Residents with foreign income** claiming credit for foreign tax (ITA s. 89, 90; CTA s. 15).
- **Out of scope**: petroleum agreements and their own tax clauses, the withholding tax on gold and diamond miners (ITA s. 33E), VAT on imported services (see gy-vat-gst), and capital gains of non-residents.

**Treaty rates are caps on Guyana's own tax.** A treaty never raises the tax: where the cap is above 20% (Canada interest, 25%), the domestic 20% applies.

## The base

**What counts as a payment (ITA s. 40).** A payment "without any deductions whatsoever" of the items in the table, other than a distribution and other than employment income under s. 93 (which goes through PAYE). The tax is on the gross amount. Where the gross payment is of the nature of expenses under s. 29(d), the tax withheld in a year equals the tax on the amount allowed to the company under that section (s. 39(2A) proviso).

**Who is outside s. 39(1)(b).** A non-resident engaged in trade or business in Guyana is taxed by assessment on its Guyana profits instead (corporation tax for a company, through its branch or agent, s. 49). A company that has an office, place of business, branch or agency in Guyana is treated as carrying on business there (CTA s. 2(2)). A non-resident company paid under a contract undertaking suffers the 10% CTA s. 10B tax whether or not it has a branch.

**Treaty claims (ITA s. 39(3)).** Where the recipient is resident in a country with a double taxation agreement or a s. 91 Order, the payer still deducts 20% "unless the person making the payments satisfies the Commissioner-General that a reduced rate of withholding tax applies". Get that acceptance before paying at the treaty rate; otherwise withhold 20% and the recipient seeks relief.

**Business profits under the CARICOM agreement.** Profits of business activities are taxed only where the activities are undertaken (Art. 8(1)). An enterprise undertakes activities in a Member State if it has there, among others, an office, a factory, a construction project in progress, a mine or well, a sales agency, a warehouse, other premises for preparatory or auxiliary purposes, or an agent or representative (Art. 8(2)). This list is wider than the usual permanent establishment test. Employment income of a resident of another Member State is taxed only in that State if the employee is present in Guyana 183 days or fewer in the tax year, the employer is not resident in Guyana and the pay is not deducted by a business in Guyana (Art. 15(2)). Professional services are taxed where rendered (Art. 16).

**Related-party pricing.** Guyana has no transfer pricing article with methods or documentation. The ITA has older tools:
- **Connected non-resident (s. 49(3), (4)).** Where a non-resident does business with a resident under its substantial control and the business is arranged so the resident makes no profit or less than ordinary profit, the non-resident is taxed in the resident's name as if the resident were its agent; if its true profit cannot be found, on a fair and reasonable percentage of turnover, appealable to a judge.
- **Group apportionment (s. 49(8) to (11)).** The profits of a branch, subsidiary or associated company of a non-resident company are deemed to be not less than the group's consolidated profit multiplied by the company's share of consolidated turnover. The company is exempt if it proves no significant part of its business was done with or influenced by non-resident group members (s. 49(9)), or that its profits were not reduced by them (s. 49(10)). A company not exempted must give particulars of group profits and turnover with its return (s. 49(11)).
- **Head office expenses** capped at 1% of sales or gross income (s. 18(i) proviso).
- **Artificial transactions (s. 74).** The Commissioner-General may disregard a transaction that reduces tax and is artificial or fictitious.
- **Treaty articles.** CARICOM Art. 10 (associated enterprises) lets a State adjust profits where conditions between related enterprises differ from those between independent ones; the interest, royalty and management fee articles apply the treaty rate only to an arm's length amount (Art. 12(5), 13(5), 14(5)).
- **Petroleum.** The CTA return guide's schedules PR.08 and PR.09 ask petroleum companies to report each transaction with a non-resident associated company and the transfer pricing method used.

**Foreign tax credit for residents (ITA s. 89, 90, Fourth Schedule; CTA s. 15).**
1. Include the foreign income gross: no deduction for the foreign tax (Fourth Schedule Part I reg. 6(1)(a)).
2. Treaty country: credit the foreign tax charged under the treaty against Guyana tax on the same income (for example, Canada treaty Art. 20(2)). The person must be resident in Guyana for the year (reg. 3).
3. Limit: the credit cannot exceed the Guyana tax on that income at the person's average rate (Guyana tax before credit divided by total income), nor the total tax payable for the year (regs 4, 5).
4. No treaty: unilateral relief for foreign tax on income arising in that country, including pay for services performed there (Part II regs 1, 2), but the total credit may not exceed one half (prescribed Commonwealth countries) or one quarter (any other country) of the sum of the regs 4 and 5 limits (s. 90(2) proviso (a)).
5. A company holding at least 25% of a foreign company's voting power counts the underlying tax on the profits behind a dividend (CTA s. 15(2); Part II reg. 3 for unilateral relief).
6. Claim within six years from the year of the income (reg. 10). A person may elect not to take a credit (reg. 9).

**How to pay.** Withholding tax is paid to the GRA and reported with the monthly withholding return; the payer reports gross payments to non-residents in its own return schedules (the individual return has Schedule 02; companies use the A.01 schedule for contractors). A treaty rate is used only after the Commissioner-General accepts it in writing (s. 39(3)).

## Penalties and interest

- **Failing to withhold or pay over ITA withholding tax** is an offence (s. 39(5)); s. 93(4) then applies, so the amount bears the 2% a month late payment penalty of s. 99(1) as if it were the payer's own tax from the date it should have been paid.
- **Contractor withholding tax not paid over**: a penalty equal to the tax in default, recoverable like the tax (CTA s. 10B(6)).
- The payer is discharged against the recipient for the tax it withholds (s. 43), so withholding protects the payer.
- Penalties count as tax and may be reduced or waived by the Commissioner-General (s. 99(4), s. 108; see gy-income-tax).

## Cross-border points

- **Temporary residents.** A person in Guyana for a temporary purpose only, without intent to settle, who did not reside there for six months in total in the year before the year of assessment, is not taxed on income arising outside Guyana (ITA s. 47).
- **Residents' foreign income** is taxable when it accrues, except foreign earned income and foreign income of a person not ordinarily resident or not domiciled, taxed only on the amount received in Guyana (s. 5 proviso; see gy-income-tax).
- **United States.** Guyana has an exchange of information agreement with the United States (Ord. 35/1992), not a double taxation treaty: payments to US residents bear the full 20%.
- **Exchange of information.** The GRA states that Guyana exchanges information under the Canada and United Kingdom conventions and the multilateral CARICOM agreement (GRA, FATCA Agreement page).
- **CARICOM Member States** listed in Schedule II of the agreement: Antigua and Barbuda, Belize, Dominica, Grenada, Guyana, Jamaica, Montserrat, St. Kitts and Nevis, Saint Lucia, St. Vincent and the Grenadines, Trinidad and Tobago.

## Two worked examples that recompute

### Example 1: royalty to a UK company, apply for the treaty rate or withhold 20%

Demerara Foods Inc., resident in Guyana, pays a UK resident company, the beneficial owner with no branch in Guyana, a royalty of G$10,000,000 for a trademark licence in March 2026.

- **Without the Commissioner-General's acceptance**: withhold 20% (ITA s. 39(1)(b), (3)), G$2,000,000; pay the UK company G$8,000,000. Pay the tax to the GRA by 14 April 2026.
- **With acceptance of the UK treaty rate** (Art. 13(2), 10%): withhold G$1,000,000; pay G$9,000,000. The UK company gains G$1,000,000 in cash flow.
- **If the licensor were in Trinidad and Tobago** (CARICOM Art. 13, cap 15%): G$1,500,000.
- **If the tax is paid over late**, say on 20 May 2026 (one month and part of a second late): 2% a month for two months, G$80,000 on the G$2,000,000 (s. 93(4), 99(1)).

The decision: the treaty claim is worth G$1,000,000 here, but only once the Commissioner-General accepts it; paying at 10% without that acceptance leaves the payer liable for the other G$1,000,000.

### Example 2: a Guyana company's Canadian royalties and the foreign tax credit

Essequibo Software Inc. is resident in Guyana and non-commercial (25% corporation tax). Its 2026 chargeable profits are G$20,000,000, which include G$4,000,000 of royalties from a Canadian licensee, counted gross. Canada withheld 10% under the treaty (Art. 12(2)), G$400,000.

- Guyana tax before credit: 25% of 20,000,000 = G$5,000,000. Average rate 25%.
- Limit (reg. 4): 25% of the G$4,000,000 royalty = G$1,000,000. Credit (treaty, Art. 20(2)): the lower of G$400,000 and G$1,000,000, so G$400,000.
- Tax payable: 5,000,000 - 400,000 = G$4,600,000.

**The other way: no treaty.** If the same royalty came from a country outside the Commonwealth with no treaty, unilateral relief is capped at one quarter of the limit (s. 90(2) proviso (a)), reading the limit as the reg. 4 figure of G$1,000,000: credit G$250,000, tax payable G$4,750,000. A treaty is worth G$150,000 here.

## Ask the client first

1. Where is the recipient resident, and is it the beneficial owner? Does it have an office, branch, agent or construction site in Guyana (trade or business in Guyana; CARICOM Art. 8)?
2. What exactly is the payment: dividend, interest, royalty, management or technical fee, rent, or a contract for goods, services or work (CTA s. 10B)?
3. Is the payment for something used or done in Guyana, or does it arise outside Guyana (no withholding on income arising outside Guyana)?
4. Has the Commissioner-General accepted a treaty rate in writing?
5. Are payer and recipient related? Head office charges (1% cap) and group apportionment (s. 49(8)) may apply.
6. For a resident's foreign income: country, foreign tax paid, and whether the country has a treaty with Guyana.
