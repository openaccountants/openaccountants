# South Africa: foreign employment income exemption, s 10(1)(o)(ii)

> **The most misunderstood point.** The exemption is not "the first R1,250,000 of foreign remuneration is exempt" in every case. The exemption covers the remuneration **for services rendered outside the Republic** during the qualifying period, and the result is then capped at R1,250,000 for the year of assessment. Remuneration for days worked in South Africa never qualifies, even inside a qualifying period. Where the payroll codes the whole assignment remuneration as foreign service income, the work-day split is what decides the exempt amount, and on most facts it binds before the cap does.

## Scope

A South African **tax resident** who renders services outside the Republic as an **employee** (holder of an office or employment), for or on behalf of any employer, South African or foreign. The exemption does not apply to non-residents, to independent contractors (their income is not remuneration), or to services rendered for an employer in the public sector or by a holder of a public office appointed under an Act of Parliament.

Not covered: s 10(1)(o)(i), the separate exemption for officers and crew of ships (SARS Interpretation Note 34); tax residency itself (see `za-tax-residency`); the full s 6quat foreign tax credit computation; double tax agreement relief, which is a separate and sometimes better route; ceasing residency (see `leaving-south-africa-tax-emigration`).

## Ask the client first

1. Is the person a South African tax resident for the whole year of assessment, and did residency end during it? (A non-resident does not need this exemption; a part-year resident needs the split year first.)
2. Employee or independent contractor, tested on the relationship, not the contract label?
3. The date-by-date travel record: every exit and entry, with passport stamps or boarding passes, across the assignment.
4. Which days services were actually rendered, inside and outside the Republic, including weekends worked?
5. What the IRP5 shows under the foreign service codes (3651, 3652 and the other foreign service codes), and whether the employer already reduced it by a time ratio.
6. Was foreign tax paid on the remuneration, and does a double tax agreement with the work country apply?

## The method, step by step

### Rates, bands and thresholds

### Step 1: settle residency and the employment relationship

Confirm the person is resident (ordinarily resident or under the physical presence test, and not treaty-resident elsewhere) and an employee. If either fails, stop: the exemption is not the route.

### Step 2: pass the gate (the day tests)

In **any 12 consecutive months** (not necessarily the year of assessment), the person must be outside the Republic for **more than 183 full days in aggregate** and for a **continuous period of more than 60 full days** within that 12-month period, rendering services for an employer. The gate decides only whether the exemption is available. It never sizes it.

Counting full days:
- A full day is 24 hours from 00h00 to 24h00. The departure day and the return day are not full days outside the Republic.
- An overnight flight: where the South African exit stamp and the foreign entry stamp fall on different dates, the arrival day abroad can be a full day; a same-day regional hop loses only that day.
- A day spent travelling between two foreign countries is a full day outside the Republic; transiting a third country does not break the absence.
- Days in transit through the Republic: SARS Interpretation Note 16 treats a person in transit through the Republic between two places outside it, who does not formally enter through a port of entry, as outside the Republic for those days.
- Build the count date by date from passport stamps, boarding passes and the assignment schedule. A count from memory does not survive verification.

### Step 3: choose the 12-month window

The period is any 12 consecutive months. An assignment that straddles 28 February often gives a thin margin on one window and a comfortable one on another. Test more than one window, and record which was used and why. A margin of two days is one disputed stamp from failing.

### Step 4: size the exempt amount

Only remuneration for services rendered **outside** the Republic during the qualifying period is exempt. Remuneration for days worked in South Africa, even during the qualifying period, does not qualify.

Where the IRP5 carries the assignment remuneration as foreign service income without a split, apportion it by work days:

exempt before cap = (work days outside the Republic / total work days in the period) x foreign service remuneration

Work days are days on which services were **actually rendered**, not a Monday to Friday proxy. Touring professionals, shift workers and staff on duty for a whole deployment often work weekends; a weekday proxy can understate the foreign work days by a third or more.

### Step 5: apply the cap

Limit the result to **R1,250,000** for the year of assessment. The cap applies after the work-day split, not before. For the cap to bind, the work-day ratio must exceed R1,250,000 divided by the foreign service remuneration; work that number out before assuming the cap is the operative limit.

### Step 6: tax the balance and claim the credit

The non-exempt balance stays in gross income and is taxed at the normal rates for the year (`za-income-tax-tables`). Where the work country also taxed it, claim the s 6quat foreign tax credit on assessment through the ITR12, within its limits. An employer may apply under para 10 of the Fourth Schedule for a directive to take the expected s 6quat credit into account in monthly PAYE.

### Step 7: check the certificate before the return

SARS's codes guide says to use code 3651 only for foreign services income (subject to PAYE) and code 3652 only for foreign services income that is non-taxable, including the s 10(1)(o)(ii) exempt portion. If the employer has already apportioned the remuneration before coding it and the work-day split is applied again on assessment, the exemption is reduced twice. The fix is a corrected IRP5 from the employer, not a recomputation on the return.

## Boundary and exception table

| Situation | Treatment |
| --- | --- |
| Passes the day tests, but most work days were in South Africa | Exemption is available but small: only the foreign-service share qualifies |
| Remuneration above R1,250,000 and a high foreign work-day share | Exemption capped at R1,250,000; the excess is taxed in South Africa, with s 6quat relief for foreign tax |
| Independent contractor | Not covered: the income is not remuneration |
| Public sector employer, or holder of a public office appointed under an Act | Not covered |
| Non-resident for the whole year | Not needed: a non-resident is taxed in South Africa only on South African source income |
| Resident who ceased residency during the year | Settle the cessation date first; the exemption is tested only for the resident part |
| Officer or crew of a ship | Different exemption, s 10(1)(o)(i), not this one |
| A double tax agreement gives the work country sole taxing rights | May beat s 10(1)(o)(ii): compare before claiming |
| Remuneration paid after the qualifying period for services in it | Still qualifies if it relates to services rendered outside the Republic during the qualifying period (Interpretation Note 16) |

## Worked cases

### Case 1: the ratio binds, not the cap

An employee on a foreign assignment has assignment remuneration of R1,200,000 coded as foreign service income. The gate is met. Services were rendered on 120 work days outside the Republic out of 200 total work days in the period.

- Ratio 120 / 200 = 0.60.
- Exempt before cap 0.60 x R1,200,000 = R720,000.
- R720,000 is below R1,250,000, so the cap does not bite: exemption **R720,000**, and R480,000 stays taxable. The remuneration was close to the cap, but the exemption is nowhere near R1,250,000. On this base the cap would bind only with a ratio above 104%, which is impossible.

```oa-check
name: Ratio binds, remuneration R1,200,000, 120 of 200 work days abroad
inputs:
  remuneration: 1200000
  days_abroad: 120
  total_days: 200
  cap: 1250000
steps:
  - ratio: days_abroad / total_days
  - before_cap: ratio * remuneration
  - exempt: min(before_cap, cap)
  - taxable_balance: remuneration - exempt
expected:
  ratio: 0.60
  exempt: 720000.00
  taxable_balance: 480000.00
```

### Case 2: the cap binds

A resident engineer on a long offshore contract earns R2,000,000 of foreign service remuneration in YoA 2027. The gate is met, and 225 of 250 work days were outside the Republic.

- Ratio 225 / 250 = 0.90; exempt before cap 0.90 x R2,000,000 = R1,800,000.
- Cap R1,250,000 applies: exemption **R1,250,000**.
- Taxable balance R750,000, taxed at the 2027 rates with s 6quat relief for any foreign tax on it.

## When to refuse or refer

- The person is an independent contractor: refer to the general rules on foreign income and s 6quat.
- Residency is in doubt, or ended during the year: settle it first with `za-tax-residency` or `leaving-south-africa-tax-emigration`.
- The day count rests on memory, not records: do not claim until the date-by-date schedule exists.
- A double tax agreement may give sole taxing rights elsewhere: refer for a treaty review before relying on s 10(1)(o)(ii).
- The IRP5 coding looks pre-apportioned: ask the employer for a corrected certificate before filing.

## Filing and payment

- The employee claims on the ITR12 for the year of assessment (filing season dates: `za-income-tax`). The exempt amount flows from the foreign service codes on the IRP5; check that the certificate shows the remuneration in full under 3651 or 3652 before filing.
- The s 6quat credit is claimed on the ITR12 with proof of the foreign tax paid.
- Keep the travel record, the work-day schedule and the window chosen, for five years.

## Completion checklist

- [ ] Residency and employee status confirmed.
- [ ] More than 183 full days and more than 60 continuous full days in one 12-month window, with the window recorded.
- [ ] A second window tested where the margin is thin.
- [ ] Work days inside plus outside equal total work days.
- [ ] Exempt amount = foreign-service share, then capped at R1,250,000.
- [ ] IRP5 foreign service coding checked for double apportionment.
- [ ] s 6quat credit computed on the taxed balance where foreign tax was paid.
