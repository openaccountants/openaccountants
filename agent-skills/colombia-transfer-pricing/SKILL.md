## Who this applies to

**Every income taxpayer with transactions with related parties abroad** must price them at arm's length (art. 260-2). The rule also covers:

- a taxpayer in the national customs territory dealing with a **related party in a free zone** (art. 260-2);
- a Colombian **permanent establishment** of a foreign entity, for transactions concluded in its favour by a related foreign entity, and Colombian residents dealing with each other through a foreign PE (art. 260-2);
- **any transaction with a person in a non-cooperative, low or no tax jurisdiction or a preferential tax regime**, whether related or not (art. 260-7 par. 2).

Purely domestic transactions between related Colombian taxpayers outside free zones are not in the regime. Transfer pricing affects income tax only (art. 260-2 par.).

**Related (vinculados), art. 260-1.** In short:

- **Subordinates:** more than 50% of the capital held by the parent, directly or through subordinates (non-voting preference shares excluded); the votes for the minimum decision majority or to elect most of the board; dominant influence by contract; control by individuals or non-corporate vehicles; or a right to 50% of the profits.
- **Branches, agencies and permanent establishments** with their head office or enterprise.
- **Other cases:** two subordinates of one parent or of one person; companies where one person holds more than 50% of the capital or controls decisions of both; companies more than 50% owned by spouses or relatives to the second degree; transactions through unrelated intermediaries; more than 50% of gross income from the shareholders or partners; and consortia, joint ventures and other collaboration contracts.

Related status covers every entity in the group even when the parent is abroad.

## The base

**The arm's length principle (art. 260-2).** Income, costs, deductions, assets and liabilities from related-party transactions in scope are set as independent parties would have set them in comparable transactions.

**Methods (art. 260-3).** The taxpayer picks the most appropriate of:

1. **Comparable uncontrolled price.** Mandatory for commodities (a quoted price is the reference, at the pricing date the contracts prove, otherwise DIAN may use the shipping date). For used tangible assets bought from a related party: the invoice price of the new asset from an independent party less depreciation.
2. **Resale price:** resale price to independents less the gross margin (gross profit / net sales) of comparable independents.
3. **Cost plus:** cost plus the gross mark-up (gross profit / cost of sales) of comparable independents.
4. **Transactional net margin:** operating profit against assets, sales, costs, expenses or cash flow.
5. **Profit split:** routine returns first, then the residual profit split as independents would.

The choice weighs the functional analysis, reliable data, the degree of comparability and the reliability of adjustments. Internal comparables come first (art. 260-4 par.). Unlisted shares and hard-to-compare assets are valued by generally accepted financial methods, in particular discounted future income; book value (valor patrimonial or intrínseco) is never accepted (art. 260-3 par. 1). Intragroup services and cost-sharing need proof the service was really provided (par. 2). Restructurings that move functions, assets or risks abroad must be paid for at arm's length (par. 3).

**Comparability (art. 260-4).** The characteristics of the transaction, functions, assets and risks, contract terms against conduct, economic circumstances and business strategies. **AUDIT FLASH POINT: related-party loans.** If a loan's amount, term, rating, guarantee, solvency and rate do not match market practice, the interest is not deductible at all and the loan is treated as a capital contribution, with the interest treated as a dividend (art. 260-4(1)(a); DUR art. 1.2.2.1.3). Thin capitalisation (art. 118-1) applies on top.

**The range and the median (DUR art. 1.2.2.2.4.5).** With two or more comparable results, build the range; the interquartile method is optional but, if used, follows this order:

1. Sort the results in ascending order and number them 1 to n.
2. Median position = (n + 1) / 2. If it has decimals, take the value at the whole-number position plus the decimal times the difference to the next value.
3. 25th percentile position = (median position + 1) / 2; same interpolation.
4. 75th percentile position = (median position minus 1) plus the 25th percentile position; same interpolation.

A result inside the range is arm's length. A result outside it is moved to the median (art. 260-3).

**Payments to non-cooperative or preferential regimes (art. 260-7 par. 3).** Payments to a related party there are not deductible unless the taxpayer documents that party's functions, assets, risks and all its costs.

**Corresponding adjustments (art. 260-6).** If a treaty partner adjusts the price of its resident and DIAN accepts it, the Colombian party may file a correcting return without penalty.

**What is not changed by TP.** For taxpayers in the regime who show arm's length pricing, the general valuation rules of arts. 35, 90, 124-1, 124-2, 143 par. 2, 151, 152 and 312(2) and (3) do not apply (art. 260-8).

The order of work: (1) list related parties and in-scope counterparties; (2) test the 100,000 / 61,000 UVT thresholds on the year's gross assets and income; (3) group transactions into types (DUR art. 1.2.2.3.2) and test each type against 45,000 UVT or 10,000 UVT; (4) do the functional and economic analysis and set prices or adjust; (5) carry any adjustment into the income tax return (Form 110, April or May 2027; see [co-corporate-income-tax](co-corporate-income-tax.md)); (6) file Form 120 and the files in September 2027. The financial information in the documentation is signed by the legal representative and the accountant or statutory auditor (art. 260-5(1)).

## Penalties and interest

Art. 260-11 ET. Penalties capped in UVT use the UVT of the year the penalty is set; the 2026 figure is shown for orientation.

**Documentation (local and master file), part A:**

| Breach | Penalty | Cap |
|---|---|---|
| Late, within 5 business days | 0.05% of the transactions to be documented | 417 UVT |
| Late, after 5 business days | 0.2% of the transactions to be documented per month or part month | 1,667 UVT a month; 20,000 UVT in total |
| Inconsistencies | 1% of the transaction concerned (0.5% fallbacks) | 5,000 UVT |
| Not filed | 4% of the related-party transactions not documented (1% fallbacks), plus loss of the costs and deductions unless filed before the official assessment; 6% (2% fallbacks) for tax-haven transactions | 25,000 UVT; 30,000 UVT for tax havens |
| Information omitted | 2% of the amount or transaction concerned (1% fallbacks), plus loss of costs; 4% (2% fallbacks) for tax havens | 5,000 UVT (1,400 UVT if documented transactions are under 80,000 UVT); 10,000 UVT for tax havens |
| Correction changing price, method, comparability or range | 1% of the corrected transactions; 4% after a special requirement or statement of charges | 5,000 UVT; 20,000 UVT |

Penalties for inconsistencies and omissions fall to 50% if fixed before the sanction or official assessment (A.6).

**Informative return (Form 120), part B:**

| Breach | Penalty | Cap |
|---|---|---|
| Late, within 5 business days | 0.02% of the transactions in the regime | 313 UVT |
| Late, after 5 business days | 0.1% per month or part month | 1,250 UVT a month; 15,000 UVT in total |
| Inconsistencies | 0.6% of the transaction concerned | 2,280 UVT |
| Information omitted | 1.3% (2.6% for tax havens) of the amount or transaction concerned, plus loss of costs | 3,000 UVT (1,000 UVT under 80,000 UVT of transactions); 6,000 UVT for tax havens |
| Not filed after a one-month summons | 4% of the transactions in the regime | 20,000 UVT |

Voluntary correction with the penalties at 50% is possible before a statement of charges (B.6). For loans, the penalty base is the principal, not the interest (par. 2). DIAN adds 30% where the taxpayer omits or miscalculates a part B penalty (par. 3). Country-by-country failures fall under art. 651, not part A (art. 260-5 par. 1). Any extra income tax from a TP adjustment bears inaccuracy penalties and late-payment interest under the general rules (see [co-corporate-income-tax](co-corporate-income-tax.md)).

## Cross-border points

- **Free zones count as "abroad"** for TP: a mainland company dealing with a related free-zone user is in scope (art. 260-2).
- **Tax havens.** The list of non-cooperative and low or no tax jurisdictions is in DUR art. 1.2.2.5.1 (as substituted by Decreto 1496 de 2024); preferential regimes follow the criteria of arts. 1.2.2.6.1 to 1.2.2.6.8. Check the counterparty's country against the current list before testing thresholds.
- **Withholding** on the payment abroad (interest, royalties, services) is separate from TP: see [colombia-cross-border](colombia-cross-border.md).
- **Mutual agreement and corresponding adjustments** run through the treaty (art. 260-6).

## Two worked examples that recompute

### Example 1: does a mid-size SAS file, and which local file sections?

Andina Distribución SAS, a Colombian subsidiary of a Spanish group, had in 2026 gross income of COP 3,600,000,000 and gross assets on 31 December of COP 2,900,000,000. It bought goods from its Spanish parent for COP 2,500,000,000, paid it COP 400,000,000 of management services, and owes it a loan with principal COP 800,000,000. None of the counterparties is in a tax haven.

- Gross income 3,600,000,000 / 52,374 = 68,736 UVT, at least 61,000 UVT, so Form 120 is due and documentation must be prepared, although assets (55,371 UVT, rounded) are under 100,000 UVT.
- Purchases: 47,734 UVT, above 45,000 UVT: local file section required.
- Services: 7,637 UVT: no local file section for this type, but the type still goes on Form 120.
- Loan: principal 15,275 UVT (interest is not counted): no local file section.
- Part of a multinational group and meeting the local file thresholds: a master file is also required.
- **Decision:** file Form 120 with all three types; prepare the local file for purchases only; file the master file.

### Example 2: a distributor below the range, adjusted to the median

Andina tests its purchases with the transactional net margin method (operating margin on sales). Its 2026 sales are COP 10,000,000,000 and its operating margin is 1.0%. Seven comparable independent distributors have margins of 1.8%, 2.5%, 3.1%, 4.0%, 4.6%, 6.0% and 7.2% (already sorted).

- Median position (7 + 1) / 2 = 4: median **4.0%**.
- 25th percentile position (4 + 1) / 2 = 2.5: 2.5% + 0.5 x (3.1% minus 2.5%) = **2.8%**.
- 75th percentile position (4 minus 1) + 2.5 = 5.5: 4.6% + 0.5 x (6.0% minus 4.6%) = **5.3%**.
- 1.0% is below the range 2.8% to 5.3%, so it moves to the median of 4.0%.
- Adjustment to taxable income: (4.0% minus 1.0%) x 10,000,000,000 = **COP 300,000,000**; income tax at 35% = **COP 105,000,000**, before penalties and interest.
- **Decision for the next year:** price the purchases so the margin lands inside 2.8% to 5.3% before year end; an adjustment made in the return is taxed, one avoided in the price is not.

```oa-check

inputs:
  sales: 10000000000
  tested_margin: 0.010
  c2: 0.025
  c3: 0.031
  c4: 0.040
  c5: 0.046
  c6: 0.060
steps:
  - median_pos: (7 + 1) / 2
  - median: c4
  - p25_pos: (median_pos + 1) / 2
  - p25: c2 + 0.5 * (c3 - c2)
  - p75_pos: (median_pos - 1) + p25_pos
  - p75: c5 + 0.5 * (c6 - c5)
  - adjustment: (median - tested_margin) * sales
  - tax: adjustment * 0.35
expected:
  median_pos: 4
  median: 0.040
  p25_pos: 2.5
  p25: 0.028
  p75_pos: 5.5
  p75: 0.053
  adjustment: 300000000
  tax: 105000000
```

## Ask the client first

1. The group chart: who holds what, who controls whom, and any free-zone users or tax-haven entities in the group or among counterparties.
2. Gross assets on 31 December 2026 and gross income for 2026, from the income tax return.
3. Every transaction with each related party or tax-haven counterparty in 2026, by type and amount; for loans, the principal, rate, term and guarantees.
4. Whether the group is multinational, where the parent files its country-by-country report, and the group's consolidated income.
5. The last digit of the NIT (deadlines) and any APA in force.
6. Whether a foreign tax authority has adjusted a price on the other side (art. 260-6).
