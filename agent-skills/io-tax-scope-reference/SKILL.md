# British Indian Ocean Territory: selected tax and employer scope, 2026 v1.0

> **Source-cited draft.** Written by Ryan Duguid; brought to the 2026 standard by Claude. Built from the BIOT Taxation, Companies, Employment and Courts Ordinances (revised edition, law as at 1 September 2020) and the BIOT Constitution Order 2004. No accountant has reviewed it.

**Answer first.** Law preserved in BIOT through Constitution Order s3(2)(a) may not require or permit income tax, corporation tax, capital gains tax or any substantially similar tax on income, profits, gains and capital, whether arising in the Territory or outside it (Taxation Ordinance 1981 s3). No body corporate is regarded as resident in the Territory for tax purposes (Companies Ordinance 1981 s3). The Employment Ordinance 1984 applies to an employer of **10 or more employees** and excludes the Crown and any country's government. This is a dated scope reference: it does not determine a person's tax residence, a payable amount or a filing obligation, and source silence does not prove an obligation is absent.

## Scope

This conditional reference describes selected provisions of the dated revised ordinances. Load the universal base. The reference does not determine present territorial governance, a person's tax residence, a payable amount or a filing obligation. All requested-period application remains conditional on the complete operative law and facts.

In scope: the territorial reach of the Taxation Ordinance s3 restriction, the Companies Ordinance s3 residence rule, and the Employment Ordinance ss2, 3 and 24 to 27 conditions, as stated in the revised edition dated 1 September 2020.

Out of scope: present sovereignty or treaty status, foreign (UK, US or other) tax on persons working in BIOT, customs and imports, service charges, permits and immigration, and any computation of tax, contributions or penalties.

## Ask the client first

1. **The requested period and the obligation**, and the exact island or location.
2. **Who the person is**: taxpayer type, citizenship and residence evidence.
3. **The payer or employer**: identity and legal status, including any Crown or government connection.
4. **The work**: place and dates of work, contract and income source, transaction direction and supporting documents.
5. **For employment**: the employee count and legal relationship, the Crown or government facts, actual insurance and compensation evidence and any Commissioner decision.
6. **For a tax-scope request**: the specific charging instrument and its legal path.

No default is authorised for unknown law, commencement, residence, employer status, source, exemptions or territorial reach. Keep the affected decision as Needs Input. Missing evidence is not a zero liability, an exemption or a negative registration requirement. Ask the base's structured questions for missing facts and put missing legal sources in the action list.

### The rules, with their conditions

| Rule | Wording and required distinction |
|---|---|
| **Taxation s3** | The restriction addresses law having effect through Constitution Order s3(2)(a): such law may not require or permit the imposition within the Territory of income tax, corporation tax, capital gains tax or a substantially similar tax upon income, profits, gains and capital, whether arising inside or outside it. This describes the named preserved-law restriction. It is not an opinion that every possible local, foreign, payroll or transaction tax is absent. |
| **Constitution ss3 and 10; Courts s3** | Establish the applicable preservation, law-making and received-law paths separately. Do not infer that the Taxation Ordinance prevents every later ordinance, or that all English law automatically applies unchanged. Requested-period hierarchy and suitability remain required. |
| **Companies s3** | No body corporate or entity treated as a body corporate for tax purposes in another territory is regarded as resident in the Territory under the captured wording. It does not determine foreign residence or liability, or supply an incorporation or registration procedure. |
| **Employment ss2 and 3** | Unless context otherwise requires, employee includes express or implied and oral or written contracts of service or personal work, and employer means a person employing **10 or more employees**. Section 3 excludes the Crown, persons in its service or employment, any country's government and persons in its service or employment. Establish the actual employer and relationship; a contractor is not proved to be a government employee by its customer. |
| **Employment s24** | Within the Ordinance's scope, the employer must keep each employee insured **at all times while employed in the Territory**, against death or personal injury **arising out of and in the course of employment**. The Commissioner must be satisfied that amount and manner are reasonable in that employee's circumstances, including the relevant laws and practices of the employer's principal-business country and the employee's prior-residence country. Personal injury includes disease and physical or mental impairment under s2. Subsection (2) permits the Commissioner to exempt an employer where satisfied that foreign law applying to that employer for employees in the Territory requires compensation of amounts satisfactory under subsection (1). Foreign insurance or compensation is not an automatic exemption. |
| **Employment ss25 to 27** | The employer must provide, or ensure availability **in the Territory** of, adequate medical-care and attention facilities for personal injury **however arising**. On termination of employment in the Territory, repatriation is to the country from which the employee came for employment, or another country that will admit the employee **and to which the employee is willing to go**. Section 27 connects non-satisfaction of ss24 and 25 to entry-permit action and non-compliance with s26 to an offence. No penalty amount or operational permit advice is supplied. These duties are separate from tax and contribution questions. |

The 2020 source statements also retain open dependencies on later UK Acts, Orders and extensions, Courts s4 declarations, Employment regulations and exemption instruments, incorporated criminal and immigration provisions and current Constitution and treaty machinery. The 1966 and 1976 US notes, imports regime and service-charge legislation have not been independently qualified for this reference.

### Steps

1. Fix the requested period and confirm no later ordinance changes the rule (R-IO-PERIOD).
2. Identify the charge or duty and its legal path; apply Taxation s3 only to law preserved through Constitution Order s3(2)(a).
3. For a company, apply Companies s3 to BIOT residence only; send foreign residence and tax elsewhere.
4. For employment, count the employees, test the s3 exclusions on the actual relationship, then apply ss24 to 27.
5. Record each decision, the provision and the evidence in the scope register; hold anything unresolved as Needs Input.

## Boundary and exception table

These invented cases resolve only the stated dated-text question. Each retains the period and amendment gate and produces no liability or complete employment opinion.

| Case | Stated facts, rule and result |
|---|---|
| **1. Preserved income-tax law** | Assume a proposed local charge is income tax imposed through law preserved by Order s3(2)(a). S1 s3 supplies a restriction on that path. Hold the actual charge and current-law determination under R-IO-PERIOD and R-IO-CALC; no universal zero-tax conclusion follows. |
| **2. Different-country corporate status** | An invented entity is documented as treated as corporate for tax in another territory. S2 s3 addresses BIOT residence under its captured wording. Foreign residence, incorporation and tax remain separate held questions. |
| **3. 9 workers** | A private person employs 9 persons, and no context-based departure from the definition is established. The simple s2 numeric definition is not met. That result does not exempt it from all laws, prove worker classification or resolve later amendments. |
| **4. 10 workers** | A private person employs 10 documented employees and no s3 exclusion is established. The numeric limb is met. Review ss24 to 27 and all other applicable duties; tax and social amounts remain held. |
| **5. Government customer** | A private contractor's sole customer is a foreign government. The contract alone does not establish that its staff are persons in that government's service or employment. R-IO-FACTS fires until the actual relationship and exclusion are established. |
| **6. Foreign compensation** | A covered employer produces evidence of a foreign compensation duty but no Commissioner exemption. S3 s24(2) makes the exemption a separate decision. Do not substitute the evidence for that decision; R-IO-FACTS holds any asserted exemption. |
| **7. Consumption-tax claim** | A request treats S1 s3 as proof that a particular import or sale has no tax. The named provision does not settle that obligation. R-IO-CALC fires pending its complete authority and facts. |

## Worked cases

**Case A: does the 10-employee limb apply?** Two private employers, with no s3 exclusion established. Employer 1 has 9 employees, employer 2 has 10. The numeric limb is met when the count is 10 or more; the check below returns 1 when met and 0 when not (valid for counts up to 19).

```oa-check
name: BIOT Employment Ordinance s2, 9 and 10 employees
inputs:
  employees_1: 9
  employees_2: 10
  threshold: 10
steps:
  - limb_met_1: min(1, floor(employees_1 / threshold))
  - limb_met_2: min(1, floor(employees_2 / threshold))
expected:
  limb_met_1: 0
  limb_met_2: 1
```

Result: employer 1 is outside the s2 definition on the number alone; employer 2 meets it and must be reviewed against ss24 to 27. Neither result settles tax.

**Case B: gap in s24 insurance cover.** A covered employer's employee works in the Territory for 200 days in 2026. The insurance policy produced covers 150 of those days. Section 24 requires cover at all times while employed in the Territory, so the uncovered days (derived) are a breach to flag, not a cost to compute.

```oa-check
name: BIOT Employment Ordinance s24, days without cover
inputs:
  days_employed_in_territory: 200
  days_covered: 150
steps:
  - uncovered_days: max(0, days_employed_in_territory - days_covered)
expected:
  uncovered_days: 50
```

Result: 50 days without cover (derived). Flag under s24 and s27; a foreign compensation duty does not cure it without a Commissioner exemption under s24(2).
