## Scope

Determine the applicable social security legislation, then obtain the institution's determination and certificate. This Guide covers employees, self-employed people, posted and habitual multi-state workers. It does not compute contributions, decide income-tax residence, grant immigration rights or replace benefit-specific eligibility rules.

**Personal and territorial scope first.** Regulation 883/2004 Article 2 covers the specified Member State nationals, resident stateless persons/refugees and family/survivor cases. Regulation 1231/2010 Article 1 extends 883/987 to qualifying legally resident third-country nationals with a situation extending beyond a single Member State; Denmark does not participate in that extension. Do not treat 883 as nationality-neutral for every EU/EEA/Swiss corridor. The EEA/Swiss agreements and the separate UK rules below need their own scope check. [R1](https://www.cleiss.fr/docs/textes/883-04/t1.html), [R5](https://www.cleiss.fr/reglements/circulaires/ci201120.pdf), [R6](https://www.cleiss.fr/reglements/883_pays.html)

**Current versus enacted future rules.** Regulation (EU) 2026/2359 entered into force on 1 November 2026. Under amended 883 Article 91 and 987 Article 97, the new posting conditions, three-month prior-coverage requirements, revised multi-state procedure and business-trip/three-day notification exemptions apply from **2 November 2028**, not in this Guide's current period. The standardized certificate and institution's duty to assess the facts under 987 Article 19(2)-(3) already apply from **1 November 2026**. Existing-case transition rules are in 883 Article 87b. Check those dates before using a consolidated page that displays both old and future text. [R4](https://www.cleiss.fr/docs/textes/883-04/am9.html)

## Ask the client first

- Nationality, legal residence, habitual residence and which EU/EEA/Swiss/UK scope instrument applies, including any Withdrawal Agreement continuity.
- Employee, self-employed or civil-servant status under the relevant national legislation; the actual employer and its real registered office/place of business.
- Physical work locations, working time and pay by country, including third countries, and the projected pattern for the following 12 calendar months; for self-employment also turnover, services and income.
- Whether this is a temporary posting or a habitual multi-state pattern; anticipated duration, previous coverage, replacements, continuing employer relationship and substantial sending-state business.
- Existing A1/determinations, objections or overlapping demands, and any Article 16 agreement or telework derogation already requested or granted.

## The method, step by step

1. Establish personal, territorial and material scope and the applicable date/version. [R1](https://www.cleiss.fr/docs/textes/883-04/t1.html), [R4](https://www.cleiss.fr/docs/textes/883-04/am9.html), [R5](https://www.cleiss.fr/reglements/circulaires/ci201120.pdf), [R6](https://www.cleiss.fr/reglements/883_pays.html), [R9](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/608ae0c0d3bf7f0136332887/TS_8.2021_UK_EU_EAEC_Trade_and_Cooperation_Agreement.pdf)
2. Locate the actual work and classify the activity. Start with Article 11; test Article 12 only for a qualifying temporary posting, Article 13 for habitual multi-state activity, then any valid Article 16 derogation. [R1](https://www.cleiss.fr/docs/textes/883-04/t1.html), [R2](https://www.cleiss.fr/docs/textes/883-04/t2.html), [R3](https://www.cleiss.fr/docs/textes/987-09/t1.html)
3. Apply the relevant table below with all its conditions. Client location or a contract label does not alone establish a work state or employer. [R2](https://www.cleiss.fr/docs/textes/883-04/t2.html), [R3](https://www.cleiss.fr/docs/textes/987-09/t1.html), [R8](https://curia.europa.eu/site/upload/docs/application/pdf/2026-01/monthly_case-law_digest_en-_december_2025.pdf)
4. Notify the designated institution and obtain the determination/A1. Record the supporting facts and monitor changes; use institutional dialogue if states disagree. [R3](https://www.cleiss.fr/docs/textes/987-09/t1.html)
5. Send the determined legislation and effective period to the relevant country Guide for registration, contribution base and rates. National law still decides whether/how each income stream is chargeable. [R2](https://www.cleiss.fr/docs/textes/883-04/t2.html), [R3](https://www.cleiss.fr/docs/textes/987-09/t1.html)

## Section 1 - Core principle: one applicable legislation within the coordination system

Article 11(1) selects a **single Member State's legislation** for persons within the Regulation. It does not promise that every worker pays a positive contribution, prohibit the voluntary-insurance exceptions in Article 14, or bind a third country outside the relevant agreement. Resolve overlapping demands through the institutions rather than telling the worker to ignore either demand. [R2](https://www.cleiss.fr/docs/textes/883-04/t2.html)

- **Included with special restrictions:** Article 3(3) expressly includes special non-contributory cash benefits meeting Article 70/Annex X. Article 70(3)-(4) disapplies the normal export rule and provides them only in the residence state under its legislation. They are not wholly excluded from 883. - **Excluded from Regulation:** social/medical assistance and the compensation benefits in Article 3(5). ### Four principles of coordination

1. **Single applicable legislation:** Article 11, subject to its scope and special rules. [R2](https://www.cleiss.fr/docs/textes/883-04/t2.html)
2. **Equal treatment:** Article 4, unless the Regulation provides otherwise. [R1](https://www.cleiss.fr/docs/textes/883-04/t1.html)
3. **Aggregation:** Article 6, to the extent necessary and subject to benefit-specific exceptions. [R1](https://www.cleiss.fr/docs/textes/883-04/t1.html)
4. **Exportability:** Article 7 normally removes residence restrictions on cash benefits within the covered states, subject to express exceptions such as Article 70. [R1](https://www.cleiss.fr/docs/textes/883-04/t1.html)

## Section 2 - Determination rules: lex loci laboris

- **General rule lex loci laboris** - The legislation of the member state where the person is employed or self-employed applies. This is the "lex loci laboris" (law of the place of work) principle.  _(Article 11(3)(a))_

**Determination rules table**  _(Art 11(3))_

| Situation | Applicable Legislation | Article |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Employed in one member state | State where employed | Art 11(3)(a) [R2](https://www.cleiss.fr/docs/textes/883-04/t2.html) |
| Self-employed in one member state | State where self-employed | Art 11(3)(a) [R2](https://www.cleiss.fr/docs/textes/883-04/t2.html) |
| Civil servant | State of the employing administration | Art 11(3)(b) [R2](https://www.cleiss.fr/docs/textes/883-04/t2.html) |
| Receiving unemployment benefits under Article 65 under residence-state law | Residence state | Art 11(3)(c); not a rule for every benefit recipient [R2](https://www.cleiss.fr/docs/textes/883-04/t2.html) |
| Called up for military/civil service | State requiring the service | Art 11(3)(d) [R2](https://www.cleiss.fr/docs/textes/883-04/t2.html) |
| No activity (economically inactive) | State of residence | Art 11(3)(e) [R2](https://www.cleiss.fr/docs/textes/883-04/t2.html) |

**Locate the physical work first.** Article 11(3) is subject to Articles 12-16. Civil servants, benefits treated as activity under Article 11(2), seafarers, flight/cabin crew, voluntary insurance and inactivity need their specific rules; this table does not exhaust those exceptions. [R2](https://www.cleiss.fr/docs/textes/883-04/t2.html)

### What "employed" means

- **Employed vs self-employed** - Article 1(a)-(b) refers to the social security legislation of the state in which the activity or equivalent situation exists. [R1](https://www.cleiss.fr/docs/textes/883-04/t1.html) There is no EU-wide definition of employment vs self-employment for SS purposes.

## Section 3 - Posted worker exception (A1 certificate)

- **Posted worker exception** - An employer may send (post) a worker to another member state while keeping that worker subject to the home country's social security legislation, provided conditions are met.  _(Article 12 of Regulation 883/2004)_

**Posted worker conditions**  _(Article 12 of Regulation 883/2004)_

| Condition | Requirement |
| --- | --- |
| Duration | Maximum 24 months (anticipated duration at outset) [R2](https://www.cleiss.fr/docs/textes/883-04/t2.html) |
| Replacement prohibition | Must not be sent to replace another posted person; not limited to a predecessor whose period expired [Official text](https://www.cleiss.fr/docs/textes/987-09/t2.html) |
| Prior insurance | For a person recruited with a view to posting, subject to sending-state legislation immediately before the beginning of employment (987 Article 14(1)) [Official text](https://www.cleiss.fr/docs/textes/987-09/t2.html) |
| Direct relationship | The worker must remain under the authority of the sending employer during the posting [Official text](https://www.cleiss.fr/docs/textes/987-09/t2.html) |
| Employer's substantial activity | The employer must normally carry out substantial activities in the sending state (not merely internal management) [Official text](https://www.cleiss.fr/docs/textes/987-09/t2.html) |

### Key limitations

- **24-month strict limit** - The 24-month limit is strict. If the posting is expected to exceed 24 months from the outset, Article 12 does NOT apply.  _(Article 12)_
- **Article 16 agreement** - If circumstances change and the posting extends beyond 24 months, an Article 16 agreement (exceptional agreement between competent institutions) can be requested.  _(Article 16)_
- **Prior insurance requirement:** immediately before the start of employment the person must already be subject to the sending state's legislation. Recruitment specifically for posting is permitted if that prior-coverage condition is met (987 Article 14(1)). The old claim that day-one recruitment can never qualify was wrong. The old typical one-month minimum is not used here as a universal legal condition; the enacted three-month rule does not apply until 2 November 2028. [R3](https://www.cleiss.fr/docs/textes/987-09/t1.html), [R4](https://www.cleiss.fr/docs/textes/883-04/am9.html)
- **Substantial business activity required:** 987 Article 14(2) requires substantial activities beyond internal management, assessed against the employer's actual characteristics. A letterbox company cannot establish eligibility merely through incorporation. [R3](https://www.cleiss.fr/docs/textes/987-09/t1.html)
- **Self-employed temporary activity:** 883 Article 12(2) requires normally pursuing self-employment in the sending state and similar activity abroad, with anticipated duration no more than 24 months. Under 987 Article 14(3)-(4), retain the conditions enabling return and examine the actual nature of the activity. [R2](https://www.cleiss.fr/docs/textes/883-04/t2.html), [R3](https://www.cleiss.fr/docs/textes/987-09/t1.html)

The table and duration/continuing-relationship conditions derive from 883 Article 12 and 987 Article 14(1)-(4). Article 16 is a discretionary agreement in the person's interest, not an automatic extension or a rule limited to changed circumstances. Request it, preferably in advance, from the authority of the state whose legislation is sought (987 Article 18). [R2](https://www.cleiss.fr/docs/textes/883-04/t2.html), [R3](https://www.cleiss.fr/docs/textes/987-09/t1.html)

### A1 certificate for posted workers

- **A1 certificate (Portable Document A1)** - The A1 certificate (Portable Document A1) is the proof that a posted worker remains subject to the sending state's legislation. It is issued by the sending state's competent institution.
- **A1 certificate binding effect:** 987 Article 5(1) requires other institutions to accept the document until withdrawn or declared invalid by the issuing state. Doubts trigger review/dialogue, not an employer's unilateral disregard. The Court's own C-359/16 summary recalls predecessor E101 case law, expressly citing A-Rosa Flussschiff, C-620/15. It explains a narrowly conditioned judicial fraud exception: evidence must be referred to the issuing institution, which fails to review within a reasonable time, and the affected parties must be able to rebut the evidence before the host court. Do not describe binding effect as absolute protection for fraud. [R3](https://www.cleiss.fr/docs/textes/987-09/t1.html), [R7](https://curia.europa.eu/jcms/upload/docs/application/pdf/2018-02/cp180010en.pdf)
- **Missing document:** an A1 evidences the applicable legislation; absence alone does not automatically make host-state law substantively applicable or remove rights of challenge. Apply for it promptly, preferably before work. Provisional coverage, national evidence/inspection requirements and penalties may still matter. [R3](https://www.cleiss.fr/docs/textes/987-09/t1.html), [R7](https://curia.europa.eu/jcms/upload/docs/application/pdf/2018-02/cp180010en.pdf)

## Section 4 - Multi-state workers (Article 13)

A person who **normally** pursues activity in two or more member states simultaneously follows different determination rules.

### Employed persons in two or more states (Article 13(1))

**Employed persons multi-state determination table**  _(Article 13(1))_

| Test | Result |
| --- | --- |
| Pursues a **substantial part**, assessed under Article 14(8), in residence state | Residence state's legislation applies [R2](https://www.cleiss.fr/docs/textes/883-04/t2.html) |
| Does NOT pursue substantial part in residence state, employed by ONE employer | Employer's registered office state applies [Official text](https://www.cleiss.fr/docs/textes/883-04/t2.html) |
| Does NOT pursue substantial part in residence state, employed by TWO+ employers in same state | That state's legislation applies [Official text](https://www.cleiss.fr/docs/textes/883-04/t2.html) |
| Does NOT pursue substantial part in residence state, employed by TWO+ employers whose offices are in two states, one being residence | Employer state (other than residence) applies [Official text](https://www.cleiss.fr/docs/textes/883-04/t2.html) |
| Does NOT pursue substantial part in residence state, employed by TWO+ employers, at least two with offices in different states other than residence | Residence state's legislation applies [Official text](https://www.cleiss.fr/docs/textes/883-04/t2.html) |

### The 25% substantial activity test

- **Substantial part definition** - "Substantial part" means a quantitatively substantial part of all activities - measured by: 1. Working time (hours/days worked in the state as a share of total working time), AND/OR 2. Remuneration (pay attributable to work in the state as a share of total pay)  _(Article 14(8) of Implementing Regulation 987/2009)_
- **25% indicator:** Article 14(8) says a share below 25% is an **indicator within an overall assessment**, not an automatic veto whenever either one of time or remuneration is below 25%. For employees use time and/or remuneration; for the self-employed use turnover, working time, services and/or income. Under Article 14(10), assess the projected following 12 calendar months. Marginal activities are disregarded under Article 14(5b). [R3](https://www.cleiss.fr/docs/textes/987-09/t1.html)
- **CJEU C-743/23:** judgment **11 December 2025**, not September. The Court's official December 2025 digest, pages 22-24, explains that an employee's third-country activity must also enter total working time when assessing the residence-state share in the EU/Swiss multi-state situation. This is not a holding that client location or every non-arithmetic fact is irrelevant for all coordination questions. Nor does counting foreign work bind that foreign country's insurance system. The digest notes the special residence-state result for a third-country employer; 987 Article 14(11) covers a worker employed in two or more Member States for an employer outside the Union even without substantial residence-state activity. [R3](https://www.cleiss.fr/docs/textes/987-09/t1.html), [R8](https://curia.europa.eu/site/upload/docs/application/pdf/2026-01/monthly_case-law_digest_en-_december_2025.pdf)
- **Identify the real employer:** AFMB, C-610/18 (16 July 2020), examines actual authority, actual wage costs and actual dismissal power, not merely the contractual employer's name. The ordinary employer-office table above must be read with this and the third-country-employer exception. [R8](https://curia.europa.eu/site/upload/docs/application/pdf/2026-01/monthly_case-law_digest_en-_december_2025.pdf)

### Determination procedure (Article 16 of Reg 987/2009)

1. The worker informs the designated institution of the residence state of multi-state activity. [R3](https://www.cleiss.fr/docs/textes/987-09/t1.html)
2. That institution determines applicable law without delay, initially provisionally, and informs each state where activity is pursued. [R3](https://www.cleiss.fr/docs/textes/987-09/t1.html)
3. The **two-month period runs after those institutions are informed**, not a two-month allowance to send the notification. The determination becomes definitive unless an institution objects or cannot yet accept it, or it is already settled by agreement. [R3](https://www.cleiss.fr/docs/textes/987-09/t1.html)
4. A disagreement calls for common agreement and Article 6 provisional rules. Under Article 6(3), referral to the Administrative Commission is possible no earlier than one month after the disagreement; its six-month conciliation period runs after referral. It is not a universal six-month wait before referral. [R3](https://www.cleiss.fr/docs/textes/987-09/t1.html)
5. The competent institution informs the person. Article 6(4)-(5) and Article 73 govern retroactive competence and adjustment of provisional contributions/benefits. Do not invent a universal refund completion time. [R3](https://www.cleiss.fr/docs/textes/987-09/t1.html)

### Habitual cross-border telework: Article 16 derogations

The ordinary Article 13 rules still apply without a granted derogation. The signed Framework Agreement effective from 1 July 2023 facilitates individual Article 16 agreements, rather than automatically changing the 25% indicator. Both residence and employer states must be signatories for the relevant period. It covers employees of one or more employers all based in one other signatory state, with habitual telework in the residence state and work in the employer state. It excludes habitual non-telework activity in the residence state, habitual activity in another state and self-employment. [R10](https://socialsecurity.belgium.be/en/internationally-active/cross-border-telework-eu-eea-and-switzerland)

| Threshold and conditions | Effect | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Residence-state telework would ordinarily bring residence-state law into play; telework is **less than 50%** of total time, usually the 25% to below 50% band | On a consensual employee/employer request accepted under the agreement, employer-state law can apply | 987/2009 Article 14(8) [R3](https://www.cleiss.fr/docs/textes/987-09/t2.html); Framework Articles 2-3; official explanatory memorandum [R10](https://socialsecurity.belgium.be/en/internationally-active/cross-border-telework-eu-eea-and-switzerland) |
| Request to the competent institution of the employer state, for no more than **3 years** at a time | A1 issued there; a fresh request can extend coverage | Framework Article 4(1),(4)-(6) [R10](https://socialsecurity.belgium.be/en/internationally-active/cross-border-telework-eu-eea-and-switzerland) |
| Past period no more than **3 months**, with contributions paid or coverage in employer state; never before entry into force for both states | Limited retrospective framework request; the former 12-month concession required submission by **30 June 2024** and has expired | Framework Article 4(2)-(3) [R10](https://socialsecurity.belgium.be/en/internationally-active/cross-border-telework-eu-eea-and-switzerland) |
| Outside framework scope, or 50% or more telework | Normal rules apply unless a separate individual Article 16 agreement is obtained | Framework Article 6(1); 883 Article 16 [R2](https://www.cleiss.fr/docs/textes/883-04/t2.html), [R10](https://socialsecurity.belgium.be/en/internationally-active/cross-border-telework-eu-eea-and-switzerland) |

The Belgian depository's current list must be checked for the dates and designated institution: it includes Estonia from 1 February 2026; the UK is expressly listed as not signing. Framework authorities are not always ordinary A1 issuers: for example Austria lists the Dachverband. This derogation does not apply to a wholly remote worker in the residence state merely because the employer is abroad. [R10](https://socialsecurity.belgium.be/en/internationally-active/cross-border-telework-eu-eea-and-switzerland)

## Section 5 - Self-employed across borders (Article 13(2))

**Self-employed across borders determination table**  _(Article 13(2))_

| Test | Result |
| --- | --- |
| Pursues substantial part of self-employed activity in residence state, overall assessment | Residence state's legislation applies [Official text](https://www.cleiss.fr/docs/textes/883-04/t2.html) |
| Does NOT pursue substantial part in residence state | State where the **centre of interest** of activities is situated [Official text](https://www.cleiss.fr/docs/textes/883-04/t2.html) |

### Centre of interest determination

- **Centre of interest factors** - For self-employed persons without a substantial part in their residence state, the "centre of interest" is determined by considering: Where the fixed and permanent premises for the activity are located; The habitual nature or duration of activities pursued; The number of services rendered; The intention of the person as revealed by all circumstances.

**Practical example:** A freelance consultant resident in Belgium who works 60% of the time at clients in the Netherlands and 40% in Belgium → the physical residence-state time is 40%, above the 25% indicator; subject to the full assessment, Belgium's legislation applies. [R2](https://www.cleiss.fr/docs/textes/883-04/t2.html), [R3](https://www.cleiss.fr/docs/textes/987-09/t1.html)

**Practical example:** A freelance developer resident in Portugal who works 90% for clients accessed remotely from Portugal but the registered business is in Estonia → physical work in Portugal is the relevant activity, not the clients' or business register's location. If this is work only in Portugal, Article 11 applies; if normally in several states, apply Article 13(2) and the overall residence-state test. [R2](https://www.cleiss.fr/docs/textes/883-04/t2.html), [R3](https://www.cleiss.fr/docs/textes/987-09/t1.html)

## Section 6 - Simultaneous employment and self-employment (Article 13(3))

- **Simultaneous employment/self-employment rule** - A person who normally pursues employment in one state AND self-employment in another state is subject to the legislation of the state where the employed activity is pursued.  _(Article 13(3))_

**Simultaneous employment/self-employment table**  _(Article 13(3))_

| Combination | Applicable Legislation |
| --- | --- |
| Employed in state A + self-employed in state B | State A (employment state) [Official text](https://www.cleiss.fr/docs/textes/883-04/t2.html) |
| Employed in states A and B + self-employed in state C | Determined per Article 13(1) rules for multi-state employees [R2](https://www.cleiss.fr/docs/textes/883-04/t2.html) |

- **Key consequence and exception** - **Key consequence:** A person employed in Germany who also freelances for clients in France pays ALL social security (including on the freelance income) in Germany. This assumes that the relevant work is actually in different states, falls within coordination and has been correctly classified; client location alone is insufficient. The national law of the selected state determines contribution liability and exemptions. [R2](https://www.cleiss.fr/docs/textes/883-04/t2.html), [R3](https://www.cleiss.fr/docs/textes/987-09/t1.html) **Exception:** If the person pursues employed activity in two or more states AND self-employment in one or more states, the multi-state employee rules of Article 13(1) determine the applicable legislation first, and then that legislation covers ALL activities.  _(Article 13(1))_

### What the A1 certificate is

- **Portable Document A1** - The Portable Document A1 (PD A1) is an official certificate confirming which member state's social security legislation applies to a person. It evidences the applicable legislation for its stated facts, conditions and period, subject to the review rules. [R3](https://www.cleiss.fr/docs/textes/987-09/t1.html)

### When you need an A1 certificate

**When A1 is needed table**

| Situation | A1 needed? [R3](https://www.cleiss.fr/docs/textes/987-09/t1.html) |
| --- | --- |
| Posted to another member state for up to 24 months | Notify and obtain A1 if Article 12 conditions are met; notify in advance whenever possible under current Article 15 [R3](https://www.cleiss.fr/docs/textes/987-09/t1.html) |
| Normally working in two or more member states | Notify residence-state institution under Article 16; obtain A1 from the institution of the applicable state [R3](https://www.cleiss.fr/docs/textes/987-09/t1.html) |
| Self-employed temporarily performing similar activity in another state | Apply Article 12(2), notify and obtain A1 [R3](https://www.cleiss.fr/docs/textes/987-09/t1.html) |
| Business trip (< 2 days, no work performed) | No universal current two-day exemption. Establish whether this is covered professional activity and the applicable national evidence rules [R3](https://www.cleiss.fr/docs/textes/987-09/t1.html) |
| Attending a conference or training | Do not assume a blanket exemption; establish the facts and request the institution's instructions [Official text](https://www.cleiss.fr/docs/textes/987-09/t2.html) |

Current legal basis: 987 Articles 15, 16 and 19. Article 15's newly enacted business-trip and three-consecutive-working-day exemptions do **not** apply until 2 November 2028 (Article 97). A1 evidence and any separate posted-worker labour-law declaration are different obligations. [R3](https://www.cleiss.fr/docs/textes/987-09/t1.html), [R4](https://www.cleiss.fr/docs/textes/883-04/am9.html)

### How to obtain an A1 certificate

1. Apply to the institution competent for the determined law; habitual multi-state cases start with notification to the residence-state institution. Provide identity, employer and real activity details, work countries, anticipated period, time/pay shares and relevant prior-coverage/replacement evidence. [R3](https://www.cleiss.fr/docs/textes/987-09/t1.html)
2. The institution checks the facts and provides a standardized attestation under current Article 19(2)-(3), stating conditions and duration where appropriate. Report changed facts. [R3](https://www.cleiss.fr/docs/textes/987-09/t1.html), [R4](https://www.cleiss.fr/docs/textes/883-04/am9.html)
3. Apply before work whenever possible. The applicable current Article 15 wording allows notification in advance whenever possible; do not recast that as a universally constitutive pre-departure certificate deadline. Retrospective coverage certification must be decided by the institution on the actual applicable law and facts. [R3](https://www.cleiss.fr/docs/textes/987-09/t1.html)
4. Use the actual certificate period. The old claims of a typical **1-8 week** processing time and a universal **24-month** renewable multi-state certificate are not verified service standards. The new Article 14(10) maximum period applies only from 2 November 2028. [R4](https://www.cleiss.fr/docs/textes/883-04/am9.html)

### Consequences of not having an A1

- **Consequences of missing A1:** a host-state demand or inspection can require evidence and a response, but the worker/employer retains the applicable-law and appeal procedures. National penalties and carrying/production requirements must be checked locally; no EU-wide penalty or **12-24 month** refund timetable is established here. The former France-specific fine assertion is retained only as an unconfirmed historical assertion below. [R3](https://www.cleiss.fr/docs/textes/987-09/t1.html)

### Practical guidance for UK-EU cross-border workers

- A UK national posted from the UK to France: if outside WA and within TCA scope and every SSC.11/SSCI.13 condition is met, request PDA1 from HMRC; UK coverage can continue for no more than 24 months. ## Worked cases

The following decisions assume personal/material scope and the stated facts are evidenced. A work/client percentage is not enough without knowing physical activity.

### Scenario A - Remote worker for foreign employer

**Facts:** Maria lives in Portugal. She is employed full-time by a German company (GmbH). She works 100% remotely from her home in Lisbon. She never travels to Germany.

**Analysis:**
- Maria pursues activity in ONE state only (Portugal - where work is physically performed)
- Article 11(3)(a) applies: legislation of the state where employed = Portugal
- Maria is subject to Portuguese social security
- German employer must meet Portuguese employer obligations under 987 Article 21; an agreement for the worker to handle payment does not remove the employer's underlying obligations [R3](https://www.cleiss.fr/docs/textes/987-09/t1.html)

**Result:** Portugal, on these facts. The German employer follows the Portuguese ISS registration/contribution rules; this Guide does not calculate the amounts. [R2](https://www.cleiss.fr/docs/textes/883-04/t2.html), [R3](https://www.cleiss.fr/docs/textes/987-09/t1.html), [R17](https://www.cleiss.fr/docs/ol/portugal.html)

### Scenario B - Posted worker

**Facts:** Johann works for a Munich-based engineering firm. He is sent to oversee a construction project in Austria for 18 months. He will return to Munich afterwards.

**Analysis:**
- The 18-month plan fits the 24-month duration condition, but duration and an intended return do not alone establish Article 12 eligibility.
- Johann must have been subject to German legislation immediately before the relevant employment; obtain evidence rather than treating this unstated fact as established.
- Confirm substantial sending-state employer activity, continuing direct relationship and no prohibited replacement. These were assumed checkmarks in the old analysis, not facts supplied by the scenario. [R2](https://www.cleiss.fr/docs/textes/883-04/t2.html), [R3](https://www.cleiss.fr/docs/textes/987-09/t1.html)

**Result:** Germany continues only if the conditions are proved; otherwise examine Austrian law and any agreed Article 16 exception. For a German posting, the employer requests A1 through the competent Krankenkasse/pension/ABV route, rather than automatically DVKA. Johann's Austrian exemption depends on the applicable determination. [R2](https://www.cleiss.fr/docs/textes/883-04/t2.html), [R3](https://www.cleiss.fr/docs/textes/987-09/t1.html), [R11](https://www.dvka.de/de/versicherte/faq/)

### Scenario C - Multi-country freelancer

**Facts:** Sophie is a freelance UX designer resident in Belgium. She works 30% of her time for clients in Belgium, 40% in the Netherlands, and 30% in France.

**Analysis:**
- Article 13(2) for self-employed in multiple states
- Does Sophie physically perform that 30% in Belgium, 40% in the Netherlands and 30% in France? If these are only client locations, ask for the actual work pattern. If they are physical working-time shares, the 30% residence share supports substantial activity, together with the other self-employment criteria and 12-month projection. [R3](https://www.cleiss.fr/docs/textes/987-09/t1.html)
- Belgium's legislation applies to ALL of Sophie's self-employed activity

**Result:** Belgium if the full physical-activity assessment supports that finding; Sophie uses the Belgian self-employed route (RSVZ/INASTI). Article 13(5) treats all covered activity/income as in the selected state; it does not itself set a worldwide contribution base or eliminate national exemptions. No parallel NL/FR compulsory charge for the coordinated activity. [R2](https://www.cleiss.fr/docs/textes/883-04/t2.html), [R3](https://www.cleiss.fr/docs/textes/987-09/t1.html), [R14](https://www.cleiss.fr/docs/ol/belgique.html)

### Scenario D - Employee plus freelancer in different countries

**Facts:** Marco is employed in Italy (full-time software engineer at an Italian company). He also freelances as a consultant for German clients, performing this work from his home in Italy.

**Analysis:**
- Article 13(3) would require relevant activity in different states; the facts here are single-state under Article 11
- Employed activity in Italy → Italy's legislation applies to ALL activity
- BUT: the freelance work is ALSO performed in Italy (from home) → this is actually a single-state case
- Italy's legislation applies regardless

**Result:** Italy under Article 11 on these single-state work facts, not Article 13(3). The relevant Italian scheme(s) and national charging rules decide whether and how both income streams bear contributions; German clients alone do not create German social insurance. [R1](https://www.cleiss.fr/docs/textes/883-04/t1.html), [R2](https://www.cleiss.fr/docs/textes/883-04/t2.html), [R15](https://www.cleiss.fr/docs/ol/italie.html)

### Scenario E - UK employee working partly in EU post-Brexit

**Facts:** James is a UK resident employed by a UK company. He works 3 days/week from London (60%) and 2 days/week from the company's Paris office (40%).

**Analysis:**
- TCA Protocol Art SSC.12 (multi-state worker)
- James is resident in UK. The 3 London days out of 5 give 60%, versus 40% in Paris. This supports substantial residence-state work in the SSCI.13 overall assessment; verify legal residence, scope and no WA/other special case. [R9](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/608ae0c0d3bf7f0136332887/TS_8.2021_UK_EU_EAEC_Trade_and_Cooperation_Agreement.pdf)
- UK legislation applies to all activity

**Result:** UK legislation on the stated assumptions. James's NIC charge follows UK law; obtain HMRC PDA1 confirming coverage for the period before treating the coordinated earnings as outside French compulsory contributions. [R9](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/608ae0c0d3bf7f0136332887/TS_8.2021_UK_EU_EAEC_Trade_and_Cooperation_Agreement.pdf)

### Recomputing the work-share checks

The checks reproduce the time shares used in the cases; they do not turn the 25% indicator into an automatic legal test.

```oa-check
name: Physical multi-country freelancer shares
inputs:
  residence_time: 30
  other_state_time: 40
  third_state_time: 30
steps:
  total_time: residence_time + other_state_time + third_state_time
  residence_share: residence_time / total_time * 100
  other_state_share: other_state_time / total_time * 100
expected:
  total_time: 100
  residence_share: 30
  other_state_share: 40
```

```oa-check
name: UK and France weekly work shares
inputs:
  uk_days: 3
  france_days: 2
steps:
  total_days: uk_days + france_days
  uk_share: uk_days / total_days * 100
  france_share: france_days / total_days * 100
expected:
  total_days: 5
  uk_share: 60
  france_share: 40
```

## When to refuse or refer

- Refer unresolved personal scope, disputed residence/status/employer facts, fraud, conflicting institutional demands or an unsupported derogation to the competent institutions and a coordination specialist. Do not issue a definitive country decision from client addresses or an unverified posting checklist.
- Do not compute contribution amounts: use the selected country's current Guide and its national base/rates/exemptions.
- Keep immigration, income tax, labour-law posting declarations and healthcare entitlements separate from the applicable social security determination.

## PROHIBITIONS

1. Do not instruct a person within the coordination system to pay two compulsory systems as the substantive result, or promise universal freedom from third-country/voluntary insurance charges. [R2](https://www.cleiss.fr/docs/textes/883-04/t2.html)
2. Do not assume residence automatically decides liability: identify actual work and special rules. [R2](https://www.cleiss.fr/docs/textes/883-04/t2.html)
3. Do not dismiss A1 as optional evidence, invent a current short-trip exemption or treat its absence as automatic host-state liability. [R3](https://www.cleiss.fr/docs/textes/987-09/t1.html), [R4](https://www.cleiss.fr/docs/textes/883-04/am9.html)
4. Do not extend 883 directly to the US, Canada, Australia or another third country; check bilateral/national rules and scope. [R5](https://www.cleiss.fr/reglements/circulaires/ci201120.pdf), [R6](https://www.cleiss.fr/reglements/883_pays.html), [R8](https://curia.europa.eu/site/upload/docs/application/pdf/2026-01/monthly_case-law_digest_en-_december_2025.pdf)
5. Do not compute contribution amounts in this coordination Guide. [R2](https://www.cleiss.fr/docs/textes/883-04/t2.html), [R3](https://www.cleiss.fr/docs/textes/987-09/t1.html)
6. Do not create artificial posting/letterbox arrangements to avoid contributions; establish the genuine activity and employer relationship. [R2](https://www.cleiss.fr/docs/textes/883-04/t2.html), [R3](https://www.cleiss.fr/docs/textes/987-09/t1.html), [R8](https://curia.europa.eu/site/upload/docs/application/pdf/2026-01/monthly_case-law_digest_en-_december_2025.pdf)
