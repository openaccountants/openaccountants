# US Virgin Islands: bona fide residence, tax year 2026

> **Source-cited draft.** Built from 26 U.S.C. 937, IRS Pub. 570 (2025), chapter 1, the Instructions for Form 8898 (Rev. October 2024) and Form 8689 (2026), read on 2026-11-13. No accountant has reviewed it. All outputs must be reviewed by a qualified tax professional before filing.

**Answer first.** A person is a bona fide resident of the USVI for 2026 only if all three tests are met: the **presence test**, **no tax home outside the USVI**, and **no closer connection** to the United States or a foreign country than to the USVI. A US citizen or resident alien meets the presence test by any one of five routes, the best known being **183 days** in the USVI in the year, or **549 days** over 2024 to 2026 with **at least 60 days** in each year. Passing the presence test alone does not make someone a resident. When residence starts or ends and **worldwide gross income is over USD 75,000**, Form 8898 is due by the return's due date; failing to file costs **USD 1,000**.

## Scope and who this is for

In scope: US citizens and resident aliens who live in, move to, or leave the USVI, for tax year 2026; the three tests; day counting; the year-of-move exceptions; Form 8898.

## Ask the client first

1. **Status:** US citizen, resident alien, or nonresident alien of the United States; military member or military spouse?
2. **A day-by-day travel log for 2024, 2025 and 2026:** days in the USVI, in the US, and elsewhere, with medical, disaster or evacuation days marked.
3. **US earned income in 2026**, and any US home, spouse or minor children living in the US.
4. **Work:** where the main place of business, employment or post of duty is.
5. **Ties:** home, family, possessions, bank accounts, driver's licence, voter registration, social, religious and business connections, and the address on official documents.
6. **Moves:** did residence start or end in 2026, and the expected facts for the three years before and after?
7. **Worldwide gross income for 2026** (the client's own, not the spouse's).

### Step 1: the presence test (any one route)

For a US citizen or resident alien ([Pub. 570, Presence Test](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/p570.pdf)):

1. At least 183 days in the USVI during 2026.
2. At least 549 days in the USVI during 2024, 2025 and 2026 together, with at least 60 days in each year.
3. No more than 90 days in the United States during 2026.
4. No more than USD 3,000 of US-source earned income in 2026, and more days in the USVI than in the United States.
5. No significant connection to the United States during 2026 (no permanent home there, no US-registered voter status, no spouse or minor child living there, as Pub. 570 defines it).

### Step 2: count the days

6. A day partly in the US and partly in the USVI counts as a USVI day. Days are also treated as USVI days when the person is outside it: for qualifying medical treatment; during a 14-day window around a declared major disaster or a mandatory evacuation; as an elected or government official in the US; and up to 30 days of business or personal travel outside both the USVI and the US, but only if USVI days exceed US days, and never towards the 60-day minimum in route 2. Source: [Pub. 570 (2025), chapter 1](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/p570.pdf).
7. Do not treat every day abroad as a USVI day, and do not drop every transit day automatically: apply the listed rules only.

### Step 3: tax home and closer connection

8. **Tax home:** the regular or main place of business, employment or post of duty; if none, the regular place of abode. It must not be outside the USVI at any time in 2026. Source: [26 U.S.C. 937(a)(2)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title26/html/USCODE-2024-title26-subtitleA-chap1-subchapN-partIII-subpartD-sec937.htm).
9. **Closer connection:** weigh the overall ties (home, family, possessions, social, cultural, religious and political ties, business, documents such as the driver's licence and voter registration). There must be no closer connection to the US or a foreign country than to the USVI. The "no significant connection" presence route is a different, defined test and does not replace this one.

### Step 4: year of the move

10. **Moving to the USVI in 2026:** the tax home and closer connection tests are treated as met for the whole year if the person was not a bona fide resident in any of 2023 to 2025, has no outside tax home or closer connection during any part of the last 183 days of 2026, and is a bona fide resident for each of 2027 to 2029. The presence test is not waived.
11. **Moving away in 2026:** the same treatment if the person was a bona fide resident for each of 2023 to 2025, has no outside tax home or closer connection during any part of the first 183 days of 2026, and is not a bona fide resident in any of 2027 to 2029.
12. Future-year facts matter: record the expected facts and revisit if they change.

### Step 5: Form 8898

13. File Form 8898 for 2026 if worldwide gross income is over USD 75,000 and either: the person takes the position that USVI residence began in 2026 after a year filed as a US citizen or resident but not as a bona fide USVI resident; or a US citizen or resident takes the position that USVI residence ended in 2026 after a year as a bona fide resident. Other territory-to-territory moves need separate review.
14. Gross income is before deductions and excludes the spouse's income. Each spouse who meets the conditions files a separate form. File it separately from the income tax return (paper or the IRS online form) by the due date of the return including extensions.

## Boundary and exception table

| Situation | Treatment |
| --- | --- |
| 183 USVI days but the main job is in Miami | Presence passes, tax home fails: not a bona fide resident |
| 180, 190 and 179 USVI days in 2024 to 2026 | Route 2 passes (549 total, each year at least 60) |
| 245, 245 and 59 USVI days | Route 2 fails (59 is under 60), though the total is 549; try the other routes |
| Worldwide gross income exactly USD 75,000 | No Form 8898: the test is "more than" |
| Moved to St. Croix in July 2026, plans to stay | Year-of-move exception may apply; presence still must be met for 2026 |
| Spouse earns over USD 75,000, client earns less | Spouse's income is not counted for the client's Form 8898 |
| Medical treatment on the mainland for 3 weeks | Those days can count as USVI days if the treatment qualifies |
| Nonresident alien of the United States | Different presence procedure; refer |

### Defaults when a fact is missing

| Missing fact | Default, and flag it |
| --- | --- |
| Travel log | No conclusion; reconstruct from passports, boarding passes and card records |
| Expected facts for 2027 to 2029 (year of move) | Do not rely on the exception; note the risk |
| Worldwide gross income | Assume Form 8898 is due if residence changed, and check |

### Red flags to raise with the client

| Flag | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| Spouse and children live in the US | Closer connection to the US is likely; route 5 also fails |
| A US home kept and used | Closer connection risk |
| A claimed move with a large gain or incentive income in the same year | Section 937(b) source rules and IRS scrutiny |
| Form 8898 missed in an earlier move year | USD 1,000 penalty for each failure |

## Worked cases

**Case 1, the three-year presence route.** Record A: 180, 190 and 179 USVI days for 2024, 2025 and 2026. Record B: 245, 245 and 59. Both total 549. A passes route 2 because every year has at least 60 days. B fails route 2 because 2026 has only 59; another route must be found for B (for example 2026 US days of 90 or less). Neither record proves the tax home or closer connection tests.

```oa-check
name: The 549-day route with the 60-day minimum (2026)
inputs:
  a_2024: 180
  a_2025: 190
  a_2026: 179
  b_2024: 245
  b_2025: 245
  b_2026: 59
steps:
  - a_total: a_2024 + a_2025 + a_2026
  - a_lowest: min(a_2024, a_2025, a_2026)
  - b_total: b_2024 + b_2025 + b_2026
  - b_lowest: min(b_2024, b_2025, b_2026)
  - b_shortfall_to_60: max(60 - b_lowest, 0)
expected:
  a_total: 549
  a_lowest: 179
  b_total: 549
  b_lowest: 59
  b_shortfall_to_60: 1
```

**Case 2, a decision: do travel days get the client to 183?** In 2026 a client spent 170 days in the USVI, 120 days in the US and 20 days on holiday in Europe (the rest at sea or not recorded, none counted). The 20 holiday days count as USVI days because USVI days (170) exceed US days (120), and 20 is within the 30-day limit: 190 days, so route 1 passes. If the client had spent 175 days in the US, the travel days would not count and route 1 would fail at 170. Recommend checking the US day count before relying on the travel-day rule, then testing the tax home and closer connection.

```oa-check
name: Travel days and the 183-day route (2026)
inputs:
  usvi_days: 170
  us_days: 120
  travel_days: 20
steps:
  - counted_travel: min(travel_days, 30)
  - total_if_rule_applies: usvi_days + counted_travel
  - usvi_lead_over_us: usvi_days - us_days
  - margin_over_183: total_if_rule_applies - 183
expected:
  counted_travel: 20
  total_if_rule_applies: 190
  usvi_lead_over_us: 50
  margin_over_183: 7
```

## Filing and payment

Residence decides the return: see `vi-individual-income-tax-filing`. Form 8898 for 2026, if due, is filed by the due date of the 2026 income tax return including extensions (15 April 2027 without extension), separately from the return. Keep the travel log, work-location evidence, housing and family facts, contracts and prior returns.

## Completion checklist

- All three tests run separately; presence route named.
- Days counted with the Pub. 570 rules, and the travel-day rule used only if USVI days exceed US days.
- Tax home and closer connection supported by documents.
- Year-of-move facts for the 3 years before and after recorded where relied on.
- Form 8898 checked against USD 75,000 of the client's own worldwide gross income.
