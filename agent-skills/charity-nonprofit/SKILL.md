# Charity and nonprofit accounting and tax v1.0

## Scope

A cross-country method for an accountant working with a charity, nonprofit, foundation, NGO, religious organisation or social enterprise in 2026. Establish the entity's actual status before applying an exemption. Incorporation, charity regulation, income-tax exemption, donor deductibility and VAT/GST relief are separate decisions.

Use `corporate-income-tax-workflow-base` and the current country jobs for a filing computation. This file is not fundraising regulation, donor-management software, or governance advice beyond the records needed for tax and accounting. It retains the former country's questions where the current source has not been established; it does not present their old figures as 2026 law.

## Ask the client first

- Where is the entity established, registered and active? What is its legal form, reporting period and accounting framework?
- Obtain its constitution, regulator registration, tax-recognition letter, donor-relief endorsement, elections, prior returns and any revocation or restrictions.
- Which activities are charitable, trading, fundraising, investing, property development or political/advocacy activities? Who benefits and where do the profits go?
- Obtain donor and grant agreements, appeal wording, restricted and endowment records, board designations, return conditions and spending commitments.
- List related parties, substantial donors, trustees/managers, sponsor arrangements, connected businesses, loans and grants to others.
- What are its taxable supplies, exempt supplies, non-business receipts, imported services, property use and input VAT/GST allocation?
- Which countries are donors and beneficiaries in? Obtain actual donor eligibility and intermediary control evidence rather than assuming domestic recognition is portable.

## The method, step by step

1. Identify the entity and each separate tax status. Record the issuing authority, statutory basis, recognition period and conditions. An organisation may be nonprofit without qualifying as a charity, and a recognised charity need not have every donor-relief permission.
2. Classify each receipt: unrestricted gift, restricted gift, conditional grant, service contract, membership, sponsorship, trading proceeds, investment return, capital receipt or money held for another. Determine recognition from the actual framework and agreement. A restriction on use is not automatically a liability, and cash receipt is not always the recognition date.
3. Map funds and reconcile opening balances, receipts, expenditure, transfers, investment results and closing balances. Preserve donor restrictions and endowment capital conditions. A board decision does not extinguish an external legal restriction.
4. Compute exemptions and taxable activity separately. Establish that the required use of income and assets is met. Do not assume that spending taxable trading profits on charity after the event always gives exemption.
5. Determine donor relief separately from the charity's exemption. Verify the donor, eligible recipient, qualifying payment, benefit limits, declaration/receipt and sufficient tax where relevant. Do not issue a deductible-donation receipt merely because the entity is tax exempt.
6. Review related-party transactions, private benefits, self-dealing and grants. Obtain conflict and approval records and determine the country's legal restrictions, correction duties and return disclosures. A fair market price is not proof that a prohibited transaction is allowed.
7. Establish VAT/GST treatment supply by supply. Separate business, non-business, exempt and taxable activity, registration, specific purchase reliefs and input recovery. Charity status is not a worldwide VAT exemption.
8. Prepare the correct entity, trust, information, taxable-trading and payroll returns, donor reports and statutory accounts. Test current thresholds, filing dates, exemptions from filing and consolidated accounts under the actual jurisdiction and year.
9. Deliver the fund reconciliation, tax computations, grant/donor evidence, unresolved conditions and a filing checklist to the credentialed nonprofit-sector reviewer. Do not certify a relief or submit a return while a material eligibility condition remains unresolved.

## United Kingdom

### Recognition and trading

Different legal forms include a CIO, charitable company, trust and unincorporated association. Do not use one form's accounting or registration rules for another. For tax recognition HMRC describes separate charitable-purpose, jurisdiction, registration and management conditions. Recognition by HMRC is required before operating Gift Aid or claiming the relevant tax exemptions. Current guidance says a non-UK charitable organisation cannot apply for this relief: the old generic foreign-charity equivalence route must not be used as current UK advice. [HMRC chapter 2](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/charities-detailed-guidance-notes/chapter-2-applications-for-recognition-as-a-charity-for-tax-purposes).

Establish primary-purpose and ancillary trading, trading mainly carried out by beneficiaries, and qualifying small-scale non-primary-purpose trading separately. Use the current statutory small-trading conditions, not the former unsupported reference to an extra-statutory concession as the entire rule. A trading subsidiary is a separate entity with its own tax, VAT, donation and timing requirements; it does not inherit all the charity's reliefs. [HMRC charities and trading](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/charities-and-trading).

Exemption for income or gains generally depends on application to charitable purposes and the particular relief. Property development, non-charitable expenditure, purchases and requests from HMRC can still create tax or filing obligations. CASCs have different reliefs. Do not turn the broad overview into a claim that every receipt, expense or property transaction is tax free. [HMRC tax reliefs for charities](https://www.gov.uk/charities-and-tax/tax-reliefs).

### Gift Aid worked case

Ordinary Gift Aid can add **25p for every £1** of a qualifying individual donation. The recipient must be recognised for the relevant tax purpose, the donor must supply a valid declaration and have sufficient UK Income Tax or Capital Gains Tax to cover the claim. Separately test whether the payment is eligible and whether benefits or other restrictions affect it. This is not an automatic top-up on company donations, trading receipts or every payment described as fundraising. [HMRC Gift Aid overview](https://www.gov.uk/claim-gift-aid/overview).

Fabricated pounds. Assume all these conditions and the relevant donor-benefit rules are satisfied. A qualifying individual gives 800. The charity's Gift Aid claim is 200 and the grossed-up donation is 1,000. If that donor's qualifying tax available to cover all their Gift Aid claims is only 150, the shortfall is 50; the ordinary rule does not make that shortfall disappear. This case isolates the Gift Aid calculation, not the donor's full personal return.

```oa-check
name: Qualifying Gift Aid donation and donor tax shortfall
inputs:
  eligible_cash_donation: 800
  gift_aid_per_pound: 0.25
  donor_available_tax: 150
steps:
  gift_aid: eligible_cash_donation * gift_aid_per_pound
  gross_donation: eligible_cash_donation + gift_aid
  tax_shortfall: gift_aid - donor_available_tax
expected:
  gift_aid: 200
  gross_donation: 1000
  tax_shortfall: 50
```

Boundary: an invalid declaration or ineligible payment must not be treated as valid ordinary Gift Aid. The small-donations scheme is separate with its own conditions; this worked case does not establish eligibility for it.

### Accounting and reporting periods

For a relevant UK charity applying the SORP, select the edition by the **start of the financial year**, not the date the accounts are signed. The 2026 SORP applies for relevant periods beginning **on or after 1 January 2026**. Separate that commencement from changes to England-and-Wales statutory income thresholds based on a financial year's ending date. The Charity Commission also requires the legal form and any other applicable sector SORP to be considered. [Charity Commission's 2026 update](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/all-charities-urged-to-check-new-rules-for-accounting-for-2026).

This edition does not reproduce an unread current SORP's full income, lease, tier or disclosure requirements. Obtain the actual applicable text before preparing the accounts. Do not simply carry forward the prior lease policy or old audit threshold because it appears in last year's accounts. Scotland and Northern Ireland require their own regulator and charity-law checks; the England-and-Wales thresholds do not apply throughout the UK automatically.

## Fund accounting and restrictions

Use these as the evidence map and translate them into the actual reporting framework's terminology:

| Fund or designation | Records and accounting question |
| --- | --- |
| Unrestricted general funds | Identify resources available within the charity's purposes, excluding external restrictions. Reconcile liabilities, fixed assets, commitments and cash before calling them free reserves |
| Designated funds or a board-designated quasi-endowment | Record the trustees' designation and spending plan. A designation remains an administrative use of unrestricted funds; it is not by itself a donor-imposed legal restriction |
| Restricted income funds | Retain the donor/appeal terms, specific permitted purpose and release evidence. Do not transfer a balance to general funds merely to cover a deficit |
| Permanent or expendable endowment | Determine whether capital must be retained/invested or may be spent and on what authority; distinguish income restrictions from capital restrictions |
| US net assets with or without donor restrictions | Obtain current ASC 958 and applicable state/endowment law before deciding recognition, releases or spending. Do not assume that UK fund headings are identical US accounting rules |

The England-and-Wales regulator distinguishes restricted funds from free reserves and a board designation from a legal restriction. There is no universal required reserves multiple; the policy must reflect the charity's own commitments and risks. [Charity Commission CC19, sections 3 and 6](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/charity-reserves-building-resilience-cc19/charities-and-reserves).

For each restricted or endowed fund, reconcile the governing terms, opening balance, additions, authorised expenditure, transfers, gains/losses and closing balance. Distinguish a return obligation on an unmet grant condition from a restriction on how a recognised donation may be used. Record unfulfilled conditions and obtain the actual framework before deciding recognition. The former US and UK parallel descriptions are a navigation aid, not permission to apply another country's GAAP.

## United States: exemption, unrelated business and foundations handoff

Keep the questions below in the current US jobs; this global file does not add US-country computations or assert that its old percentages are current:

- Separate section 501(c)(3) charitable/religious/educational/scientific/literary organisations from social-welfare organisations, business leagues, social clubs, veterans' organisations and charitable trusts. Exemption, political-activity limits and donor deductibility differ. Do not infer unrestricted political activity or a business-expense deduction for every membership due.
- Establish public-charity versus private-foundation status and all available public-support tests. The former single public-support fraction is not a complete classification test. A family-funded organisation is not classified solely by that label.
- Identify the correct recognition application and exemptions, annual Form 990-series return or notice, foundation return, donor/support schedules and any Form 990-T requirement. Applications and annual returns are separate; the wrong form cannot be selected only by calling an organisation “small”.
- Analyse an unrelated trade/business regularly carried on, statutory exclusions and modifications, debt-financed income, separate-business computations, losses and exempt-function activity. Advertising, royalties, volunteer labour, convenience activity, donated-goods sales and bingo have facts-specific rules. The former shorthand for split-interest trusts and section 511(b) is not a sufficient legal classification.
- For private foundations, preserve net-investment-income excise tax, self-dealing/disqualified persons, minimum qualifying distributions, excess business holdings, jeopardising investments, taxable expenditures, first-tier/additional tax and correction questions. Do not use the old percentage list or its inaccurate legislative history as an excise-tax computation.
- Distinguish a donor-advised fund's sponsoring organisation, legal control, donor advisory rights, grants and prohibited benefits. A contribution does not guarantee an immediate deduction; qualification, substantiation, limits and donor facts matter. Expenditure responsibility is not itself a donor's deduction route for a direct foreign gift.

## VAT/GST and cross-border philanthropy

Charity VAT/GST treatment must be tested for the activity and purchaser, not inferred from income-tax exemption. The UK overview confirms that only certain goods/services qualify for purchase relief and eligibility evidence is needed. Registration and input recovery are separate. CASCs do not receive the same charity VAT reliefs. [HMRC VAT for charities](https://www.gov.uk/vat-charities).

Preserve the following country questions, held until the actual conditions have been read: UK donated-goods sales, fuel/power and charity-building or capital-goods relief; EU public-interest exemptions and national implementation; Australian gifts, donated goods, GST-free concessions and input credits; US state sales/use-tax exemptions. Do not treat all charitable sales as zero rated, all donations as taxable supplies, or all charity input tax as recoverable. A pure gift and a paid supply may require different analyses.

For an international donation, analyse the donor's domestic deduction and the recipient's exemption separately. Obtain source and destination rules, an intermediary's genuine discretion/control and any required equivalence or expenditure-responsibility evidence. US “friends of” arrangements and EU Persche/Stauffer equivalence questions are not automatic worldwide recognition. Current UK guidance requires UK establishment and does not retain the old blanket foreign-charity equivalence claim. Sanctions, transfer restrictions and fundraising permissions require their own current review where relevant; no new country clearance is implied by this file.

## Output and self-checks

- Name the entity, recognised status, authority, period and conditions, separately from the donor-relief approval.
- Reconcile each restricted, designated and endowed fund. Confirm that a proposed release or transfer is authorised and that amounts described as reserves are available for the stated purpose.
- Separate trading, unrelated business, investments, property, donor gifts, grants and VAT/GST supplies. Document each tax exemption rather than labelling all nonprofit activity exempt.
- Prepare the correct annual reports, returns, foundation/UBI computations and donor documentation using current country sources.
- Check self-dealing, private benefits, connected transactions, qualifying distributions and prohibited expenditure before approving them. Historic percentages are not an approval.
- Retain the source, date, law and unresolved issue beside every material relief. Have a credentialed nonprofit-sector practitioner review the result before reliance or filing.
