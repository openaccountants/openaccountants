# US section 1202 qualified small business stock (QSBS) exclusion, tax year 2026

> **Source-cited draft.** Built from Public Law 119-21 section 70431, 26 U.S.C. 1, 57, 1045, 1202 and 1411, Treas. Reg. 1.1202-2 and 1.83-4, and the 2025 instructions for Schedule D, Form 8949 and Form 6251, read on 2026-11-13. No accountant has reviewed it. All outputs must be reviewed by a qualified tax professional before filing.

**Answer first.** The rules split on the **applicable date, July 4, 2025** (the date the Act was enacted). Stock **acquired** on or before that date must be held more than 5 years; the exclusion is 50, 75 or 100 percent depending on when it was bought (100 percent if after 27 September 2010), and gain from one issuer counts only up to **USD 10,000,000** (or 10 times basis, if more). Stock **acquired** after that date qualifies after 3 years: **50%** at 3 years, **75%** at 4 years, **100%** at 5 years or more, with a **USD 15,000,000** per-issuer cap. A corporation that **issues** stock after July 4, 2025 must have gross assets of no more than **USD 75,000,000** before and immediately after the issue. The part of capped gain not excluded is taxed at a maximum of 28 percent; gain above the cap is ordinary long-term capital gain. For a calendar-year seller, 2026 is the first year the new cap rules apply, and no new-regime stock can reach 3 years before July 2028.

## Scope and who this is for

In scope: a non-corporate US taxpayer (individual, trust, estate, or a partner or shareholder of a pass-through entity) who sells stock in a domestic C corporation in tax year 2026 and wants to exclude gain under section 1202 or defer it under section 1045. The result lands on Form 8949 Part II (code Q) or Part I or II (code R), Schedule D, the 28% Rate Gain Worksheet, Form 6251 and Form 8960.

## Ask the client first

1. **Who is the seller?** Individual, trust or estate, or a pass-through entity (partnership, S corporation, RIC, common trust fund). A corporation cannot use section 1202 ([26 U.S.C. 1202(a)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title26/html/USCODE-2024-title26-subtitleA-chap1-subchapP-partI-sec1202.htm)).
2. **How was the stock acquired, and when?** At original issue from the corporation (directly or through an underwriter) for money, property other than stock, or services? Or by gift, at death, in a partnership distribution, by converting other stock of the same corporation, or in a section 351 or 368 exchange? Stock bought from another shareholder does not qualify. Get the exact acquisition date and which side of July 4, 2025 it falls on; for options, warrants, SAFEs and convertible notes, the exercise or conversion date; for restricted stock received for services, whether a section 83(b) election was filed ([Treas. Reg. 1.83-4(a)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-26/section-1.83-4)).
3. **The corporation's history.** Exact issue date; aggregate gross assets (cash plus adjusted basis of other property, contributed property at its value when contributed) at all times before issue and immediately after, for every member of a more-than-50 percent parent-subsidiary group; C corporation status and the active business test for substantially all of the holding period; any S election or partnership classification; what the company actually does (revenue breakdown and asset list).
4. **Buybacks near the issue date** from the seller, a related person or anyone else: dates, amounts and values ([Treas. Reg. 1.1202-2](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-26/section-1.1202-2)).
5. **Gain from this issuer already used.** How much section 1202 gain from this issuer the seller (and, on past joint returns, the spouse) has taken into account in earlier years.
6. **The sale.** Date, amount realized, basis, installment payments, escrow or earn-out, any short sale, put or hedge; and if the holding period is too short, whether other QSBS was bought within 60 days and the seller wants a section 1045 election ([26 U.S.C. 1045](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title26/html/USCODE-2024-title26-subtitleA-chap1-subchapO-partIII-sec1045.htm)).

**Effective dates.** The percentage and holding-period changes (section 70431(a)) and the per-issuer limit changes (section 70431(b)) apply to taxable years beginning after July 4, 2025, so for a calendar-year taxpayer they first apply in 2026. The USD 75,000,000 gross-assets limit applies to stock issued after July 4, 2025. The USD 15,000,000 and USD 75,000,000 amounts are indexed for taxable years beginning after 2026, rounded to the nearest USD 10,000; no indexed figure has been published.

### Steps

1. **Check the seller.** A taxpayer other than a corporation. For a pass-through entity, the owner must have held the interest on the day the entity acquired the stock and at all times after, until the entity sold it; the owner's share is capped by the interest held when the stock was acquired ([26 U.S.C. 1202(g)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title26/html/USCODE-2024-title26-subtitleA-chap1-subchapP-partI-sec1202.htm)).
2. **Check the stock is QSBS:** original issue, qualified small business status at issue (gross assets), C corporation status and the active business test for substantially all of the holding period, and redemptions. All must pass (tables below).
3. **Fix the acquisition date.** "The acquisition date for purposes of this section shall be the first day on which such stock was held by the taxpayer determined after the application of section 1223" ([Public Law 119-21, section 70431(a)(3)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/PLAW-119publ21/html/PLAW-119publ21.htm)). Tacking from a gift, a death, a conversion of other stock of the same corporation (section 1202(f)) or a section 1045 rollover can move the date earlier, and the date picks the regime.
   - Stock received for property other than money or stock, including in a section 351 incorporation or an LLC's conversion to a corporation, is treated as acquired on the date of that exchange; the property's holding period does not tack (section 1202(i)(1)(A)).
   - Stock received for services and subject to vesting is held from just after it substantially vests, or just after the transfer if a section 83(b) election was made ([Treas. Reg. 1.83-4(a)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-26/section-1.83-4)). Restricted stock granted before July 4, 2025 but vesting after it, with no section 83(b) election, is new-regime stock for those tranches.
4. **Check the holding period:** more than 5 years for stock acquired on or before July 4, 2025; at least 3 years for stock acquired after it.
5. **Cap the eligible gain for each issuer:** the greater of the applicable dollar limit or 10 times the aggregate adjusted basis of the issuer's QSBS sold in the year. The cap limits the gain taken into account; the exclusion percentage is then applied to the capped gain.
   - Old regime: USD 10,000,000 less eligible gain from the issuer taken into account in earlier years.
   - New regime: USD 15,000,000 less earlier years' eligible gain from the issuer (any stock) and less same-year eligible gain from the issuer's old-regime stock.
   - **No top-up once used:** if eligible gain from new-regime stock of an issuer exceeds the dollar limit in any year, that issuer's dollar limit is zero for every later year, despite indexing.
   - The 10-times-basis cap uses adjusted basis without additions after original issue. For stock received for property, basis is never less than the property's fair market value at the exchange (section 1202(b)(1) and (i)).
6. **Apply the exclusion percentage** for the regime and holding period.
7. **Classify what remains.** Gain above the cap is long-term capital gain at the normal rates (`us-capital-gains`). The part of capped gain not excluded only because the percentage is below 100 percent is "section 1202 gain", taxed at a maximum of 28 percent.
8. **AMT and NIIT.** Only stock acquired on or before 27 September 2010 has an AMT preference (7 percent of the excluded amount); new-regime 50 and 75 percent stock has none. NIIT reaches gain only to the extent taken into account in computing taxable income, so excluded gain is outside it; the taxable remainder counts.
9. **If the holding period fails**, test a section 1045 rollover.
10. **Report** on Form 8949 with code Q (exclusion) or code R (rollover), then Schedule D and, for stock acquired before 28 September 2010, Form 6251.

### Excluded fields (not a qualified trade or business)

Under [26 U.S.C. 1202(e)(3)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title26/html/USCODE-2024-title26-subtitleA-chap1-subchapP-partI-sec1202.htm), these do not qualify:

- services in the fields of health, law, engineering, architecture, accounting, actuarial science, performing arts, consulting, athletics, financial services or brokerage services;
- any business whose principal asset is the reputation or skill of one or more employees;
- banking, insurance, financing, leasing, investing or similar businesses;
- farming, including raising or harvesting trees;
- production or extraction of products eligible for percentage depletion under section 613 or 613A;
- operating a hotel, motel, restaurant or similar business.

Assets used in start-up activities, research and experimental work, or in-house research for a future qualified business count as active. Software rights producing active business computer software royalties also count as active (section 1202(e)(2) and (e)(8)).

### Section 1045 rollover (60 days)

- **Who and what:** a taxpayer other than a corporation selling QSBS held for more than 6 months can elect to recognize gain only to the extent the amount realized exceeds the cost of other QSBS bought in the 60-day period beginning on the date of sale. Gain treated as ordinary income cannot be rolled ([26 U.S.C. 1045(a)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title26/html/USCODE-2024-title26-subtitleA-chap1-subchapO-partIII-sec1045.htm)).
- **Replacement stock** must be QSBS and meet the active business test for at least its first 6 months after purchase (section 1045(b)(4); [Pub. 550](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/p550.pdf)).
- **Basis:** postponed gain reduces the basis of the replacement stock, in the order acquired (section 1045(b)(3)).
- **Holding period:** the replacement stock's holding period includes the old stock's, for section 1202 too, except for section 1202(a)(2) and (c)(2)(A); it does not count for the 6-month test on a later rollover (section 1223(13); Pub. 550). Because the section 1202 acquisition date is found after section 1223, the text treats replacement stock bought after July 4, 2025 for old-regime stock as acquired on the old stock's date: old-regime stock (USD 10,000,000 limit, more than 5 years). No IRS guidance confirms this yet; document the reliance.
- **Election deadline:** by the due date, including extensions, of the return for the year of sale. If the original return was filed on time, an amended return filed within 6 months after the due date (excluding extensions) marked "Filed pursuant to section 301.9100-2" is accepted ([Instructions for Schedule D](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/i1040sd.pdf)).
- **Pass-through entities** can make the election. An owner who held the interest for the entity's whole holding period can also buy the replacement stock personally within the 60 days.

### Tax on what is not excluded

- **Section 1202 gain** (eligible gain minus the exclusion) is 28-percent rate gain. On the 28% Rate Gain Worksheet, line 2, enter the exclusion for 50 percent stock, 2/3 of it for 60 percent stock, 1/3 of it for 75 percent stock, and nothing for 100 percent stock ([Instructions for Schedule D](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/i1040sd.pdf)).
- **Gain above the per-issuer cap** is long-term capital gain at the normal rates.
- **AMT:** for stock acquired before 28 September 2010 and held more than 5 years, multiply the excluded gain by 7 percent and enter it on Form 6251, line 2h ([Instructions for Form 6251](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/i6251.pdf)). The Act confirms no preference for later stock, including 50 and 75 percent new-regime stock (section 70431(a)(4)).
- **NIIT:** 3.8 percent on the taxable remainder if modified AGI exceeds USD 250,000 (joint or surviving spouse), USD 200,000 (other individuals) or half the joint amount (married filing separately) ([26 U.S.C. 1411](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title26/html/USCODE-2024-title26-subtitleA-chap2A-sec1411.htm)).

### 2025 returns still open (extended to 15 October 2026)

- No new-regime stock can have met 3 years in 2025 (first possible in July 2028), and the new cap rules start for taxable years beginning after July 4, 2025. So every 2025 exclusion is old-regime stock held more than 5 years, with the USD 10,000,000 or 10-times-basis cap.
- The 2025 Schedule D instructions give the 50, 75 and 100 percent windows and the gross-assets test ("$75 million ($50 million if the stock was issued on or before July 4, 2025)").
- The 2025 Schedule D instructions say the 7 percent AMT amount goes on "line 13 of Form 6251"; the 2025 Form 6251 instructions put it on line 2h, and those govern the form. Use line 2h.
- A section 1045 election for a 2025 sale is due with the 2025 return, including extensions (15 October 2026 if extended) ([IRS extensions page](https://www.irs.gov/filing/get-an-extension-to-file-your-tax-return)).
- Pub. 550 (2025) still describes only the pre-Act dollar limit and uses loose holding-period wording. Rely on the statute and the Schedule D instructions.

| Conversion of QSBS into other stock of the same corporation | New stock is QSBS and the holding period tacks | [26 U.S.C. 1202(f)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title26/html/USCODE-2024-title26-subtitleA-chap1-subchapP-partI-sec1202.htm) |
| Section 351 or 368 exchange of QSBS for non-QSBS stock | New stock is QSBS acquired when the old stock was. If the new issuer is not a qualified small business, the exclusion is limited to the gain at the exchange. A section 351 exchange also needs control of the corporation whose stock was exchanged | [26 U.S.C. 1202(h)(4)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title26/html/USCODE-2024-title26-subtitleA-chap1-subchapP-partI-sec1202.htm) |
| Offsetting short position (short sale, put or similar, including by a related person) | No exclusion unless the stock had already met the holding period when the position began and the seller elects to recognize gain as if sold at fair market value that day | [26 U.S.C. 1202(j)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title26/html/USCODE-2024-title26-subtitleA-chap1-subchapP-partI-sec1202.htm) |
| Multiple trusts | Trusts with substantially the same grantor and primary beneficiaries, and a principal purpose of tax avoidance, are treated as one trust; a husband and wife count as one person | [26 U.S.C. 643(f)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title26/html/USCODE-2024-title26-subtitleA-chap1-subchapJ-partI-subpartA-sec643.htm) |

### Defaults when a fact is missing

| Missing fact | Default, and flag it |
| --- | --- |
| Acquisition date | No exclusion until documented; the date picks the regime |
| Gross-asset history before and immediately after issue | Treat the stock as not QSBS until it can be documented |
| Earlier gain from the same issuer | Ask for prior returns; do not assume the full cap is available |
| Section 83(b) election on restricted stock | Assume none: the holding period starts at vesting, tranche by tranche |
| Joint or separate limit for spouses | One limit per married couple |
| Excluded-field analysis | Not qualified until a documented analysis is on file |

### Red flags to raise with the client

| Flag | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| The company was ever an S corporation or taxed as a partnership | C corporation status for substantially all of the holding period may fail |
| Revenue from consulting, health, finance or other listed fields | Section 1202(e)(3) excludes the business |
| Large cash or securities balances after year 2 | The 50 percent working capital and 10 percent portfolio limits |
| A buyback near a funding round | Either redemption window can taint the whole issue |
| Gifts to several trusts before a sale | Section 643(f) aggregation and assignment-of-income risk |
| A sale of new-regime stock before the 5-year date | Only 50 or 75 percent is excluded; the rest is taxed at up to 28 percent plus NIIT |

## Worked cases

These are hypothetical; each assumes every QSBS test passes. Indexed dollar limits for 2028 and later are not yet published, so cases that fall in later years use the unindexed USD 15,000,000 and say so.

**Case 1, a decision: sell new-regime stock at 3, 4 or 5 years?** A founder acquired stock at original issue in September 2025 for USD 1,000,000 and has an offer that values the gain at USD 8,000,000; the buyer will pay the same whenever the deal closes. The cap is the greater of USD 15,000,000 (unindexed) or 10 x USD 1,000,000, so the whole USD 8,000,000 is eligible. At 3 years (September 2028 or later): 50% excluded, USD 4,000,000 of section 1202 gain; at the 28 percent maximum plus 3.8 percent NIIT, tax up to USD 1,272,000. At 4 years: 75% excluded, USD 2,000,000 taxable, tax up to USD 636,000. At 5 years: 100% excluded, no federal tax. Waiting from year 3 to year 5 saves up to USD 1,272,000; recommend waiting unless the risk of the deal (or the company) falling away is worth more than that, and check the active business test still holds until the sale.

```oa-check
name: QSBS new regime, sell at 3, 4 or 5 years (gain USD 8,000,000)
inputs:
  gain: 8000000
  basis: 1000000
  dollar_limit: 15000000
  top_rate: 0.318
steps:
  - eligible: min(gain, max(dollar_limit, 10 * basis))
  - taxable_3yr: eligible - eligible * 0.50
  - taxable_4yr: eligible - eligible * 0.75
  - taxable_5yr: eligible - eligible * 1.00
  - tax_3yr: taxable_3yr * top_rate
  - tax_4yr: taxable_4yr * top_rate
  - saving_waiting_to_5yr: tax_3yr - taxable_5yr * top_rate
expected:
  eligible: 8000000.00
  taxable_3yr: 4000000.00
  tax_3yr: 1272000.00
  tax_4yr: 636000.00
  saving_waiting_to_5yr: 1272000.00
```

**Case 2, a 2026 sale of 2015 stock (old regime, 100 percent).** Acquired at original issue in March 2015 for USD 50,000 cash; sold in 2026 for USD 30,000,000. Gain USD 29,950,000. Cap = greater of USD 10,000,000 or 10 x USD 50,000 = USD 500,000, so USD 10,000,000. Excluded 100% of that: USD 10,000,000. Taxable long-term gain USD 19,950,000 at the normal rates (not 28 percent gain); no AMT preference. Had the seller bought the same stock after July 4, 2025, the cap would be USD 15,000,000, but no such stock can be sold with an exclusion in 2026.

```oa-check
name: QSBS old regime, 2026 sale of 2015 stock
inputs:
  amount_realized: 30000000
  basis: 50000
  dollar_limit: 10000000
  exclusion_pct: 1.00
steps:
  - gain: amount_realized - basis
  - cap: max(dollar_limit, 10 * basis)
  - excluded: min(gain, cap) * exclusion_pct
  - taxable_ltcg: gain - excluded
expected:
  gain: 29950000.00
  cap: 10000000.00
  excluded: 10000000.00
  taxable_ltcg: 19950000.00
```

**Case 3, 2026 sale of 2008 stock (50 percent, AMT).** Basis USD 400,000; gain USD 2,000,000. Cap = greater of USD 10,000,000 or USD 4,000,000, so the whole USD 2,000,000 is eligible. Excluded 50%: USD 1,000,000. Section 1202 gain (28 percent rate): USD 1,000,000, with USD 1,000,000 on line 2 of the 28% Rate Gain Worksheet. AMT preference: 7% x USD 1,000,000 = USD 70,000 on Form 6251, line 2h.

**Case 4, gross-assets boundary.** A C corporation with aggregate gross assets of USD 74,000,000 issues stock for USD 2,000,000 cash in October 2025. Immediately after issue, gross assets are USD 76,000,000, above USD 75,000,000: stock issued in that round is not QSBS. Stock issued earlier, when the corporation was within the limit, is not affected.

**Case 5, excluded field.** Consulting is a listed field. A company whose main business is placing its engineers with clients to deliver consulting projects should be treated as excluded unless counsel concludes otherwise; its stock fails however long it is held. A company selling software that customers run themselves is not in a listed field. Mixed businesses need a documented analysis.

**Case 6, section 1045 rollover.** QSBS held 8 months (basis USD 500,000) is sold for USD 3,000,000, a gain of USD 2,500,000. Within 60 days, USD 2,000,000 of other QSBS is bought. Recognized gain = USD 3,000,000 minus USD 2,000,000 = USD 1,000,000. Postponed gain USD 1,500,000. Replacement basis USD 2,000,000 minus USD 1,500,000 = USD 500,000. Report on Form 8949 with code R and USD 1,500,000 in column (g) as a negative.

## When to refuse or refer

| Code | Situation | Action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| R-QSBS-SCORP | The corporation was ever an S corporation or taxed as a partnership during the holding period, or the C corporation period is unclear | Refer to counsel |
| R-QSBS-REORG | Stock from a section 351 or 368 exchange, an LLC-to-corporation conversion, a merger for acquirer stock, or a section 1045 rollover spanning July 4, 2025 | Refer: acquisition date and limit need specialist analysis |
| R-QSBS-FIELD | Material revenue in any excluded field, or value that depends on key people | Refer for a documented section 1202(e)(3) analysis; do not guess |
| R-QSBS-ASSETS | Gross-asset history before and immediately after issue cannot be documented | Treat as not QSBS until it can |
| R-QSBS-REDEEM | A buyback near the issue date that may exceed the de minimis amounts | Refer |
| R-QSBS-STACK | Gifts to several trusts, or pre-sale gifts to multiply the per-issuer limit | Refer (section 643(f), assignment of income) |
| R-QSBS-SPECIAL | Empowerment zone (60 percent) stock, SSBIC stock, a short position or hedge, a non-resident alien seller, a foreign trust or estate | Refer |
| R-QSBS-STATE | Any state tax question | Check the state's statute and revenue department |

## Filing and payment

1. Report each QSBS sale on Form 8949, Part II (long-term), as if no exclusion were claimed; enter code **Q** in column (f) and the exclusion as a negative number in column (g) ([Instructions for Form 8949](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/i8949.pdf)).
2. For a section 1045 rollover, report in Part I or Part II by holding period; enter code **R** and the postponed gain as a negative number in column (g).
3. For installment sales of unlisted QSBS, report the gain from Form 6252 on Schedule D, line 11, and the prorated exclusion with code Q.
4. Complete the 28% Rate Gain Worksheet (line 2) for 50, 60 and 75 percent stock, and Form 6251 line 2h for stock acquired before 28 September 2010.
5. The 2026 return is due 15 April 2027. An extension moves filing (not payment) to 15 October 2027; the section 1045 election must be on a return filed by the extended due date.

## Completion checklist

- Seller is not a corporation; pass-through holding tests met if relevant.
- Original-issue evidence (board consent, subscription, payment, ledger) and the acquisition date after section 1223, placed on one side of July 4, 2025.
- Gross-asset evidence at all times before issue and immediately after, against USD 50,000,000 or USD 75,000,000 by issue date.
- C corporation status and active business test for substantially all of the holding period, with the 80 percent, working capital, portfolio and real estate limits checked.
- Excluded-field analysis on file.
- Redemption review for both windows, with the de minimis tests.
- Holding period met (more than 5 years, or at least 3 years for new stock).
- Per-issuer cap computed, with prior-year and same-year reductions and the zero-after-exceeded rule; running tracker kept.
- Exclusion applied to the capped gain; section 1202 gain, gain above the cap, AMT and NIIT classified.
- Form 8949 code Q or R, the 28% worksheet and Form 6251 line 2h done; section 1045 election on a timely return.
- State treatment checked separately.
