# Maryland corporation income tax, 2026

> **General reference only.** Source-cited draft, not approved by an accountant. For US Treasury Circular 230 §10.37 purposes this is general reference material; a qualified professional must check the facts, current law and forms before filing or taking a position.

## Who this applies to

Maryland Form500 corporation income tax. A Maryland-incorporated corporation generally files even when inactive or reporting a loss; an out-of-state corporation files when its Maryland activity/income creates liability, subject to applicable federal protection. Federal `us-form-1120-c-corp` owns the federal computation. This is a **2025-2026 filing cycle** annual-form workflow with the published **2026 Form500D** estimate rules. Final 2026 annual instructions and post-2025 federal conformity have not been assumed.

Partnerships, S corporations and LLCs treated as partnerships ordinarily use Form510 or elective Form511, with member returns, rather than Form500. An S corporation with federal entity-level income tax may also need Form500. A disregarded single-member LLC follows its owner. Insurance, fiduciaries, investment conduits, captive REITs and qualified subsidiaries require their stated exceptions. [Form500 instructions](https://www.marylandcomptroller.gov/content/dam/mdcomp/tax/instructions/2025/corporate-booklet.pdf).

## The base

Prepare a separate-company federal-to-Maryland bridge. Affiliated federal consolidated filers submit separate Maryland returns for each member, with the required separate federal computation and reconciliation; federal consolidation adjustments are not automatically permitted. Start with the federal return income required by Form500 lines1a-1c, then apply Maryland adjustments, NOL and net addition modifications in the form's order. Attach Form500DM and related-party expense explanations where applicable. Federal NOL, interest and depreciation figures belong in their federal skills; state decoupling needs a separate record. [Form500 and instructions](https://www.marylandcomptroller.gov/content/dam/mdcomp/tax/forms/2025/500.pdf); [instructions](https://www.marylandcomptroller.gov/content/dam/mdcomp/tax/instructions/2025/corporate-booklet.pdf).

The 2025 booklet expressly decouples from specified 2025 federal domestic research expensing, business-interest and production-property depreciation provisions. Do not silently carry that tax-year statement into a final 2026 conclusion. Track each addition, subtraction, vintage of NOL/net addition modification and carryover, including use or recapture on a carryback. Retain member-level records even when the federal group reports a loss. Final 2026 conformity/carryover instructions remain an open item. [2025 booklet](https://www.marylandcomptroller.gov/content/dam/mdcomp/tax/instructions/2025/corporate-booklet.pdf).

For ordinary multistate unitary corporations the general method is a **single receipts factor** for years beginning after December31, 2021. Maryland-delivered tangible sales go in the numerator; service receipts follow customers-within-the-state rules. Intangible receipts may require property/payroll factors. Unistate businesses cannot simply apportion because an owner lives elsewhere. Rental/leasing, financial institutions, transportation and worldwide-headquartered companies have special methods. For example, rental/leasing generally uses equally weighted receipts/property; financial institutions use specially defined single receipts; trucking/rail/shipping/airlines have their own formulas. Alternative allocation or separate accounting can require Comptroller approval. Calculate required factors even for a multistate loss return. [ScheduleA instructions, pp.13-14](https://www.marylandcomptroller.gov/content/dam/mdcomp/tax/instructions/2025/corporate-booklet.pdf).

Apply the factor and remaining form adjustments, then multiply positive Maryland taxable income by 8.25%. Claim only supported, current certified credits on the applicable 500CR/502S schedules; distinguish refundable from nonrefundable amounts and do not revive expired credits. Maintain tax, credits, estimates, extension payments and prior overpayment separately. Maryland Digital Advertising Gross Revenues Tax, SDAT annual reports/personal property and entity formation fees are separate obligations, not included in this income-tax number.

## Deadlines and filing

Form500 is ordinarily due on the 15th day of the fourth month after year-end; apply actual weekend/holiday or official relief. A calendar-year **2025 return in the 2025-2026 filing cycle is due April15, 2026**. Form500E can extend filing seven months under its conditions; pay full expected tax by the original due date. File the state return, required federal schedules, ScheduleA, modifications, credits and signature through an approved state channel, and retain transmission/payment proof. Do not treat a federal consolidated filing as a Maryland consolidated return. [Instructions](https://www.marylandcomptroller.gov/content/dam/mdcomp/tax/instructions/2025/corporate-booklet.pdf).

The 2026 calendar-year estimate dates are April15, June15, September15 and December15, 2026. Corporate annualization is **not permitted**. On revising the estimate reconcile previous payments and adjust remaining installments. Short-period required payments depend on the number of installment dates; a period shorter than four months has no Form500D estimate requirement. Electronic filers/EFT payers retain Form500D rather than mail a duplicate voucher. [2026 Form500D](https://www.marylandcomptroller.gov/content/dam/mdcomp/tax/forms/2026/500d.pdf).

For an amended return use the current amended Form500 instructions and limitation period; the booklet requires an amendment within90days of a final federal determination increasing Maryland taxable income. Refund limitation rules differ for an original timely filing, later payment and NOL carryback; inspect the actual dates rather than default to one period. [Instructions](https://www.marylandcomptroller.gov/content/dam/mdcomp/tax/instructions/2025/corporate-booklet.pdf).

## Penalties and interest

Late payment and estimated-tax underpayment can produce interest/penalty despite an extension. Form500UP calculates corporate estimate underpayment, and the Comptroller can assess it when no schedule is supplied. The 2025 booklet gives 0.9011% per month or part for late 2025 tax paid before January1, 2027; it does not establish the rate for a 2026 return paid in 2027. Obtain the applicable later rate/notice and actual due dates. [2025 booklet, p.11](https://www.marylandcomptroller.gov/content/dam/mdcomp/tax/instructions/2025/corporate-booklet.pdf).

## Cross-border points

Document nexus and the actual protected/unprotected activities before invoking federal solicitation protection. It is not a protection for every service or digital business. Map receipts to Maryland under the correct customer/product rule and identify special-industry formulas, nonunitary activity, related parties and federal consolidated members. Nonresident PTE withholding/election and members' credits need Forms510/511 and individual/corporate owner analysis; they are not discharged by filing an ordinary corporation return.

## Two worked examples that recompute

**Ordinary multistate corporation.** After separately supported Maryland modifications and NOL treatment, modified income is USD1,000,000. Maryland receipts are USD400,000 out of USD2,000,000 everywhere; assume the ordinary single-receipts method, no special adjustments and no credits. The factor is .200000, taxable income USD200,000 and tax USD16,500. Four equal installments based on 90% of that current-year tax would be USD3,712.50 each.

```oa-check
name: Maryland single receipts corporation
inputs:
  modified_income: 1000000
  md_receipts: 400000
  all_receipts: 2000000
  rate: 0.0825
steps:
  - factor: md_receipts / all_receipts
  - taxable: modified_income * factor
  - tax: taxable * rate
  - current_required: tax * 0.9
  - installment: current_required / 4
expected:
  factor: 0.2
  taxable: 200000
  tax: 16500
  installment: 3712.5
```

**Estimate decision.** Current expected tax is USD20,000 and prior-year developed tax USD10,000. Assume the prior-year route is available and estimates are paid on time: 90% current is USD18,000 versus 110% prior USD11,000. Select the USD11,000 annual route, USD2,750 each installment. The remaining USD9,000 expected final tax is still due by the original annual payment deadline. The safe-harbor installment amount does not reduce the final tax.

```oa-check
name: Maryland corporate prior-year estimate decision
inputs:
  current_tax: 20000
  prior_tax: 10000
steps:
  - current_required: current_tax * 0.9
  - prior_required: prior_tax * 1.1
  - selected: min(current_required, prior_required)
  - installment: selected / 4
  - balance: current_tax - selected
expected:
  current_required: 18000
  prior_required: 11000
  installment: 2750
  balance: 9000
```

## Ask the client first

1. Federal/entity classification, Maryland incorporation/nexus, protected activities, unitary operations and tax-year dates?
2. Separate-company federal return, group reconciliation, Maryland modifications, NOL/NAM vintages and related-party payments?
3. Maryland/everywhere receipts and customer locations, intangible receipts and any special-industry/apportionment approval?
4. Current/prior developed tax, each dated estimate/extension payment, credits/certificates and overpayment carryforward?
5. PTE owners/elections, federal adjustments, notices, amendments and other Maryland tax accounts?
