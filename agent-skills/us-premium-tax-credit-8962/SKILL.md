# Premium tax credit and Form 8962, tax year 2026

> **Source-cited draft.** Built from Rev. Proc. 2025-25, Rev. Proc. 2025-32, Rev. Proc. 2024-35, the 2026 draft Instructions for Form 8962, the IRS premium tax credit questions and answers, and Public Law 119-21 sections 71302, 71304 and 71305, read on 2026-11-13. No accountant has reviewed it. All outputs must be reviewed by a qualified tax professional before filing.

**Answer first.** For 2026 the credit is allowed only if household income is at least 100 percent and not more than 400 percent of the federal poverty line for the family size (with the narrow under-100 percent exception below). The 2021 through 2025 rule that allowed the credit above 400 percent of the poverty line has ended. If advance payments exceed the credit, the taxpayer repays the full excess. There is no repayment cap for a tax year beginning after 31 December 2025. The share of income a household is treated as able to pay toward the benchmark plan is the applicable figure from Rev. Proc. 2025-25 (2.10 percent below 133 percent of the poverty line, up to 9.96 percent from 300 percent through 400 percent). Employer self-only coverage is affordable for 2026 if the employee's share is not more than 9.96 percent of household income.

## Scope and who this is for

In scope: the section 36B premium tax credit for calendar year 2026 coverage bought through a Health Insurance Marketplace, and the reconciliation of advance payments on Form 8962 filed with the 2026 Form 1040, 1040-SR or 1040-NR.

## Ask the client first

1. **Filing status**, and whether a married client who will file separately lived apart at filing and is claiming the domestic abuse or spousal abandonment exception.
2. **Tax family:** the taxpayer, the spouse on a joint return, and every person claimed as a dependent. Someone who can claim the taxpayer as a dependent blocks the credit.
3. **Form 1095-A** for every Marketplace policy: monthly enrollment premiums, second lowest cost silver plan (SLCSP) premiums, and advance payments.
4. **Household income:** adjusted gross income, tax-exempt interest, excluded foreign earned income, and nontaxable Social Security (not Supplemental Security Income), for the taxpayer and for each family member who must file a return.
5. **Other coverage:** for each month, Medicaid, Medicare, CHIP, TRICARE, or an employer plan, and the employee's annual cost for self-only employer coverage that meets minimum value.
6. **Residence** used for the poverty line (48 states and the District of Columbia, Alaska, or Hawaii) and whether the family moved.

## The method, step by step

### What changed for 2026

For tax years 2021 through 2025, the applicable percentage table had no 400 percent cutoff: household income of 400 percent of the poverty line and higher used 8.50 percent. Below 150 percent the initial and final percentages were 0.00 percent. Indexing of that table was suspended for those years. Source: [Rev. Proc. 2024-35, section 2.01](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/rp-24-35.pdf).

For a taxable year beginning in 2026:

- Household income above 400 percent of the federal poverty line for the family size ends the credit. The IRS questions and answers state the general limit as at least 100 percent and no more than 400 percent, and describe the removal of the 400 percent ceiling as a rule for tax years 2021 through 2025. The 2026 draft Form 8962 instructions say that for tax years beginning after 2025 a taxpayer over 400 percent is not eligible. Sources: [IRS questions and answers, Q7](https://www.irs.gov/affordable-care-act/individuals-and-families/questions-and-answers-on-the-premium-tax-credit); [2026 draft Instructions for Form 8962, What's New](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-dft/i8962--dft.pdf).
- Excess advance payments are repaid in full. Section 71305 of Public Law 119-21 strikes section 36B(f)(2)(B) for taxable years beginning after 31 December 2025. Rev. Proc. 2025-32 drops the inflation adjustment for that limitation. The IRS questions and answers state that for tax years after 2025 there is no repayment cap. Sources: [Public Law 119-21, section 71305](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/PLAW-119publ21/html/PLAW-119publ21.htm); [Rev. Proc. 2025-32, section 2.04](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/rp-25-32.pdf); [IRS questions and answers](https://www.irs.gov/affordable-care-act/individuals-and-families/questions-and-answers-on-the-premium-tax-credit).
- The applicable percentage table is again the indexed table in section 36B(b)(3)(A)(i). The 2026 percentages are in Rev. Proc. 2025-25. Source: [Rev. Proc. 2025-25, section 3.01](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/rp-25-25.pdf).
- A lawfully present individual who was ineligible for Medicaid only because of immigration status is not eligible for the credit for 2026 merely because household income is under 100 percent of the poverty line. Section 71302 strikes section 36B(c)(1)(B) for taxable years beginning after 31 December 2025. Source: [Public Law 119-21, section 71302](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/PLAW-119publ21/html/PLAW-119publ21.htm).
- Coverage enrolled in during a Marketplace special enrollment period that is based on expected household income as a percentage of the poverty line, and that is not tied to an event or change in circumstances the Secretary of Health and Human Services specifies, is not a qualified health plan for the credit. Section 71304 applies to plan years beginning after 31 December 2025. Source: [Public Law 119-21, section 71304](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/PLAW-119publ21/html/PLAW-119publ21.htm).

Section 71301 (limits on which lawfully present aliens can take the credit) applies to taxable years beginning after 31 December 2026, so it is not a 2026 rule. Section 71303 (exchange verification before a month counts) applies to taxable years beginning after 31 December 2027. Do not apply either section to a 2026 return. Source: [Public Law 119-21, sections 71301(e) and 71303(c)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/PLAW-119publ21/html/PLAW-119publ21.htm).

### Who may take the credit

All of the following are required. Source: [IRS questions and answers, Q5 and Q7](https://www.irs.gov/affordable-care-act/individuals-and-families/questions-and-answers-on-the-premium-tax-credit).

1. Household income meets the limits above.
2. The taxpayer does not file married filing separately, unless the domestic abuse or spousal abandonment rule below applies, or the taxpayer is treated as unmarried.
3. No one else can claim the taxpayer as a dependent.
4. For at least one month, the taxpayer or a family member was enrolled in Marketplace coverage other than catastrophic coverage; that person could not get affordable employer coverage that provides minimum value; that person was not eligible for Medicaid, Medicare, CHIP or TRICARE; and the share of the premium not covered by advance payments was paid, or a special rule kept the coverage in force.

A married person who files separately can still take the credit if, at filing, they live apart from their spouse, they cannot file jointly because they are a victim of domestic abuse or spousal abandonment, and they certify that on Form 8962. The relief is limited to three consecutive years. Do not attach the supporting papers to the return. Source: [IRS questions and answers, Q9](https://www.irs.gov/affordable-care-act/individuals-and-families/questions-and-answers-on-the-premium-tax-credit).

Employer coverage is affordable for the employee if the annual premium the employee pays for self-only coverage that provides minimum value is not more than 9.96 percent of household income. If the employer offered coverage for the employee but not the family, family members may still be able to take the credit. Source: [Rev. Proc. 2025-25, section 3.02](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/rp-25-25.pdf); [2026 draft Instructions for Form 8962](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-dft/i8962--dft.pdf).

### Household income and the poverty line percentage

Family is the taxpayer, the spouse on a joint return, and each dependent. Household income is modified adjusted gross income of the taxpayer and spouse, plus modified adjusted gross income of each family member who must file a return. Modified adjusted gross income is adjusted gross income plus excluded foreign income, nontaxable Social Security benefits (including tier 1 railroad retirement benefits), and tax-exempt interest. It does not include Supplemental Security Income. Source: [IRS questions and answers, Q6 and Q8](https://www.irs.gov/affordable-care-act/individuals-and-families/questions-and-answers-on-the-premium-tax-credit).

On the draft Form 8962, multiply the poverty line by 4.0. If household income is more than that product, enter 401. The credit is zero and every advance payment is repaid (two exceptions are in the draft: the alternative calculation for the year of marriage, and the rules for allocating a policy). If household income is not more than four times the poverty line, divide household income by the poverty line, multiply by 100, and drop the digits after the decimal point. That whole number is the percentage used to read the applicable figure. Source: [2026 draft Instructions for Form 8962, Worksheet 2](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-dft/i8962--dft.pdf).

If the percentage is under 100, the credit is still allowed when advance payments were made, the Marketplace estimated household income at enrollment at 100 to 400 percent of the poverty line, nobody can claim the taxpayer as a dependent, and the taxpayer did not give the Marketplace incorrect information with intentional or reckless disregard. Source: [2026 draft Instructions for Form 8962](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-dft/i8962--dft.pdf).

### The credit and the repayment

For each coverage month the premium assistance amount is the lesser of the enrollment premium for that month and the SLCSP premium for that month minus one-twelfth of household income times the applicable figure. The annual credit is the sum of those months. It is refundable: it can exceed income tax. Source: [26 U.S.C. 36B](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title26/html/USCODE-2024-title26-subtitleA-chap1-subchapA-partIV-subpartC-sec36B.htm); [Rev. Proc. 2025-25](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/rp-25-25.pdf).

If every month has the same enrollment premium and the same SLCSP premium, the annual credit equals the lesser of the annual enrollment premium and the annual SLCSP premium minus household income times the applicable figure.

Subtract advance payments. If the credit is larger, the difference reduces the tax or increases the refund. If advance payments are larger, add the entire difference to the tax. There is no dollar cap. Source: [IRS questions and answers](https://www.irs.gov/affordable-care-act/individuals-and-families/questions-and-answers-on-the-premium-tax-credit).

A shared policy is allocated on Form 8962 Part IV before this arithmetic. The draft instructions' allocation examples are the method when two tax families are on one policy.

Do not also deduct the premium dollars that the credit or the advance payments covered. The self-employed deduction side of that rule is in `us-self-employed-health-insurance`.

## Boundary and exception table

| Situation | Treatment |
| --- | --- |
| Household income more than 4.0 times the poverty line | No credit. Repay all advance payments, unless the year-of-marriage alternative or a policy allocation in the Form 8962 instructions applies |
| Household income under 100 percent, and the Marketplace estimated at least 100 percent, with advance payments made | Credit still allowed if the other tests are met |
| Household income under 100 percent, and that estimate was not made | No credit. Repay advance payments |
| Married filing separately, no abuse or abandonment certification, not treated as unmarried | No credit. Advance payments are still reconciled |
| Catastrophic Marketplace plan | Not coverage for which the credit is allowed |
| Enrolled in employer coverage for the month | Treated as eligible for that coverage for the month even if it was not affordable |
| Self-employed person also claiming a deduction for the same premiums | Use `us-self-employed-health-insurance` for the deduction. Do not deduct the dollars the credit covers |

## Worked cases

**Case 1. Credit larger than the advance payments.** A single person lives all year in one of the 48 states. Tax family size is 1. Household income is USD 31,300, which is 200 percent of USD 15,650. The applicable figure at 200 percent is 0.0660. The person was enrolled in the same Marketplace plan for all 12 months. Annual enrollment premiums are USD 5,400. Annual SLCSP premiums are USD 6,000. Advance payments were USD 3,600. No employer coverage and no government program.

Contribution is USD 31,300 times 0.0660 = USD 2,065.80. The benchmark gap is USD 6,000 minus USD 2,065.80 = USD 3,934.20. The credit is the lesser of USD 5,400 and USD 3,934.20, which is USD 3,934.20. Advance payments are smaller, so the return claims an additional credit of USD 334.20. Nothing is repaid.

```oa-check
name: Single enrollee at 200 percent of the poverty line (2026)
inputs:
  income: 31300
  applicable: 0.066
  slcsp: 6000
  enrollment: 5400
  aptc: 3600
steps:
  - contribution: income * applicable
  - benchmark_gap: slcsp - contribution
  - ptc: min(enrollment, benchmark_gap)
  - additional_credit: ptc - aptc
expected:
  contribution: 2065.80
  benchmark_gap: 3934.20
  ptc: 3934.20
  additional_credit: 334.20
```

**Case 2, a decision: one more dollar of income crosses 400 percent.** The same person can keep household income at USD 62,600, which is exactly 4.0 times USD 15,650, or recognize one more dollar and finish at USD 62,601. Annual enrollment premiums and the annual SLCSP premium are both USD 8,000 for all 12 months. Advance payments were USD 4,000. The applicable figure from 300 percent through 400 percent is 0.0996.

At USD 62,600, income is not more than four times the poverty line, so the percentage is 400. Contribution is USD 62,600 times 0.0996 = USD 6,234.96. The credit is USD 8,000 minus USD 6,234.96 = USD 1,765.04. Excess advance payments repaid are USD 4,000 minus USD 1,765.04 = USD 2,234.96.

At USD 62,601, income is more than four times the poverty line, so the form percentage is 401, the credit is zero, and the person repays all USD 4,000. The extra dollar increases the repayment by USD 1,765.04. There is no cap either way. Unless that dollar is income the person cannot avoid, keep household income at or under four times the poverty line.

```oa-check
  income_over: 62601

  aptc: 4000

  - contribution: income_at_limit * applicable
  - ptc_at_limit: slcsp - contribution
  - repay_at_limit: aptc - ptc_at_limit

  - extra_repayment: repay_over - repay_at_limit

```

## Filing and payment

File Form 8962 with the 2026 Form 1040, 1040-SR or 1040-NR if advance payments were made, or if the taxpayer wants the credit and no advance was paid. The Marketplace reports the policy on Form 1095-A. The additional credit or the repayment is the Form 8962 result that flows to the return. The draft instructions place the repayment on Schedule 2 (Form 1040), line 1a, when income is over 400 percent of the poverty line. The due date of the return is the Form 1040 date in `us-form-1040-individual-return`. Report income and family changes to the Marketplace during the year so the advance payment tracks the credit.

## Completion checklist

- Family size matches the people on the return, and nobody else claims the taxpayer as a dependent.
- Poverty line is the 2025 line for the 2026 form, for the correct state table.
- Percentage over 400 is entered as 401, and the credit is zero.
- Applicable figure is from Rev. Proc. 2025-25 or Table 2 of the Form 8962 instructions, not from the 2025 table in Rev. Proc. 2024-35.
- Excess advance payments are repaid in full.
- Employer affordability uses 9.96 percent of household income for self-only minimum-value coverage.
- Shared policies are allocated before the credit is computed.
