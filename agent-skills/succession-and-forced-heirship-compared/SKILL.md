# Succession, forced heirship and inheritance tax compared: France, Italy, Spain, Germany, Switzerland, Malta, UK, UAE and US

Figures are for tax year 2026; the UK residence basis below applies from 6 April 2025. This source-cited working paper is for a family and its qualified advisers deciding who gets what on a death and what it will cost. It keeps two questions apart for every country. First, civil law: does the governing law reserve a share of the estate for children or a spouse, whatever the will says? Second, tax: what does each country charge on death, at what rates, with what allowances, and who is caught (residence, domicile or where the asset sits)? Each figure has its official source on its table row. Unconfirmed points at the end are not usable rules or zero-tax assumptions. This is general reference, not advice for a particular estate.

Sibling Guides carry the detail this Guide does not repeat: `mt-inheritance-and-succession` (Malta duty on death and the reserved portion), `trusts-vs-foundations-by-country` and `mt-foundations-and-trusts` (vehicles and forced heirship claims), `exit-taxes-when-a-family-moves` and `family-office-residency-regimes-compared` (moving country), and `us-estate-gift-706-709` (US federal estate and gift tax).

## The method, step by step

1. Fix the facts at death for the person who will die first: nationality, where they live and for how long, marital status, every child (including adopted children), and where each asset sits. Each country below tests different facts.
2. Find which country's succession law governs the estate before choosing a country's reserved-share table. For deaths on or after 17 August 2015 within its scope, a participating Member State applies EU Regulation 650/2012: normally the law of the deceased's habitual residence at death governs the succession as a whole; exceptionally the law of a manifestly more closely connected state applies. A person may instead choose the law of a nationality held at the time of the choice or at death, expressly in a disposition upon death or demonstrated by its terms. The designated law applies even if it is not a Member State's law; this does not bind every foreign court. The UK, Ireland and Denmark did not participate (recitals 82–83). Articles 1, 20–23 and 83 exclude revenue matters and matrimonial-property regimes; the Regulation does not choose a tax system or settle the creation, administration or dissolution of trusts. Articles 34–36 have separate rules for renvoi, public policy and states with several legal systems; a choice under article 22 excludes renvoi. Transitional dispositions need article 83's separate tests. Have a succession lawyer apply these conditions, not Spain's domestic nationality rule in article 9.8 alone ([Regulation 650/2012](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2012/650/oj); [Código Civil, article 9.8](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-1889-4763)). Malta's Civil Code article 958B expressly applies the Regulation to the relevant cross-border successions from that date ([Cap. 16](https://legislation.mt/eli/cap/16/eng/pdf)).
3. Apply that law's reserved shares (the civil-law tables below). A will that leaves less than a reserved share can be cut back on a claim by the heir.
4. For each country that can tax, test its basis (the residence or nationality of the deceased or of the heir, or the location of the asset), then apply that country's allowances and rates to its prescribed taxable unit: the estate or the individual acquisition, as applicable (the tax tables below).
5. Where two countries tax the same share, check for a treaty or a credit. GOV.UK says an executor "might be able to reclaim tax through a double-taxation treaty if Inheritance Tax is charged on the same assets by the UK and the country where you lived" ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/inheritance-tax/when-someone-living-outside-the-uk-dies)). Germany credits, on application, foreign tax on foreign assets where no treaty applies and the liability is unlimited; the foreign tax must have been assessed, fall on the heir, have been paid, and not be subject to a claim for reduction. The credit covers only the foreign assets that also bear German tax, and only if the German tax arose within five years of the foreign tax ([§ 21 ErbStG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/erbstg_1974/__21.html)). Spain lets an heir taxed on worldwide receipts deduct the lower of two amounts: the tax actually paid abroad under a similar tax, and the Spanish average effective rate applied to the foreign assets taxed abroad by a similar tax ([Ley 29/1987, article 23](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-1987-28141)). The German foreign-asset definition depends on whether the deceased was an Inländer, and the domestic credit cap is calculated separately for each foreign country; a treaty credit needs its own terms and § 21(4). French, Italian and Swiss credit or treaty calculations have not been established here: refer those.
6. For a US person, or US assets, stop at `us-estate-gift-706-709`. For Malta assets, use `mt-inheritance-and-succession`.

## Layer 1: civil law, country by country

### France

Where French law governs the succession, children cannot be disinherited: "Si la loi française est applicable à votre succession, vous ne pouvez pas déshériter vos enfants" ([service-public F606](https://www.service-public.gouv.fr/particuliers/vosdroits/F606)). The reserved heirs ("héritiers réservataires") cannot be excluded. The testator may leave freely only the "quotité disponible". An heir who has not received the reserved share can bring an "action en réduction" ([service-public F770](https://www.service-public.gouv.fr/particuliers/vosdroits/F770)).

F606 states which law applies as France reads it. The law of the country of habitual residence applies in principle, but a person may choose the law of a nationality held when the will is made or at death, by saying so clearly in a valid will ("vous pouvez décider d’appliquer la loi de l’État de votre nationalité plutôt que la loi de votre pays de résidence"). For a succession opened on or after 1 November 2021, a child disinherited by a foreign law may take the equivalent out of assets situated in France at the death (the "prélèvement compensatoire"). Both conditions must hold: the deceased or at least one child was a national or habitual resident of an EU member state at the death, AND "La loi étrangère applicable à la succession ne doit pas prévoir de mécanisme réservataire protecteur des enfants". The page cites a European Commission letter of 4 June 2026 on how France applies this right; refer every such case.

### Germany

A descendant excluded by a will may claim the "Pflichtteil" from the heir: one-half of the value of the statutory share. Parents and the spouse have the same right when excluded by a will ([§ 2303 BGB](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/bgb/__2303.html)). The Pflichtteil is a money claim against the heir, not a share of the assets. The statutory share itself depends on the other heirs and, for the spouse, on the marital property regime (§ 2303(2) keeps § 1371 BGB in force); work it out before halving it.

### United Kingdom

Where a person "dies domiciled in England and Wales", section 1 of the Inheritance (Provision for Family and Dependants) Act 1975 lets an eligible person apply to court for reasonable financial provision. The classes include the spouse or civil partner, a former spouse or civil partner who has not remarried or formed a subsequent civil partnership, a child, the specified child-of-family/dependency classes and a qualifying cohabitant. The cohabitant must have lived in the same household as the deceased as if married or civil partners for the whole two years before a death on or after 1 January 1996. The spouse/civil-partner standard is reasonable provision in all the circumstances, whether or not needed for maintenance, except where the specified separation order remains in force and separation continues at death. For other applicants the standard is provision reasonably required for maintenance. An applicant relying on section 1(1)(e), the maintained-person class, needs the deceased's substantial contribution to reasonable needs immediately before death; a full-value commercial arrangement does not qualify. This is a court claim, not a fixed reserved fraction ([section 1(1)–(3)](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1975/63/section/1)). Scotland and Northern Ireland are outside this comparison.

### United Arab Emirates

Federal Decree-Law 41/2022 has a specific non-Muslim civil-personal-status route, effective 1 February 2023. Article 1 applies to non-Muslim UAE nationals and non-Muslim foreign residents unless the foreign person invokes their own country's law, preserves the listed Civil Transactions Law conflicts provisions, and permits agreement on another applicable UAE personal-status law. It is not a statement of every UAE succession regime ([Arabic original](https://uaelegislation.gov.ae/ar/legislations/1586); [official English text, articles 1 and 18](https://uaelegislation.gov.ae/en/legislations/1586)).

Within that route, article 11(1) permits a will leaving the whole of the person's property **in the State** to a chosen person, subject to the Executive Regulations' controls. If there is no will and the deceased leaves both a spouse and children, article 11(2) gives half to the spouse and divides the other half equally among the children, without distinguishing male and female. Other parent/sibling configurations have their own branches; do not extend this spouse-and-children split to them. Article 11(3) permits any heir of a foreign deceased person to request application of the law applicable to the estate under the Civil Transactions Law unless a registered will provides otherwise. Article 13 concerns registration in the designated register and again refers to the Executive Regulations. Obtain UAE legal confirmation of the applicable regime and registration before relying on a will. Muslim/Sharia shares, the operative registration procedures and local overlays have not been established in this update ([articles 11–13](https://uaelegislation.gov.ae/en/legislations/1586)).

### United States

Identify the relevant state and ask its qualified adviser to establish the governing succession and surviving-spouse claim rules. USA.gov directs wills-and-estates matters to state/territory probate courts; it does not establish a universal reserved share or elective-share fraction, and neither does this Guide ([USA.gov, state courts](https://www.usa.gov/courts)). The original question about a child's or spouse's protected entitlement remains a state-law handoff. Federal estate and gift tax is in `us-estate-gift-706-709`, a Guide by a named accountant; this Guide does not repeat or alter it.

The home increase is a residence nil-rate band of up to GBP 175,000 for a qualifying home closely inherited by direct descendants, limited by the qualifying home value. For that taper, use estate assets less liabilities before exemptions and reliefs, ignoring property specifically excluded from Inheritance Tax; do not reduce the estate by a spouse exemption or business relief to avoid the taper ([HMRC residence nil-rate band guidance](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/inheritance-tax-residence-nil-rate-band)).

Basis from 6 April 2025 ([GOV.UK, long-term UK residents](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/inheritance-tax-if-youre-a-long-term-uk-resident)). The domicile and deemed domicile rules were replaced by long-term UK residence. A person is long-term UK resident in a tax year if UK tax resident for the previous 10 consecutive years, or for a total of 10 years or more within the previous 20. Inheritance Tax is charged on overseas assets owned outright on death when the person is long-term UK resident. Long-term residence can continue for up to 10 tax years after leaving; it is shorter if the person was not UK resident for all of the previous 20 years (for 10 to 13 years of residence, it ends 3 years after leaving). Do not use this for a death before 6 April 2025.

For a person who is not long-term UK resident, the UK-property charge and statutory excluded-property rules still need to be applied. Do not decide the exemption from labels such as foreign-currency account, overseas pension or unit trust alone; those original asset categories require their specific conditions ([GOV.UK, outside-UK deaths](https://www.gov.uk/inheritance-tax/when-someone-living-outside-the-uk-dies)).

There are two transitional rules on the long-term residence page. First, a person is not long-term UK resident if all three hold: on 30 October 2024 they did not have UK domicile or deemed domicile status, they are non-resident for the tax year 6 April 2025 to 5 April 2026, and they do not return. Second, a person who had deemed UK domicile on 30 October 2024, is non-resident for that tax year and does not return stops being long-term UK resident after three years of non-residence. After ten consecutive nonresident tax years, the residence test resets as the guidance describes. Obtain the actual departure, return and residence history; do not use a universal ten-year tail ([HMRC long-term residence guidance](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/inheritance-tax-if-youre-a-long-term-uk-resident)).

Trusts need their own charging-event analysis. For foreign settled property, the living settlor's long-term residence at the charge matters; for a settlor dying from 6 April 2025, the relevant status is fixed at death, while earlier deaths retain the old domicile-at-settlement test. A qualifying interest-in-possession beneficiary can add a residence condition. The restricted protection for foreign property already excluded on 30 October 2024 is separate from the general rule and from periodic/exit charges. The old shorthand "trust assets escape tax" is not usable. Apply the detailed conditions in `trusts-vs-foundations-by-country`, including its enacted charge-cap scope, and refer the actual death/end-of-interest event ([IHTM47050](https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm47050); [IHTM47052](https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/inheritance-tax-manual/ihtm47052)).

## Traps a family office meets

- **A trust or foundation does not beat forced heirship everywhere.** For a Maltese foundation, article 29A preserves overriding mandatory rules, including succession rules, where its stated founder-domicile-at-endowment or immovable-situs connections apply. Article 29B manages the application of mandatory rules while preserving the foundation and endowment as far as possible, subject to court directions and the specified property-return, value-payment and claim routes; do not collapse the two articles into either blanket immunity or automatic invalidity ([Civil Code, Second Schedule, articles 29A–29B](https://legislation.mt/eli/cap/16/eng/pdf)). In France the reserved heirs may bring an "action en réduction" ([F770](https://www.service-public.gouv.fr/particuliers/vosdroits/F770)). See `trusts-vs-foundations-by-country` and `mt-foundations-and-trusts`.
- **Gifts during life are not outside the count.** France takes certain earlier gifts into account against the child allowance (F14198 above). Malta fictitiously adds donated property at its value at donation when determining abatement under article 648; the net death estate and surviving-spouse rights also enter that calculation ([Cap. 16, articles 647–648](https://legislation.mt/eli/cap/16/eng/pdf)); see `mt-inheritance-and-succession`.
- **A move does not end the old country's tax at once.** The UK keeps long-term residence for up to 10 tax years after leaving (it ends after 3 years of non-residence for someone who had deemed UK domicile on 30 October 2024, is non-resident for the tax year 6 April 2025 to 5 April 2026 and does not return). Germany treats a German national as an Inländer for five years abroad without a German home. See `exit-taxes-when-a-family-moves` and `family-office-residency-regimes-compared`.
- **The heir's residence matters too.** Spain taxes a resident heir on everything received, wherever it is. Germany taxes worldwide if the heir is an Inländer, even when the deceased was not.
- **A will valid in one country may still owe the reserved share of another.** Where Spain's article 9.8 applies, a will valid under the testator's national law stays valid, but the legítimas follow the law that governs the succession. Under French law, a child disinherited by a foreign law may take the equivalent from French assets in the cases set out in the France section.
- **Spain's state figures are the fallback.** Check the applicable regional reductions, rates and connecting rules before using the state fallback.

## Ask the client first

- Where did the person who may die first live in each of the last 20 years, and what nationalities do they hold?
- Where does each heir live, and for how long, and what nationality does each hold?
- Where is each asset (land, company shares, bank and broker accounts), and is any of it in a trust, foundation or company?
- Is there a will in each country, and has the person chosen a governing law in it?
- Is there a spouse or registered partner, under which marital property regime, and any divorce pending?
- What gifts were made during life, to whom and when?

## When to refuse or refer

- Refer application of governing law, renvoi, territorial-unit rules and nationality elections to a succession lawyer. The bounded Regulation rules above do not settle a particular estate or will.
- Refer Italian valuation/exemptions/filing, Swiss cantonal charges and UAE tax; do not substitute an unconfirmed zero-tax rule. French territory is sourced above, but any treaty override, indirect-situs or disputed fiscal-domicile case needs its own analysis.
- Refer any Spanish case to someone who applies the region's own rules.
- Refer US federal estate and gift tax to `us-estate-gift-706-709`, and US state law to a state adviser.
- Refuse to give a final figure where two countries tax the same share and step 5 has not been worked through.
- Refuse to say a trust or foundation defeats a reserved share without a lawyer's opinion for each country concerned.
