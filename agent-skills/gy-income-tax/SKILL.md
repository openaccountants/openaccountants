# Guyana: personal income tax for employees and the self-employed (year of income 2026) v1.0

> **General reference only.** This skill is general tax/accounting reference material for AI-assisted workflows. It has not been reviewed for any specific person's facts, documents, elections, deadlines, residency, filing status, or local procedures. Do not rely on it to file, pay, amend, or take a tax position without review by a qualified professional in the relevant jurisdiction.

**Which text of the law.** The Income Tax Act, Chapter 81:01 (ITA), in the consolidation "revised April 1, 2026" published by the Guyana Revenue Authority (GRA), which already includes Act No. 3 of 2026. The 2026 changes come from the Fiscal Enactments (Amendment) Act 2026, Act No. 3 of 2026, passed on 13 February 2026 and published in the Official Gazette (Extraordinary) of 17 February 2026. Its section 1(2) says the Income Tax Act amendments apply "with respect to and from the year of income commencing on the 1st January, 2026".

**Year of income and year of assessment.** Guyana taxes the income of a calendar year (the year of income) in the following year (the year of assessment). Income earned in 2026 is year of assessment 2027, and its return is due on 30 April 2027. The GRA's own schedules label figures by year of assessment, so "YA 2026" in a GRA table means income earned in 2025. This skill covers the 2026 year of income.

## Who this applies to

- **Every person other than a company**: individuals, including employees, self-employed people and people with rental, interest or other income (ITA s. 36, 36A). Companies pay corporation tax instead; see gy-corporate-income-tax.
- **Residents.** An individual is "resident in Guyana" if they reside permanently, or being in Guyana intend to reside permanently, apart from temporary absences the Commissioner-General accepts, or if they reside in Guyana for more than 183 days in the year (ITA s. 2). Tax is charged on income "accruing in or derived from Guyana or elsewhere, and whether received in Guyana or not" (s. 5). Foreign income that is earned income, or that arises to a person not ordinarily resident or not domiciled in Guyana, is taxed only on the amount received in Guyana (s. 5 proviso).
- **Employment exercised in Guyana** is income derived from Guyana whether the pay is received in Guyana or not (s. 6). This catches non-resident employees working in Guyana.
- **Non-residents** with no trade or business in Guyana are taxed by 20% withholding on most Guyana-source payments (s. 39(1)(b), Third Schedule); see "Cross-border points".
- **Who must file.** An individual whose income is not less than G$1,680,000 must deliver a return (s. 60(1)(a), as amended by Act No. 3 of 2026). The GRA's return instructions also require a return where a loss is carried forward.
- **Out of scope**: companies, capital gains tax under the Capital Gains Tax Act, property tax (individuals are exempt from property tax from the 2025 year of income: Property Tax Act s. 6(1)(y), inserted by Act No. 3 of 2026), and special regimes (gold and diamond miners, s. 33E; the small business rate for manufacturing and construction, s. 36B, outlined below only).

**Monthly equivalents.** For a full year, G$1,680,000 is G$140,000 a month and the G$3,360,000 band is G$280,000 a month. These are divisions by 12, not figures the law states. The GRA's PAYE "Rates and Calculations" page still shows the 2024 figures (G$100,000 a month, 28% and 40%) and its income tax rates page stops at year of income 2023. The Act wins over both.

**How the rates work.** There are two slices of chargeable income: 25% on the first G$3,360,000 and 35% on the rest (s. 36 and 36A). The personal allowance is a deduction from income, not a credit. Above total income of G$5,040,000 (three times G$1,680,000), one third of income gives the larger allowance.

## The base

Chargeable income is the aggregate income from the sources in s. 5 after the deductions and exemptions for each source and those for aggregate income (s. 2, "chargeable income"). The GRA individual return (form G0004) follows this order, lines 24 to 55.

1. **Income (lines 24 to 33).** Employment income, including the value of quarters, board and taxable allowances (s. 5(b)); trade, business and professional profits (s. 5(a)); foreign dividends, interest and discounts (s. 5(c)); annuities (s. 5(d)); rents, royalties and other property income (s. 5(e)). Dividends from companies resident in Guyana are not in the s. 5(c) charge. Payments that suffered withholding tax under s. 39 are not taxed again (s. 39(6)).
2. **Not taxable**: severance pay, medical discharge and station allowance (s. 5(b)(iv)); subsistence, travelling, entertainment or expense allowances shown to have been spent for their purpose (s. 5(b)(ii)); vacation allowance up to one month's gross salary (s. 5(b)(iiA)). The GRA list of non-taxable allowances (revised 22 July 2025) gives the full list with the section for each.
3. **Business deductions and allowances (lines 34 to 37).** Wear and tear allowances (s. 17) and trade losses brought forward. A loss is carried forward and set off only against later profits from the same source (s. 19(a)). The GRA return guide (line 35) states that loss relief may not reduce the tax payable below one half of the tax that would have been payable without it.
4. **Personal deductions (lines 39 to 43).** Personal allowance (line 39), employee NIS (line 40), mortgage interest (line 41), medical and life insurance premiums (line 42). The child, second-job and overtime deductions of s. 20(1)(b) to (d) are taken in the same way. Mortgage interest can bring chargeable income to nil but not below (line 44 note).
5. **Tax (line 45)**: 25% and 35% as above. Then subtract PAYE deducted per the 7B slips (line 46), advance tax paid (line 47), tax deducted at source (line 48), relief for foreign tax (lines 49 and 50) and the small business credit (line 51). Line 54 is tax due; line 55 is a refund.

**Employees under PAYE.** The employer deducts tax from every payment of emoluments (s. 93) and pays it to the GRA by the 14th of the following month (Deduction of Income Tax (Employment) Regulations, reg. 11). Only the primary employer gives the personal allowance; a second employer does not (GRA, File a Return page). At the year end the employer issues a 7B slip. An employee with more than one employer files one return covering all income and attaches every 7B slip.

**Self-employed.** Profit is turnover less expenses wholly and exclusively incurred, with wear and tear. Self-employed persons pay advance tax quarterly (s. 69(1)). For service providers whose services turnover exceeds G$10,000,000, the tax is the higher of the regular income tax and 2% of that turnover (s. 37A). Section 37A(1) refers to "circumstances specified in subsection (3)", but the GRA consolidation has no subsection (3); see "Not yet confirmed". Below G$10,000,000, s. 28A allows a presumptive method by regulations.

**Married couples.** Section 34 deals with the income of a married woman living with her husband. Check how it applies before combining spouses' income.

## Deadlines and filing

| What | When | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| PAYE deducted in a month, paid by the employer | 14th of the following month | Deduction of Income Tax (Employment) Regulations, reg. 11(1) |
| Withholding tax on payments and distributions in a month | 14th of the following month | ITA s. 39(2) |
| Advance tax, 2026 year of income, each one quarter of estimated tax | 1 April, 1 July, 1 October and 31 December 2026 | ITA s. 69(1) |
| Individual return (G0004) for YI 2026 (YA 2027), and balance of tax | 30 April 2027 (next business day if a holiday) | ITA s. 60(1), s. 69(1), (6), (7); GRA return instructions |

**Advance tax.** The estimate is based on the chargeable income in the return for the preceding year (s. 69(2)). The Commissioner-General can revise it on application if this year's income will be lower (s. 69(3)). An employee taxed under PAYE does not pay advance tax on the salary. If PAYE-taxed pay is at least three quarters of total income, the person pays the remainder by 30 April of the next year (s. 69(5), (6)). The GRA's advance tax page says a person who was both employed and self-employed pays advance tax where self-employed income is 25% or more of the total.

**How to file.**
1. Get a TIN and an eServices account at https://eservices.gra.gov.gy (TIN as user name, plus a scan of an ID card, passport or driver's licence), or register in the GRA Padna mobile app.
2. In eServices, open the "Returns" menu, choose "File a Return", choose the year of assessment (2027 for 2026 income), and complete the G0004 lines above.
3. Upload every 7B slip, Form 4 for mortgage interest relief and the medical or life insurance documents. A self-employed person attaches an income and expenditure statement and the return schedules (for example Schedule 02 for gross payments to non-residents).
4. Submit. A return filed electronically counts as filed on the day the Commissioner-General acknowledges receipt (s. 60B(2)). No paper copy is needed.
5. Pay any balance (line 54) by 30 April 2027. Paper filing is still possible at a GRA office: a printed, signed G0004 with the same attachments.

## Penalties and interest

- **Late payment**: 2% a month, or part of a month, on the tax outstanding. Under an instalment arrangement with the Commissioner-General, 1% a month from the date the arrangement takes effect (s. 99(1)).
- **Late return showing tax**: 10% of the tax assessed (s. 99(2)). Failure to file within the time set in a notice under s. 60(4): also 10% (s. 99(3)).
- **Late nil or loss return**: G$50,000 (s. 99(2A)).
- Penalties are part of the tax assessed. The Commissioner-General may reduce or waive them (s. 99(4), s. 108).
- **Offences.** The GRA states a penalty of up to G$100,000 for failing to file. An incorrect return made without reasonable excuse carries a fine of up to G$100,000 and double the tax undercharged. Knowingly false returns carry treble the tax undercharged and up to six months' imprisonment (GRA, Interest and Penalties page).
- If tax is unpaid after the due date the GRA serves a demand note and can enforce after 30 days (s. 97(5)).

## Cross-border points

- **Residents** report worldwide income; foreign earned income, and foreign income of a person not ordinarily resident or not domiciled, is taxed on the amount received in Guyana (s. 5).
- **Non-residents** working in Guyana are taxed on that employment income (s. 6) and file like residents. Non-residents with no trade or business in Guyana bear 20% withholding tax on gross payments of interest, rent, royalties, management charges, personal services, commissions and fees, and on distributions (s. 39(1)(a),(b), s. 40, Third Schedule). A double taxation agreement can reduce the rate, but the payer withholds 20% unless the Commissioner-General accepts the reduced rate (s. 39(3)).
- **Relief for foreign tax**: double taxation agreements (s. 89) and unilateral relief (s. 90), claimed on return lines 49 and 50.

## Two worked examples that recompute

### Example 1: employee, one job, all of 2026

Asha is resident and works for one employer all year at G$400,000 a month, G$4,800,000 for 2026. She has no other income, no children and no insurance.

- NIS: 5.6% of pay up to the G$280,000 monthly ceiling, G$15,680 a month, G$188,160 for the year (rates in gy-payroll-social).
- Personal allowance: the greater of G$1,680,000 and one third of G$4,800,000 (G$1,600,000), so G$1,680,000.
- Chargeable income: 4,800,000 - 1,680,000 - 188,160 = G$2,931,840. All of it is under G$3,360,000, so all at 25%.
- Tax: G$732,960. If her employer deducted that much under PAYE, line 54 of her 2026 return (due 30 April 2027) is nil.

```oa-check
name: GY YI 2026, employee G$400,000 a month, 25% band only
inputs:
  monthly_pay: 400000
  months: 12
  nis_rate: 0.056
  nis_ceiling: 280000
  allowance_floor: 1680000
  band: 3360000
steps:
  - income: monthly_pay * months
  - nis: min(monthly_pay, nis_ceiling) * nis_rate * months
  - allowance: max(allowance_floor, income / 3)
  - chargeable: income - allowance - nis
  - tax: min(chargeable, band) * 0.25 + max(0, chargeable - band) * 0.35
expected:
  income: 4800000
  nis: 188160
  allowance: 1680000
  chargeable: 2931840
  tax: 732960
```

### Example 2: self-employed consultant, regular tax or the 2% minimum tax

Dev is a resident consultant. In 2026 his services turnover is G$18,000,000 and his business expenses G$6,000,000, so his profit is G$12,000,000. He pays G$500,000 of medical insurance premiums. Turnover is over G$10,000,000, so he must compare regular tax with the 2% minimum tax.

- Personal allowance: the greater of G$1,680,000 and one third of G$12,000,000, so G$4,000,000.
- Insurance: the lowest of G$500,000 paid, 10% of income (G$1,200,000) and G$600,000, so G$500,000.
- Chargeable income: 12,000,000 - 4,000,000 - 500,000 = G$7,500,000.
- Regular tax: 25% of 3,360,000 = 840,000, plus 35% of 4,140,000 = 1,449,000, total G$2,289,000.
- Minimum tax: 2% of G$18,000,000 = G$360,000. Regular tax is higher, so he pays G$2,289,000.

**The other way.** If expenses were G$17,000,000, profit would be G$1,000,000. The allowance of G$1,680,000 would take chargeable income to nil and regular tax to nil. The minimum tax of G$360,000 would then be due instead (s. 37A(4)). Turnover, not profit, decides whether the minimum tax can bite.

**Advance tax for 2027.** His 2026 tax becomes the base for the 2027 instalments: G$572,250 on each of 1 April, 1 July, 1 October and 31 December 2027, if 2027 income is expected to be the same.

```oa-check
name: GY YI 2026, self-employed services, regular tax vs 2% minimum tax
inputs:
  turnover: 18000000
  expenses: 6000000
  premiums: 500000
  low_case_expenses: 17000000
  allowance_floor: 1680000
  band: 3360000
steps:
  - profit: turnover - expenses
  - allowance: max(allowance_floor, profit / 3)
  - insurance: min(premiums, profit * 0.10, 600000)
  - chargeable: max(0, profit - allowance - insurance)
  - regular_tax: min(chargeable, band) * 0.25 + max(0, chargeable - band) * 0.35
  - minimum_tax: turnover * 0.02
  - tax_payable: max(regular_tax, minimum_tax)
  - quarterly_advance: tax_payable / 4
  - low_profit: turnover - low_case_expenses
  - low_chargeable: max(0, low_profit - max(allowance_floor, low_profit / 3))
  - low_regular_tax: min(low_chargeable, band) * 0.25 + max(0, low_chargeable - band) * 0.35
  - low_tax_payable: max(low_regular_tax, minimum_tax)
expected:
  profit: 12000000
  allowance: 4000000
  insurance: 500000
  chargeable: 7500000
  regular_tax: 2289000
  minimum_tax: 360000
  tax_payable: 2289000
  quarterly_advance: 572250
  low_chargeable: 0
  low_regular_tax: 0
  low_tax_payable: 360000
```

## Ask the client first

1. Were they resident in Guyana in 2026 (permanent home, or more than 183 days), and are they ordinarily resident and domiciled? Any foreign income, and how much was brought into Guyana?
2. What income did they have: employment (with every 7B slip), a second job, overtime, self-employment, rent, interest or foreign dividends?
3. If self-employed: services turnover (the G$10,000,000 minimum tax test), expenses, losses brought forward, and the advance tax paid in 2026.
4. Children under 18 at the start of 2026 or born in 2026, and who claims each child.
5. NIS contributions deducted, medical and life insurance premiums paid, and any first-home mortgage (lender, loan amount, Form 4).
6. Were they 60 or over, or incapacitated (savings interest exemption)?
