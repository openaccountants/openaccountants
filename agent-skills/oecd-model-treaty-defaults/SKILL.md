# OECD Model treaty provisions

## Scope

Use this Guide to interpret the structure of a tax treaty, then read the actual treaty before deciding a person's liability or a withholding rate. The OECD Model is a template, not binding bilateral law and not a fallback treaty for an uncovered country pair. This is a 2026 source reading, not professional approval. Written by Codex; not reviewed by an accountant.

This preserves the original job: residence tie-breakers; fixed, construction, agent and remote-work PE; freelancer profits and software royalties; gains; employment; exemption/credit; MAP; negotiated deviations and UN comparisons. It does not establish domestic tax rates, national filing procedures, or unverified national interpretations. The original metadata's reference to the update's art 9, Amount B and art 26 work is an edition signpost, not an operative rule for those topics here; obtain their actual text before advising on them.

## Ask the client first

1. Which two states, person or entity, income category, payment date and tax period are involved? Obtain the authentic in-force treaty, protocols and effective matching MLI positions.
2. Why does each state claim domestic residence, and is treaty residence/entitlement established? For an individual, obtain home availability, relations, presence and nationality; for an entity, management, constitution and any competent-authority determination.
3. What work, premises, projects, personnel and contract-making occur in each state? Obtain dates, home-working time, commercial reasons, related enterprises and functions/assets/risks.
4. What exactly is paid for, who beneficially owns it, what rights transfer, and what PE or employer bears it? For dividends obtain direct capital holding and its full period; for shares obtain underlying property values and history.
5. Which source tax was imposed, under which treaty permission, what residence tax is attributable to that same income, and what documents support relief or refund?
6. Is there a first notification of disputed taxation, a domestic appeal deadline, a MAP submission or an arbitration/court decision? Do not wait for an estimated resolution time before preserving rights.

## The method, step by step

### 1. Establish the actual treaty and its scope

Read the treaty rather than inferring its terms from membership of the OECD or from an older Model. Check persons and covered taxes, effective dates, protocols, transparent-entity treatment, entitlement limitations and the principal-purpose test where adopted. OECD art 29(9) can deny a benefit where obtaining it was one of the principal purposes unless the object-and-purpose exception is established; it is not automatically in every bilateral treaty. [OECD Model arts 1-4, 29 and 30-32](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2026/06/model-tax-convention-on-income-and-on-capital-2025-full-version_4fd97b89/db32e18b-en.pdf).

### 2. Residence: Article 4

Under Model art 4(1), residence rests on liability to tax by domicile, residence, place of management or a similar criterion; the definition also includes the state, its subdivisions/local authorities and a recognised pension fund. It excludes someone liable only on that state's source income or situated capital. Do the domestic-law analysis before using the treaty tie-breaker. [Art 4](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2026/06/model-tax-convention-on-income-and-on-capital-2025-full-version_4fd97b89/db32e18b-en.pdf#page=39).

For a dual-resident person other than an individual, art 4(3) says the authorities shall **endeavour** to determine treaty residence by agreement, considering effective management, incorporation/constitution and other relevant factors. Without agreement there is no treaty relief or exemption except to the extent and in the manner agreed. It is not an automatic effective-management tie-breaker or a guaranteed determination. An older bilateral treaty may retain a different test; its text controls. [Art 4(3)](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2026/06/model-tax-convention-on-income-and-on-capital-2025-full-version_4fd97b89/db32e18b-en.pdf#page=39).

### 3. PE: Article 5

The fixed-place test requires a place of business, a fixed location with sufficient permanence and business carried on through it. Offices, rooms, workshops, desks or a market pitch can be relevant facilities; ownership or a formal lease is not the test. The inclusion list names management places, branches, offices, factories, workshops and extraction places such as a mine, oil/gas well or quarry. These examples still require the relevant conditions. [Art 5(1)-(2), Commentary 5 paras 6-18](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2026/06/model-tax-convention-on-income-and-on-capital-2025-full-version_4fd97b89/db32e18b-en.pdf#page=43).

There is **no general statutory six-month minimum** in art 5(1). The Commentary's usual duration observations are subject to recurrent activities, a business wholly carried on for a short period, interruptions and the facts establishing permanence; a place initially intended to be short-lived can qualify retrospectively once the conditions become established. [Commentary 5 paras 28-34](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2026/06/model-tax-convention-on-income-and-on-capital-2025-full-version_4fd97b89/db32e18b-en.pdf).

#### Remote work and home offices: 2025 Commentary

The new guidance addresses an individual's home or other relevant place across the border; it does not replace all PE tests. Use the relevant facts and treaty interpretation, not a percentage as a universal PE safe harbour. [2025 update, Commentary 5 paras 44.1-44.21](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2025/11/the-2025-update-to-the-oecd-model-tax-convention_c7031e1b/5798080f-en.pdf).

### 4. Business profits: Article 7

Model art 7 assigns business profits to residence unless business is carried on through a PE in the other state; that state can tax the profits attributable to the PE. Attribution treats it as a separate independent enterprise in the same or similar conditions, considering functions performed, assets used and risks assumed through the PE and other parts of the enterprise. There is no general force of attraction for unrelated profits merely because the enterprise has other activity in that state. Apply separately governing income articles first under art 7(4). [Art 7(1)-(4)](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2026/06/model-tax-convention-on-income-and-on-capital-2025-full-version_4fd97b89/db32e18b-en.pdf#page=50).

Executive and general administrative costs remain an attribution/deduction question: do not quote the older Model's express expenses clause as the wording of current art 7. Assess expenses under the actual treaty version, attributable functions and applicable domestic rules; current art 7(2) is not an unlimited deduction entitlement. Corresponding adjustment under art 7(3) and consultation can be relevant to double taxation. [Art 7](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2026/06/model-tax-convention-on-income-and-on-capital-2025-full-version_4fd97b89/db32e18b-en.pdf#page=50).

For a freelancer, first classify the income and establish treaty scope and entitlement. If actual Article 7 applies and there is no PE in the client's state, that article does not give that state a business-profits taxing right just because the payer is there. A royalty, technical-service, independent-service or other special article can change the answer; absence of a PE alone does not resolve those classifications. [Arts 7(4), 12 and deleted art 14 Commentary](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2026/06/model-tax-convention-on-income-and-on-capital-2025-full-version_4fd97b89/db32e18b-en.pdf).

#### Royalties and software classification

Model art 12(2) covers payment for use/right to use the listed copyright, patent, trademark, design/model, plan, secret formula/process and industrial/commercial/scientific experience. Analyse the rights actually acquired, not an invoice's word "licence". [Art 12(2), Commentary 12 paras 12-17.4](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2026/06/model-tax-convention-on-income-and-on-capital-2025-full-version_4fd97b89/db32e18b-en.pdf#page=63).

### 6. Independent personal services and deleted Article 14

The OECD deleted Article 14 on **29 April 2000**. The Commentary records that no difference had been intended between PE and fixed-base concepts or between the relevant computation methods; it does not establish that every fixed base was an easier legal threshold. Current Model business income goes through art 7, but an actual treaty can retain an independent-services article, including a fixed-base or presence branch. Apply that actual text, not its date or article number alone. [Deleted art 14 Commentary opening](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2026/06/model-tax-convention-on-income-and-on-capital-2025-full-version_4fd97b89/db32e18b-en.pdf#page=836).

Where an actual article retains the fixed-base test, establish regular availability and limit source taxation to attributable income as that article requires. The historical UN illustration below demonstrates another presence branch; it is not automatically the bilateral rule.

### 7. Employment: Article 15

Subject to the separately named director, pension and government-service articles, employment income is residence-only unless employment is exercised in the other state. Even then the Model short-assignment exception preserves residence-only taxation **only if all three tests pass**: presence **not exceeding 183 days in any twelve-month period commencing or ending in the fiscal year**, payment by/on behalf of an employer not resident in that other state, and remuneration not borne by the employer's PE there. Source taxation after a failed test concerns remuneration derived from work there, not automatically all salary. [Art 15(1)-(2)](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2026/06/model-tax-convention-on-income-and-on-capital-2025-full-version_4fd97b89/db32e18b-en.pdf#page=68).

Use physical-presence counting: part days, arrival/departure, weekends and holidays spent there count. Transit between two outside points does not; whole days outside do not. Sickness days ordinarily count, with the Commentary's inability-to-leave exception where sickness prevented departure and the exemption would otherwise have applied. Days during which the person is resident in the source state are excluded from the art 15(2)(a) calculation under para 5.1; residence and the relevant period therefore need explicit analysis. The habitual-abode residence test is a different exercise. [Commentary 15 paras 4-6.2](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2026/06/model-tax-convention-on-income-and-on-capital-2025-full-version_4fd97b89/db32e18b-en.pdf).

### 9. MAP and arbitration: Article 25

Taxation contrary to the treaty can be presented to **either state's competent authority within three years of the first notification** under Model art 25(1), irrespective of domestic remedies. If the objection appears justified and unilateral relief is unavailable, the authority shall endeavour to resolve it with the other authority. An agreement is implemented notwithstanding domestic time limits; the endeavour is not a guaranteed agreement. Authorities also address interpretation/application difficulties and may consult about double taxation not otherwise provided for. [Art 25(1)-(4)](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2026/06/model-tax-convention-on-income-and-on-capital-2025-full-version_4fd97b89/db32e18b-en.pdf#page=88).

The current Model includes arbitration in art 25(5): after **two years from all necessary information being supplied to both authorities**, unresolved issues go to arbitration **on the person's written request**, subject to its conditions. No arbitration of issues already decided by a court/administrative tribunal; the decision binds both states unless a directly affected person does not accept the implementing agreement. Check whether the actual treaty contains this paragraph and its operative procedural arrangements. It is wrong to say the Model has no arbitration or that either state alone elects it. [Art 25(5)](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2026/06/model-tax-convention-on-income-and-on-capital-2025-full-version_4fd97b89/db32e18b-en.pdf#page=88).

## Worked cases

### A. UK employee in Germany: original short and long assignment cases

Assume an ordinary UK treaty-resident employee, no special director/pension/government/ship/offshore category, an actual UK employer rather than a German employer, no German PE bearing the remuneration, and **150 total presence days in every relevant twelve-month period**. The actual UK-Germany employment article is **14**, not the Model's 15. All art 14(2) conditions pass, so remuneration from that employment is taxable only in the UK under the stated treaty assumptions. If presence becomes **190 days** in a relevant period, the exception fails and Germany may tax the remuneration derived from work exercised there; a failed employer or PE-burden condition can also remove the exception even on the shorter stay. This does not calculate either country's domestic tax or payroll filing obligation. [UK-Germany art 14(1)-(3), with neighbouring arts 15-20 checked for excluded categories](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/germany-tax-treaties/2010-uk-germany-double-taxation-convention-as-amended-by-the-2021-protocol-in-force).

### B. Exemption versus credit: original comparison made explicitly illustrative

Assume the same foreign income of **10,000 units**, a lawfully treaty-permitted source charge of **10%**, and attributable pre-relief residence tax of **40%** on the same base. These are supplied hypothetical inputs, **not Indian, German or UK tax rates**. If the applicable relief article truly exempts this income without an applicable switch, source tax is 1,000 and residence tax on that item is zero; progression on other income is outside this example. Under an ordinary art 23B-style credit, residence tax is 4,000, credit is 1,000 and residual residence tax is 3,000; combined tax is 4,000. Which method applies is a legal condition, not a taxpayer election. [OECD arts 23A(1)-(4), 23B(1)](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2026/06/model-tax-convention-on-income-and-on-capital-2025-full-version_4fd97b89/db32e18b-en.pdf).

```oa-check
name: Hypothetical exemption versus ordinary credit, identical base
inputs:
  income: 10000
  source_rate: 0.10
  residence_rate: 0.40
steps:
  source_tax: income * source_rate
  residence_before: income * residence_rate
  exemption_residence: 0
  exemption_combined: source_tax + exemption_residence
  credit: min(source_tax, residence_before)
  credit_residual: residence_before - credit
  credit_combined: source_tax + credit_residual
expected:
  source_tax: 1000
  exemption_combined: 1000
  credit: 1000
  credit_residual: 3000
  credit_combined: 4000
```

### C. Credit edge case: foreign tax exceeds the residence ceiling

Assume **10,000 units**, lawfully permitted source tax **40%** and attributable residence tax **10%**, all on the same base. These remain hypothetical inputs. Source tax is 4,000, pre-credit residence tax is 1,000 and the ordinary credit is capped at 1,000; residual residence tax is zero and combined tax is 4,000. The remaining 3,000 foreign tax is not refunded by this Model credit formula. Whether some national deduction, carryforward, treaty refund or dispute route exists requires its own law; no permanent unrecoverability conclusion is drawn. [OECD art 23B(1), art 25](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2026/06/model-tax-convention-on-income-and-on-capital-2025-full-version_4fd97b89/db32e18b-en.pdf).

## When to refuse or refer

1. Never assume a bilateral treaty matches the OECD Model, or apply its figures where a pair is absent from another Guide.
2. Never apply the deleted independent-services article without an actual retained provision; equally, do not overwrite a retained article with the current Model.
3. Never assume the employment counting period or presence alone settles the exemption; read the actual period and all conditions.
4. Never ignore anti-fragmentation, agency conditions or relevant remote-work Commentary. Establish whether those provisions and that interpretation apply to the actual treaty; later Commentary is not itself an amendment.
5. Never assume source-state royalty exemption without checking the actual article, classification and connection/beneficial-owner conditions.
6. Never promise quick MAP resolution or a universal duration. Preserve the actual filing deadline and domestic procedural rights.
7. Never treat this negotiating Model as binding law. Refer unresolved treaty interpretation or entitlement to a qualified international tax professional.

## Filing and payment

This is an allocation/interpretation Guide, not a national filing manual. Do not supply a domestic form, portal field, due date, withholding relief-at-source procedure or repayment route from these Model articles. Hand off the person, tax period, income type, source/residence computations, actual treaty permission, supporting certificates and any disputed first-notification date to the relevant country filing Guide.
