# Armenia corporate and personal cross-border preparation v0.2

## Who this applies to

Use this Guide for ordinary corporate 2026 cross-border receipts/payments and associated residence evidence, with the personal departure screen below. It is a working-paper method, not a certificate of treaty entitlement. Domestic rules come first; a ratified treaty and its current protocol/MLI require their own analysis. Published residence, nonresident-return and treaty-application forms are mapped below; no bilateral treaty rate or taxpayer's entitlement is established by their existence. [Code 2/19(7)][code]

Resident organisations are those located in Armenia, with organisation location defined by state registration; do not replace this statutory test with an invented universal place-of-effective-management rule. Resident profit taxpayers bring Armenian and foreign income into the tax base; nonresident corporate profit taxpayers are taxed on Armenian-source income, with separate PE and no-PE bases. Individual entrepreneurs, investment funds and other special regimes require their own classification rather than automatic application of the ordinary corporate rate. [22–23, 103–105, 125][code]

For individual residence screening, Article 25 has the 183-or-more-days test, counting both arrival and departure days, and independent vital-interests/state-service limbs. If resident on a day under the stated limbs, the Code treats the person as resident for the entire tax year. Fewer than 183 days alone does not prove nonresidence. This is screening only: personal income categories, payroll, pension/social obligations and treaty dual-residence allocation are not computed by the corporate withholding table below. [25][code]

### Establish the PE and registration position

Article 27(1) lists places of business including production, management, extraction, construction, branch/representative operations and specified joint activity. Several listed activities do not have a universal 183-day grace period. The separate services/work limb in paragraph 2 uses at least 183 calendar days in a tax year and related-project rules. Exhibition/fair goods sales have a separate more-than-30-day rule. Preparatory/support relief has its own conditions, direct-beneficiary requirement and less-than-three-year period. Dependent agents and joint activity need distinct tests. [27(1)–(5), (9)–(10)][code]

Paragraphs 7–8 distinguish activity characteristics, tax registration and the statutory calculation/payment consequence of an unregistered PE-like operation. No registration does not prove no PE risk. Conversely, do not claim that registration alone resolves treaty attribution. Retain contracts, actual conduct, staff/project days, premises, authority, registration date and income documents. The former paragraph 6 labour-supply rule was repealed by HO-377-N, effective after its December 2025 publication; do not use an older English consolidation to claim a blanket labour-supply PE exception in 2026. [27][code]; [HO-377-N Articles 1, 7][pe-amend]

| No-PE dividends | 5% | 2026, [125(4)(3.1)][code] |

Treaty relief, exempt-income provisions, beneficial receipt, instrument and recipient status must be resolved for the particular transaction. The examples assume no relief and ordinary established corporate scope. [103–105, 107–108, 125][code]

Article 14 generally starts those amendments on **1 February 2027**, with a separate expense provision effective earlier. It also reaches dividends distributed/paid from earlier-period profits. Neither July enactment nor an earlier profit year makes the future 15% bank-dividend rate current for an October 2026 event. Recheck the complete transition and later implementing instruments for 2027. [HO-385-N Articles 2, 4–7, 14][dividend-amend]

## The base

### Classify the source before the rate

Article 107 determines source under the profit-tax rules, separately from VAT place of supply. Goods, actual work/services, intermediary activities, property, transport/post, telecoms, audit and passive receipts have different connecting factors. Interest/royalty and dividend payer connections are not interchangeable with the place where a supplier's staff sit. Armenian real-property rent is expressly sourced by property location. Inseparable work/service income can be fully Armenian-source under the stated rule, rather than apportioned by an arbitrary percentage. [107][code]

Offshore consultancy, legal, accounting, management, expert, marketing, advertising, translation, engineering and similar services can be Armenian-source under paragraph 4. **From 1 July 2026**, HO-234-N Article 20 broadens that paragraph's recipient language to a resident organisation, Armenia-registered individual entrepreneur or the stated nonresident PE. Do not use the older English phrase limited to a resident profit taxpayer to omit a payment merely because the Armenian recipient uses a special regime. An unrelated offshore supplier is not automatically exempt. Actual treaty business-profits/PE and domestic relief remain separate, unverified questions here. [107(4)][code]; [HO-234-N Articles 20, 33][july-amend]

Nonresident capital appreciation has document-specific initial/book/revalued-value rules in Article 107(3)(5); absent the specified evidence, the statute can treat the entire alienation value as appreciation. No reconstructed acquisition cost without documents. Full asset/security/immovable-property, related-party and treaty methods remain open, so no property-gain return is certified here. [107(3)(5), 125(4)(4)][code]

### Separate attributable PE profit from other receipts

For an ordinary registered corporate PE, Article 105(1)(3) uses taxable profit with applicable deductions, and Article 133 requires separated accounting and settlement-document identification of the PE for deductible expense. A head-office charge is not deductible just because a group spreadsheet allocates it. Pricing, actual benefit and current deduction restrictions require further evidence. [105, 110, 133][code]; [companion TP method](am-transfer-pricing.md)

Article 125(1)/(5) ties attribution to the PE's supporting income documents, regardless of merely naming/not naming the PE as receiver in a contract. Non-attributed receipts have a separate gross-income/rate route. Article 132(4) separately addresses nonresident dividends regardless of attribution. Keep a receipt-by-receipt attribution register, then reconcile any tax-agent withholding credits under Article 130 rather than taxing the same receipt twice. The example below assumes the attribution and allowable costs already established; it does not verify every deduction. [105, 125, 130, 132–133][code]

### Payment events and gross/net agreement

Article 132 withholding operates on payment events, including part payments, property, set-off, restructuring/novation, specified claim assignment/waiver and the Article 109(3)(5) dividend-distribution decision. Cash leaving a bank is not the only trigger. From July 2026, HO-234-N Article 27 changes claim-assignment wording and clarifies the waiver event; use current Armenian text. Returned payments have paragraph-3 timing/corrected-report conditions, not automatic erasure of a filed liability. [109, 132][code]; [HO-234-N 27, 33][july-amend]

For a contract whose taxable amount is established as gross, withholding = gross × applicable rate and net = gross − withholding. If the agreement instead guarantees the recipient a net amount and the payer bears tax, first establish its legal/contractual character and tax base; under the examples' single-rate assumptions gross = net / (1 − rate). This is algebra under an assumed taxable gross base, not a statutory election or proof all payer-borne taxes are deductible. [105, 125, 132][code]

### Resident foreign-tax relief

Article 20 includes foreign taxable objects and permits reduction for corresponding taxes collected abroad under foreign law, capped at Armenian tax on those foreign objects calculated under the Code. Separate the foreign income/deduction/base reconciliation, comparable tax and collection evidence, currency/period and any treaty restriction. Do not credit VAT, penalties or an unsupported foreign assessment as corporate profit tax. Actual tax equivalence and multi-country/category allocation need the underlying instruments. [20, 130(1)(2)][code]

**Two limits are different.** First calculate the Article 20(3) foreign-object cap; foreign tax above that cap is not automatically eligible carryforward. Then compare the legally deductible amount with the year's corresponding tax liability. Article 20(4) carries the deductible amount exceeding that annual liability into subsequent reporting years of the same tax type. Do not label all excess foreign tax refundable or carryable. Use the corporate Guide's signed 93–98 mapping and statutory eligible-use/carry workpaper; its printed field 95 formula defect must not consume credit above the annual liability. Competing relief, foreign adjustments and actual electronic acceptance require the stated separate evidence. [20(2)–(4), 130–131][code]

Amounts are AMD without luma. Reconcile all payment events, including noncash events under 132, with the original-period ledger. ### Annual no-agent and PE return routes

Current **749-N Annex 4** is the actual no-PE/non-agent annual return under 134(3). Fields 1–4 are TIN if any, full name, location and reporting year. Fields 5–10 record ordinary taxable receipts by code and corresponding 125(4) tax; 11–16 separately record treaty-reduced taxable receipts/tax; 17 totals tax 10+16. Instruction 1 excludes 108 nonincome and fully treaty-exempt receipts from this form. Do not import that omission into the quarterly agent form, whose section 2 includes exempt treaty payments. The annual form uses **001 insurance, 002 reinsurance, 003 freight, 004 dividend, 005 interest, 006 royalty, 007 rent, 008 appreciation, 009 securities appreciation, 010 specified Pan-Armenian Bank dividend, 099 other**. A printed historic label does not create a current rate or relief. [749-N Annex 4 form/instructions 1–15][annualform]; [134(3)/136(3)][code]

Registered PEs use **749-N Annex 1**: section 1 contains only attributable income/deductions; section 2 contains non-attributed income **received from non-agents**. Section 2 ordinary tax is field 6, treaty-reduced tax field 12, total field 13; this feeds section 1 field 99. PE-attributable withholding credit belongs in section 1 field 97 only for the corresponding income included in that section's 6–40 rows; it is not a resident foreign-tax credit. Field 98 reconciles attributable tax after the eligible withholding credit; 100 adds 99. Then reconcile calculated advances 101 and the actual payment/account record. Final agent withholding on non-attributed receipts is not a second non-agent annual liability. [130(3)–(5)/134(1)][code]; [749-N Annex 1 instructions 1/26–37][annualform]

Ordinary tax returns follow electronic filing 53(5), with its stated exceptions. Keep 53(7)'s system receipt/registration acknowledgment as filing evidence; a locally completed spreadsheet is not a return. Corrections under 54 retain the original period and are subject to 53(10)–(12), including the distinct liability-increasing route. Account settlement follows 325: pension obligations have priority, then the statutory oldest-due liability order; a payment into the unified account is not itself tax revenue until allocated to discharge a liability. Do not assume a bank debit settled this particular nonresident tax. [53–54/325][code]

### Armenian residence evidence

Current Article 314 provides electronic applications/certification, including qualifying foreign-country forms, separate years/countries and Armenian certificates with English/Russian on request. It distinguishes the organisation/registered-entrepreneur route from other individuals, including the latter's vital-interests evidence, and category-specific immediate/two-working-day processing provisions. Those legal clocks do not establish that a particular incomplete application was accepted. [314][code]; [HO-377-N 6–7][pe-amend]

Actual 345-N as revised by 1629-N has Form 1 for organisations/individual entrepreneurs, Form 2 for individuals and Form 3 certificate. Reconcile TIN/legal name, tax year, destination country, identity/status grounds, supporting explanation and any foreign form. Certificate QR/control code, tax year and country must match the intended use. An Armenian certificate supports Armenian residence evidence; it is not a foreign recipient's certificate or blanket treaty relief. The SRC foreign-citizen service page describes that specific application route; do not apply it as every corporate process. No login, submission or issued certificate was obtained here. [314][code]; [345-N forms][certificate]; [SRC service][service]

## Penalties and interest

Article 401 ordinary tax late-payment interest is 0.075% per overdue day, within its scope and 730-day maximum. Article 402 provides a late-return fine based on 5% of the relevant calculated tax for each completed 15-day period, subject to its calculation stop and total-tax cap. Article 403 provides ordinary under-declaration sanctions of 50%, or 100% for its specified recurrence, with express exceptions. Do not stack these automatically or reuse transfer-pricing documentation sanctions as nonresident-return fines. Establish the exact offence, tax-agent liability, original due date, service, correction and applicable exceptions. [401–403][code]

The former PE staff-supply treatment, missing noncash withholding events and unjustified treaty relief are material classification risks. Full administrative-offence rules, amended-return relief, audit limitation, service/appeal/suspension deadlines, refunds and enforcement are not cleared by these numeric summaries. Do not invent an appeal period. [27, 54, 132, 401–403][code]

## Cross-border points

Before a treaty conclusion obtain the actual treaty/protocol, operative year, covered taxes/persons, residence/tie-breaker, payment article, PE/business-profits interaction and applicable anti-abuse/MLI provisions. Article 19(7) delegates the procedure to Government. No treaty count, residence certificate alone or generic model establishes exemption. [2/19/20/27][code]

### Published treaty applications: original order and actual amendment

ARLIS currently records **18 May 1998 order 01/29** and its **31 August 1999 amendment 01/13** as active. They are distinct from repealed Government 1398-N. The base order's old separate Armenian/English-copy requirement was changed: 01/13 allows **Armenian, Russian or English** and replaces the forms/appendices. Read both acts; the base `/latest` text does not silently incorporate this amendment. Do not revive 1398-N or describe its 613-N repealer as a replacement. The current Code 304 service and SRC specimen below supply the ordinary claimant/approval/account mapping; Code 447(8) supplies the conditional continuation rule. Current statutory requirements prevail over old office names and labels. [01/29 metadata and both procedures][treatyforms]; [01/13 paragraphs 1–3/forms][treatyformsamend]

### Personal source, property and no-agent filing

Build a **person-by-year and receipt-by-receipt** workpaper. Domestic residence under 25 is separate from citizenship, permission to reside and a treaty tie-breaker. Resident personal income includes Armenian and foreign receipts under 141(1)(1); an established nonresident's personal scope is Armenian-source income, with 141(1)(2)'s PE-attribution and defined external-economic-activity exclusions. Registered business profit remains separate from personal PIT. [25, 140–144][code]

| Receipt | Domestic source test before rate/exemption |
|---|---|
| Salary/equivalents and civil-contract income | 144(1)(1)–(2) tests the contract with a resident taxpayer or a nonresident profit-taxpayer operating through an Armenian PE. Do not use physical workdays alone instead of that statutory contract test. |
| Nonresident employer's hired staff in Armenia | 144(1)(5) separately covers Armenian work/services where the wage is an Armenian registered PE's expense, regardless of deductibility, and/or the staff are engaged in that PE's activity. Keep the actual duties, place, PE and expense evidence. |
| Passive income | Apply 144(1)(3)'s reference to 107(3): resident-company participation for dividends; the specified resident organisation/PE/Armenian-citizen payer for interest and royalties; Armenian immovable-property or the stated movable-property connection for rent; and the applicable Armenian asset/right connection for alienation. The corporate appreciation computation printed in 107 does not replace the individual's 143/147/150 base and rate. |
| Other income | Apply 144(1)(4)'s 107(5) payer/source connection and 144(2)'s rule treating otherwise unassignable other income as wholly Armenian-source. Income outside the preceding domestic-source provisions is foreign-source under 144(3). Do not automatically import the corporate offshore-service list in 107(4) into personal income. |

[144, 107(3)/(5)][code]. Preserve contract, title, payer status, place/source facts, actual 142 income event and foreign evidence. A foreign bank account is not proof of foreign source, and an Armenian bank receipt is not proof of Armenian-source taxable profit.

For an **ordinary personally owned apartment/property sale**, identify title, seller's personal versus entrepreneurial/developer status, buyer's status, source and receipt date first. Under 147(1)(16), proceeds received from a natural-person buyer who is **neither entrepreneur nor notary** are exempt, except the specified developer-premises and crypto-asset branches. A company buyer does not meet that private-buyer exemption. For an otherwise taxable ordinary personal property disposal, 150(9) charges **10% of total income**, not 10% of net appreciation after cost. The developer branch has its own 145 base and 150(11) rate; it is not this ordinary branch. Land-alienation income has the separate exemption in **147(1)(38)** regardless of designated land purpose; separate the actual land component from a building rather than describing every real-estate sale as land. [141–145, 147(1)(16)/(38), 150(9)/(11)][code]

Keep exemption and declaration omission separate: the universal resident-citizen declarant's private-property sale follows the individual Guide's actual family/225-N receipt threshold rules. If the receipt is taxable, establish the payer's **tax-agent** duty under 152–153. Calculate tax only once, reconcile actual withholding/settlement, and use the agent or individual's actual reporting population. Personal rent uses 150(7)'s basic 10% and separate annual rent-excess test; do not apply the corporate nonresident rate table to an individual. [150–153, 156][code]; [individual declaration method](armenia-income-tax.md)

**No agent does not always mean annual filing.** Code 151(2) uses a **monthly** period for salary/equivalents and civil-contract work/services where an Armenian-ratified treaty releases the payer from tax-agent duties. That individual files the **300-N Annex 2 simplified return** and pays by the **20th of the next month**, under 156(4)(1)/158(2)(1). Establish that precise payer exemption before using it. A foreign employer by itself does not prove the 151(2) treaty branch. [151–154, 156(4), 158][code]; [300-N Annex 2](https://www.arlis.am/hy/acts/223829)

Other personal self-assessed income in **151(3)** uses the **annual** period. Under 156(4)(2), file the **300-N Annex 3 annual return** from **2 March through 1 July of the following year**, moving the opening day as the statute states; pay the independently calculated annual tax by **1 July** under 158(2)(2). Separately, 156(4.1)'s universal declaration covers adult Armenian-resident Armenian citizens as at 31 December. Do not treat that citizenship/status gate as removing an otherwise applicable 156(4) obligation for a foreign citizen or nonresident. The special 2025-income November-2026 transition must be tested against its actual statutory population, not extended to every no-agent foreign filer. [151(3), 154, 156(4)/(4.1), 158][code]; [300-N Annex 3](https://www.arlis.am/hy/acts/223829); [HO-76-N 5(2)](https://www.arlis.am/hy/acts/206337)

Reconcile category totals, non-taxable but reportable amounts, agent withholding, separate monthly self-assessments, foreign tax and available credit in their proper form fields. Use 20's corresponding-foreign-tax object cap and separate annual-liability/carry test, supported by the foreign-authority evidence required by 300-N; do not deduct foreign social insurance as foreign PIT or count a monthly liability again as unpaid annual tax. The companion individual Guide maps the actual published annual form and signed-refund condition. No live prefill or filing receipt was obtained. [20, 53–54, 143, 150–158][code]; [300-N Annexes 2–3](https://www.arlis.am/hy/acts/223829)

**Departure:** re-test residence each year from actual travel days and retained vital interests. Code 25(5)'s whole-year rule means a flight does not itself end that year's worldwide-income treatment. In a later established nonresident year, classify continuing Armenian salary, rent, dividends, royalties and property receipts through the source/rate/exemption and agent/no-agent sequence above. Retain bank/portal access, authority notices, the relevant calendar and evidence of paid tax so that next-year filing and refunds are actually followed through. No unsupported special pre-departure deadline or automatic tax clearance is imposed here. A treaty override still requires the actual treaty and operative relief procedure identified separately below. [20, 25, 141, 144, 150–158][code]

## Two worked examples that recompute

All amounts are hypothetical AMD. Corporate recipient/scope, source, no relief and the stated dates/classification are assumed established. Examples are workpapers, not accepted filings.

### 1. Ordinary no-PE corporate royalty

October 2026 Armenian tax agent pays an established gross royalty of 10 million to a foreign company without an attributable PE. No treaty/domestic relief. At the 10% passive-income rate, withholding is 1 million and recipient net 9 million. Report/payment follow the quarter; an individual royalty requires separate PIT analysis. [107(3), 125(4)(2), 132, 134, 136][code]

```oa-check
name: Armenia corporate royalty gross withholding
inputs:
  gross_royalty: 10000000
steps:
  - withholding: gross_royalty * 0.10
  - recipient_net: gross_royalty - withholding
expected:
  withholding: 1000000
  recipient_net: 9000000
```

### 4. Foreign-object cap is not unlimited carryforward

Ordinary resident company: established foreign taxable profit 30 million and domestic taxable profit 20 million, no other relief/prepayment. At 18%, total Armenian tax 9 million and foreign-object cap 5.4 million. Corresponding foreign tax actually collected is 7 million: deductible credit 5.4 million, Armenian balance 3.6 million. The 1.6 million above the foreign-object cap is not an Article 20(4) carryforward merely because it was paid abroad. [20, 125(1), 130][code]

```oa-check
name: Armenia foreign object tax credit limitation
inputs:
  foreign_taxable_profit: 30000000
  domestic_taxable_profit: 20000000
  corresponding_foreign_tax_collected: 7000000
steps:
  - total_armenian_tax: (foreign_taxable_profit + domestic_taxable_profit) * 0.18
  - foreign_object_cap: foreign_taxable_profit * 0.18
  - deductible_credit: min(corresponding_foreign_tax_collected, foreign_object_cap)
  - armenian_balance: total_armenian_tax - deductible_credit
  - foreign_tax_above_object_cap: corresponding_foreign_tax_collected - deductible_credit
expected:
  total_armenian_tax: 9000000
  deductible_credit: 5400000
  armenian_balance: 3600000
  foreign_tax_above_object_cap: 1600000
```
