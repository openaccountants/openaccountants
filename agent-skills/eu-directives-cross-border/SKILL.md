# EU Directives Cross Border

## Scope

Use this skill to identify EU cross-border corporate dividend, interest, royalty, anti-avoidance, disclosure and reorganisation rules for 2026. It is a framework for the EU-27; it does not establish that a particular member state implemented an option, that a named legal form qualifies, or that a filing is complete. Contributor: OpenAccountants. Skill version 1.1. The former May 2026 validation/proposal status is superseded by the source reads below.

It covers Parent-Subsidiary Directive 2011/96/EU (PSD), Interest and Royalties Directive 2003/49/EC (IRD), ATAD I 2016/1164 as amended by ATAD II 2017/952, DAC6 2018/822, DAC7 2021/514 and DAC8 2023/2226 through the consolidated Administrative Cooperation Directive, and Merger Directive 2009/133/EC. Open the cited operative articles and the relevant domestic transposition before giving a relief or reporting conclusion.

Individual freelancers, sole traders and ordinary unrelated B2B payments do not acquire PSD/IRD group-company relief merely because the transaction crosses an EU border. Parent companies receiving subsidiary dividends and multi-entity EU groups are the PSD use cases; associated-company interest/royalties are the IRD use case. Domestic law and a bilateral treaty can provide separate relief. See `withholding-tax-matrix.md` and `tax-residency-planning.md`.

## Ask the client first

- Identify both states, the payment/transaction date and tax period, each legal form, residence (including treaty residence), tax status and any permanent establishment (PE).
- Obtain direct and indirect ownership, voting/profit rights, related-party holdings, holding dates, beneficial-owner evidence, agreements and the commercial history of the structure.
- For ATAD, obtain tax-adjusted borrowing costs/EBITDA, group accounts, foreign actual tax and the domestic counterfactual, income categories, significant people functions, substance and mismatch treatment in each jurisdiction.
- For DAC6, obtain every step/date, all intermediaries and their EU nexus, privilege position, hallmarks, tax-benefit analysis and evidence of any duplicate report. For platforms/crypto, obtain activity, operator/provider nexus, seller/user residence, exclusion data, transaction counts/amounts and national registration details.
- For reorganisations, obtain legal operation, company/shareholder tax bases, PE links, cash consideration and business reasons. Require a specialist; do not compute exit tax.

### 1. Parent-Subsidiary Directive

PSD relief concerns distributions between qualifying companies of different member states and specified cross-state PE situations. Each company must have an Annex I Part A form, EU tax residence without treaty residence outside the EU, and liability without an option or exemption to an Annex I Part B tax (or its replacement). Do not equate every GmbH, Ltd, SA, SRL or BV label with eligibility. [PSD arts 1–2 and Annex I](https://publications.europa.eu/resource/cellar/1d1ad431-c8c4-11e4-bbe1-01aa75ed71a1.0020.02/DOC_1).

| PSD rule or threshold | Conditions and consequence | Official basis |
|---|---|---|
| Capital relationship | Minimum 10% capital holding in the qualifying subsidiary. Voting rights replace capital only through the bilateral-agreement option, not automatically. | [PSD art 3(1)–(2)](https://publications.europa.eu/resource/cellar/1d1ad431-c8c4-11e4-bbe1-01aa75ed71a1.0020.02/DOC_1) |
| Holding period | A member state may decline relief where the qualifying holding is not maintained for an uninterrupted period of at least 2 years. This is a national option, not a universal Directive holding-period requirement. Local provisional relief/refund procedures remain to be checked. | [PSD art 3(2)(b)](https://publications.europa.eu/resource/cellar/1d1ad431-c8c4-11e4-bbe1-01aa75ed71a1.0020.02/DOC_1) |
| Source withholding | Qualifying subsidiary distributions are exempt from withholding; the parent state cannot charge withholding on the profits received. The definition excludes the subsidiary-state corporate-tax prepayment described in art 7(1). | [PSD arts 5–7](https://publications.europa.eu/resource/cellar/1d1ad431-c8c4-11e4-bbe1-01aa75ed71a1.0020.02/DOC_1) |
| Parent-state double-tax relief | Except subsidiary liquidation, the parent/PE state exempts profits to the extent not deductible by the subsidiary and taxes deductible profits, or taxes with underlying-tax credit. Lower-tier credit requires the company/holding conditions at every tier and is capped at corresponding tax due. Fiscal-transparency rules preserve the art 4(2) relief mechanics. | [PSD art 4(1)–(2)](https://publications.europa.eu/resource/cellar/1d1ad431-c8c4-11e4-bbe1-01aa75ed71a1.0020.02/DOC_1) |
| Holding expenses | States may deny holding-related charges and losses caused by distributions. If management costs are fixed as a flat rate, the amount cannot exceed 5% of distributed profits. Do not present this option as a universal exemption haircut. | [PSD art 4(3)](https://publications.europa.eu/resource/cellar/1d1ad431-c8c4-11e4-bbe1-01aa75ed71a1.0020.02/DOC_1) |

Apply PSD art 1(2)–(4): benefits are denied to non-genuine arrangements put in place for a main purpose, or one of the main purposes, of a tax advantage defeating the Directive’s object or purpose. Assess all relevant facts; non-genuine means lacking valid commercial reasons reflecting economic reality. Separate non-genuine steps can matter. Domestic/treaty anti-fraud and anti-abuse rules remain relevant. [PSD art 1(2)–(4)](https://publications.europa.eu/resource/cellar/1d1ad431-c8c4-11e4-bbe1-01aa75ed71a1.0020.02/DOC_1).

Nordcurrent, C-228/24, was decided on 3 April 2025. It concerns the parent’s corporation-tax exemption as well as the PSD’s wider anti-abuse framework. A non-conduit subsidiary can be abusive, but only if the constituent elements are established. An initially commercial arrangement can later become non-genuine; a dividend-date-only snapshot is insufficient. Non-genuineness alone does not establish the required tax advantage: assess the overall tax effect and purpose, including relevant foreign taxation. The old September 2024 date is corrected. Do not automatically transplant this judgment into every IRD or Merger Directive case. [CJEU C-228/24, paras 21–55 and operative ruling](https://juris.curia.europa.eu/juris/document/document.jsf?cid=6640856&dir=&docid=297541&doclang=en&mode=req&occ=first&pageIndex=0&part=1&text=).

### 2. Interest and Royalties Directive

IRD exempts qualifying interest/royalty payments from source-state taxes, whether withheld or assessed, when the beneficial owner is a qualifying company of another member state or a qualifying PE in another member state. It does not exempt the recipient’s normal corporate tax. Company conditions are Annex form, EU tax residence without treaty residence outside the EU, and liability without exemption to an art 3(a) tax or its replacement. [IRD arts 1(1), 1(9), 3(a)](https://publications.europa.eu/resource/cellar/108ba38b-c91c-4625-87f8-2a7fb03fbb38.0006.03/DOC_2).

| IRD test | Complete condition | Official basis |
|---|---|---|
| Association | Direct minimum 25% capital holding in either direction, or a third qualifying EU company directly holding at least 25% of each. A member state may replace capital by voting rights. Indirect ownership alone does not satisfy this rule. | [IRD art 3(b)](https://publications.europa.eu/resource/cellar/108ba38b-c91c-4625-87f8-2a7fb03fbb38.0006.03/DOC_2) |
| Holding period | A state may refuse the Directive where the art 3(b) conditions were not maintained uninterrupted for at least 2 years (in words on the page). Confirm the actual national choice and procedure. | [IRD art 1(10)](https://publications.europa.eu/resource/cellar/108ba38b-c91c-4625-87f8-2a7fb03fbb38.0006.03/DOC_2) |
| Beneficial owner | Company receives for its own benefit, not as agent, trustee, authorised signatory or other intermediary. For a recipient PE the debt/right must be effectively connected and the receipt subject to the specified PE-state tax. | [IRD art 1(4)–(5)](https://publications.europa.eu/resource/cellar/108ba38b-c91c-4625-87f8-2a7fb03fbb38.0006.03/DOC_2) |
| PE direction | A paying PE counts only to the extent the payment is tax-deductible there; associated-company conditions also apply. Art 1(2) locates the source, rather than imposing the old blanket recipient-PE exclusion. Third-state PEs are excluded; apply art 1(6)–(8) to avoid duplicate company/PE treatment. | [IRD art 1(2)–(8)](https://publications.europa.eu/resource/cellar/108ba38b-c91c-4625-87f8-2a7fb03fbb38.0006.03/DOC_2) |

Interest includes income from debt claims of every kind, including security/mortgage/profit-participation forms and bond/debenture premiums or prizes; late-payment penalty charges are excluded. Royalties cover literary/artistic/scientific copyright including software, patents, trade marks, designs/models, plans, secret formula/processes, information concerning industrial/commercial/scientific experience, and use of industrial/commercial/scientific equipment. [IRD art 2](https://publications.europa.eu/resource/cellar/108ba38b-c91c-4625-87f8-2a7fb03fbb38.0006.03/DOC_2).

Source states may exclude profit distributions/repayment of capital, debt rights to share profits, debt exchangeable into profit-participation rights, and debt with no principal repayment or repayment more than 50 years after issue. Where a special relationship inflates a payment, the Directive covers only the arm’s-length amount. Art 5 preserves fraud/abuse provisions and permits refusal for tax evasion, avoidance or abuse purposes; beneficial-owner and company conditions still apply. [IRD arts 4–5](https://publications.europa.eu/resource/cellar/108ba38b-c91c-4625-87f8-2a7fb03fbb38.0006.03/DOC_2).

For actual relief, identify the state’s attestation, prior-decision or refund procedure under art 1(11)–(16), including the evidence of company status, beneficial ownership and association. Do not treat legal eligibility as an automatically completed filing. [IRD art 1(11)–(16)](https://publications.europa.eu/resource/cellar/108ba38b-c91c-4625-87f8-2a7fb03fbb38.0006.03/DOC_2).

The Directive does not supply association relief for unrelated B2B loans, unrelated IP licences, or individual-to-company payments. Research domestic law and the relevant bilateral treaty independently; do not apply the IRD percentage as a treaty rate.

Historic art 6 transitional withholding is not a general 2026 entitlement to reduced withholding, nor proof that every EU payment is automatically exempt. The original named-country transition claim is retained for historical source recovery under Sources; apply current company, ownership, PE, beneficial-owner and anti-abuse conditions. [IRD art 6 and arts 1–5](https://publications.europa.eu/resource/cellar/108ba38b-c91c-4625-87f8-2a7fb03fbb38.0006.03/DOC_2).

### 3. ATAD I and II: minimum corporate anti-avoidance rules

ATAD applies to corporate-tax taxpayers, including EU PEs of non-EU entities, with the specified reverse-hybrid scope. The original OECD BEPS policy context is retained: art 11(6) itself refers to the BEPS Action 4 minimum standard; that history does not replace the current operative tests. It sets minimum protection; domestic law can be stricter. National options and derogations must be checked, rather than inferred from this EU framework. [ATAD arts 1, 3, 9a, 11](https://publications.europa.eu/resource/cellar/5b1ee753-4c71-11ec-91ac-01aa75ed71a1.0006.02/DOC_1).

| Measure | 2026 EU framework and complete boundary | Official basis |
|---|---|---|
| Interest | Exceeding borrowing costs: 30% of tax-adjusted EBITDA, excluding exempt income. A state may allow deduction up to EUR 3,000,000, measured for the entire group where grouped under art 4(1), and/or standalone relief under the defined no-group/no-associate/no-PE test. Do not automatically choose the higher of 30% and EUR 3,000,000 without the national option. | [ATAD arts 2(1)–(2), 4(1)–(3)](https://publications.europa.eu/resource/cellar/5b1ee753-4c71-11ec-91ac-01aa75ed71a1.0006.02/DOC_1) |
| Other interest options | Check national financial-undertaking exclusions, pre-existing-loan grandfathering (excluding subsequent modifications), qualifying EU public-infrastructure financing, group equity/group-ratio escapes, and the adopted carryforward/carryback scheme. These are conditional options, not universal deductions. | [ATAD art 4(4)–(7)](https://publications.europa.eu/resource/cellar/5b1ee753-4c71-11ec-91ac-01aa75ed71a1.0006.02/DOC_1) |
| GAAR | Ignore arrangements for corporate-tax calculation where a main purpose or one main purpose is a tax advantage defeating the applicable law’s object or purpose, and the arrangement is not genuine considering all facts. Valid commercial reasons reflecting economic reality matter; apply national law to the resulting tax calculation. A state can have broader domestic rules. | [ATAD arts 3, 6](https://publications.europa.eu/resource/cellar/5b1ee753-4c71-11ec-91ac-01aa75ed71a1.0006.02/DOC_1) |
| CFC entry tests | For an entity, taxpayer alone or together with associated enterprises has direct/indirect control of more than 50% of votes, capital or profit rights; an exempt PE can also fall within art 7. Foreign actual corporate tax must be less than the home-computed tax minus that actual foreign tax: equivalently, less than half the home counterfactual (derived algebra). This is not merely a nominal-rate comparison. | [ATAD art 7(1)](https://publications.europa.eu/resource/cellar/5b1ee753-4c71-11ec-91ac-01aa75ed71a1.0006.02/DOC_1) |
| CFC model A | Art 7(2)(a): specified non-distributed income categories: interest/financial assets, IP royalties, dividends/share gains, financial leasing, insurance/banking/other financial activity, and low-value related-party invoicing income. The substantive-economic-activity exception looks to staff, equipment, assets and premises; states may decline that exception for non-EEA third countries. It is not automatic inclusion of all income. | [ATAD art 7(2)(a)](https://publications.europa.eu/resource/cellar/5b1ee753-4c71-11ec-91ac-01aa75ed71a1.0006.02/DOC_1) |
| CFC model B | Art 7(2)(b): non-distributed income from non-genuine arrangements put in place for the essential purpose of a tax advantage, tied to assets/risks whose significant people functions are performed by the controlling company. This is not model A’s enumerated passive-income list. | [ATAD art 7(2)(b)](https://publications.europa.eu/resource/cellar/5b1ee753-4c71-11ec-91ac-01aa75ed71a1.0006.02/DOC_1) |

CFC inclusion also requires the chosen model’s actual conditions. Art 7(3)–(4) offers further specified exclusions; art 8 governs computation, proportional inclusion, timing, arm’s-length attribution under model B and relief against double taxation on later distribution/disposal and foreign tax. A subsidiary’s EU location does not automatically turn off CFC rules, while the low-tax test alone does not establish an inclusion. The old country/model assignments are individually reserved below. [ATAD arts 7(3)–(4), 8](https://publications.europa.eu/resource/cellar/5b1ee753-4c71-11ec-91ac-01aa75ed71a1.0006.02/DOC_1).

Exit taxation under art 5 concerns loss of a state’s taxing rights on corporate assets, residence or PE business: head office to foreign PE, PE to head office/another foreign PE, residence transfer except assets remaining effectively connected to the original-state PE, and PE business transfer. Use market value less tax value only to identify the statutory trigger/base concept; never compute an exit-tax amount with this skill. Personal founder exit tax is a separate domestic question. [ATAD arts 2(6)–(8), 5(1), 5(6)](https://publications.europa.eu/resource/cellar/5b1ee753-4c71-11ec-91ac-01aa75ed71a1.0006.02/DOC_1).

| Exit boundary | Rule | Official basis |
|---|---|---|
| Instalments | 5-year instalment deferral (in words on the page) for the listed EU transfers; EEA third-state treatment requires an equivalent mutual-assistance agreement on tax-debt recovery. Interest may apply. A guarantee requires demonstrable actual recovery risk, subject to the group-recovery exception. | [ATAD art 5(2)–(3)](https://publications.europa.eu/resource/cellar/5b1ee753-4c71-11ec-91ac-01aa75ed71a1.0006.02/DOC_1) |
| Deferral ends | Check disposal, onward non-qualifying transfer, bankruptcy/winding up and instalment default, subject to the allowed correction period. No EU entitlement to instalments is established for other third-country transfers; domestic relief still needs checking. | [ATAD art 5(4)](https://publications.europa.eu/resource/cellar/5b1ee753-4c71-11ec-91ac-01aa75ed71a1.0006.02/DOC_1) |
| Temporary movement and national derogation | Art 5 does not apply to qualifying collateral, securities-financing or prudential/liquidity transfers set to return within 12 months. Art 11(4) contains an Estonia derogation; do not state that ATAD imposes an identical exit-tax system in every member state. | [ATAD arts 5(7), 11(4)](https://publications.europa.eu/resource/cellar/5b1ee753-4c71-11ec-91ac-01aa75ed71a1.0006.02/DOC_1) |

Hybrid mismatch rules cover the defined double-deduction or deduction-without-inclusion outcomes, including third-country, imported, reverse-hybrid and dual-residence situations. Test the defined causal mismatch and associated-enterprise/PE/structured-arrangement nexus, not merely a low-tax result. Art 9 gives primary/defensive denial or inclusion ordering and dual-inclusion-income limits; art 9a has the qualifying collective-investment-vehicle exception; art 9b has dual-residence conditions. Use the matching-country treatment before deciding an adjustment. [ATAD arts 2(4), 2(9)–(11), 9–9b](https://publications.europa.eu/resource/cellar/5b1ee753-4c71-11ec-91ac-01aa75ed71a1.0006.02/DOC_1).

### 4. DAC6: cross-border arrangements

Start with DAC art 3(18)–(25): the arrangement needs a qualifying cross-border feature and at least a hallmark; intermediary status depends on design/organisation/implementation or knowledgeable assistance plus an EU nexus. Being an accountant, lawyer, adviser or bank alone is not the test. The ordinary reporting framework applied from 1 July 2020, with first-step history from 25 June 2018 to 30 June 2020; art 27a allowed national COVID deferrals, so do not assert one universal historical due date. [DAC arts 3(18)–(25), 8ab(12), 27a](https://publications.europa.eu/resource/cellar/4328aa6c-1209-11f1-8870-01aa75ed71a1.0002.02/DOC_1).

| DAC6 element | Rule and exceptions | Official basis |
|---|---|---|
| Timing | Intermediaries generally report within 30 days using the earliest statutory trigger: day after availability, day after readiness, or when the first implementation step is made; assistance intermediaries use day after the aid/advice/assistance. Marketable-arrangement updates have a separate periodic rule. | [DAC art 8ab(1)–(2)](https://publications.europa.eu/resource/cellar/4328aa6c-1209-11f1-8870-01aa75ed71a1.0002.02/DOC_1) |
| Reporter and place | Apply intermediary/taxpayer priority-of-member-state tests, rather than simply a postal location. Multiple intermediaries report unless legally sufficient evidence shows the same information already reported. Where no reporting intermediary is available or a privilege waiver applies, check the relevant taxpayer’s obligation and any other intermediary duty. The current waiver clause requires notification of the client; do not inherit an old blanket duty to notify all intermediaries. | [DAC art 8ab(3)–(11)](https://publications.europa.eu/resource/cellar/4328aa6c-1209-11f1-8870-01aa75ed71a1.0002.02/DOC_1) |
| Exchange and meaning | Authorities exchange through the central-directory framework with other member states, not only a selected affected-state list. An authority’s failure to react is not acceptance of validity or tax treatment. | [DAC art 8ab(13)–(15), art 21(5)](https://publications.europa.eu/resource/cellar/4328aa6c-1209-11f1-8870-01aa75ed71a1.0002.02/DOC_1) |
| Hallmark | Main-benefit-test boundary | Official basis |
|---|---|---|
| A | Generic confidentiality, success-fee and standardised-document/structure hallmarks require the main benefit test. | [DAC Annex IV I, II A](https://publications.europa.eu/resource/cellar/4328aa6c-1209-11f1-8870-01aa75ed71a1.0002.02/DOC_1) |
| B | Loss-company acquisitions, conversion of income and circular transactions require the main benefit test. | [DAC Annex IV I, II B](https://publications.europa.eu/resource/cellar/4328aa6c-1209-11f1-8870-01aa75ed71a1.0002.02/DOC_1) |
| C | Only C1(b)(i), C1(c) and C1(d) require the main benefit test: zero/almost-zero corporate tax, full exemption and preferential regime. C1(a) (no tax residence), C1(b)(ii) (non-cooperative list), C2 duplicate depreciation, C3 duplicate double-tax relief and C4 materially differing valuation do not. Apply the associated-enterprise/deductible-payment conditions of C1. | [DAC Annex IV I, II C](https://publications.europa.eu/resource/cellar/4328aa6c-1209-11f1-8870-01aa75ed71a1.0002.02/DOC_1) |
| D | Automatic-exchange avoidance and opaque beneficial-ownership chains: no main benefit test, but the actual hallmark conditions still apply. | [DAC Annex IV II D](https://publications.europa.eu/resource/cellar/4328aa6c-1209-11f1-8870-01aa75ed71a1.0002.02/DOC_1) |
| E | Unilateral safe harbours, hard-to-value intangibles and qualifying intra-group cross-border function/risk/asset transfers: no main benefit test. E3 compares projected annual EBIT in the 3 years (in words on the page) after transfer with the no-transfer counterfactual; the hallmark uses less than 50%, not an EBITDA-shift test. | [DAC Annex IV II E](https://publications.europa.eu/resource/cellar/4328aa6c-1209-11f1-8870-01aa75ed71a1.0002.02/DOC_1) |

The main benefit test asks whether, considering all relevant facts, the main benefit or one of the main benefits a person may reasonably expect is a tax advantage. It is not a universal motive requirement: hallmarks without that test can be reportable without a tax-saving motive, but their own conditions must be met. Do not design avoidance of a reportable disclosure. [DAC Annex IV I](https://publications.europa.eu/resource/cellar/4328aa6c-1209-11f1-8870-01aa75ed71a1.0002.02/DOC_1).

### 5. DAC7: platform sellers

Reporting began for the 2023 period, with the first ordinary report due 31 January 2024 (derived from the following-year rule). Test a Platform/Platform Operator under Annex V, including the EU and qualifying non-EU operator nexus, qualified non-Union/equivalence and excluded-operator rules. Software solely processing payments, listing/advertising or redirecting without further intervention is excluded from the Platform definition. [DAC art 8ac; Annex V I A, III A(1)](https://publications.europa.eu/resource/cellar/4328aa6c-1209-11f1-8870-01aa75ed71a1.0002.02/DOC_1).

Relevant activities are immovable-property rental, personal services, goods sales and rental of transport, for consideration. Reportable sellers are active sellers resident in a member state or renting EU immovable property, after exclusions. Governmental entities, qualifying listed entities/related entities, high-volume entity property lessors under the defined property-listing test, and the narrow low-volume goods seller can be excluded. Do not treat every platform user or every type of income as reportable. [DAC Annex V I A–C](https://publications.europa.eu/resource/cellar/4328aa6c-1209-11f1-8870-01aa75ed71a1.0002.02/DOC_1).

| Goods seller exclusion | Condition beside the boundary | Official basis |
|---|---|---|
| Low-volume goods seller only | Operator facilitated fewer than 30 goods-sale activities AND total paid/credited consideration did not exceed EUR 2,000 in the reportable period. At 30 activities the exclusion fails even at EUR 2,000 (derived boundary); at 29 activities it also fails if consideration exceeds EUR 2,000. Other reportability/exclusion conditions still apply. This is not an income-tax exemption and does not exempt personal services or rentals. | [DAC Annex V I B(4)(d)](https://publications.europa.eu/resource/cellar/4328aa6c-1209-11f1-8870-01aa75ed71a1.0002.02/DOC_1) |

Report annually by 31 January following the reportable calendar year. Consideration is net of fees, commissions or taxes withheld/charged by the operator; the reporting includes the separate fees/commissions/taxes, quarterly consideration/activity counts and seller/property information as applicable. Apply due diligence, exclusion verification and record requirements. EU multi-nexus election and non-EU single registration are distinct rules, with equivalence and compliance conditions. [DAC Annex V I A(10), II, III A–B, IV E–F; art 8ac](https://publications.europa.eu/resource/cellar/4328aa6c-1209-11f1-8870-01aa75ed71a1.0002.02/DOC_1).

Platforms such as Fiverr, Upwork, Airbnb, Uber and Etsy were the original examples. Treat them as illustrations of activity types only; no individual platform’s reporting status, every user’s reportability, or automated matching outcome has been confirmed. Reconcile any actual seller report with the filed return; reporting itself is not the tax calculation.

### 6. DAC8: crypto-asset reporting

First covered reporting period starts 1 January 2026. Authority-to-authority exchange is within 9 months (in words on the page) after the calendar year, giving 30 September 2027 for 2026 (derived). This is not a universal provider filing deadline of 30 September: the provider reports annually to its competent national authority under the national implementation. Obtain that state’s actual deadline, portal and form before filing. [DAC art 8ad(1)–(6); Annex VI II B](https://publications.europa.eu/resource/cellar/4328aa6c-1209-11f1-8870-01aa75ed71a1.0002.02/DOC_1).

Test Reporting Crypto-Asset Service Provider under Annex VI: the defined authorised service provider or Crypto-Asset Operator providing the specified services, plus jurisdiction nexus, branch and equivalent-reporting/exemption rules. Exchanges, custodians, brokers and decentralised platform operators are not automatically all in or all out by label. Reportable Crypto-Assets exclude central bank digital currencies, defined electronic-money products and assets the provider adequately determines cannot be used for payment/investment. [DAC Annex VI I; IV A–B](https://publications.europa.eu/resource/cellar/4328aa6c-1209-11f1-8870-01aa75ed71a1.0002.02/DOC_1).

A Reportable User is a user resident in a member state who is a Reportable Person, with entity/controlling-person tests where applicable. Defined governmental, international, central-bank and listed/related entities are excluded; the financial-institution exclusion does not extend to the specified managed Investment Entities in IV E(5)(b). The original “all EU-resident and non-resident users of EU providers” statement was too broad. Apply self-certification, reasonableness and other due-diligence rules before classifying users. [DAC Annex VI III; IV D–E](https://publications.europa.eu/resource/cellar/4328aa6c-1209-11f1-8870-01aa75ed71a1.0002.02/DOC_1).

Report by asset type the applicable acquisitions/disposals against fiat, crypto exchanges, qualifying retail payments and transfers, including aggregate monetary value, units and transaction counts as specified. Fiat/value conversion is at the transaction time consistently applied; report the currency. Do not substitute a single net income figure or assume these reports determine capital-gain or business-income tax. [DAC art 8ad(3); Annex VI II B–C](https://publications.europa.eu/resource/cellar/4328aa6c-1209-11f1-8870-01aa75ed71a1.0002.02/DOC_1).

### 7. EU Merger Directive

For qualifying cross-state mergers, divisions, partial divisions, transfers of assets and exchanges of shares, determine the art 2 operation and art 3 company first: Annex I Part A form, EU/treaty residence condition, and liability without option or exemption to an Annex I Part B tax or replacement. The Directive also has a specific SE/SCE registered-office transfer regime; do not assume every transaction called a merger or every corporate form qualifies. [Merger Directive arts 1–3; Annex I](https://publications.europa.eu/resource/cellar/a8a3f464-4c94-4665-9e88-ad6b53f41989.0006.03/DOC_1).

| Operation | Conditional treatment | Official basis |
|---|---|---|
| Merger/division | No immediate gain taxation on qualifying transferred assets/liabilities to the extent they remain effectively connected with a receiving-company PE in the transferring state and play a part in generating taxable results there. Continued tax values and art 4 computation conditions matter. | [Merger Directive art 4](https://publications.europa.eu/resource/cellar/a8a3f464-4c94-4665-9e88-ad6b53f41989.0006.03/DOC_1) |
| Branch/asset transfer | Arts 4–6 apply through art 9 to qualifying transfers of a branch of activity; preserve the defined operation, PE/tax-value and reserve/loss conditions rather than promising an unconditional tax-free transfer. | [Merger Directive arts 2, 5–6, 9](https://publications.europa.eu/resource/cellar/a8a3f464-4c94-4665-9e88-ad6b53f41989.0006.03/DOC_1) |
| Share exchange/shareholder relief | Qualifying allotment does not itself trigger shareholder gain taxation under art 8, subject to the old-value conditions. Later disposals can be taxed; cash consideration can be taxed. This is deferral/carryover treatment, not permanent exemption of all gains. | [Merger Directive arts 2(e), 8](https://publications.europa.eu/resource/cellar/a8a3f464-4c94-4665-9e88-ad6b53f41989.0006.03/DOC_1) |

Check third-member-state PE treatment and the applicable credit in art 10, transparent-entity/shareholder treatment in art 11 and the dedicated SE/SCE rules in arts 12–14 when relevant. Art 15 permits refusal/withdrawal for a principal tax-evasion/avoidance objective; lack of valid restructuring/rationalisation reasons may be evidence, not a stand-alone universal ban. The separate employee-representation condition can also permit refusal. [Merger Directive arts 10–15](https://publications.europa.eu/resource/cellar/a8a3f464-4c94-4665-9e88-ad6b53f41989.0006.03/DOC_1).

Original use cases remain: a founder merging companies in different states, a group moving a branch across states, and a share-for-share group restructuring. ## Worked cases

### 1. Interest deduction: national option changes the result

Hypothetical 2026 corporate taxpayer: tax-adjusted EBITDA EUR 5,000,000, exceeding borrowing costs EUR 4,000,000, and no other exclusions, group escapes, carryforward amounts or stricter rules. Under the art 4 baseline, 30% × EUR 5,000,000 = EUR 1,500,000 deductible and EUR 2,500,000 restricted in this period (derived). If the applicable national law has adopted the art 4(3)(a) EUR 3,000,000 option on these facts, deductible costs become EUR 3,000,000 and the current restriction EUR 1,000,000, a EUR 1,500,000 difference (derived). The decision depends on the actual national option; this example establishes no country’s deduction. [ATAD art 4(1)–(3)](https://publications.europa.eu/resource/cellar/5b1ee753-4c71-11ec-91ac-01aa75ed71a1.0006.02/DOC_1).

```oa-check
name: ATAD baseline compared with adopted de minimis option
inputs:
  tax_ebitda: 5000000
  exceeding_costs: 4000000
  baseline_fraction: 0.30
  adopted_option: 3000000
steps:
  - baseline_capacity: tax_ebitda * baseline_fraction
  - baseline_deduction: min(exceeding_costs, baseline_capacity)
  - baseline_restriction: exceeding_costs - baseline_deduction
  - option_deduction: min(exceeding_costs, max(baseline_capacity, adopted_option))
  - option_restriction: exceeding_costs - option_deduction
  - difference: option_deduction - baseline_deduction
expected:
  baseline_deduction: 1500000
  baseline_restriction: 2500000
  option_deduction: 3000000
  option_restriction: 1000000
  difference: 1500000
```

### 2. CFC actual-tax screen: foreign tax is not a nominal rate

Hypothetical company: the control condition is already satisfied and the home-state corporate tax recomputed under its rules is EUR 50,000. If actual foreign corporate tax is EUR 20,000, the statutory comparator is EUR 50,000 minus EUR 20,000 = EUR 30,000; EUR 20,000 is less than EUR 30,000, so the low-tax entry test is met. If actual foreign tax is EUR 30,000, the comparator is EUR 20,000 and the entry test fails. At EUR 25,000 it equals the comparator, so the strict less-than condition also fails. All comparators are derived. This tests only entry: it does not calculate a CFC inclusion, select a national model or override substance/other exceptions. [ATAD art 7(1)(b)](https://publications.europa.eu/resource/cellar/5b1ee753-4c71-11ec-91ac-01aa75ed71a1.0006.02/DOC_1).

```oa-check
name: CFC actual tax against home tax less actual tax
inputs:
  home_computed_tax: 50000
  actual_low: 20000
  actual_high: 30000
  actual_equal: 25000
steps:
  - low_comparator: home_computed_tax - actual_low
  - low_gap: low_comparator - actual_low
  - high_comparator: home_computed_tax - actual_high
  - high_gap: high_comparator - actual_high
  - equal_comparator: home_computed_tax - actual_equal
  - equal_gap: equal_comparator - actual_equal
expected:
  low_comparator: 30000
  low_gap: 10000
  high_comparator: 20000
  high_gap: -10000
  equal_comparator: 25000
  equal_gap: 0
```

## When to refuse or refer

1. Never advise a structure specifically to access PSD/IRD benefits without genuine commercial reasons and economic reality. Do not manufacture substance or an anti-abuse clearance.
2. Never assume the PSD capital test below 10% or the IRD direct-capital test below 25% is met. Research any distinct domestic/treaty relief and valid voting-right option; do not relabel it Directive eligibility.
3. Never design avoidance of DAC6 reporting. A reportable arrangement must follow the applicable reporting law.
4. Never compute ATAD exit-tax amounts. Flag the relevant corporate transfer and obtain a specialist.
5. Never assume intra-EU subsidiaries escape CFC rules, or that the low-tax test alone proves an inclusion. Apply the full implemented model and substance/exclusion rules.
6. Never state that the Merger Directive makes all reorganisations tax-free. Require each condition, anti-abuse analysis and specialist review.
7. Refer disputed beneficial ownership, treaty residence, abuse, hybrid mismatch, CFC attribution, privilege and all cross-border reorganisations to the appropriate EU specialist. Missing current national procedures prevent a filing-ready conclusion.
