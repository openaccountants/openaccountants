# South Dakota payroll and reemployment assistance, 2026

> **Built from the law. Not yet approved.** Written by Codex. Not reviewed by an accountant. A qualified professional must review the established facts and filing result. Circular 230 section 10.37: consider relevant facts and circumstances; do not assume missing facts or rely on audit risk.

**Answer first.** South Dakota has no individual income tax or state income-tax wage withholding. Covered employers still pay reemployment assistance (RA), the state unemployment tax. Employees do not fund RA through wage deductions. Experienced employers' administrative fee is **0.08% in 2026**. [DOR tax basics](https://dor.sd.gov/media/avyep2sr/2026-7_sales-use-tax-guide.pdf); [DLR FAQ](https://dlr.sd.gov/ra/businesses/faq.aspx); [61-5-28.1](https://sdlegislature.gov/api/Statutes/61-5-28.1.html).

## Scope

Ordinary contributory employers for 2026, including state income-tax withholding status, RA coverage and wage reporting, base, employer rates/fees and quarterly payment. Federal withholding/FICA/FUTA belong to `us-form-941-940-payroll`. Reimbursable employers, interstate localization and common-paymaster arrangements require separate facts.

## Ask the client first

1. Entity, registration/ownership changes, account, covered workers and work locations.
2. Quarter-paid wages and each employee's year-to-date covered wages and predecessor records.
3. Exact 2026 contribution notice, experience eligibility, construction status, investment/admin fees and surcharge notices.
4. Service descriptions, owners/officers/contractors and exclusion evidence.
5. Prior reports/payments, online authentication key/security code and payment authorization.

## The method, step by step

DLR's current FAQ and statute explicitly update the 2026 administrative fee. The older employer handbook's conditional 0.02% administrative fee and old experience-rate table are superseded for 2026; its operational reporting explanations are used subject to current law. Obtain the rate notice rather than guessing the applicable fund schedule or adding a fee already included in a total.

1. Register new/acquired businesses and ownership/entity changes with RA. Ordinary liability includes paying USD 1,500 or more in a calendar quarter or employing someone in 20 different weeks in the current/prior year; acquisition/FUTA and agricultural/domestic/nonprofit triggers differ. Agricultural trigger is USD 20,000 quarterly or 10 workers in 20 weeks; domestic USD 1,000 quarterly; qualifying 501(c)(3) nonprofits generally 4 workers in 20 weeks. Do not treat one generic threshold as universal.
2. Establish covered employment. Corporate officers are employees. Contractor exclusion requires freedom from direction/control **and** an independently established business; a contract label is insufficient. Keep separate covered/exempt wage records and refer owner/family, nonprofit/public and interstate exceptions.
3. Report wages in the quarter **paid**. Include cash, commissions, bonuses and cash value of remuneration; employee retirement/cafeteria deductions generally remain reportable, so federal taxable wages are not automatically the state base. Employer qualified retirement contributions and qualifying documented expenses have different treatment. Resolve disputed components under the state rules.
4. For each worker, taxable wages are min(current covered wages, max(15,000 minus prior same-employer covered wages, 0)). Report excess gross wages too. Confirm successor/prior-state wage credits before applying them. Sum individual taxable amounts; never cap the whole business payroll at one wage base.
5. Multiply taxable wages separately by contribution, investment, applicable administrative fee and any current surcharge. The new-employer rates do not govern experienced employers. Experience eligibility comes from 61-5-24, not simply an assumption that the third calendar anniversary has passed.
6. Reconcile Form 21 employee detail, total and excess/taxable wages, employer rates/fees, prior credits and payment. No RA contribution, investment or administrative fee is deducted from employee pay.

| Facts | Treatment |

| New employer not experience eligible | Do not add the experience-only administrative fee |
| Nonprofit/public reimbursable election | Separate benefit-cost reimbursement method; ordinary contribution example does not apply |
| Multi-state work/common paymaster | Establish statutory localization and authorized arrangement; residence or payroll processor alone does not decide |

## Worked cases

**Case 1.** An invented first-year nonconstruction employer pays one covered employee USD 20,000 in 2026 with no prior wages. Taxable wage base is USD 15,000. Contribution 180 plus investment 82.50 gives USD 262.50. Employee RA withholding is zero and no experience-only admin fee applies.

```oa-check
name: South Dakota new employer annual base and fees
inputs:
  wages: 20000
  wage_base: 15000
steps:
  - taxable: min(wages, wage_base)
  - contribution: taxable * 0.012
  - investment: taxable * 0.0055
  - total: contribution + investment
expected:
  taxable: 15000
  contribution: 180
  investment: 82.5
  total: 262.5
```

**Case 2, employer category decision.** An invented experience-eligible employer's 2026 notice supplies 0% contribution and 0% investment, with the statutory 0.08% administrative fee. A covered employee has USD 14,000 prior wages and USD 4,000 current wages:USD 1,000 remains taxable and admin fee is USD 0.80. Zero ordinary contribution does not eliminate this administrative fee.

```oa-check
name: South Dakota experienced administrative fee despite zero contribution
inputs:
  prior_wages: 14000
  current_wages: 4000
  wage_base: 15000
steps:
  - taxable: min(current_wages, max(wage_base - prior_wages, 0))
  - contribution_and_investment: taxable * 0
  - administrative: taxable * 0.0008
  - total: contribution_and_investment + administrative
expected:
  taxable: 1000
  administrative: 0.8
  total: 0.8
```

## When to refuse or refer

Refer missing/contested notices, worker classification, owner/family exceptions, acquisitions/successor liability, reimbursable employers, common paymasters and interstate localization. Do not infer RA exemption from no state income tax or federal S corporation treatment. Do not deduct employer taxes/fees from employees.

## Filing and payment

From DLR's Reemployment Assistance for Businesses page, register the business or open quarterly wage reporting. Associate credentials with the employer authentication key/security code. Enter Form 21 wages by employee and the paid quarter, reconcile total/excess/taxable wages and notice rates, submit and retain the timestamp/confirmation. EFT is available with online quarterly reporting; separately confirm debit/posting. DLR's FAQ says credit cards can pay outstanding debts but cannot be used while filing the quarterly report.

Ordinary quarter reports and contributions must be **received** by April 30, July 31, October 31 and January 31. File zeros while active. Domestic employers need advance approval for annual reporting; Form DE is due by December 31 for the following year and the annual return/payment by January 31. Report corrections with explanation or Form 21C rather than leaving employee wage errors unresolved.

The read handbook imposes USD 25 per month or part for each late report and separately USD 25 for late contributions, maximum USD 150 each and USD 300 combined. Interest is 1.5% per month or part from the due date. The older handbook says reports are due 30 days after quarter-end in one paragraph, but its explicit table/current DLR page say last day of the following month, which controls the operational calendar here. State business sales-tax penalty rules do not substitute for RA rules.

## Completion checklist

- No state income-tax withholding distinguished from employer RA.
- Liability, coverage, paid-quarter wages and employee base established.
- Actual 2026 notice and all separate fees reconciled without duplication.
- Correct quarterly or approved annual reporting and zero-report duty followed.
- Acceptance and payment verified; federal payroll handled separately.
