# Saint Kitts and Nevis VAT and import charges

> **General reference only.** This skill is general tax/accounting reference material for AI-assisted workflows. It has not been reviewed for any specific person's facts, documents, elections, deadlines, residency, filing status, or local procedures. Do not rely on it to file, pay, amend, or take a tax position without review by a qualified professional in the relevant jurisdiction.

## Scope

The federal VAT on taxable supplies in Saint Christopher and Nevis by taxable persons and on non-exempt imports of goods and services, in 2026, under the Value Added Tax Act, Cap. 18.47 (revision of 31 December 2017) and its regulations, administered by the Inland Revenue Department; and the charges on importing goods (customs duty, Customs Service Charge) under the Customs Act and Customs Tariff Act. The supplier accounts for domestic output VAT, the importer pays on goods and the recipient accounts for taxable imported services. Amounts are EC$.

## Ask the client first

1. Taxable supplies over the last 12 months and expected over the next 365 days; what kind of supplies (goods, accommodation, restaurant, commercial or timeshare lease, professional services)?
2. Is the business registered, and does it file monthly?
3. Any imports of goods (tariff code, origin, customs value) or of services from abroad?
4. Exempt or zero-rated supplies (exports, financial services)?
5. Will the business take part in a discounted VAT rate day?

### The base

- **Time of supply (s.36).** The earliest of delivery or making available of goods, completion of services, invoice or receipt of consideration, with special rules for periodic supplies.
- **Place of supply (s.37).** Goods follow delivery or availability; services the supplier's place of business. Exceptions: services connected with immovable property, and specified professional, digital and other services where the recipient uses or benefits from them. Qualifying services exported from a local place of business have their own rule.
- **Exempt and zero-rated (ss.31 to 35 and Schedules).** The Second Schedule includes qualifying exports (with evidence); the Third Schedule specified exempt financial services and other categories.
- **Input tax (ss.40 and 41).** Deduct input VAT attributable to taxable supplies with the invoice, adjustment note or import evidence; apportion mixed inputs; apply the disallowances and the financial-institution rules. Input tax attributable to 10% supplies is capped at their output VAT for the period (s.41(8)).
- **Imported goods (s.29).** Value = customs value + insurance and freight not in it + customs duty, excise, environmental surcharge and other fiscal charges + Customs Service Charge. Count each amount once. Services incidental to imported goods are part of the goods import.
- **Customs value.** The 2020 Customs Act uses the sale-for-export transaction value with statutory adjustments, and alternative methods where that cannot be used. Customs uses ASYCUDA World and a public tariff search.

### Steps

1. **Register** if a threshold is crossed (within 14 days).
2. **Classify** each supply: 17%, 10%, zero-rated, exempt; apply the discounted day rate only where the seller participates and the goods qualify.
3. **Output tax** on taxable supplies by time of supply; **input tax** with valid invoices.
4. **Imports:** pay import VAT at entry on the s.29 value; account for imported services within 15 days.
5. **File** the monthly return within 15 days and pay.

## Worked cases

### Case 1: an ordinary import

Customs value EC$1,000 (insurance and freight included), an assumed 10% tariff, the 6% Customs Service Charge, standard VAT and no relief. Duty 100, service charge 60, VAT base 1,160, VAT 197.20; total 357.20. The tariff rate is assumed for the arithmetic only.

```oa-check
  value: 1000
  duty_rate: 0.10

  - duty: value * duty_rate
  - csc: value * 0.06
  - vat_base: value + duty + csc
  - vat: vat_base * 0.17
  - total: duty + csc + vat
expected:
  duty: 100
  csc: 60
  vat_base: 1160
  vat: 197.2
  total: 357.2
```

### Case 2 (decision): register now, or wait?

A consultant (professional services) bills EC$8,500 a month and expects the same for the next nine months. The three-month test for these services is EC$24,000 actual and EC$96,000 expected: after three months, actual 25,500 and the 12-month forecast 102,000 both exceed, so the consultant must apply within 14 days of the end of month 3. At the general EC$150,000 test the same business would not need to register.

## When to refuse or refer

- Classification of financial services, exports and mixed supplies.
- Non-resident and digital suppliers.
- Customs classification, origin, excise and relief eligibility.
- December 2026 discounted days until the terms are published.

## Filing and payment

1. Register with IRD within 14 days of crossing a threshold.
2. File the monthly VAT return within 15 calendar days of the month end, through SMARTS, and pay by the same date.
3. Imports: declare in ASYCUDA World with the commercial invoice, transport and insurance evidence, any origin certificate or permit; pay duty, Customs Service Charge and VAT at entry.
4. Keep records for six years.

## Completion checklist

- Threshold tested on the right figure; return filed monthly.
- Rate for each supply, including 10% and discounted days.
- Import VAT on the full s.29 base; input tax backed by invoices.
