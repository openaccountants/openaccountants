# US new hire reporting: the federal rule and every state, 2026

> **Source-cited draft.** Built from 42 U.S.C. 653a and the Office of Child Support Services (OCSS) "State Contact and Program Information: State New Hire Reporting" chart, which each state updates (entries dated 2025 and 2026), read on 2026-11-13. Each row links the state's own new hire site as listed by OCSS. No accountant has reviewed it.

**Answer first.** Every employer reports each newly hired or rehired employee to the state directory of new hires: within 20 days under federal law, sooner in 10 states. The report goes to a child support agency, not the tax department. Several states also require reports of independent contractors. An employer with staff in several states can elect to report all of them to one state, but that election does not cover contractor reporting.

## Scope and who this is for

In scope: any employer (including sole proprietors and single-member LLCs) that hires a W-2 employee or engages an independent contractor in a US state or DC, for 2026. Out of scope: Form I-9 and E-Verify, Forms W-4 and W-2, unemployment insurance wage reports and payroll taxes (`us-state-payroll-matrix`), and 1099 filing (`us-1099-nec-issuance`).

## Ask the client first

1. In which states do new employees live and work?
2. Is payroll run in one state for staff in several (multistate election candidate)? Has the election been filed?
3. Does the business pay independent contractors, how much each, and in which states?
4. Does the payroll provider file new hire reports, and to which state?
5. Any rehires after a break in service?

## The method, step by step

1. **Report every new hire and rehire** to the work state's directory of new hires, by that state's deadline (table). The federal maximum is 20 days after hire, or two monthly electronic transmissions 12 to 16 days apart ([42 U.S.C. 653a(b)(3)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title42/html/USCODE-2024-title42-chap7-subchapIV-partD-sec653a.htm)).
2. **Include the federal data elements:** employee name, address and SSN; employer name, address and federal EIN. Many states add date of hire, date of birth, state of hire or health insurance availability: see the OCSS chart.
3. **Multistate employers:** an employer with employees in two or more states that transmits reports electronically may designate one state for all of them, by notifying the Secretary of HHS ([42 U.S.C. 653a(b)(1)(B)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title42/html/USCODE-2024-title42-chap7-subchapIV-partD-sec653a.htm)). It still registers for withholding and unemployment tax in each work state.
4. **Independent contractors:** check the work state's row. Where required, contractor reports are separate from the multistate election and from year-end 1099s.
5. **Penalties:** a state may impose a civil penalty of not more than USD 25 per failure, or not more than USD 500 where the failure results from a conspiracy between employer and employee ([42 U.S.C. 653a(d)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title42/html/USCODE-2024-title42-chap7-subchapIV-partD-sec653a.htm)). These are caps on what federal law allows, not minimums.

## Boundary and exception table

| Situation | Treatment |
| --- | --- |
| Multistate election filed | All employee reports go to the chosen state; contractor reports still go to each state that requires them |
| Employer in a 7-day state (Alabama, Maine) using a 20-day payroll default | Late: set the provider to the state deadline |
| California business pays a freelancer USD 600 or more in a year | DE 542 within 20 days of the earlier of reaching USD 600 or contracting for it; a 1099-NEC at year end does not replace it |
| New York contractor on a contract over USD 2,500 | Report as for an employee |
| Rehire after a separation | Report as a new hire |
| State penalty above USD 25 per failure (non-conspiracy) | Not allowed by 42 U.S.C. 653a(d): check the state statute |

## Worked cases

**Case 1, penalty exposure.** An employer in a state that applies the federal maximum missed reports for 30 hires. Maximum exposure: 30 x USD 25 = USD 750. If a state found a conspiracy for 2 of them, those two are capped at USD 500 each: 28 x 25 + 2 x 500 = USD 1,700.

```oa-check
name: New hire penalty caps, 30 missed reports
inputs:
  missed: 30
  conspiracy: 2
steps:
  - all_ordinary: missed * 25
  - mixed: (missed - conspiracy) * 25 + conspiracy * 500
expected:
  all_ordinary: 750
  mixed: 1700
```

**Case 2, a decision: does a California payer file DE 542?** A San Francisco agency pays a freelance designer USD 450 in March and USD 200 in May, with no written contract. Total USD 650 reaches the USD 600 threshold with the May payment, so DE 542 is due within 20 days of that payment. A second freelancer paid USD 550 in total needs no report.

```oa-check
name: California DE 542 threshold
inputs:
  first: 450
  second: 200
  other_freelancer: 550
  threshold: 600
steps:
  - total: first + second
  - margin: total - threshold
  - other_margin: other_freelancer - threshold
expected:
  total: 650
  margin: 50
  other_margin: -50
```

A margin of zero or more means report; a negative margin means no report.

## Filing and payment

File online on the state's new hire site (table), by electronic file transfer, or on paper or a W-4 where the state allows. The multistate employer notification is made to HHS through the OCSS employer services pages. There is no fee.

## Completion checklist

- Every work state's deadline set in payroll (7, 10, 14, 15 or 20 days).
- Multistate election filed or deliberately not used.
- Contractor reports filed in each state that requires them.
- Rehires reported.
- Provider's filing confirmations kept.
