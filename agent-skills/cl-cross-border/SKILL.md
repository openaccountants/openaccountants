# Chile: cross-border income tax and withholding (2026) v1.0

> **General reference only.** This skill is general tax/accounting reference material for AI-assisted workflows. It has not been reviewed for any specific person's facts, documents, elections, deadlines, residency, filing status, or local procedures. This skill covers what a Chilean payer must withhold when it pays a person without domicile or residence in Chile in 2026, how a treaty changes that, the thin capitalisation tax on interest paid to related lenders abroad, and the cross-border filings of a Chilean resident. Currency: Chilean peso (CLP).

## Who this applies to

- **Non-residents with Chilean-source income.** Individuals without domicile or residence in Chile, and companies incorporated abroad, pay IA on Chilean-source income: profits remitted by a Chilean permanent establishment (art. 58 No. 1), dividends from Chilean companies (art. 58 No. 2), gains on indirect transfers of Chilean assets (art. 58 No. 3), the payments listed in art. 59, and any other Chilean-source income (art. 60).
- **Chilean payers.** Anyone who remits abroad, credits to an account, puts at the disposal of, or pays income subject to IA withholds it at the IA rate that applies (art. 74 No. 4) and declares and pays it in F50 (art. 79). If the payer does not withhold, or under-withholds on a treaty claim that turns out not to apply, the payer answers for the tax, with a right to recover it from the non-resident (art. 74 No. 4).
- **Chilean companies borrowing from related parties abroad,** for the excess-debt tax of art. 41 F.
- **Chilean residents with foreign income or investments,** for the foreign tax credit (art. 41 A) and DJ 1929.
- **Out of scope:** treaty rate tables and treaty-specific clauses, the art. 41 G controlled foreign company rules, the art. 107 regime for listed securities, and DL 600 invariability.

## The base

**1. Is it Chilean-source and is the payee non-resident?** Residence follows CT art. 8 No. 8 (see cl-income-tax). If yes, classify the payment: dividend or withdrawal (art. 58), one of the art. 59 items, or other income (art. 60).

**2. Base and rate.** Art. 59 tax is on the total amount paid or credited, with no deduction (art. 59, first and fourth paragraphs). Art. 60 second-paragraph income is taxed on the gross amount. For art. 58 No. 1 and art. 60 first paragraph, the base follows art. 62 (all categories of Chilean income, including income exempt from the cedular taxes) and, where the payment carries IDPC, the base is grossed up by the art. 63 credit, which then comes off the tax (arts. 58, 62, 63, 74 No. 4; worked in cl-corporate-income-tax).

**3. Treaty relief at source (art. 74 No. 4).** Where the payee is resident in a country with a treaty in force and is entitled to its benefits, the payer may withhold at the treaty rate, or not withhold where the treaty gives the taxing right only to the other country, if the payee hands over:

- a **certificate of residence** issued by the competent authority of the other state; and
- a **declaration**, in the form the SII sets by resolution, that it has no permanent establishment or fixed base in Chile to which the income belongs and that it meets the treaty's requirements.

A residence certificate is presumed, unless shown otherwise, to prove residence for the calendar year in which it was issued. The SII's treaties page lists the treaties in force with their date of application in Chile (for example the United States from 1 January 2024, the Netherlands and the United Arab Emirates from 1 January 2023), the Pacific Alliance convention (from 1 January 2024) and the MLI (in force for Chile from 1 March 2021).

**4. Thin capitalisation (art. 41 F).**

- Total annual debt: all credits and liabilities of the company in the year, foreign or domestic, related or not, at their average value for the months they were outstanding, plus accrued unpaid interest that itself bears interest; unrelated credits of 90 days or less are left out (No. 5).
- Equity (patrimonio): capital propio at 1 January (or at start of activities) under art. 41, plus contributions and capital increases of the year in proportion to the time they stayed, less reductions, withdrawals and distributions, and less contributions financed directly or indirectly with related-party debt; never below 1 (No. 4).
- Excess exists only if total annual debt is more than 3 times equity at year end (No. 3).
- Base: the percentage (total debt - 3 x equity) / total debt, applied to the interest and charges paid to related parties abroad in the year that bore IA at 4%, or less than 35%, or none, because of a reduction, exemption or treaty (No. 8).
- Tax: 35%, less the IA already withheld on those amounts (No. 9). It is borne by the debtor and is deductible as an expense (No. 10). Project finance mainly from unrelated lenders can be excluded (No. 11). Declared annually in the F22 (No. 2).

**5. Residents with foreign income (art. 41 A).** Foreign tax on dividends, royalties, professional or technical services, wages and fees, foreign permanent establishments and treaty-country income is creditable, up to the lower of the individual and global caps in the table. Each type of income is capped separately.

## Deadlines and filing

| What | When | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| Declare and pay IA withheld | By the 12th of the month after the month the income was paid, distributed, withdrawn, remitted, credited or put at the payee's disposal | LIR art. 79 |
| IA withheld on indirect transfers (art. 10 third paragraph, art. 58 No. 3) and on art. 21 items | In the annual F22, not F50 | LIR art. 79 |
| Art. 60 second paragraph (artists, scientists, sportspeople) | Withheld and paid before the person leaves Chile | LIR art. 60 |
| Excess-debt tax of art. 41 F | Annual F22 for AT 2027, April 2027 | LIR art. 41 F No. 2 |
| DJ 1929 on operations abroad, DJ 1930 on debt abroad (AT 2026 calendar) | 30 June | SII declaraciones juradas 2026 table |

**How to file F50.** Log in at sii.cl with the payer's RUT and Clave Tributaria, open Servicios online, Impuestos mensuales, Declaración mensual (F50), and choose the month. Lines 1 to 46 are the IA withheld under art. 74 No. 4: lines 1 to 4 dividends and withdrawals, 5 to 10 intangibles (line 7 software at 30% or 15%), 11 to 15 interest (35% or 4%, with a treaty-rate column), 16 and 17 engineering or technical services in Chile or abroad (20% or 15%), 18 other services abroad (35%), 20 and 21 art. 60 scientific, cultural or sports activities (35% for companies, 20% for individuals), 22 other art. 60 income (35%); line 45 is the subtotal (code 782). Enter the base in the general, reduced or treaty column for each line, check the tax, submit and pay; keep the folio. Treaty claims also need the certificate and declaration on file at the payer.

**How to file DJ 1929.** Residents (including fund managers investing abroad) and Chilean permanent establishments of foreign entities report their investments abroad, controlled foreign entities, foreign permanent establishments, operations producing foreign income and other operations abroad, with the income, passive income of controlled entities and losses, in pesos (SII DJ 1929 instructions).

## Penalties and interest

- **Tax not withheld or under-withheld:** the payer is liable for it (art. 74 No. 4), with the right to recover it from the non-resident.
- **Late payment of tax withheld:** CT art. 97 No. 11 (10% plus 2% a month, up to 30%; higher when found in an audit), set out in [cl-income-tax](cl-income-tax.md).
- **Late payment interest:** CPI adjustment and the CT art. 53 penal interest, 7.70% a year for July to December 2026 (in [cl-income-tax](cl-income-tax.md)).
- **Treaty relief that did not apply:** the payer answers for the tax not withheld (art. 74 No. 4).

## Cross-border points

- **Dividends to non-residents** with the IDPC credit and the 35% restitution, and the treaty clause that removes it, are worked in [cl-corporate-income-tax](cl-corporate-income-tax.md).

## Two worked examples that recompute

### Example 1: two invoices from a consultancy abroad

Minera Valle SpA pays a consultancy incorporated in Singapore (no tax treaty with Chile on the SII list) in March 2026:

- CLP 40,000,000 for a geotechnical engineering report: technical work, 15% (art. 59 No. 2). IA CLP 6,000,000.
- CLP 10,000,000 for general administrative support rendered from Singapore: other services rendered abroad, 35%. IA CLP 3,500,000.
- A CLP 2,000,000 commission on a sale abroad is exempt (art. 59 No. 2).

Total withheld CLP 9,500,000, declared in F50 for March (lines 17 and 18) and paid by 12 April 2026. If the consultancy were in a preferential regime under art. 41 H, the report would bear 20% (CLP 8,000,000).

### Example 2: excess debt, and whether to capitalise instead

Andes Energía SpA has equity (patrimonio) of CLP 1,000,000,000 for 2026 and total annual debt of CLP 4,500,000,000. It paid CLP 270,000,000 of interest in 2026 to a related foreign financial institution, which bore IA at 4% (art. 59 No. 1 b)).

- Excess: 4,500,000,000 > 3 x 1,000,000,000 = 3,000,000,000.
- Percentage: (4,500,000,000 - 3,000,000,000) / 4,500,000,000 = 33.33%.
- Base: 270,000,000 x 1/3 = CLP 90,000,000.
- Tax at 35% = 31,500,000, less the IA withheld on that part (4% of 90,000,000 = 3,600,000): CLP 27,900,000, paid in the F22 in April 2027, and deductible as an expense.

**Capitalise instead?** Had the parent contributed CLP 500,000,000 of capital on 1 January 2026 (not financed with related-party debt) and the same amount of debt been repaid on that date, equity would be 1,500,000,000 and debt 4,000,000,000 < 4,500,000,000: no excess and no art. 41 F tax. The figure that decides is total debt against 3 times equity: the 41 F cost (CLP 27,900,000 here) against the cost of the capital route (dividends later bear IA with the IDPC credit). A contribution made mid-year counts only for the part of the year it stayed (No. 4).

```oa-check
name: CL art. 41 F 2026, excess debt tax, and the capitalised alternative
inputs:
  equity: 1000000000
  debt: 4500000000
  interest_related: 270000000
  ia_rate_paid: 0.04
  extra_equity: 500000000
steps:
  - excess_share: (debt - 3 * equity) / debt
  - base: round(interest_related * excess_share, 2)
  - tax_gross: base * 0.35
  - credit_ia: base * ia_rate_paid
  - tax_net: tax_gross - credit_ia
  - debt_after: debt - extra_equity
  - excess_after: max(0, debt_after - 3 * (equity + extra_equity))
expected:
  base: 90000000
  tax_gross: 31500000
  credit_ia: 3600000
  tax_net: 27900000
  excess_after: 0
```

## Ask the client first

1. Who receives the payment: an individual or a company, where is it resident, and is that country on the SII's treaty list or in a preferential regime (art. 41 H)? Is it related to the payer?
2. What exactly is paid for: dividend, interest (who is the lender), royalty or software, technical or engineering service, other service, insurance, freight, a share sale? Where was the service rendered?
3. When was it paid, credited or put at the payee's disposal (the F50 month)?
4. For a treaty rate: does the payer hold the residence certificate for this calendar year and the SII declaration of no permanent establishment and entitlement?
5. For interest to related parties abroad: total annual debt, equity at 1 January and capital movements in the year.
6. For a resident with foreign income: the foreign tax paid on each type of income, and the investments abroad for DJ 1929.
