# Comoros: electronic filing and selected tax procedures

## Who this applies to

Taxpayers of the Union of the Comoros and their representatives using, or required to use, the tax administration's electronic procedures; anyone subject to the tax administration's right of communication; banks and microfinance institutions; and businesses facing an on-site audit or a fiscal-flagrance report. Tax year 2026.

- Electronic procedures are **open to every taxpayer** (L.10 ter(1)).
- They become **mandatory in stages** fixed by decision of the Director General of Taxes for large enterprises, medium enterprises and any other category set by order according to turnover thresholds or tax regime (L.10 ter(2)).

This skill does not replace the full appeals and penalty regime. No live filing, payment or private-account interaction was performed. No independent accountant review has occurred.

## The base

### Enacted framework, operational commencement still to verify

Article 14 of [Finance Law 2026](https://finances.gouv.km/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Decret-portant-promulgation-du-loi-des-finances-2026.pdf) inserts LPF articles L.10 bis to L.10 undecies on electronic procedures. L.10 bis defines tele-procedures (filing, payment, consultation and information exchange through an official platform), e-filing, e-payment (mobile payment, transfer, bank card or other approved means) and the tax platform designated by the administration. L.10 ter provides phased mandatory use through decisions for large enterprises, medium enterprises and other designated categories; the arrangements, commencement dates and temporary exemptions are to be set by regulation. L.10 undecies calls for an order fixing a progressive enrolment timetable and support measures, and a ministerial order fixing commencement, with possible limited trials. **The enacted framework alone does not prove the rollout dates or the status of a particular taxpayer.** [Pages 10 to 12](https://finances.gouv.km/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Decret-portant-promulgation-du-loi-des-finances-2026.pdf).

An earlier draft of this skill relied on an amendment to LPF L.4 by the Finance Law 2024 (Law 23-019/AU) that made specified electronic returns optional in 2024 to 2025 and stated a mandatory electronic-filing rule from 1 January 2026. That text was read only from a reproduction on a non-government legal repository (Munganyo), and no official copy has been found. The 1 January 2026 mandate is therefore held: it is not usable guidance until the official text is read and reconciled with the 2026 provisions and current orders. Do not infer from the staged language that paper filing is optional, or that every taxpayer can already use every electronic payment channel. This skill does not certify either proposition.

### Filing proof and payment proof are different

Under L.10 quater, an electronic return is treated as filed at the date and time recorded by the designated platform and trusted timestamp mechanism. Its electronic acknowledgement is evidence unless rebutted. Electronic payment is treated as made on the date of successful settlement ("bonne fin") communicated by the approved payment provider. A payment instruction alone is not the successful-settlement evidence identified in the statute. [2026 law, page 10](https://finances.gouv.km/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Decret-portant-promulgation-du-loi-des-finances-2026.pdf).

L.10 septies requires the administration to keep data for the legal limitation period and taxpayers to retain acknowledgements and payment evidence electronically or on paper. Platform logs carry evidential weight in a dispute, subject to expert examination. Keep separate return and payment confirmations; one does not establish completion of the other. [Page 11](https://finances.gouv.km/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Decret-portant-promulgation-du-loi-des-finances-2026.pdf).

### Access, representative authority, data and fees

L.10 quinque requires a NIF and strong authentication (password plus another factor, a certificate or equivalent). Transmissions through the taxpayer or authorised representative account count as electronic signatures; electronic mandates, their scope and revocation are managed in the platform. An accountant should establish the mandate and its scope, rather than treat possession of a password as sufficient authority. [Page 11](https://finances.gouv.km/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Decret-portant-promulgation-du-loi-des-finances-2026.pdf).

L.10 sexies covers data sent electronically by professional secrecy and personal data protection law, and binds technical and payment providers to the same confidentiality and security duties. [Page 11](https://finances.gouv.km/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Decret-portant-promulgation-du-loi-des-finances-2026.pdf).

L.10 decies subjects participating banks, electronic-money institutions and mobile-payment operators to approval by order and agreement with the administration. Providers send reconciliation files and successful-payment notices daily. Transaction fees are borne by the taxpayer unless an order provides otherwise. The list of payment methods in the definitions is not evidence that every operator is approved. [Pages 10 and 12](https://finances.gouv.km/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Decret-portant-promulgation-du-loi-des-finances-2026.pdf).

### Other charges enacted for 2026

The 2026 law also creates a household waste charge (REOM, art. 15) and an eco-contribution on producers, importers and distributors under extended producer responsibility (art. 16). Both have rates set by joint order of the finance and environment ministers, and those orders have not been found. [Page 15](https://finances.gouv.km/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Decret-portant-promulgation-du-loi-des-finances-2026.pdf). Import charges are under Cross-border points.

## Deadlines and filing

### Outages and failed payment

L.10 octies distinguishes a planned outage, for which the administration publishes notice and suspends deadlines for the announced period, from a major incident at a deadline, for which additional time is granted by the Director's notice without penalties for that period. Failing that, filing at the counter may be accepted exceptionally, on terms set by the administration. Preserve the relevant notice and incident evidence; a local connection problem is not automatically an announced extension. [Page 11](https://finances.gouv.km/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Decret-portant-promulgation-du-loi-des-finances-2026.pdf).

### Information requests

New L.44 bis requires those subject to the right of communication to provide requested information electronically within **eight working days**, during legal service hours, counted from receipt of the notice or administration letter. This is not eight calendar days. Establish the receipt date, requested material and lawful deadline rather than applying the rule to every informal enquiry. [2026 law, page 14](https://finances.gouv.km/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Decret-portant-promulgation-du-loi-des-finances-2026.pdf).

Under the 2023 code, procedure documents sent by post are deemed received fifteen days after sending, the postmark governing, at the postal address given to the administration (L.43). A taxpayer who has not filed on time receives a reminder that counts as formal notice and has seven days from receipt to regularise, after which the base may be set by the administration (L.5). [2023 code, L.5 and L.43](https://justice.gouv.km/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/comores-cgi-2023.pdf).

### Bank reporting and beneficial owners

New L.46 bis in the 2026 law requires banks and microfinance institutions to report opening, change and closure of business accounts of enterprises of every legal form to the tax directorate (its Financial Operations Directorate), within thirty days of opening. Breach leads to the sanctions of the procedure book. [2026 law, page 14](https://finances.gouv.km/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Decret-portant-promulgation-du-loi-des-finances-2026.pdf). The 2024 amending law had already used the number L.46 bis for a duty on commercial and civil companies to keep a beneficial-owner register, kept up to date and shown on request. [2024 amending law, page 16](https://finances.gouv.km/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/LOI-DE-FINANCES-RECTIFICATIVES-2024.pdf). The 2026 text says it adds an article; it does not say it replaces the register. Both are treated as in force until the numbering is settled.

### Site visit length

Under L.42, as amended in 2024, on-site audit work cannot exceed three months for sole traders and six months for companies. This can be extended by three months for a company under the L.21 bis documentation duty, or where a treaty information request goes abroad. [2024 amending law, page 14](https://finances.gouv.km/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/LOI-DE-FINANCES-RECTIFICATIVES-2024.pdf).

## Penalties and interest

L.10 nonies links failure to use mandatory electronic procedures to the code's penalties (specific fines and surcharges). Partial payments or bank rejections carry late interest and penalties from the day after the deadline. This skill does not invent a uniform electronic-filing penalty rate or assume a failed bank instruction satisfies payment. Sworn officers may check the conformity of tele-procedures, including the authenticity of mandates and electronic payments. [Page 12](https://finances.gouv.km/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Decret-portant-promulgation-du-loi-des-finances-2026.pdf).

Amended L.113 specifies **KMF 100,000** for attempting to evade or obstruct the right of communication or third-party collection notice, and a **KMF 50,000 per-day** coercive amount for the stated delaying conduct, with distinct timing language. The 2023 code amounts were KMF 20,000 and KMF 10,000 a day. Those are specific enforcement provisions; do not treat them as the automatic penalty for every late tax return. [Page 14](https://finances.gouv.km/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Decret-portant-promulgation-du-loi-des-finances-2026.pdf).

General rules in the 2023 code, not amended by the laws read: late payment carries interest of 10% per month, capped at 50% of the principal. It runs from the first day of the month after receipt of a collection notice, from the day after the legal filing date (every month begun counting as a whole month), or from the day after the legal due date, to the last day of the month of payment (L.115). Reassessments carry the same 10% a month (a month being 30 working days under L.96), capped at 50% (L.96, L.99), plus surcharges of 50%, 100% or 150% (L.97). Assessment after an unanswered formal notice to file carries a 100% surcharge, 150% on a repeat (L.98). Not filing a nil return after formal notice costs KMF 10,000 (L.100). Late provisional instalments under CGI article 38 have a separate rule in L.115 bis: 10% per month of delay, with no 50% cap stated in that article, calculated on what it calls the "tiers provisionnel". Article 38 describes each instalment as a quarter of prior-year tax. The applicable instalment and interest base must be reconciled with the tax authority before use; these excerpts do not support an instalment-interest calculation. [2023 code, CGI art.38 and LPF L.95 to L.115 bis](https://justice.gouv.km/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/comores-cgi-2023.pdf).

### Fiscal flagrance and response

New L.43 bis addresses serious, consistent indications of concealed activity or deliberate failures. Examples include undeclared revenue-generating activity, missing or false invoices or documents, concealed receipts or stocks and refusal to produce mandatory accounting documents. It permits authorised officers to record findings on site, access accounting and financial records, issue an immediately enforceable report, initiate assessment without prior formal demand and propose protective measures over goods, stock or funds. [2026 law, page 9](https://finances.gouv.km/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Decret-portant-promulgation-du-loi-des-finances-2026.pdf).

The report is notified to the affected person and sent to the Director General, with possible referral for criminal proceedings. The provision gives **fifteen days from notification** for an administrative or contentious challenge. That is a rule for this procedure, not a universal tax-appeal deadline. A notice under it needs immediate professional assessment of the response route and any stay or security requirements. [L.43 bis, page 9](https://finances.gouv.km/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Decret-portant-promulgation-du-loi-des-finances-2026.pdf).

## Cross-border points

- An on-site audit can run three months longer where the administration sends an information request to a foreign tax administration under a treaty or international agreement (L.42).
- Import licences enacted for 2026: ordinary rice FC 0.5 per kg imported each year (art. 17); luxury rice FC 1 per kg, replacing the earlier flat KMF 2,000,000 per importer (art. 18, amending art. 45 of the 2006 finance law). [Page 15](https://finances.gouv.km/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Decret-portant-promulgation-du-loi-des-finances-2026.pdf).
- The Finance Law 2025 replaces the single administrative fee with a management and maintenance fee (FGM) of 5% of the CIF value of imported goods (art. 20); exempt goods are to be set by ministerial order, not found. [2025 law, page 10](https://finances.gouv.km/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/Decret-N%C2%B024-186PR-du-19-decembre-2024-portant-promulgation-loi-N%C2%B024-016PR-Portant-Loi-de-Finances-2025.pdf).
- Customs withholding for importers without a tax clearance attestation is in [km-registration-and-tax-clearance](km-registration-and-tax-clearance.md); transfer pricing documentation is in [km-transfer-pricing](km-transfer-pricing.md).

## Two worked examples that recompute

A taxpayer delays a right of communication request by 6 days past the stated deadline. At KMF 50,000 a day under L.113, that is KMF 300,000.

```oa-check
name: Six days of delay
inputs:
  days: 6
steps:
  - amount: days * 50000
expected:
  amount: 300000
```

A 20 day delay gives KMF 1,000,000. The KMF 100,000 fixed fine is a separate charge for evading or obstructing the right.

```oa-check
name: Twenty days of delay
inputs:
  days: 20
steps:
  - amount: days * 50000
expected:
  amount: 1000000
```

An edge case on the L.115 cap: tax of KMF 1,000,000 is paid 7 months late. Uncapped interest would be 70%, KMF 700,000; the cap is 50% of the principal, so interest is KMF 500,000. Paid 3 months late, interest is KMF 300,000, below the cap.

```oa-check
name: Late-payment interest with the 50% cap
inputs:
  tax: 1000000
  months_late: 7
  months_late_short: 3
steps:
  - interest: min(tax * 0.10 * months_late, tax * 0.50)
  - interest_short: min(tax * 0.10 * months_late_short, tax * 0.50)
expected:
  interest: 500000
  interest_short: 300000
```

## Ask the client first

- Is the taxpayer a large or medium enterprise, and has it received a Director General's decision or enrolment notice making e-filing mandatory?
- For a disputed filing or payment: what do the platform acknowledgement and the payment provider's successful-settlement notice say, with dates and times?
- Was there an announced outage or a Director's notice extending the deadline?
- For an information request: when was the notice or letter received, and what exactly does it ask for?
- Has a fiscal-flagrance report been notified, and on what date?
- How many months late was the tax paid, and from which starting point does interest run?
