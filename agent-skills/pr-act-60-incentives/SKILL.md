# Puerto Rico Act 60 decrees: resident investors and export services, 2026

> **Source-cited draft.** Built from the Puerto Rico Incentives Code, Act 60-2019, legislative-library revision of 16 July 2026, which includes the Act 38-2026 amendments, read on 2026-11-13. No accountant has reviewed it. A decree is granted by the Secretary of the Department of Economic Development and Commerce (DDEC). This skill does not say that any person qualifies.

**Answer first.** An individual resident investor who **files the decree application on or before 31 December 2026** has interest and dividends, and capital gain that accrued after the move, **fully exempt** from Puerto Rico income tax, including the alternate basic tax, when that income is earned after the move and recognized before 1 January 2036. The same items for an application **filed on or after 1 January 2027** are taxed at a **4 percent** preferential rate through 2055. Long-term gain that accrued before the move is **5 percent** only if it is recognized after ten years of residence and before the cohort's end date. Export-service net income under a Chapter 3 decree is taxed at **4 percent**. The investor's annual reports must show a contribution of at least **USD 10,000**, and those reports carry an annual charge of **USD 5,000**.

## Scope and who this is for

In scope: two Act 60 decrees.

- Chapter 2, individual resident investors (the successor to Act 22-2012): sections 2021.01, 2022.01, 2022.02, 2023.01 and the investor paragraphs of section 6020.10.
- Chapter 3, export of services and promoter services: sections 2031.01 and 2032.01, plus the decree term in section 6020.03.

An individual resident investor is a person who can receive the benefits of sections 2022.01 and 2022.02, who is a Puerto Rico resident individual, who was not a Puerto Rico resident between 17 January 2006 and 17 January 2012, and who becomes a Puerto Rico resident no later than the tax year ending 31 December 2055. A student who lived in Puerto Rico before leaving to study, and a person temporarily outside Puerto Rico for the Government of Puerto Rico, does not qualify, because the domicile remains Puerto Rico. For an application submitted after 31 December 2026, the person must also show that they were not a Puerto Rico resident for at least six years before the date they move. [Section 1020.02(a)(4)](https://bvirtualogp.pr.gov/ogp/Bvirtual/leyesreferencia/PDF/60-2019.pdf). The Internal Revenue Code treats a resident individual as a person domiciled in Puerto Rico, and presumes residence after 183 days of presence in the calendar year. [Section 1010.01(a)(30) of the Internal Revenue Code](https://bvirtualogp.pr.gov/ogp/Bvirtual/leyesreferencia/PDF/C%C3%B3digos/1-2011/1-2011.pdf).

A hard-to-recruit professional who takes the salary benefit in section 2022.03 cannot also take sections 2022.01 and 2022.02, and cannot hold an Act 22 decree. [Section 2022.03(b)](https://bvirtualogp.pr.gov/ogp/Bvirtual/leyesreferencia/PDF/60-2019.pdf).

## Ask the client first

1. **Which decree:** individual resident investor, export services, or both. They are different applications.
2. **Application date:** on or before 31 December 2026, or on or after 1 January 2027. The rates change on that line. If a decree was requested by 31 December 2026 and is not yet granted, the applicant may elect the post-2026 rates. [Section 6020.03(d)](https://bvirtualogp.pr.gov/ogp/Bvirtual/leyesreferencia/PDF/60-2019.pdf).
3. **Residence history:** dates of Puerto Rico residence between 17 January 2006 and 17 January 2012, the move date, and, for a post-2026 application, the six years before the move. Student and government-temporary absences do not help.
4. **For each investment:** whether it was owned before the move, whether the gain is long-term, and how many years after the move it will be recognized.
5. **For export services:** the services, the customer (foreign person or not), any Puerto Rico nexus, whether the activity existed in the three tax years before the application, and that there is a bona fide office in Puerto Rico.
6. **Compliance already due:** decree date, whether the second tax year has started (the USD 10,000 contribution), and whether a principal residence was bought within two years.

### Individual resident investor

The person applies to the Secretary of the DDEC. [Section 2023.01(a)](https://bvirtualogp.pr.gov/ogp/Bvirtual/leyesreferencia/PDF/60-2019.pdf). Separate the income into three piles before using the table.

1. Interest and dividends from all sources, earned after the person became a resident and before the cohort's end date. Use 0 percent or 4 percent from the application date.
2. Capital gain on appreciation after the move. Use 0 percent or 4 percent. This covers assets owned at the move and assets bought after the move, but only the post-move appreciation.
3. Long-term capital gain on appreciation before the move. Use 5 percent only when recognition is after ten years of residence and before the cohort's end date. If the gain is recognized earlier, or it is not long-term, do not use 5 percent. Use the ordinary individual rules in `pr-individual-income-tax` for that slice.

Do not apply the investor rates to wages, pensions or business income. Those are not sections 2022.01 and 2022.02.

### Export services

An eligible business is a person with a bona fide office or establishment in Puerto Rico that carries on a listed service and that service is an export service or a promoter service. The list includes research and development, consulting, professional services (legal, tax and accounting), software, call centers, shared-service centers, and the other activities in section 2031.01(a). Work performed as an employee does not qualify. The business may have other activities if its books segregate the export income, costs and expenses to the satisfaction of the Secretary of Hacienda. [Section 2031.01](https://bvirtualogp.pr.gov/ogp/Bvirtual/leyesreferencia/PDF/60-2019.pdf).

A service is an export service when it is for the benefit of a foreign person, a trust or an estate with no Puerto Rico-resident beneficiaries or fiduciaries of the kinds the section lists, or a person doing business in Puerto Rico whose own customer meets those tests, and the service has no nexus with Puerto Rico. Nexus includes a business or income-producing activity in Puerto Rico, advice on Puerto Rico law, lobbying the Puerto Rico government, and a sale of property for use, consumption or disposition in Puerto Rico. [Section 2031.01(b) and (c)](https://bvirtualogp.pr.gov/ogp/Bvirtual/leyesreferencia/PDF/60-2019.pdf).

If the business was already providing the export or promoter services on the application date, or at any time in the three preceding tax years, the 4 percent applies only to the increase in net income over the average net income of that base period. The base-period net income is taxed at Internal Revenue Code rates. See `pr-company-income-tax` for a regular corporation. Do not copy those rates into this computation. The statute reduces the base-period amount by 25 percent a year until it is zero for the fourth tax year of the decree. Predecessor operations count. [Section 2032.01(d)](https://bvirtualogp.pr.gov/ogp/Bvirtual/leyesreferencia/PDF/60-2019.pdf).

The decree lasts 15 years if the holder meets its conditions, unless the Code provides otherwise. A further 15 years is a separate request under section 6020.03(c), not an automatic renewal.

### Reports, the contribution and the residence

An exempt business with a decree files a Puerto Rico income-tax return even if gross or net income is zero, separate from any other return, for the exempt operations. A shareholder files an individual return when the Internal Revenue Code requires one. [Section 6020.10(a)(1) and (a)(2)](https://bvirtualogp.pr.gov/ogp/Bvirtual/leyesreferencia/PDF/60-2019.pdf).

Two compliance reports go to the Incentives Office electronically:

- For a calendar-year business, no later than 15 November after the close of the calendar year. For a fiscal year, the 15th day of the 11th month after the year ends. [Section 6020.10(a)(3)](https://bvirtualogp.pr.gov/ogp/Bvirtual/leyesreferencia/PDF/60-2019.pdf).
- No later than 30 days after the income-tax return due date, including extensions. [Section 6020.10(a)(4)](https://bvirtualogp.pr.gov/ogp/Bvirtual/leyesreferencia/PDF/60-2019.pdf).

For tax years beginning after 31 December 2024, every report required by section 6020.10, including the compliance report, is filed electronically with the Secretary of Hacienda as part of the exempt business's income-tax return, with the fee. The Secretary may postpone that rule for one more year by a general publication. [Section 6020.10(f)](https://bvirtualogp.pr.gov/ogp/Bvirtual/leyesreferencia/PDF/60-2019.pdf).

The investor contribution is not a tax. It starts in the second tax year after the decree and is paid directly to the nonprofit. The annual report must include the evidence. The USD 5,000 charge is in addition to the USD 10,000 contribution.

Federal residence and the section 933 exclusion are not decided by the decree. Use `pr-federal-residence-and-filing`.

## Boundary and exception table

| Situation | What changes |
| --- | --- |
| Application on or before 31 December 2026 | 0 percent on post-move interest, dividends and post-move gain, through 2035 |
| Application on or after 1 January 2027 | 4 percent on those items, through 2055, and the six-year nonresidence test applies |
| Pre-move gain recognized before ten years of residence | Not the 5 percent rate |
| Pre-move gain that is not long-term | Not the 5 percent rate |
| Decree revoked under section 6020.09(a) | The preferential rates stop |
| Hard-to-recruit professional salary benefit | Cannot be combined with sections 2022.01 and 2022.02 |
| Export service with a Puerto Rico nexus | Not export-service income |
| Services performed as an employee | Not an eligible business |
| Activity existed in the three years before the application | 4 percent only on the increase over base-period average net income |
| Second tax year of an investor decree | The USD 10,000 contribution starts. It is not due in the first tax year under section 2023.01(c) |
| No principal residence bought within two years | The section 6020.10(c) condition fails |

## Worked cases

**Case 1. Investor who applied on 1 June 2026.** The decree is granted and is not revoked. In 2026, after the move, the holder receives USD 100,000 of dividends and recognizes USD 200,000 of gain on securities bought after the move. Both are before 1 January 2036, so both are exempt. The holder also recognizes USD 50,000 of long-term gain that accrued before the move. Recognition is 11 years after the move and before 1 January 2036, so that slice is 5 percent: USD 2,500. Puerto Rico tax on these three items is USD 2,500. Wages or business income, if any, are not in this figure. The USD 10,000 contribution is not part of this tax. It starts in the second tax year.

```oa-check
name: Pre-2027 investor, dividends and two gains (2026)
inputs:
  dividends: 100000
  post_move_gain: 200000
  pre_move_gain: 50000
steps:
  - dividend_tax: dividends * 0
  - post_move_tax: post_move_gain * 0
  - pre_move_tax: pre_move_gain * 0.05
  - tax: dividend_tax + post_move_tax + pre_move_tax
expected:
  dividend_tax: 0.00
  post_move_tax: 0.00
  pre_move_tax: 2500.00
  tax: 2500.00
```

**Case 2, a decision: file by 31 December 2026 or wait.** The same person will have USD 80,000 of interest after becoming a resident, recognized before 2036. An application filed on 1 June 2026 taxes that interest at 0 percent. An application filed on 2 January 2027 taxes it at 4 percent: USD 3,200. Filing by 31 December 2026 saves USD 3,200 of Puerto Rico tax on this interest, if the decree is granted and not revoked. Waiting also adds the six-year nonresidence test and the Property Registry requirement for the residence. The 0 percent cohort ends for later recognition after 31 December 2035. The 4 percent cohort runs to 2055. This comparison is only the statutory rate. It is not a statement that DDEC will grant either application.

```oa-check
name: Investor interest, apply by 31 Dec 2026 or on 2 Jan 2027
inputs:
  interest: 80000
steps:
  - tax_if_by_2026: interest * 0
  - tax_if_from_2027: interest * 0.04
  - saving: tax_if_from_2027 - tax_if_by_2026
expected:
  tax_if_by_2026: 0.00
  tax_if_from_2027: 3200.00
  saving: 3200.00
```

**Export services, new activity.** A business with a bona fide office in Puerto Rico has USD 250,000 of net income from export services to foreign persons, with no Puerto Rico nexus, and it did not provide those services in the three tax years before the application. There is no base-period amount. Tax is 4 percent: USD 10,000. A dividend of that profit to a shareholder is not taxed again under section 2032.01(e). If the same activity had a base-period average, only the increase over that average would be at 4 percent, and this USD 10,000 figure would be wrong.

```oa-check
name: New export-service business, net income 250000 (2026)
inputs:
  net_income: 250000
steps:
  - tax: net_income * 0.04
expected:
  tax: 10000.00
```

## When to refuse or refer

| Code | Situation | Action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| R-60-DATE | The application date is unknown | Do not choose 0 percent or 4 percent |
| R-60-HISTORY | Residence between 17 January 2006 and 17 January 2012, or the six-year test, is not documented | Do not say the person is an individual resident investor |
| R-60-GAIN | The file does not split pre-move and post-move appreciation, or does not show the holding period | Do not use 0, 4 or 5 percent on that gain |
| R-60-NEXUS | The export customer or the Puerto Rico nexus is unclear | Do not apply 4 percent |
| R-60-BASE | The activity may have existed in the three years before the application and the average is not computed | Do not apply 4 percent to the whole net income |
| R-60-FED | The question is the federal return or section 933 | Use `pr-federal-residence-and-filing` |
| R-60-OTHER | Manufacturing, tourism, film, agriculture or a physician decree | Outside this skill |

## Filing and payment

Apply to the Secretary of the DDEC. The statute requires the reports and the fees to be paid by electronic transfer through the Incentives Office portal, in the form that office sets. [Sections 2023.01 and 6020.10(a)](https://bvirtualogp.pr.gov/ogp/Bvirtual/leyesreferencia/PDF/60-2019.pdf). The income-tax return for the exempt operations still goes to Hacienda. An individual decree holder uses the individual due date, not the 15 June corporate-decree date. The Internal Revenue Code says section 1061.16(e) does not apply to an individual who holds an exemption decree. [Internal Revenue Code section 1061.16(e)](https://bvirtualogp.pr.gov/ogp/Bvirtual/leyesreferencia/PDF/C%C3%B3digos/1-2011/1-2011.pdf).

Order for an investor each year after the decree:

1. File the income-tax return on the individual due date. Pay any tax on items that are not at 0 percent, including a 5 percent pre-move gain or a 4 percent post-2026 item.
2. File the compliance reports by 15 November (calendar year) or the fiscal-year date, and again within 30 days after the return due date, including extensions.
3. From the second tax year, attach evidence of the USD 10,000 contribution, split as section 6020.10(b) requires unless the whole amount goes to the published child-poverty list.
4. Pay the USD 5,000 report charge.
5. Keep evidence that the principal residence is still owned, and that it was bought within two years of the decree from an unrelated person.

A missed or late report can draw an administrative fine of up to USD 10,000. An incomplete report can be treated as not filed after 15 days' notice.

## Completion checklist

- Application date is on or before 31 December 2026, or on or after 1 January 2027, and the rate matches that date.
- The 2006-2012 window and, where required, the six-year test are recorded.
- Each gain is tagged pre-move or post-move, long-term or not, and with the year of recognition.
- Export income is tested for a foreign beneficiary and for Puerto Rico nexus, and for a base period.
- The USD 10,000 contribution is not deducted from the tax in the first tax year.
- The USD 5,000 charge is not described as the contribution. They are two amounts.
- Federal filing is left to `pr-federal-residence-and-filing`.
