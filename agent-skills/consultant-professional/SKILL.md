# Consultant and professional services v2.0

## Scope

For accountants classifying management consulting, legal, architectural, accounting, coaching and advisory work, including WIP, retainers, disbursements and client-money accounts. It is not a worldwide professional-licensing or client-money compliance certificate.

This is a cross-country method for 2026. Establish the entity, country, tax year, reporting framework and permitted accounting basis before using it. Financial-reporting recognition is separate from income-tax deductions, capital allowances and VAT/GST. The IFRS principles below use the Australian adopted paragraph text as an official public reference; Australian-specific paragraphs and local simplified rules are not automatically rules in another country. Match the compilation and local adoption to the reporting period. Do not silently apply this method as US GAAP or a country's cash-basis tax computation.

## Ask the client first

- Which person/entity earns the income, owns the assets and signs the contracts? Obtain the legal structure, residence, business locations and reporting dates.
- Which reporting framework and tax accounting basis apply? What registrations, elections, prior balances and tax losses exist?
- Obtain contracts, invoices, credit notes, bank and platform statements, withholding certificates, refunds, chargebacks and opening/closing balances.
- What is sold, when and to whom? Identify customer location and status, performance obligations, intermediaries, private use and any money held for another person.
- Obtain cost invoices, asset ownership/use records, funding agreements, payroll/contractor documents, related-party dealings and the actual terms of insurance and licences.

## The method, step by step

1. Establish legal ownership and accounting/tax scope before coding transactions. Keep each business entity, owner and client-money account separate.
2. Map each contract to the actual promised goods/services and the consideration the business is entitled to. Determine recognition from performance and control. Billing, payout and cash dates are evidence, not universal recognition rules.
3. Reconcile each income source from transactions to returns/refunds, fees, withheld tax, reserves and bank settlement. Review principal/agent status before choosing gross or net reporting. Do not double-count a payout and the underlying sales.
4. Reconcile receivables, conditional contract assets, customer deposits, contract liabilities, deferred fees and client funds. Separate price concessions from credit losses. Obtain the applicable impairment standard before quantifying a loss allowance.
5. Establish business purpose, private proportion, capital versus revenue treatment and timing for every cost. Obtain the current country law before claiming a tax deduction; an accounting expense is not proof of tax deductibility.
6. Prepare an asset register with invoices, costs, ownership, available-for-use date, residual value, useful life, method and disposal proceeds. Maintain a separate tax-capital-allowance schedule. The prior purchase-price and useful-life ranges were unsupported planning estimates, not statutory values, and are held below.
7. Apply the country's VAT/GST/sales-tax rules by supply, customer, location and registration. Include reverse charges/imported services where relevant. Do not reclaim tax on exempt/private activity just because a supplier charged it.
8. Test payroll/contractor status, withholding, permanent establishment, cross-border reporting and elections using current country jobs. A platform tax form, incorporation or a merchant-of-record arrangement does not remove every obligation.
9. Deliver reconciled journals and schedules, source-linked tax adjustments, missing facts and a review checklist. Require the relevant professional review before a return, tax payment or material accounting judgment is relied on.

## Industry profile and records

Keep sole practitioner, partnership, LLP, personal-service company and single-member LLC classifications separate. Revenue models include hourly/day work, fixed fees, access or work retainers, success/contingent fees, value-based and productised services, speaking and training. The label does not determine recognition or employment status.

Monitor utilisation, effective rates, retention, pipeline, debtor ageing, WIP and capacity using the practice's actual data. Do not treat the former margin, revenue, payment-term or seasonal benchmarks as industry facts. Engagement terms determine payment dates; a long credit period does not establish when the service was earned.

For WIP, distinguish revenue earned but conditional on further performance, an unconditional receivable and qualifying fulfilment costs. Do not value all unbilled time as hours multiplied by cost or a hoped-for billing rate. Do not automatically reduce revenue for a later credit loss: distinguish contractual price concessions, work never entitled to billing and credit impairment. Reconcile opening unbilled balances, earned amounts, invoices, adjustments and closing balances before using the country's tax-basis rules.

### Client and trust money

Identify who beneficially owns each balance. Money held for client tax, court fees, property completion or onward payment is not practice fee revenue merely because it passes through the practice's bank. Keep client-ledger reconciliations, regulatory account requirements and the practice's own books distinct. Do not mix money held for another with fee receipts or write it off through revenue on onward payment.

A transfer from a client account to an office account is **not itself the revenue-recognition event**. Verify entitlement, authority to transfer, service performance and prior revenue first. Otherwise the same fee can be recognised twice. Interest may belong to the client, a designated body or the practice under the applicable rules; no global ownership rule is asserted here. Exact client-money regulations, segregation rules and interest entitlement remain held in Sources until the relevant regulator's law has been read.

## Worked case: earned fees and client funds

Fabricated currency units, excluding tax and credit losses. Assume an enforceable over-time service has earned fixed consideration of 12,000 at the reporting date. Only 9,000 has been billed; the remaining right depends on further performance. The billed receivable is unpaid. A separate 5,000 is held solely to pay a client's filing obligation, and the practice has no entitlement to it as a fee. Revenue is 12,000, with 9,000 receivable and 3,000 contract asset. The client funds do not increase revenue. [AASB 15, paragraphs 31-45 and 105-109](https://standards.aasb.gov.au/aasb-15-dec-2022).

```oa-check
name: Professional fee contract balance without counting client funds as income
inputs:
  earned_fees: 12000
  billed_fees: 9000
  fees_collected: 0
  client_money_received: 5000
steps:
  revenue: earned_fees
  receivable: billed_fees - fees_collected
  contract_asset: earned_fees - billed_fees
  client_money_liability: client_money_received
expected:
  revenue: 12000
  receivable: 9000
  contract_asset: 3000
  client_money_liability: 5000
```

Boundary: if the remaining right is unconditional, classify it as a receivable instead. If the service does not meet the over-time criteria, do not use this case to recognise unperformed deliverables. If no obligation to the client exists for a receipt, investigate ownership before assigning a liability.

## Costs to investigate

- **Indemnity and run-off:** obtain the policy, insured entity, coverage period, exclusions, retroactive date, claims-made terms and cessation obligations. Run-off is not automatically deductible when paid or automatically a provision. Review the actual obligation and period under the framework and local tax law.
- **Practice permissions:** practising certificates, firm/individual authorisations, compensation-fund contributions and regulator levies. Identify the actual professional requirement and fees; do not invent common international limits.
- **Memberships and CPD:** CPA, ACCA, law societies, Bar Associations, RICS and specialist groups; conferences, journals, databases, technical standards and books. Establish business purpose and any employer/private or initial-qualification element. Opportunity cost of the owner's study time is not a paid expense.
- **Office and technology:** rent, serviced office, home-office allocation, furniture, equipment, stationery, printing, postage, meeting rooms, reception space and communications. Review capital and mixed-use items separately.
- **Software:** Clio, PracticePanther, Xero Practice Manager, Toggl, Harvest, Clockify, SharePoint, iManage, NetDocuments, Xero, QuickBooks, FreeAgent, Zoom, Teams, Slack, Microsoft 365, Google Workspace, HubSpot, Salesforce, Pipedrive, DocuSign, Adobe Sign, Asana, Monday.com and Notion. Determine actual subscription/service rights and period, not a blanket deduction from the brand.
- **Client development:** advertising, websites, LinkedIn, directories, Chambers/Legal 500 submissions, client seminars, proposals and unsuccessful tenders. Separate client entertainment, gifts and business promotion and apply actual local rules. No global meals deduction percentage applies.
- **Travel:** client sites, conferences, other offices and international engagements. Keep itinerary, business/private purpose and records. Determine commuting and temporary-workplace rules locally rather than applying a universal “never deductible” label.
- **Connected-party staff:** document real work, duties, pay, employment and withholding. A payment to a spouse is not justified by a generic salary example.

## Professional IP and acquisition questions

Distinguish internally developed methods, frameworks, software, templates, training materials and content for sale from bought licences and client deliverables. Establish control, identifiable rights, the applicable standard and measurable qualifying costs. Do not presume all internal software or course development must be expensed because the practice is small. Client billings do not themselves decide whether the practice retains a separate asset, and retaining copyright alone does not establish capitalisable cost.

For licensed third-party training/methods, determine whether the contract creates a controlled intangible, a service or another arrangement. Do not automatically spread every licence over its contractual term without identifying its nature. Purchased client lists and goodwill must be distinguished, including whether a practice acquisition is a business combination or an asset acquisition.
## Asset, lease and intellectual-property checks

Do not copy the former asset-price and useful-life ranges into an accounting or tax register. Use actual acquisition cost and the applicable framework; review private use, ownership, finance, impairment and disposal separately. For a lessee applying IFRS 16 there is no general exemption merely because the business is a sole practitioner or the lease is called operating. Specific short-term and low-value exemptions have conditions. [AASB 16, paragraphs 5-8 and 22](https://standards.aasb.gov.au/aasb-16-nov-2022).

Research costs and internally generated goodwill/brands are not automatically capitalisable. Development requires all recognition criteria, including technical feasibility, intent, ability to use/sell, probable benefits, resources and reliable cost measurement. Once the criteria are met, qualifying subsequent development costs are not excluded merely because the developer is self-employed. Previously expensed expenditure is not reinstated as an asset later. Finite-life intangibles are amortised from availability for use using a supported life and pattern; indefinite-life intangibles are not amortised and require impairment review. Purchased rights, service subscriptions and business-combination goodwill require separate classification. [AASB 138, paragraphs 48-71 and 97-110](https://standards.aasb.gov.au/aasb-138-dec-2022).

For an IFRS business combination, goodwill is not routinely amortised; it is carried subject to impairment, with annual testing. Separately identified finite-life customer lists or other intangibles can have amortisation. Other local GAAP and tax amortisation may differ. Do not post tax relief or a generic goodwill amortisation period without identifying the regime. [AASB 3, paragraph B63](https://standards.aasb.gov.au/aasb-3-dec-2022-0); [AASB 136, paragraphs 9-10 and 90](https://standards.aasb.gov.au/aasb-136-jan-2026-0).

## Platform, withholding and foreign-currency reporting

Identify the legal payer and contract separately for every platform or processor. Reconcile gross entitled consideration, platform fees, refunds, taxes withheld, rolling reserves, exchange conversion and settlements. A platform statement can include non-revenue transfers; a tax report can report gross payments that differ from accounting income. Check refunds, duplicated forms and the actual issuing entity. Do not assume every named platform issues the same tax form.

The former US Form 1099-K shorthand is not a taxability threshold. Current IRS guidance distinguishes payment-card transactions from third-party settlement organisations, notes that a form may be issued below the statutory reporting threshold, and explains that a form alone does not decide taxable income. Retain the reporting question in the current US job; this global method does not introduce a new US return computation. [IRS, Understanding Form 1099-K](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/understanding-your-form-1099-k).

For an international payment, establish the income's character, source, work location, beneficial recipient, withholding documentation and any treaty claim. A US payer does not make every payment a US-source royalty. A platform's withholding is not automatically the final liability, and a credit requires its own evidence. Record foreign-currency recognition at the applicable transaction date and review the framework's translation and settlement requirements; invoice date is not universal where performance occurred earlier. Exact exchange-rate methods, treaty rates and withholding forms remain country-specific items below.

## United Kingdom: expense boundary

For a UK sole trader or individual in a business partnership, use the business-expense rules and private-use allocation, with the correct cash or traditional basis. Do not apply the sole-trader overview as company tax law. The owner's drawings, loan principal and an invented charge for the proprietor's own unpaid time are not ordinary paid business costs. [HMRC self-employed expenses](https://www.gov.uk/expenses-if-youre-self-employed).

Do not use the former global half-deduction default for client meals: UK client entertaining is not generally allowable under the cited self-employed guidance. Business advertising, qualifying subscriptions, professional/legal costs and training have their own conditions. The UK training guidance can cover new skills supporting the existing business, not only maintaining old skills; training to start a new unrelated business is different. Accountancy fees for the business and the individual's own Self Assessment preparation/submission are also distinguished. Review the actual cost and its purpose. [HMRC marketing/entertainment](https://www.gov.uk/expenses-if-youre-self-employed/marketing-entertainment-subscriptions); [HMRC legal/financial costs](https://www.gov.uk/expenses-if-youre-self-employed/legal-financial); [HMRC training](https://www.gov.uk/expenses-if-youre-self-employed/training-courses).

## Other countries and professional practice decisions

For Australia, Canada, EU jurisdictions and other countries, obtain the relevant official local rules for business deductions, WIP/cash-basis eligibility, professional-client accounts, capital allowances, professional-body fees, VAT/GST and income tax. This global file does not transfer UK deduction outcomes or IFRS balance-sheet amounts automatically to a local return. US employment, professional licensing, platform-reporting and tax-basis questions remain in the current US jobs.

Review principal-residence relief and exclusive-business-use consequences before a home-office claim. They are country- and fact-dependent, not a universal loss of relief whenever a room is used for work. For professional insurance and cyber/D&O policies, distinguish the insured person and actual business/private coverage; “claims made” remains subject to retroactive cover and exclusions. A policy does not guarantee cover for every historic act.

## Scaling and completion checklist

- Consider sole practitioner, partnership/LLP, associate and company options using actual profits, retention, pension arrangements, licensing permissions, liability and client requirements. No revenue threshold makes incorporation automatically optimal or reduces indemnity premiums by law.
- Test VAT/GST registration using the country's turnover definition, period, non-established-supplier rules, exemptions and treatment of disbursements. “Billings rather than collections” is not a universal statutory measure. Voluntary registration can affect clients differently and does not guarantee they recover the tax.
- Consider admin support, junior associates/paralegals, a bookkeeper/practice manager, another principal and marketing support when capacity and economics justify them. Prior hour, client and revenue benchmarks were planning suggestions without official support, not legal triggers.
- Monitor time and realisation, WIP ageing, debtor ageing, collection risk, pipeline, multiple consulting/training/product streams and management reporting using agreed practice targets. Do not inherit the old fixed cash buffer or debt-day cutoffs as law.
- Before a first hire, establish contracts, payroll, employer obligations and insurance. Classify employees, associates, of-counsel and subcontractors using actual control, delegation, equipment, exclusivity and local tests; the label alone does not decide.
- Before acquiring a practice, reconcile WIP, fees, client retention, liabilities and indemnity claims. For retirement, plan succession, sale, client-money closure, run-off and tax with the actual contract/regulator, not an invented common tail period.
- Refuse to certify regulatory compliance, owner entitlement to client funds, an unsupported success fee or a tax deduction while the needed documents or country rules are missing. Provide the unresolved list to the relevant professional reviewer.
