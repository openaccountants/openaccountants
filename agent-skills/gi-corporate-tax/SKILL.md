# Gibraltar corporate tax and filing v0.1

> **Source-cited draft.** Professional review is pending. Figures are limited to the periods and assumptions below. This Guide prepares working papers; it is not authority to file or pay.

## Scope

Prepare a corporate tax working paper for accounting periods wholly after 1 July 2024, checked in 2026. This draft covers ordinary rates and filing controls; it does not compute Pillar Two, specialised exemptions, losses or treaty permanent establishments.

Sources were accessed on 22 October 2026. A dated rate table controls its stated period; an undated guidance page does not prove that all later amendments have been incorporated.

## Charge and rates

Apply the source-of-profit analysis under section 11 and Schedule 1, not a blanket worldwide or tax-free-offshore assumption. The standard company rate is 15% from 1 July 2024. The higher rate is 20% for the statutory utility/dominant-position categories. Mixed activities require classification before rates are assigned. [ITO, Guidance Notes for Companies, August 2025, pp2–3](https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi/uploads/Income%20Tax%20Office/Corporate/Guidance%20Notes%20for%20Companies,%2008-2025.pdf); [Income Tax Act](https://www.gibraltarlaws.gov.gi/legislations/income-tax-act-2010-2627/download).

## Filing requirements

The complete CT1 and balancing payment are due nine months after the end of the month containing the accounting-period end. The return includes accounts, a tax computation where assessable income exists, and a PAYE reconciliation where required for a non-June year-end. Instalments are due on 28 February and 30 September; allocate them to the correct accounting period using Schedule 10. Ceasing trade does not itself end returns. [ITO corporate guidance](https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi/income-tax-office/businesses-and-corporations/corporate).

The August 2025 guidance states an assessable-income audit threshold of £1,750,000 for periods ending on or after 1 July 2024; below it, an Independent Accountant's Report accompanies unaudited accounts. Confirm the exact boundary and any applicable exemption in section 30. This tax-return test is separate from company-law filing and audit tests. [Guidance Notes, p2](https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi/uploads/Income%20Tax%20Office/Corporate/Guidance%20Notes%20for%20Companies,%2008-2025.pdf).

## Computation and examples

1. Reconcile accounts to a profit bridge: accounting profit, non-chargeable items, disallowances, capital allowances and separately supported reliefs.
2. Map each adjustment to the Act and retain the ledger transaction behind it. Unsupported deductions remain review questions.
3. Separate ordinary and higher-rate profit streams. Check connected-party dealings and interest restrictions independently.
4. Compute current liability, subtract period-matched payments and reconcile the residual to CT1.
5. Keep company-registry accounts, corporation-tax filing and payroll reporting as separate calendar events.

For **already established** ordinary taxable profit of £100,000, tax is **£15,000**. If £10,000 was paid on account for that period, the residual is **£5,000**. An accounting period ending 30 June 2026 ordinarily produces a 31 March 2027 return/balance date; retain any applicable official extension. Zero accounting profit does not prove zero taxable profit.

## Failure and boundary checks

Section 64 applies a 10% late-payment surcharge, followed after 90 days by a further 20% on the outstanding tax plus surcharge. A fully outstanding £1,000 therefore becomes £1,100 and then £1,320 before other liabilities. Section 65 return penalties are separate and depend on company size. Check periods and payment dates before calculating either. [Income Tax Act, ss64–65](https://www.gibraltarlaws.gov.gi/legislations/income-tax-act-2010-2627/download).

**Open scope:** full deductibility/capital-allowance schedules, loss use, connected-party adjustments, minimum tax, all filing exemptions, and registry reporting must be completed before calling corporate coverage comprehensive.
