# Thailand payroll and social contributions v1.0

> **General reference only.** This skill is general tax/accounting reference material for AI-assisted workflows. It has not been reviewed for any specific person's facts, documents, elections, deadlines, residency, filing status, or local procedures. Do not rely on it to file, pay, amend, or take a tax position without review by a qualified professional in the relevant jurisdiction.

## Who this applies to

**2026 answer layer:** prepare ordinary employee salary withholding and employer/employee Social Security Fund working papers, keeping Workmen's Compensation Fund and any Employee Welfare Fund duties separate. Employee social security is not an employer deduction from the employer's own contribution. Thai tax withholding and social-security wages are different bases.

For ordinary Social Security Act §33 coverage, the Labour Ministry's handbook describes entry at ages 15-60, with continuing coverage for an existing insured employee who remains employed after the upper entry age; exclusions and voluntary §39/§40 status require separate analysis. This is not a universal rule for every director, contractor or self-employed person. Confirm the SSO registration/status before applying the formula. [Labour Ministry handbook, social-security chapter](https://ccpl.mol.go.th/wp-content/uploads/sites/4/2019/11/article_20190329091926.pdf).

## The base

1. Obtain the employment contract, actual pay period, gross salary, taxable benefits, bonuses, reimbursements, joining/leaving dates, residence facts and supported allowances. Record whether each amount enters tax income, social-security wages, or neither, with its legal reason. Do not use net bank pay as gross wages.
2. Confirm §33 status and the legally applicable SSO base, caps and any local/monthly relief. For an ordinary full month with covered wages, use the legal base between THB 1,650 and THB 17,500, multiply by 5%, and round each employee contribution to whole baht: 50 satang or more rounds up and less is discarded. The employer contributes the same rounded amount. The [current SSO contribution-form instructions 1, 4 and 6](https://www.sso.go.th/wpr/assets/upload/files_storage/sso_th/f2ebf7a94c878bdc9fa50ccca33d19f1.pdf) require actual paid wages in column 4 and state separately that a person with no wages is reported with zero in columns 4 and 5. Do not impose the minimum contribution merely because an insured person remains on the register with no wage payment. Partial months, multiple employers, irregular pay and legal wage-base exclusions still require their specific governing rules.
3. Build PIT withholding under [Revenue Code §50(1)](https://www.rd.go.th/5937.html): annualise the assessable periodic pay by the number of payments, apply the supported annual tax rules, then divide by that number of payments, with the statutory residual/adjustment mechanics. Use [PIT](thailand-pit.md) for the actual bands and allowance conditions. A bonus, changing salary or leaver requires the appropriate adjustment rather than blindly reusing the last month's withholding. Employee social payments enter the individual allowance only as actually legally paid; do not deduct the employer contribution as that allowance.
4. Reconcile gross pay minus employee social contribution, PIT withholding and separately supported deductions to take-home pay. Employer cost includes gross pay plus employer social security and other employer liabilities; the government contribution is neither an employee deduction nor the employer's own payroll expense.
5. Keep the Workmen's Compensation Fund distinct. The old employer-only 0.2%-1.0% industry-risk range was not sourced to the current official assessment schedule in the original. Do not deduct it from employees or pick the lowest rate by default; obtain the employer's risk classification, legal annual wage limit and actual assessment before computing it.
6. Check the separate Employee Welfare Fund position for the actual payroll month and employer. The opened [government notice of 14 October 2026](https://www.thaigov.go.th/th/news/170175) reports a proposed postponement and explicitly says the legislative steps remain to be completed. It is not proof by itself that the amended instrument has entered into force. Do not apply an obsolete launch notice or assume a deduction/exemption from this report; the enacted instruments and employer-specific scope are an explicit unresolved boundary below.

## Deadlines and filing

### Social security

The [SSO form, part 2 instruction 2](https://www.sso.go.th/wpr/assets/upload/files_storage/sso_th/f2ebf7a94c878bdc9fa50ccca33d19f1.pdf) requires SSP.1-10 parts 1 and 2 and remittance by the 15th of the month following the contribution deduction; its instruction describes surcharge starting on the 16th. The [official PRD notice](https://www.prd.go.th/th/content/category/detail/id/2940/iid/452819) describes that ordinary due date while reporting proposed relief for specified affected southern provinces/months. It is not automatic nationwide relief. Obtain the actual enacted extension/temporary-rate instrument and confirm employer location and payroll month before moving a due date or reducing a rate. The payroll calculation and the payment extension are separate questions.

Register the employer and insured employees through the ordinary SSO service/office process before submitting the monthly contribution record; the Labour Ministry handbook describes employer duties and changes to registered particulars. For the monthly SSO process, reconcile each insured person's identifier, covered wage, employee share and employer share; submit through the approved employer channel, pay against the generated reference and retain the accepted schedule and receipt. The exact current online screen, form layout and any electronic extension were not tested in a signed-in session, so do not claim an automatic additional payment period.

### Tax withholding and annual summary

Use the Revenue Department's [official e-Filing service index](https://www.rd.go.th/rdeservice/63813.html) for PND.1 and PND.1 Kor with attachments. Select taxpayer/employer, month/year and original/amended status; reconcile each employee's tax identifier, income category, gross payment and withholding to the payroll register and certificates; review the return total, submit and pay any tax against the generated reference. Save the filed form, employee schedule, acknowledgement and payment receipt separately. Reconcile annual PND.1 Kor to the monthly filings, benefits and leavers; issue the employee's 50 bis certificate on its own deadline. A nil computed withholding does not by itself establish that no employer report is required.

The 7th-next-month ordinary monthly deadline and February annual summary are supported by the RD guide; the older English §52 translation says payment-date timing and refers to the Ministry extension, so it is not used alone to assert the present ordinary monthly date. A current electronic extension must be checked in the actual period's calendar.

## Penalties and interest

Keep SSO arrears surcharge separate from tax withholding arrears and administrative/criminal non-registration or false-declaration offences. The stated social-security surcharge is not a complete penalty bill. PIT withholding arrears, current reductions, fine settlement and partial-month surcharge calculation require their own governing provisions; do not replace them with an invented universal fine or net them against an employee refund.

## Cross-border points

Foreign staff can have Thai-source salary tax regardless of where salary is paid; residence and treaties require their own analysis. A foreign employee's visa/work permit is separate from SSO coverage and payroll tax. Do not treat a foreign name, remote work or a director's label as proof of exclusion. Contractors, foreign secondments and simultaneous employers require factual classification and specific social-security rules. Use [formation](th-company-formation.md) for the distinction between business permission and employee work permission.

## Two worked examples that recompute

**Below the upper cap, ordinary full month.** Assume a registered §33 employee, covered wages THB 10,000, no special reduction and no unusual base exclusion or partial month. Employee and employer each contribute THB 500, giving THB 9,500 before PIT/other employee deductions and employer cost THB 10,500 before other employer liabilities. This is the ordinary example given by the opened SSO statement; the minimum base and rounding are independently stated in the current contribution-form instructions.

```oa-check
name: Thailand ordinary covered wage contribution
inputs:
  covered_wage: 10000
  rate: 0.05
  cap: 17500
steps:
  - employee: min(covered_wage, cap) * rate
  - employer: min(covered_wage, cap) * rate
  - before_pit: covered_wage - employee
  - employer_cost: covered_wage + employer
expected:
  employee: 500
  employer: 500
  before_pit: 9500
  employer_cost: 10500
```

**Current ceiling compared with the old payroll configuration.** Assume the same ordinary conditions and covered wages THB 30,000. The current share is THB 875 each. A stale cap of THB 15,000 would produce THB 750, understating each side by THB 125 and the combined remittance by THB 250. The decision is to correct the payroll configuration for the actual current period, not choose the cheaper historical cap. PIT is calculated separately.

```oa-check
name: Thailand current ceiling versus stale configuration
inputs:
  covered_wage: 30000
  current_cap: 17500
  historical_cap: 15000
  rate: 0.05
steps:
  - current_share: min(covered_wage, current_cap) * rate
  - historical_share: min(covered_wage, historical_cap) * rate
  - per_party_shortfall: current_share - historical_share
  - combined_shortfall: per_party_shortfall * 2
expected:
  current_share: 875
  historical_share: 750
  per_party_shortfall: 125
  combined_shortfall: 250
```

**Minimum-base and rounding decision.** Assume a covered full-month wage of THB 1,200 and the ordinary rate, with no temporary relief. The minimum calculation base is THB 1,650; 5% gives THB 82.50, rounded to THB 83 for each party. A zero-wage insured person is separately reported as zero under instruction 6; do not use this low-wage example for a partial-month or zero-wage fact pattern.

```oa-check
name: Thailand minimum contribution and half-baht rounding
inputs:
  covered_wage: 1200
  floor_base: 1650
  cap: 17500
  rate: 0.05
steps:
  - base: min(max(covered_wage, floor_base), cap)
  - unrounded: base * rate
  - employee: floor(unrounded + 0.5)
  - employer: employee
expected:
  base: 1650
  unrounded: 82.5
  employee: 83
  employer: 83
```

## Ask the client first

- Employer/employee registration status, role, joining/leaving dates and actual payroll month?
- Gross salary, benefits, bonus and the legally covered SSO wage amounts?
- SSO status, multiple employers, partial months or any local/monthly relief?
- Employee residence, foreign duties, supported allowances and tax certificates?
- Compensation-fund assessment and any provident/welfare-fund arrangement?
- Filed schedules, actual payments and any SSO/RD arrears notice?
