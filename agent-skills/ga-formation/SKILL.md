# Georgia entity formation and annual registration, 2026

> **Source-cited draft.** Built from the Georgia Secretary of State Corporations Division filing fee schedule (effective 6 September 2025), its Business Division FAQ, its how-to guides for forming a domestic entity and filing the annual registration and its corporation filing procedure, plus the Department of Revenue 2026 Employer's Tax Guide and sales tax registration FAQ and the Department of Labor employer FAQs, read on 2026-11-13. No accountant or attorney has reviewed it. All outputs must be reviewed by a qualified professional before filing.

**Answer first.** A Georgia LLC or corporation is formed online on eCorp (ecorp.sos.ga.gov) for **$110** ($100 fee plus a $10 service charge). A corporation must also send a **notice of intent to incorporate** to the legal organ newspaper of its registered office's county by the next business day, to run once a week for two weeks. Every entity then files an **annual registration** ($60 including the service charge) between 1 January and **1 April** each year; a corporation files its first one **within 90 days** of incorporation. A late annual registration adds **$25**; failure can end in administrative dissolution, reversed within 5 years for **$260**. A foreign entity registers for a **certificate of authority** for **$235**.

## Ask the client first

1. **Which entity, domestic or foreign?** A domestic entity is created in Georgia for the first time; a foreign one already exists elsewhere and needs a certificate of authority instead.
2. **The name** (or a name reservation number), and is it available on eCorp?
3. **Registered agent and office:** a person or entity located in Georgia at a Georgia street address (no post office box or mail drop). The entity cannot be its own agent.
4. **Organizers or incorporators, principal office mailing address, and for a corporation the number of authorized shares.**
5. **For a professional corporation:** are all owners licensed by the right Georgia board in a listed profession?
6. **What will it do in Georgia in its first year:** sell goods (sales tax), hire staff (withholding and unemployment insurance), and in which county is the registered office (for the corporation's notice)?

### Forming a Georgia LLC

1. **Search the name and register on eCorp** (ecorp.sos.ga.gov). Gather: the LLC name or reservation number, filer's name and address, a valid email, principal office mailing address, registered agent's name and Georgia address, each organizer's name and address, any optional provisions, and payment ([SOS how-to guide](https://sos.ga.gov/how-to-guide/how-guide-register-domestic-entity)).
2. **File the articles of organization** online (generated on screen), as an uploaded paper filing, or by mail. Pay $110. Expedited review is available for extra fees.
3. **What comes back:** the Secretary of State files the articles and issues the filing record, which banks ask for. The operating agreement is not filed.
4. **First annual registration:** between 1 January and 1 April of the year after formation, then every year ([SOS Business Division FAQ](https://sos.ga.gov/page/business-division-faq)).

### Forming a Georgia corporation

1. **Gather** the same items plus each incorporator and the number of authorized shares, and **file the articles of incorporation** on eCorp for $110 ([SOS how-to guide](https://sos.ga.gov/how-to-guide/how-guide-register-domestic-entity)). On approval, a Certificate of Incorporation is issued.
2. **Publish the notice of intent to incorporate.** "All corporations must publish a notice of intent to incorporate in the newspaper which is the official legal organ of the county where the initial registered office of the corporation is to be located, or in a newspaper of general circulation in such county and for which at least 60 percent of its subscriptions are paid." The notice and the publication fee go "directly to the newspaper no later than the next business day after filing articles of incorporation", asking the publisher to run it "once a week for two consecutive weeks". The list of legal organs is at gsccca.org/clerks, or ask the clerk of superior court ([SOS filing procedure for corporations](https://sos.ga.gov/sites/default/files/forms/Filing%20Procedure%20-%20Corporation.pdf)). The newspaper sets the fee. LLCs do not publish.
3. **Initial annual registration within 90 days** of incorporation, listing three principal officers, $60. A corporation formed between 2 October and 31 December files the initial registration between 1 January and 1 April of the next year. Up to 3 calendar years may be filed in advance ([SOS filing procedure for corporations](https://sos.ga.gov/sites/default/files/forms/Filing%20Procedure%20-%20Corporation.pdf)).
4. **Professional corporations** are profit corporations formed under the Georgia Business Corporation Code that elect the Georgia Professional Corporation Act (O.C.G.A. Title 14, Chapter 7). The persons making the election must be licensed by the appropriate Georgia board in a listed profession: certified public accountancy, architecture, chiropractic, dentistry, professional engineering, land surveying, law, pharmacy, psychology, medicine and surgery, optometry, osteopathy, podiatry, veterinary medicine, registered professional nursing or harbor piloting (same source).
5. **Net worth tax:** a new corporation files an initial net worth return by the 15th day of the 4th month (C corporation) or 3rd month (S corporation) after incorporation (`ga-net-worth-tax`).

### Registering a foreign entity

1. A corporation or LLC formed elsewhere that will transact business in Georgia files an application for a certificate of authority on eCorp, $235, usually with a certificate of existence from its home state ([SOS filing fees](https://sos.ga.gov/sites/default/files/forms/Reference%20-%20Filing%20Fees_0.pdf); [SOS how-to guide](https://sos.ga.gov/how-to-guide/how-guide-register-domestic-entity)).
2. Late registration costs $500 for a foreign corporation, and $500 a year or part year plus all the fees that would have been paid for a foreign LLC.
3. It then files annual registrations from the year after registration, between 1 January and 1 April, like an LLC ([SOS Business Division FAQ](https://sos.ga.gov/page/business-division-faq)).

### Keeping it in good standing

1. **Annual registration** on eCorp between 1 January and 1 April; it confirms the entity exists and is how officers, agent and addresses are changed. One Click Annual Registration needs no login if nothing changes and the entity is current on fees ([SOS annual registration guide](https://sos.ga.gov/how-to-guide/how-file-annual-registration)). Fees are not refundable, and the Secretary of State cannot rescind a filing.
2. **Late:** a $25 penalty. Entities that do not file "may be subject to administrative dissolution or revocation of their authorization to transact business in this State" (O.C.G.A. §§ 14-2-1420, 14-11-603 and related sections).
3. **Notice of administrative dissolution:** the entity has 60 days from the notice to respond (O.C.G.A. §§ 14-2-1421, 14-11-603). After dissolution, a corporation or LLC may apply for reinstatement within 5 years ($260) ([SOS Business Division FAQ](https://sos.ga.gov/page/business-division-faq)).

### Tax registrations after formation

1. **Department of Revenue, Georgia Tax Center** (gtc.dor.ga.gov): register for sales and use tax before selling taxable goods or services (every dealer registers, even if all sales are wholesale or exempt; [DOR registration FAQ](https://dor.georgia.gov/taxes/business-taxes/sales-use-tax/sales-and-use-tax-registration-faq)); register for a withholding number before the first payroll ([2026 Employer's Tax Guide](https://dor.georgia.gov/document/document/2026-employers-tax-guide-updated-september-2026/download)).
2. **Department of Labor:** file Form DOL-1A after the first Georgia payroll once liable for unemployment insurance (quarterly payroll of $1,500, or a worker in 20 weeks; [GDOL employer UI FAQs](https://dol.georgia.gov/faqs-employers/employers-faqs-unemployment-insurance)). Details in `ga-payroll`.
3. **Federal:** obtain an EIN from the IRS; an S corporation election is made federally (see `us-s-corp-election-decision`), and Georgia S treatment then needs every nonresident shareholder's Form 600S-CA (`ga-corporate-and-ptet`).

## Worked cases

The corporation pays $110, plus its initial annual registration of $60 within 90 days, plus the newspaper's publication fee (set by the paper, not included here): $170 plus publication, with the registration falling a year earlier. If either files its 2027 registration after 1 April, it pays $60 plus $25 = $85; reinstatement after dissolution costs $260 plus the registrations owed.

## Filing and payment

1. **Portal:** eCorp, ecorp.sos.ga.gov; create an account, choose the filing, enter the details, pay by card (online) or by check or money order (mail). No cash.
2. **Forms:** articles of organization (LLC), articles of incorporation (corporation), application for certificate of authority (foreign), annual registration, amended annual registration, application for reinstatement.
3. **After filing:** the filed articles or certificate come back by email for online filings; keep them with the newspaper's affidavit of publication for a corporation. Diary 1 January to 1 April each year (and the 90-day first registration for a corporation).
4. **Then register for taxes:** Georgia Tax Center for sales tax and withholding, GDOL for unemployment insurance.

## Completion checklist

- Name cleared; registered agent with a Georgia street address.
- Articles filed on eCorp; $110 paid.
- Corporation: notice sent to the county legal organ by the next business day, to run two consecutive weeks; affidavit kept.
- First annual registration diarised (90 days for a corporation; 1 January to 1 April next year for an LLC or foreign entity).
- Foreign entity: certificate of authority before transacting business.
- Sales tax and withholding numbers on the Georgia Tax Center; DOL-1A once liable for unemployment insurance.
