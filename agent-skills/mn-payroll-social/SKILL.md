# Mongolia Payroll & Social Contributions

## Who this applies to

For ordinary mandatory employment payroll in Mongolia during 2026: employee/employer social and health insurance, resident employment PIT, and non-resident withholding boundaries. It is not a voluntary-insurance calculator. Tax residence and insurance coverage are different determinations. Confirm employment category, risk classification, residence and any social-security agreement before calculating.

**Quick reference:** the ordinary combined employee contribution is 11.5%, subject to its monthly base cap; employer contributions are 12.5%, 13.5% or 14.5% on the applicable payroll base. Resident PIT is progressive on employment taxable income after paid employee social/health contributions, with the applicable annual credit. Current consolidated texts contain changes starting in 2027; do not use them for 2026 payroll.

## The base

1. Determine social-insurance coverage and contractual category. The contributory base includes covered wages, civil-contract remuneration and specified cash allowances, bonuses and other remuneration under Social Act art. 19.1; not every payroll ledger item is automatically outside the base.
2. Apply the relevant monthly floor and employee ceiling, and employer risk class. Health contributions are included in the combined figures above. Keep employer expense separate from deductions from employee pay.
3. Determine resident employment taxable income under PIT art. 14: covered employment income less paid employee social and health contributions. Add taxable indirect benefits under the relevant rules. Apply the resident scale and eligible credit; do not give the resident base automatically to a non-resident.
4. For the employment credit, PIT art. 25.5 divides the art. 23.1 annual credit income bands and credit amounts equally across 12 months. It does not prescribe division of the art. 21.1 progressive scale. Art. 25.7 requires annual reconciliation of both tax and credit for 2026. Keep employee-level cumulative records, withholding, payments, adjustments and net pay. The annual examples are final full-year computations, not a substitute for uneven-month withholding.

[Social Act arts. 18-21](https://legalinfo.mn/mn/detail?lawId=16760148379551); [PIT arts. 14, 20-21, 23, 25](https://legalinfo.mn/mn/detail?lawId=14410); [2022 amendment, annual reconciliation](https://legalinfo.mn/mn/detail?lawId=16532027552761).

## Penalties and interest

Investigate missed or understated contribution reports and any inspector's assessment under Social Act art. 25.2. Tax late-payment interest has a separately determined annual rate under General Tax Law art. 73. Do not apply an invented percentage or assume payroll tax and social-insurance sanctions are identical. [Social Act](https://legalinfo.mn/mn/detail?lawId=16760148379551); [General Tax Law](https://legalinfo.mn/mn/detail?lawId=14403).

## Cross-border points

PIT residence includes the statutory presence test of at least 183 days in a consecutive 12-month period or the income-source test of at least 50%, applied in the statutory order. There are special provisions for government personnel abroad, diplomats and early registration based on employment contracts. Inspect the complete facts and treaty position rather than treating nationality as residence. Non-resident PIT and social-security agreement coverage require separate determinations. [PIT art. 5](https://legalinfo.mn/mn/detail?lawId=14410).

## Two worked examples that recompute

### Ordinary resident, below the employee ceiling

Assume the same MNT 3,000,000 contributory monthly gross salary for all 12 months, ordinary mandatory coverage, the lowest employer risk rate, no other income, deductions or relief. Annual gross is MNT 36,000,000. Employee contributions are MNT 345,000 monthly, MNT 4,140,000 annually. Taxable employment income is MNT 31,860,000; PIT before credit is MNT 3,186,000 and the annual credit is MNT 120,000. Final PIT is **MNT 3,066,000**, and net annual salary **MNT 28,794,000**. Employer contributions are MNT 4,500,000 and total employer cost MNT 40,500,000. Example inputs/derived results use the sourced 2026 components and annual credit above.

```oa-check
name: Resident annual salary below employee cap
inputs:
  monthly: 3000000
  months: 12
  employee_rate: 11.5
  employer_rate: 12.5
  pit_rate: 10
  credit: 120000
steps:
  - gross: monthly * months
  - employee_monthly: monthly * employee_rate / 100
  - employee_annual: employee_monthly * months
  - taxable: gross - employee_annual
  - pit: taxable * pit_rate / 100 - credit
  - net: gross - employee_annual - pit
  - employer: gross * employer_rate / 100
  - cost: gross + employer
expected:
  gross: 36000000
  employee_monthly: 345000
  employee_annual: 4140000
  taxable: 31860000
  pit: 3066000
  net: 28794000
  employer: 4500000
  cost: 40500000
```

### Ordinary resident above the employee ceiling and top PIT threshold

Assume the same MNT 20,000,000 contributory monthly gross salary for all 12 months and otherwise the same ordinary conditions. Annual gross is MNT 240,000,000. Employee contributions are capped at MNT 910,800 monthly, MNT 10,929,600 annually. Taxable income is MNT 229,070,400. PIT is MNT 21,000,000 + 20% × MNT 49,070,400 = **MNT 30,814,080**, with no annual credit. Net annual salary is MNT 198,256,320. Employer contributions remain uncapped: MNT 30,000,000; total cost MNT 270,000,000, derived.

```oa-check
name: Resident annual salary with monthly employee cap and top PIT band
inputs:
  monthly: 20000000
  months: 12
  employee_monthly_cap: 910800
  pit_threshold: 180000000
  fixed_pit: 21000000
  top_rate: 20
  employer_rate: 12.5
steps:
  - gross: monthly * months
  - employee: employee_monthly_cap * months
  - taxable: gross - employee
  - pit: fixed_pit + (taxable - pit_threshold) * top_rate / 100
  - net: gross - employee - pit
  - employer: gross * employer_rate / 100
  - cost: gross + employer
expected:
  gross: 240000000
  employee: 10929600
  taxable: 229070400
  pit: 30814080
  net: 198256320
  employer: 30000000
  cost: 270000000
```

## Ask the client first

Obtain employee residence/presence and contract facts, social coverage and any agreement certificate, insurance category, approved workplace risk class, monthly wages/benefits/bonuses, paid employee contributions, cumulative income and credits, other employment, special relief eligibility, prior filings, payment dates and notices. For uneven pay or special categories, obtain the current official withholding implementation before giving net-pay instructions.
