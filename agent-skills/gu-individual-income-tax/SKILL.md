# Guam: individual income tax, tax year 2026

> **Source-cited draft.** Built from 48 U.S.C. 1421i, IRS Pub. 570 (2025), Form 5074 (2026) with its instructions, the Guam Department of Revenue and Taxation (DRT) 2025 Guam Form 1040 and the DRT tax calendar, read on 2026-11-14. No accountant has reviewed it. All outputs must be reviewed by a qualified tax professional before filing.

**Answer first.** Guam has its own income tax, the **Guam Territorial income tax**: the US income tax law applied with "Guam" read for "United States" (the mirror code, 48 U.S.C. 1421i). Rates, brackets and deductions are the US ones. US citizens and resident aliens generally file **one income tax return**. A nonresident alien who is not a bona fide Guam resident may need returns in both jurisdictions. A **bona fide resident of Guam** for 2026 files with the **DRT**, reports worldwide income, and counts tax withheld or paid to either government. A **US citizen or resident alien who is not a bona fide resident** files only with the **IRS**, reports worldwide income, and attaches **Form 5074** if AGI is **USD 50,000 or more** and Guam-source gross income is **USD 5,000 or more**. On a mixed-residence joint return the **spouse with the higher AGI** determines the filing route. The ordinary calendar-year deadline is **15 April 2027**. Check the exceptions under Filing and payment.

## Scope and who this is for

In scope: individuals (US citizens, resident aliens, citizens of Guam, and the outline of the nonresident alien route) who live in Guam or have Guam-source income, for tax year 2026: which government, which form, residence, sourcing of common items, joint returns, estimated tax, self-employment tax and where to send the return.

## Ask the client first

1. **Residence for 2026**: days in Guam (and in the US) in 2026 and in 2024 and 2025, where the tax home (main place of work) is, and where home, family, car, bank, voter registration and driver's licence are. Did residence start or end in 2026?
2. **Citizenship or immigration status**: US citizen, resident alien, citizen of Guam only, or nonresident alien.
3. **Income by source**: wages (where the work was done), business income, interest (payer's location), dividends (where the company is organised), rents and gains on real property (where it is), pensions.
4. **Spouse**: filing jointly or separately, and each spouse's AGI and residence.
5. **Payments already made**: Guam tax withheld (Form W-2GU box 2), US tax withheld, estimated tax paid to either government.
6. **Self-employment income** and any military service or MSRRA claim.

### Step 1: settle bona fide residence

1. A US citizen or resident alien is a bona fide resident of Guam for 2026 only if all three tests are met for the year: the **presence test** (183 days in Guam, or 549 days over 2024 to 2026 with at least 60 each year, or the other Pub. 570 routes), the **tax home test** (check the regular or principal place of business, or the regular place of abode if there is none, and any move-year exception) and the **closer connection test** (closer to Guam than to the US or a foreign country). Source: [Pub. 570, chapter 1](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/p570.pdf).
2. Year of a move: Test the move-year rules in [26 CFR 1.937-1(f)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-26/section-1.937-1), as well as the presence test. For a move to Guam, check the final 183 days and residence in each of the 3 preceding and 3 following tax years. For a move from Guam, check the first 183 days and the applicable 3-year periods. If you take the position that bona fide residence began or ended and worldwide gross income exceeds USD 75,000, check the [Form 8898 instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i8898).
3. Military: presence solely under active duty orders does not establish bona fide residence for a person who previously failed to qualify. A previously qualified resident keeps residence if absent solely under those orders. Source: [Pub. 570, Guam, U.S. Armed Forces](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/p570.pdf).

| US citizen or resident alien, not a bona fide resident, with Guam income | Form 1040 to the IRS only, worldwide income; Form 5074 if the two thresholds are met; nothing to Guam | [Pub. 570](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/p570.pdf); [Form 5074 (2026)](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/f5074.pdf) |

| Nonresident alien of the US, not a bona fide resident of Guam | Guam return for Guam-source income only, and Form 1040-NR to the IRS for US-source income | [Pub. 570, Nonresident Alien](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/p570.pdf) |

### Step 3, the resident's Guam return

4. Use the **Guam Form 1040** for the filing year. Line 1a of the published 2025 form takes Form W-2 and W-2GU wages. Verify the line on the DRT’s 2026 release before filing. Report worldwide income. Payments include Guam and US withholding, overpayment credits and estimated tax paid to either.
5. If the return is properly filed with, and the tax fully paid to, Guam, the person generally has no US income tax return or payment (Pub. 570).
6. **Self-employed**: check the [Form 1040-SS filing requirements](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1040ss). A bona fide Guam resident with at least USD 400 net self-employment earnings who has no US Form 1040 filing requirement generally files Form 1040-SS with the IRS. Check any other filing triggers and see `us-schedule-c-and-se-computation` for the tax. Additional Medicare Tax may also be due to the IRS.

### Step 4, the non-resident's US return and Form 5074

7. Report worldwide income on Form 1040, counting tax withheld and paid to either government.
8. **Form 5074** is required if AGI is USD 50,000 or more **and** at least USD 5,000 of gross income is from Guam. Part I lists Guam-source income, Part II the adjustments that belong to it (self-employment deductions and IRA deductions are apportioned by the Guam share of the related income), line 30 Guam AGI, Part III Guam payments (estimated tax on line 31, wage withholding on lines 32 to 34 and total payments on line 35). The US and Guam use the form to divide the tax between them; the taxpayer does not file a separate Guam return.
9. **Sourcing**: wages where the work was done, but the **de minimis rule** can exclude pay for temporary work in Guam of 90 days or less and USD 3,000 or less. Apply it to a US citizen or resident who is not a bona fide resident of Guam and who is neither employed by nor under contract with a person, partnership or corporation engaged in a Guam trade or business. Interest generally follows the payer’s location and dividends the place where the company was organised. Real property gains generally follow the property’s location. Gains on nondepreciable personal property generally follow the seller’s residence. Source: [Form 5074 (2026) instructions](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/f5074.pdf).
10. **MSRRA spouse**: check the residence or domicile under [50 U.S.C. 4001](https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/119-120/t50/s4001/), including any election under subsection (a)(3). A civilian spouse with qualifying residence or domicile in a state or DC who is in Guam solely to accompany the serving spouse under military orders generally excludes Guam wages, salaries, tips and self-employment income from Guam-source income. Report worldwide income to the US and test any other Guam-source income for Form 5074.

### Step 5, joint returns

11. File where the spouse with the **greater AGI** would file if filing separately, ignoring community property laws. If that spouse is a bona fide resident of Guam, file the joint return with Guam; if not, with the US, reporting both spouses' income. If the higher-AGI spouse is not a full-year bona fide resident and the couple files in the US, apply both Form 5074 thresholds to the joint return. Source: [Pub. 570, Joint return](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/p570.pdf) and [Form 5074 (2026) instructions](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/f5074.pdf).

### Step 6, estimated tax

12. Pay estimated tax to the government that would get the return if the year ended on the first instalment due date, and keep paying there; payments to either are treated as paid to the one with which the return is filed. A joint payment follows the spouse with the greater estimated AGI, disregarding community property laws. Source: [Pub. 570, Guam, Estimated tax payments](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/p570.pdf). Amounts: `us-quarterly-estimated-tax`.

### Red flags to raise with the client

| Flag | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| Return filed with both Guam and the IRS | Check each jurisdiction’s filing requirement, including the separate route for nonresident aliens who are not bona fide Guam residents |
| Guam residence claimed with a period of mainland employment | Check the ordinary tax-home rule and any move-year exception |
| Self-employed Guam resident with no Form 1040-SS | Check the US filing tests and any separate self-employment or Medicare tax owed |
| US income tax return meets both Form 5074 thresholds and lacks the form | Check Form 5074 and the allocation of Guam’s share |
| W-2GU box 2 treated as US withholding on a resident return | It is Guam withholding; both count as payments, but must not be double entered |

## Worked cases

**Case 1, a decision: which government gets the joint return?** (adapted from the Pub. 570 example). Spouse A, a US citizen living in the US, earned USD 45,000. Spouse B, a bona fide resident of Guam, earned USD 15,000. A has the greater AGI and is not a Guam resident, so the joint return of USD 60,000 goes to the **IRS**, and Form 5074 is needed because AGI is at least USD 50,000 and Guam-source income (B's USD 15,000 wages) is at least USD 5,000. If B had earned USD 50,000 instead, B would be the higher-AGI spouse and the joint return would go to **Guam**, with no Form 5074. Recommend confirming both AGIs on the final figures before choosing; a small change can flip the route.

```oa-check
name: Guam joint return route and Form 5074 test (2026)
inputs:
  agi_us_spouse: 45000
  agi_guam_spouse: 15000
steps:
  - joint_agi: agi_us_spouse + agi_guam_spouse
  - us_spouse_lead: agi_us_spouse - agi_guam_spouse
  - agi_test: min(floor(joint_agi / 50000), 1)
  - guam_income_test: min(floor(agi_guam_spouse / 5000), 1)
  - form_5074_needed: min(agi_test, guam_income_test)
expected:
  joint_agi: 60000
  us_spouse_lead: 30000
  form_5074_needed: 1
```

**Case 2, de minimis and the Form 5074 threshold.** A single US citizen living in California with AGI of USD 95,000 spent 40 days in Guam on a job for a California firm with no Guam trade or business and earned USD 2,900 there. She is not a bona fide Guam resident. She also has USD 4,500 of interest from a Guam bank. The USD 2,900 is not Guam-source (90 days or less, USD 3,000 or less). Guam-source gross income is USD 4,500, under USD 5,000, so no Form 5074. If the job had paid USD 3,100, the de minimis rule would fail, Guam income would be USD 7,600, and Form 5074 would be required.

```oa-check
name: De minimis wages and the USD 5,000 Guam-income test (2026)
inputs:
  guam_wages: 2900
  days_in_guam: 40
  guam_interest: 4500
  agi: 95000
steps:
  - wage_is_guam_source: 1 - min(min(floor(90 / days_in_guam), 1), min(floor(3000 / guam_wages), 1))
  - guam_gross: guam_interest + guam_wages * wage_is_guam_source
  - form_5074_needed: min(min(floor(agi / 50000), 1), min(floor(guam_gross / 5000), 1))
  - guam_gross_if_3100: guam_interest + 3100
expected:
  wage_is_guam_source: 0
  guam_gross: 4500
  form_5074_needed: 0
  guam_gross_if_3100: 7600
```

## Filing and payment

Returns for 2026 are **generally due 15 April 2027** under [26 U.S.C. 6072(a)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title26/html/USCODE-2024-title26-subtitleF-chap61-subchapA-partV-sec6072.htm). Subsection (c) sets a **15 June 2027** date for calendar-year nonresident alien returns where wages are outside chapter 24 withholding. Check the applicable jurisdiction and whether a filing extension applies. A Saturday, Sunday or legal holiday moves a Guam deadline to the next working day. Residents send the Guam Form 1040 and payment to the Department of Revenue and Taxation, Taxpayer Services Division, P.O. Box 23607, GMF, Guam 96921; the DRT calendar lists a **15 June** date for off-island individuals. Confirm qualification and the corresponding payment date with DRT. Taxpayers mailing the US return with Form 5074 send it to the IRS, Austin, TX 73301-0215 (or P.O. Box 1303, Charlotte, NC 28201-1303 if a check is enclosed). Form 1040-SS goes to the IRS.

## Completion checklist

- Residence settled on all three tests before choosing a route; Form 8898 considered for a move.
- Joint return route set by the higher-AGI spouse.
- Residents: Guam Form 1040, worldwide income, withholding and payments to both governments counted.
- US citizens and resident aliens who are not bona fide Guam residents generally file the US income tax return. Check both Form 5074 thresholds and the sourcing rules. Refer nonresident aliens for the applicable Guam and US filings.
- Check Form 1040-SS filing requirements for a self-employed Guam resident.
