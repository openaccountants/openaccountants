# UK self-employment: sole trader profits on the SA103 (2026-27, with 2025-26)

## Who this applies to

Use this Guide for individual sole-trader profit calculations. Combine the result with the rest of the return using `uk-income-tax-sa100`; Scottish taxpayers use `uk-scottish-income-tax`. Use SA104 for partnership cases, `uk-corporation-tax` for companies and `uk-rental-sa105` for property income. ## The taxable base

**Accounting method.** Cash basis is the standard method for eligible sole traders: record income when received and expenses when paid. Traditional accounting can be chosen instead and records income and expenses by invoice/bill date. Eligibility, excluded trades (including special Lloyd's rules), changes of method and capital spending under cash basis: Check this point with one of our partner accountants. [HMRC cash basis](https://www.gov.uk/simpler-income-tax-cash-basis), read 2026-11-26.

**Tax-year basis.** From 2024-25 use profits arising in the tax year. Where accounts do not coincide with it, add or apportion the relevant accounting periods, normally by days; a reasonable alternative must be applied consistently. Later accounts unavailable, provisional figures or a changed accounting date: Check this point with one of our partner accountants. [HMRC BIM81201](https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/business-income-manual/bim81201), read 2026-11-26.

**Expenses.** Deduct only expenses incurred wholly and exclusively for the trade. For mixed expenses, deduct an identifiable business part or proportion. Own drawings/pay, pension contributions, tax/NIC, fines, depreciation, asset losses, borrowing, legal fees, travel/subsistence, business entertainment and gifts need the correct classification and exceptions. Check this point with one of our partner accountants. [ITTOIA 2005 s 34](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2005/5/section/34), read 2026-11-26.

**Trading allowance.** The GBP 1,000 allowance is shared across all trades and relevant miscellaneous income. Full relief may cover combined relevant gross income not exceeding GBP 1,000, subject to exclusions and an election out. Above that amount, partial relief requires an election and replaces actual expenses and capital allowances; it cannot create a loss. It is not another GBP 1,000 for each source. It does not apply to partnership trading income or rent-a-room trades. Neither allowance can be used for a year with trade or property income from a company the person or a connected person owns/controls, a partnership in which either is a partner, or their employer or their spouse/civil partner's employer. Do not ignore a notice to file: ask HMRC to withdraw it if no return is needed. [HMRC allowance guidance](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/tax-free-allowances-on-property-and-trading-income), read 2026-11-26; [full-relief gateway](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2005/5/section/783AE), [partial-relief election](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2005/5/section/783AH), [shared allocation](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2005/5/section/783AK), read 2026-11-26.

## Simplified expenses

For a qualifying car or goods vehicle, the 2026-27 mileage deduction is 55p for the first 10,000 business miles and 25p thereafter (45p for the first 10,000 before 6 April 2026); motorcycles use 24p. This replaces buying/running costs. Do not use it if capital allowances or the vehicle cost have already been claimed, and do not duplicate those costs. Cars designed for commercial use (including black cabs, hackney carriages and dual-control driving instructors' cars) are excluded. Once chosen for a vehicle, continue while it is used in the business. Other travel costs and parking can be added where allowable.

For working at home at least 25 hours a month, monthly flat rates are GBP 10 for 25-50 hours, GBP 18 for 51-100 and GBP 26 for 101 or more. Telephone and internet are outside that flat rate: claim the actual business proportion separately. Living at business premises and choosing between actual and flat costs: Check this point with one of our partner accountants. [HMRC simplified expenses](https://www.gov.uk/simpler-income-tax-simplified-expenses/print), read 2026-11-26.

## Transition profits

First establish the actual positive 2023-24 transition profit after the statutory calculation and overlap relief; a non-March accounting date alone is insufficient. Missing overlap records, acceleration elections, cessation or unclaimed historical relief: Check this point with one of our partner accountants.



That standard spreading assumes no acceleration election or earlier cessation. The transition component is excluded from net income and its additional income tax is calculated separately using the statutory notional comparison, including any allowance taper; do not simply add it to ordinary net income for every purpose. It remains trading profit chargeable under ITTOIA Part 2 Chapter 2 for Class 4 purposes. [FA 2022 Sch 1 paras 72/75](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2022/3/schedule/1), [HMRC BIM81320](https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/business-income-manual/bim81320), [SSCBA 1992 s 15](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1992/4/section/15), exact passages read 2026-11-26.

## Income tax and National Insurance

The examples use the 2026-27 standard personal allowance of GBP 12,570 and England's non-savings rates: the next GBP 37,700 at 20%, then 40% for the remaining example income. The allowance reduces by GBP 1 for every GBP 2 of adjusted net income over GBP 100,000 and is nil at GBP 125,140. Establish entitlement and all other income/reliefs before using an example. [HMRC income tax rates](https://www.gov.uk/income-tax-rates), read 2026-11-26.

Class 4 for 2026-27 is 6% on liable profits over GBP 12,570 up to GBP 50,270 and 2% above GBP 50,270. Overseas trade, age, residence and other exemptions: Check this point with one of our partner accountants. [SSCBA 1992 s 15](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1992/4/section/15), read 2026-11-26.

For ordinary self-employed Class 2 in 2026-27, profits at or above GBP 7,105 are treated as paid without a payment; below it voluntary contributions may be available at GBP 3.65 per week. Special occupations, eligibility, contribution record, payment route and deadlines: Check this point with one of our partner accountants. See `uk-national-insurance` and [HMRC self-employed NI](https://www.gov.uk/self-employed-national-insurance-rates), read 2026-11-26.

## Deadlines and filing

If a first-time or reactivating filer needs a 2025-26 return, tell HMRC by 5 October 2026. Ordinary 2025-26 deadlines are receipt of paper by 31 October 2026 and online submission by 31 January 2027. For 2026-27 the corresponding ordinary dates are 31 October 2027 and 31 January 2028. [HMRC deadlines](https://www.gov.uk/self-assessment-tax-returns/deadlines), [TMA 1970 s 8](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1970/9/section/8), read 2026-11-26.

For a notice after 31 July but on/before 31 October following the tax year, the paper deadline is three months from the notice and the online deadline remains 31 January. A notice after 31 October gives three months for either return route (s 8(1F)-(1G)). The balancing payment is normally due on 31 January following the tax year, after allowing for tax deducted and payments on account. If chargeability was notified under s 7 within six months of year end but HMRC gave the s 8/8A notice only after 31 October, the balancing payment is due at the end of three months beginning with the notice day. [TMA 1970 s 59B](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1970/9/section/59B), read 2026-11-26. Payments on account: `uk-payments-on-account`.

Use the correct year's SA103S/SA103F instructions; do not transplant 2025-26 boxes into 2026-27. ## Worked examples

### Example 1: Sole trader on the cash basis, turnover GBP 48,000 (2026-27)

Priya is a self-employed graphic designer in Manchester. For this illustration assume she is entitled to the full personal allowance, is liable to Class 4 for the whole year, and her car qualifies for simplified mileage with no previous capital-cost or capital-allowance claim. In 2026-27 she receives GBP 48,000 from clients and pays GBP 9,000 of allowable expenses (software, phone, accountant, insurance). She drives 6,000 business miles in her own car and uses the flat rate. She has no other income and uses the cash basis.

- Mileage: 6,000 x 55p = GBP 3,300 (all within the first 10,000 miles).
- Profit: 48,000 - 9,000 - 3,300 = GBP 35,700. The trading allowance would give only 48,000 - 1,000 = GBP 47,000, so actual expenses are better.
- Taxable income: 35,700 - 12,570 = GBP 23,130, all at 20% = GBP 4,626.00.
- Class 4 NIC: 23,130 x 6% = GBP 1,387.80.
- Total income tax and Class 4 for the illustration: GBP 6,013.80. The normal balancing-payment date is 31 January 2028; payments on account and any tax already paid must be reconciled separately, and the late-notice exception below may change the date.
- MTD: apply the combined qualifying-income test below, including property and other trades. Her 2024-25 income and exemptions are missing, so this example does not establish whether she already had to join in April 2026 or must join in April 2027. Check this point with one of our partner accountants.

```oa-check
name: Sole trader, cash basis, turnover 48,000, 2026-27
inputs:
  turnover: 48000
  expenses: 9000
  business_miles: 6000
  personal_allowance: 12570
steps:
  - mileage: min(business_miles, 10000) * 0.55 + max(business_miles - 10000, 0) * 0.25
  - profit: turnover - expenses - mileage
  - profit_with_trading_allowance: turnover - 1000
  - taxable: profit - personal_allowance
  - income_tax: min(taxable, 37700) * 0.20
  - class4: (min(profit, 50270) - 12570) * 0.06
  - total: income_tax + class4
expected:
  mileage: 3300
  profit: 35700
  profit_with_trading_allowance: 47000
  taxable: 23130
  income_tax: 4626.00
  class4: 1387.80
  total: 6013.80
```

### Example 2: 30 September year end, apportionment and transition profit (2026-27)

Tom has run a plumbing business for years with accounts to 30 September. His accounts show a taxable profit (after capital allowances) of GBP 60,000 for the year to 30 September 2026 and GBP 72,000 for the year to 30 September 2027. His 2023-24 transition profit was GBP 25,000, spread at 20% a year. He has no other income and lives in England. Assume full personal-allowance entitlement, no other reliefs or adjustments, Class 4 liability for the whole year, no election to accelerate transition profits and no cessation.

- Year to 30 September 2026: the part from 6 April to 30 September 2026 is 178 of 365 days: 60,000 x 178 / 365 = GBP 29,260.27.
- Year to 30 September 2027: the part from 1 October 2026 to 5 April 2027 is 187 of 365 days: 72,000 x 187 / 365 = GBP 36,887.67.
- Profit of the tax year 2026-27: GBP 66,147.95.
- Transition profit for 2026-27 (year 4 of 5): 25,000 x 20% = GBP 5,000. The last GBP 5,000 is taxed in 2027-28.
- Total trading profit: GBP 71,147.95. His income including the transition part is under GBP 100,000, so the full personal allowance stays and the transition part is taxed as if it were ordinary profit.
- Income tax: 37,700 x 20% = GBP 7,540.00; (71,147.95 - 50,270) x 40% = GBP 8,351.18; total GBP 15,891.18.
- Class 4 NIC (the transition profit counts): (50,270 - 12,570) x 6% = GBP 2,262.00; (71,147.95 - 50,270) x 2% = GBP 417.56; total GBP 2,679.56.
- Total: GBP 18,570.74.
- Use full precision until the final rounding: the displayed rounded components do not sum to the full-precision total. If the later accounts are unavailable or the accounting date changes, establish the provisional-return and adjustment requirements. Check this point with one of our partner accountants.

```oa-check
name: Sole trader, 30 September year end with transition profit, 2026-27
inputs:
  profit_ye_sep_2026: 60000
  profit_ye_sep_2027: 72000
  days_in_2026_27_from_first: 178
  days_in_2026_27_from_second: 187
  days_in_year: 365
  transition_profit: 25000
  personal_allowance: 12570
steps:
  - part_first: profit_ye_sep_2026 * days_in_2026_27_from_first / days_in_year
  - part_second: profit_ye_sep_2027 * days_in_2026_27_from_second / days_in_year
  - tax_year_profit: part_first + part_second
  - transition_part: transition_profit * 0.20
  - total_profit: tax_year_profit + transition_part
  - taxable: total_profit - personal_allowance
  - basic_tax: min(taxable, 37700) * 0.20
  - higher_tax: (min(taxable, 112570) - 37700) * 0.40
  - income_tax: basic_tax + higher_tax
  - class4_main: (min(total_profit, 50270) - 12570) * 0.06
  - class4_upper: max(total_profit - 50270, 0) * 0.02
  - class4: class4_main + class4_upper
  - total: income_tax + class4
expected:
  part_first: 29260.27
  part_second: 36887.67
  tax_year_profit: 66147.95
  transition_part: 5000
  total_profit: 71147.95
  basic_tax: 7540.00
  higher_tax: 8351.18
  income_tax: 15891.18
  class4_main: 2262.00
  class4_upper: 417.56
  class4: 2679.56
  total: 18570.74
```

### Example 3: Trading allowance against actual expenses

Side income of GBP 4,000 from occasional consulting, with actual expenses of GBP 700. Actual expenses give a profit of GBP 3,300; the trading allowance gives GBP 3,000 (ITTOIA 2005 s 783AH). If eligible for partial relief and there is no competing use of the shared allowance, electing for it gives the lower profit. These inputs do not establish connected-source eligibility, other qualifying income or continuing MTD obligations. Check this point with one of our partner accountants.

```oa-check
name: Trading allowance against actual expenses
inputs:
  income: 4000
  expenses: 700
steps:
  - profit_actual: income - expenses
  - profit_allowance: income - 1000
expected:
  profit_actual: 3300
  profit_allowance: 3000
```

### Example 4: Home office, flat rate against actual costs

A trader works from home 130 hours a month for 11 months. The flat rate gives 11 x GBP 26 = GBP 286 (GOV.UK simplified expenses). Actual costs: one room of five, with household rent GBP 12,000, utilities GBP 2,400 and broadband GBP 480, gives 1/5 of GBP 14,880 = GBP 2,976. The GBP 286 home flat rate excludes telephone and internet, so an eligible actual business share of broadband can be added to it. The executable block below only reconciles the two printed amounts before any broadband add-on or business-use/period adjustment. GBP 2,976 is the arithmetic for the stated one-fifth allocation, not a confirmed deduction or a completed comparison: establish room and time use and reconcile the 11-month flat-rate period with the annual actual costs before choosing ([HMRC simplified expenses](https://www.gov.uk/simpler-income-tax-simplified-expenses/print), read 2026-11-26). Check this point with one of our partner accountants.

```oa-check
name: Home office flat rate against actual costs
inputs:
  months: 11
  rent: 12000
  utilities: 2400
  broadband: 480
steps:
  - flat_rate: months * 26
  - actual: (rent + utilities + broadband) / 5
expected:
  flat_rate: 286
  actual: 2976
```

### Example 5: Voluntary Class 2 on low profits (2026-27)

Profit of GBP 4,200 is below the small profits threshold of GBP 7,105. For an ordinary self-employed trader with no other trading profits, Class 4 is nil (below GBP 12,570) and no compulsory Class 2 is due. If eligible to pay voluntary Class 2 at the ordinary 2026-27 rate for 52 contribution weeks, the cost is 52 x GBP 3.65 = GBP 189.80. The National Insurance record, other contributions or credits and payment route/deadline must be checked before deciding whether this improves entitlement. Check this point with one of our partner accountants.

```oa-check
name: Voluntary Class 2, 2026-27
inputs:
  weeks: 52
  weekly_rate: 3.65
steps:
  - voluntary_class2: weeks * weekly_rate
expected:
  voluntary_class2: 189.80
```

### Example 6: which should I use, cash basis or traditional accounting? (2026-27)

Sana is a self-employed consultant with a 5 April year end and no other income. In 2026-27 she receives GBP 52,000 and pays GBP 12,000 of expenses. At 5 April 2027 clients owe her GBP 6,000 and she owes suppliers GBP 500. She held nothing unpaid at the start of the year. Assume all stated costs are allowable, both methods are available, she has full personal-allowance entitlement and is liable to Class 4 for the whole year. Cash basis records receipts and payments; traditional accounting records income and costs by invoice or bill date ([HMRC cash basis](https://www.gov.uk/simpler-income-tax-cash-basis), read 2026-11-26).

- Cash basis: profit 52,000 - 12,000 = GBP 40,000.
- Traditional: income 58,000 less costs 12,500 = GBP 45,500.
- The deciding figure is year-end debtors less creditors: GBP 5,500 of extra profit this year. At 20% income tax and 6% Class 4, that is GBP 1,430 more due for 2026-27.
- This is the current-year difference under the stated assumptions. Future receipts/payments, rates and adjustments on changing method can change later tax; no equal lifetime tax or preferred method is established. Check this point with one of our partner accountants.

```oa-check
name: Cash basis against traditional accounting, 2026-27
inputs:
  received: 52000
  paid: 12000
  debtors: 6000
  creditors: 500
steps:
  - profit_cash: received - paid
  - profit_traditional: received + debtors - paid - creditors
  - gap: profit_traditional - profit_cash
  - extra_tax: gap * 0.26
expected:
  profit_cash: 40000
  profit_traditional: 45500
  gap: 5500
  extra_tax: 1430
```

## Ask the client first

1. What is your accounting year end, and do you still have transition profit being spread from 2023-24?
2. What was your turnover from self-employment and gross property income on your 2024-25 and 2025-26 returns? (This decides when MTD applies.)
3. Do you use the cash basis or traditional (accruals) accounting, and do you hold stock?
4. What did you buy for the business this year (equipment, vans, cars), when, new or used, and how much is private use?
5. Did the business make a loss in this or an earlier year, and do you have other income to set it against?
6. Were you UK resident for the whole tax year, and do you trade anywhere outside the UK?
