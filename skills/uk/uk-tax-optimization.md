# UK Tax Optimization

Tax year: **2026/27, 6 April 2026 to 5 April 2027**. Historical cases below keep their original 2025/26 period. Currency: GBP.

**General reference only.** Review the person's documents, residence, relief eligibility and elections before acting. This Guide produces a planning comparison, not a return or an investment recommendation.

## Scope

UK sole traders, individual partners and small-company owners. The ordinary non-savings income-tax figures below cover England, Wales and Northern Ireland; Scottish non-savings rates differ ([HMRC](https://www.gov.uk/scottish-income-tax)). For Scottish status, non-residence, treaty entitlement or cross-border planning: Check this point with one of our partner accountants.

## Ask the client first

- What are the expected profit, other income, adjusted net income, residence and taxpayer status?
- Sole trader, partnership or company? Who owns associated companies? How much profit must be extracted and how much can stay invested in the business?
- What pension scheme, gross contributions, employer contributions and defined-benefit accrual exist? Obtain the previous three years' input statements and any flexible-access statement.
- What equipment or vehicles are planned, with purchase dates, emissions, private use, accounting basis and prior claims?
- What losses exist, in which trade and years, and when did that trade start or cease?
- What is taxable VAT turnover, customer VAT recovery, input VAT and scheme history?
- Does a family employee do genuine work, for what remuneration? Are transfers, investments or avoidance schemes proposed?

## The method, step by step

1. Establish income, allowances, applicable bands and NIC liability. Compute the baseline before suggesting changes.
2. Compare actual allowable expenses with simplified expenses; do not deduct the same cost twice.
3. Compare eligible AIA, first-year and writing-down allowances on the correct accounting basis.
4. Test pension relief and annual-allowance charges separately, then recompute adjusted net income and tax.
5. Compare sole trader and company using company profit **after** deductible salary/employer NIC, corporation tax, distributable reserves and personal extraction taxes. State the cash retained or extracted and administration costs separately.
6. Compare the available loss routes, VAT schemes and investment reliefs only after their eligibility checks.

### Deductions and timing

ITTOIA [s34](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2005/5/section/34) requires expenditure wholly and exclusively for the trade; an identifiable business part of mixed expenditure can qualify. Review trade-related subscriptions, bank/payment-processing charges, business phone/broadband, protective clothing and tools under that test. A professional subscription is not automatically deductible because it appears on an employee List 3. For family wages, document genuine duties and commercially justified pay; private support is not a trade expense.

| Choice | Treatment | Official source |
|---|---|---|
| Actual home costs | Identify the trade part using supportable use/time evidence; do not assume all household costs qualify. Business telephone/internet is outside the simplified home flat rate | [s34](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2005/5/section/34), [HMRC](https://www.gov.uk/simpler-income-tax-simplified-expenses/print) |
| Simplified home | Instead of the qualifying home-use deduction: GBP 10/month for 25-50 qualifying hours, GBP 18 for 51-100, GBP 26 for 101+. Count simultaneous work in the same home once, and multiple homes as one | [s94H](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2005/5/section/94H) |
| Simplified vehicles | 55p for the first aggregate 10,000 qualifying car/goods-vehicle business miles and 25p after, in 2026/27; 45p first-band rate before 6 April 2026. Replaces acquisition/running costs, continues for that vehicle and excludes prior capital-allowance claims or relevant cash-basis acquisition deductions | [ss94D-94F](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2005/5/section/94F), [HMRC](https://www.gov.uk/simpler-income-tax-simplified-expenses/print) |
| Pre-trading costs | Up to seven years before starting; treated incurred at commencement only if not otherwise deducted and deductible had they been incurred then | [s57](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2005/5/section/57) |
| Bad/doubtful debts | Under traditional accounting, s35 permits bad debts, debts estimated bad and qualifying statutory-insolvency releases, subject to the normal profit rules. Do not deduct an unpaid sale again if it was never cash-basis income | [s35](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2005/5/section/35), [cash basis](https://www.gov.uk/simpler-income-tax-cash-basis) |

Simplified expenses are not restricted to cash-basis accounts; partnerships with a non-individual partner cannot use these provisions ([s94C](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2005/5/section/94C)). For commercially designed vehicles, mixed premises used as a home, unusual shared-home facts or training/CPD eligibility: Check this point with one of our partner accountants.

Cash basis is the default unless excluded or traditional accounting elected ([s24A](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2005/5/section/24A)). It normally records receipts and payments when money is received or a bill paid; invoice delay alone does not establish tax deferral ([HMRC](https://www.gov.uk/simpler-income-tax-cash-basis)). For transition adjustments, exclusions, prepayments and cash-basis capital expenditure: Check this point with one of our partner accountants. The capital-allowance comparison below assumes traditional accounting; do not substitute allowances for an equipment cost already deducted.

Compare these routes before claiming. Do not accelerate expenditure solely to manufacture a deduction or assume the cap is an entitlement.

|---|---|---|

| General relief cap | Aggregate specified reliefs limited to greater of GBP 50,000 or 25% of adjusted total income. ## Worked cases

### Case 1: GBP 110,000 income; GBP 10,000 gross personal pension

2026/27: assume adequate relevant earnings, registered relief-at-source scheme, paid before age 75, sufficient allowance and no flexible-access/taper charge. Without pension, allowance GBP 7,570 and tax GBP 33,432. Pay GBP 8,000 net; provider adds GBP 2,000 to make the original **GBP 10,000 gross** contribution. Adjusted net income becomes GBP 100,000, allowance GBP 12,570 and basic band GBP 47,700. Personal tax becomes GBP 29,432: GBP 4,000 less, plus provider relief GBP 2,000 = **GBP 6,000 total relief**, effective cost GBP 4,000. Do not add another GBP 2,000 for allowance recovery to that total. Dependencies: rates table, ITA ss35/58 and FA 2004 s192.

```oa-check
name: Pension and allowance recovery, original 110000 and 10000 gross (2026/27)
inputs:
  income: 110000
  gross_pension: 10000
  pa: 12570
  basic_band: 37700
steps:
  - pa_before: pa - (income - 100000) / 2
  - tax_before: basic_band * 0.20 + (income - pa_before - basic_band) * 0.40
  - pa_after: pa - max(income - gross_pension - 100000, 0) / 2
  - extended_band: basic_band + gross_pension
  - tax_after: extended_band * 0.20 + (income - pa_after - extended_band) * 0.40
  - provider_relief: gross_pension * 0.20
  - relief: tax_before - tax_after + provider_relief
  - effective_cost: gross_pension - relief
expected:
  pa_before: 7570
  pa_after: 12570
  tax_before: 33432
  tax_after: 29432
  relief: 6000
  effective_cost: 4000
```

### Cases 3-5 and historical variants

- **Case 3, GBP 20,000 equipment, 40% marginal income-tax rate:** assume traditional accounting, full-year continuing trade, wholly business qualifying main-rate equipment, ownership, sufficient income/AIA headroom and no restriction. AIA deduction GBP 20,000 saves GBP 8,000. Choosing only 14% WDA in 2026/27 deducts GBP 2,800 and saves GBP 1,120; if 40% FYA eligibility is separately established it deducts GBP 8,000 and saves GBP 3,200. WDA is not the only alternative to AIA. Historical 2025/26 full-year WDA-only comparison at 18%: GBP 3,600 deduction and GBP 1,440 saving; same GBP 20,000 input and GBP 8,000 AIA saving. ## Filing and payment

For ordinary 2026/27 Self Assessment, the online annual return and balancing payment are due **31 January 2028**; late-notice exceptions can change these dates ([TMA ss8](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1970/9/section/8), [59B](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1970/9/section/59B)). Where payments on account apply, 2026/27 advances fall on **31 January and 31 July 2027**. A lower valid estimated relevant liability, not merely lower income or inability to pay, can support reduction online or by SA303; under-reduction can create interest ([s59A](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1970/9/section/59A), [HMRC](https://www.gov.uk/understand-self-assessment-bill/payments-on-account)). For the tests, calculation and actual notices use `uk-payments-on-account`.

Calendar: estimate profits and review VAT/payments on account during the year; obtain pension and investment documentation before year end. Personal pension contributions for this year must be paid in it, by **5 April 2027** (FA 2004 s188). Use the relevant ISA year rather than an April instruction to use the already-ended year's allowance. Review capital acquisitions against the actual accounting period, not an assumed 31 December. For notification/registering, MTD duties, penalties, interest, special notices or payment-date exceptions: Check this point with one of our partner accountants.
