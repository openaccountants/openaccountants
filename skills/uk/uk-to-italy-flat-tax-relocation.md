# Moving from the UK to Italy: the flat-tax corridor (2026/27)

Choose the Italian regime from the income it covers, then test Italian residence and the UK departure separately. An Italian election does not end UK residence or the UK return and inheritance-tax rules.

The UK year 2026/27 runs from 6 April 2026 to 5 April 2027; Italy uses calendar 2026. The legislation and official guidance below were checked on 28 November 2026, with applicable UK and Italian residence proof reused from 26–27 November. The civil-law move date fixes the flat-tax price cohort; the fiscal-residence test fixes the resident year. These are different questions.

## Scope

This Guide covers UK residents considering Italy: wealthy families, founders, working professionals and foreign pensioners. It compares the new-residents flat tax, impatriati relief and the pensioner regime, including foreign assets, UK property, treaty pensions and the two countries’ residence clocks.

For the full UK tests use `uk-statutory-residence-test`; for the UK return use `uk-non-resident-sa109`; for the UK foreign income and gains regime use `uk-non-dom`. The UAE comparison remains in `uk-to-uae-relocation-tax`. Immigration, trusts, individual estate calculations and unproved ordinary Italian rates are referral boundaries below.

## Ask the client first

Obtain the residence and day records before proposing a regime: the last 10 Italian tax years; exact civil-law move and family move dates; Italian work location, qualification and employer-group history; pension payer and proposed municipality; UK homes, ties and workdays; the last 20 UK tax years and sole-UK-residence history for the seven years before departure. Identify Italian salary/professional income, foreign portfolios/trusts, pensions, UK rent and any planned shareholding disposal. Record each family member separately, including a minor child’s Italian residence. Identify existing immigration rights and the proposed visa route with the Italian consulate.

## The method, step by step

1. Establish UK residence under the ordered SRT tests and, if relevant, a departure split-year case. Build the diary around the actual conditions.
2. Establish Italian residence for each calendar year. Do not infer it from registration, a visa, a flight date or the election alone.
3. Test the chosen regime’s prior non-residence and income conditions below. Compare its charge with a two-country ordinary-tax computation and available credits; there is no universal income break-even point.
4. Record the civil-law move date separately. Do not apply the 2026 price to an earlier eligible entrant merely because their return is filed in 2026.
5. Make the election and payment through the applicable return procedure. Keep the eligibility evidence, excluded-country choices and payment confirmations.
6. Diarise expiry, annual payments, qualified-participation gains, any UK return, asset reporting and the actual IHT exit test. Review the ordinary regime before relief expires.

## Italian residence: establish the year before choosing the election

From 2024, art.2 TUIR treats a person as resident if, for most of the tax period, counting fractions of days, they have civil-code residence in Italy, domicile there, **or physical presence there**. Domicile means the principal place of personal and family relations. Population-register entry for most of the period creates a rebuttable presumption. These are alternatives: absence from the register does not settle the result.

Most of 365 days is at least 183; most of a leap year’s 366 days is at least 184. Residence applies for the calendar year; residents are in principle taxed on worldwide income, subject to the special regime and treaty. The historic “arrive before end-June / after end-June” shortcut is not a legal cutoff. An October UK departure can qualify for UK split-year treatment while Italy’s first resident year starts the following 1 January **only if the full Italian residence facts support that result**. Earlier Italian domicile or residence can change it. Resolve dual residence under treaty art.4; do not presume the UK split date is the Italian start date.

Sources: [TUIR arts.2,3,7](https://www.normattiva.it/uri-res/N2Ls?urn:nir:presidente.repubblica:decreto:1986-12-22;917~art2!vig=2026-11-27) (applicable proof read 27 November); [UK–Italy treaty art.4](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/italy-tax-treaties/1988-uk-italy-double-taxation-convention-in-force).

## Door 1: the new-residents flat tax

The option covers foreign-source income identified under art.165(2), not Italian-source income. It requires at least nine non-resident periods in the ten before the option starts. There is no statutory wealth minimum in art.24-bis. Apply the price cohort in the table to the actual civil-law residence transfer, rather than the filing date: an eligible 2025 mover does not acquire the EUR 300,000 main charge merely by filing later.

A main taxpayer can request extension to eligible art.433 family members, each satisfying the residence conditions. The option can be extended during its life; record each person’s move, first option year and charge. Do not assume every relative qualifies. Revocation or loss of the main election also ends family extensions; one family member’s failure to pay does not itself end the main taxpayer’s regime.

The option lasts up to 15 years, is revocable, and cannot be re-elected after revocation or lapse. Pay the annual substitute tax **in one payment by the income-tax balance deadline**. The official payment method is F24-ELIDE, code **NRPP**; this is not an instalment plan. Art.24-bis(4) ends the option for omitted or partial payment; Circular 17/E says ordinary late-payment correction (ravvedimento operoso) is unavailable. A specific future payment day is not supplied here in place of the applicable annual deadline.

Art.24-bis(3) links the option to the return for the residence-transfer period and refers to a favourable interpello. Agenzia’s implementing procedure, explained in Circular 17/E PartIII §4, permits election in the return for the transfer year or following year and treats the prior interpello as **optional**: without it, complete the required eligibility details and retain the supporting documents. This is a choice of the option’s valid starting period, not permission to leave the relevant election/payment late. A ruling request does not dispense with the return and payment.

A taxpayer may exclude one or more foreign states from the option. The income from those states then follows ordinary treatment with the applicable foreign-tax credit; no credit offsets the flat charge for covered income. Italian-source income remains under its ordinary rules. Gains within art.67(1)(c) on qualifying participations are outside the flat tax in its **first five tax periods**.

During a valid option, Law 232/2016 art.1(153) provides RW-monitoring and IVIE/IVAFE relief, including eligible extended family. Circular 17/E §5.2 limits it to **covered jurisdictions**, and retains RW reporting of foreign qualifying participations during the first five option periods. It is not a blanket permission to omit all foreign assets.

For a death or gift during the donor/deceased’s valid option, art.1(158) limits Italian succession/gift taxation to Italian-situated assets and rights. Circular 17/E §5.3 applies the territorial relief to eligible extended family too, but excluded-country assets remain under ordinary rules. The beneficiary merely receiving an inheritance is not enough; identify the donor/deceased, situs and option at the event. UK IHT must still be tested separately. Trust and individual estate outcomes are held below.

Sources: TUIR24-bis and Law 232/2016 linked above; [Circular 17/E,23 May 2017, PartIII §§3–7](https://def.finanze.it/DocTribFrontend/getPrassiDetail.do?id=%7BDF6F5518-4D04-4718-B611-8AC67E8C08BC%7D); [Agenzia payment method and NRPP](https://www1.agenziaentrate.gov.it/servizi/scadenzario/main.php/main.php?chi=3871&come=528&cosa=10983&entroil=30-07-2025&op=4). The 2025 payment page is used only for method/code and late-payment guidance: its amount/date wording does not override the statutory cohorts or establish a 2026/2027 deadline.

## Door 2: impatriati for relocating workers

For fiscal-residence moves from 2024, D.Lgs. 209/2023 art.5 relieves Italian employment income, assimilated employment income and self-employment income from arts/professions, within the **EUR 600,000 annual limit**. Ordinarily 50% of eligible income enters taxable income; investment income is not relieved by this work-income provision.

The worker must have the required prior non-residence, undertake to remain Italian resident for at least four years, and perform the work in Italy for most of the tax period. They must satisfy the high-qualification/specialisation definitions in D.Lgs. 108/2012 and D.Lgs. 206/2007, **or have carried out research, including applied research, in artificial-intelligence technologies**. A job title alone is not proof of the statutory condition. Same-employer/group work requires the six- or seven-period test in the table; group includes statutory direct/indirect control or common control. For Italian citizens, the statutory AIRE/treaty evidence rule applies to the relevant earlier periods. Professional activities must also satisfy applicable EU de minimis conditions; this Guide does not calculate an individual state-aid entitlement.

The taxable share falls to **40%** when moving with a minor child, or from the birth/adoption period for the remaining benefit window if that happens during the regime. The child must be resident in Italy during the worker’s benefit period. The ordinary benefit window is the move period and the next four; failing to maintain residence for at least four years triggers recovery with interest.

Do not apply old **70–90%** exemptions or legacy extensions to a 2026 arrival. Art.5 preserves specified pre-2024 cases. Its separate three-period extension is confined to the **2024** anagrafe-move cohort with the specified residential-home purchase by **31 December 2023** and in the **12 months before transfer**; those are not the Turin 2026 facts. Impatriati is an income reduction, not the flat-tax RW/IVIE/IVAFE exemption: assess foreign reporting and taxes under the ordinary asset-specific rules.

Source: [D.Lgs. 209/2023 art.5(1–10), version applicable in 2026](https://www.normattiva.it/atto/caricaDettaglioAtto?atto.articolo.numero=5&atto.codiceRedazionale=23G00222&atto.dataPubblicazioneGazzetta=2023-12-28&bloccoAggiornamentoBreadCrumb=true&classica=true&dataVigenza=09%2F09%2F2026&generaTabId=true&tipoDettaglio=singolavigenza).

## Door 3: the 7% foreign-pensioner regime

Art.24-ter requires pension income within art.49(2)(a), paid by a **foreign payer**, a transfer from a country with administrative-cooperation agreements, and non-residence in Italy in the **five preceding tax periods**. The destination must be in Sicily, Calabria, Sardinia, Campania, Basilicata, Abruzzo, Molise or Puglia, or a municipality in the statutory 2016-earthquake annexes or affected by the 6 April 2009 earthquake. The statutory population ceiling is **no more than 30,000 from 7 April 2026**; it was 20,000 immediately before that amendment. A town elsewhere in Italy does not qualify simply because it is small. Verify the actual municipality and relevant population evidence before electing.

The 7% substitute tax covers foreign-source income of any category under the statutory sourcing test, not just the pension. Italian-source income remains outside it. Elect in the return for the first qualifying Italian resident period; the option runs for that period plus the next nine, **10 periods total**, not nine total. Pay in one payment by the income-tax balance deadline.

One may exclude foreign states, making their income subject to ordinary treatment and the applicable foreign-tax credit instead. Law 145/2018 art.1(274) provides RW/IVIE/IVAFE relief; Circular 21/E §6 limits that relief to covered jurisdictions. The regime ends if the requirements cease or for revocation. Art.24-ter(7) allows a payment omission/shortfall to be remedied by the following period’s balance deadline, with applicable sanctions and interest; this differs from the art.24-bis flat-tax lapse rule. Revocation or forfeiture prevents a new option.

## The UK end: departure, return and inheritance

**Residence.** Apply the automatic overseas tests first, then the automatic UK tests, then sufficient ties if neither set is met. The full-year overseas-work test needs statutory sufficient hours and no significant break, fewer than **91 UK days**, and fewer than **31 UK workdays exceeding three hours**; the statutory calculation and transport-worker exception matter. It is not simply a foreign employment contract.

For a person resident in at least one of the previous three UK years, the sufficient-ties table requires at least **four ties at 16–45 days, three at 46–90, two at 91–120, and one at 121–182**. These are residence thresholds when the sufficient-ties test applies, not unconditional permitted visit allowances.

**Split year.** A UK-resident departure year may be split under Case 1 (overseas work), Case 2 (the qualifying partner route) or Case 3 (ceasing all UK homes), only if the actual statutory conditions are met. For example, the original **October** departure illustration could use Case 3 if the person was resident in the preceding year, had a UK home at the start, ceases all UK homes for the rest of the year, spends fewer than 16 UK days from cessation, is non-resident next year and has the required overseas link at the end of six months. The link is domestic tax residence there, presence there every midnight throughout those six months, or all homes there. Apply priority if more than one case fits. The overseas part starts on the relevant statutory date, not automatically the flight date; split year changes specified tax charges rather than the whole-year residence determination.

**Temporary non-residence.** The rule is not “stay away for five calendar years”. Broadly, sole UK residence is followed by a temporary non-sole-UK period of **five years or less**, with the required sole-UK residence in at least **four of the seven preceding tax years**. Count the actual residence periods, including split-year parts and treaty residence. TCGA1992 s.1M can bring relevant gains into the return period, subject to its exclusions, including s.1N for certain assets acquired while away. A founder selling in **year2** must test this separately from Italy’s first-five-option-period exclusion; neither country’s rule guarantees tax-free disposal. Section1M(4) expressly prevents treaty arrangements from blocking its resulting CGT charge.

For close-company distributions, HMRC RFIG21600 requires a UK close company or overseas equivalent and a material participator/associate at the relevant time in the departure year/UK part or previous three years. For returns **on or after 6 April 2026**, the charge includes post-departure trade profits; the pre-6-April-2026 exception must not be reused for a later return. Foreign-tax credit requires the actual qualifying foreign tax and return claim, not an assumed credit equal to Italy’s flat charge.

**Inheritance tax.** From 6 April 2025 the ordinary long-term-residence rule uses **10 of the preceding20 UK tax years**, rather than domicile. Overseas assets may remain within IHT after departure; the ordinary required non-resident run is **3–10 consecutive tax years**, depending on the residence record in the20 years ending with the last UK-resident year. There is also the statutory ten-consecutive-non-resident-years exclusion within the previous19 years. This is not a universal charge on all worldwide assets for a fixed tail. Young-person rules, transitional cases, elections, trusts, exclusions and any estate-tax treaty position require separate assessment. Italian relief does not determine UK IHT.

## Pensions, UK property and treaty relief

The [UK–Italy treaty](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/italy-tax-treaties/1988-uk-italy-double-taxation-convention-in-force) first requires treaty residence under art.4, with the permanent-home/centre-of-vital-interests/habitual-abode/nationality sequence if both states claim residence. Circular 17/E treats flat-tax optants as convention residents, subject to the particular treaty. An election alone is not an HMRC relief notice.

Art.18 generally allocates pensions and similar remuneration **for past employment**, and treaty-defined annuities, exclusively to the residence state, subject to art.19(2). A qualifying UK government-service pension is normally UK-only under 19(2)(a), but becomes Italy-only where the recipient is **both an Italian national and Italian resident** under 19(2)(b); government business pensions follow the art.19(3) boundary. Classify State Pension, lump sums and unusual pension payments under the actual treaty provisions rather than treating every pension as art.18 income.

Use [DT-Individual](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/double-taxation-treaty-relief-form-dt-individual) for an eligible claim for relief at source or repayment, with treaty-residence certification and the required income details. Do not stop UK PAYE on an assumption about the 7% regime or flat tax.

UK property rent and gains may be taxed in the UK under arts 6 and13. A landlord whose usual abode is abroad must consider the **Non-resident Landlord Scheme**, even where tax residence differs; gross-payment approval does not exempt the rent from tax. A non-resident disposing of their UK home must report it within **60 days of completion**, even without tax to pay; assess other land, indirect interests and residence at disposal under the applicable reporting rules rather than treating every asset sale identically.

For ordinary Italian-taxed UK income, art.24(3) limits Italy’s credit to the relevant Italian-tax share and denies it in the specified requested-final-withholding situation. Covered substitute-tax income does not simply produce an ordinary credit; consider the country exclusion in the relevant regime. UK property may also raise Italian asset-reporting/IVIE questions: apply the option’s actual country coverage and exemptions.

Sources: treaty arts 4,6,13,18,19,24; [DT-Individual instructions](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/637e192f8fa8f56eabf75e5b/Double_Taxation_Treaty_Relief_Form_DT-Individual.pdf); [NRLS](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/paying-tax-on-rent-to-landlords-abroad); [non-resident UK-home disposal](https://www.gov.uk/tax-live-abroad-sell-uk-home).

## Matching the mover

| Mover | Route to compare | Deciding question |
|---|---|---|

## Worked cases

All amounts are illustrations, not client data.

**Case 1: a family electing the flat tax in 2026.** A couple and their adult son move residence from **London to Milan in March 2026**, each non-resident in Italy for the previous **10 years**. March 2026 is in UK tax year **2025/26**; test that departure year separately from Italy’s calendar 2026. Assuming each becomes Italian fiscal resident, qualifies for the option and valid family extension, and has the stated 2026 civil-law move, the annual substitute charge is **EUR 300,000 + EUR 50,000 ×2  = EUR 400,000**; the family extensions total EUR 100,000. With the option effective for 2026, payment is by that period’s balance deadline in 2027. This covers only the income within the option. Qualifying-participation gains realised in **2026–2030** remain outside the flat tax, and the RW exception still applies.

```oa-check
  annual_flat_tax: 400000.00
```

**Case 2: an impatriato with a minor child.** An engineer moves from **Manchester to Turin in 2026** with her **8-year-old son**, after **three prior years** of Italian non-residence, earning **EUR 120,000** of Italian salary. Those inputs alone do not prove qualification. If she meets the work and qualification conditions, the same-employer/group six/seven-period restriction does not disqualify her, and the child-residence condition is satisfied, **40% × EUR 120,000  = EUR 48,000 taxable; EUR 72,000 excluded**. The salary is below the EUR 600,000 cap. The ordinary window is **2026 and the next four periods**, subject to continuing requirements and the four-year residence rule; this calculates income included, not her final IRPEF liability.

```oa-check
name: Impatriati taxable share with a minor child (40%)
inputs:
  salary: 120000
  taxable_pct: 40
  cap: 600000
steps:
  - eligible: min(salary, cap)
  - taxable: eligible * taxable_pct / 100
  - excluded: eligible - taxable
expected:
  taxable: 48000.00
  excluded: 72000.00
```

## Filing and ongoing checklist

- Establish both countries’ resident years, including treaty residence where needed. Preserve the day diary, homes and employment evidence.
- [Tell HMRC about departure](https://www.gov.uk/tax-right-retire-abroad-return-to-uk): normally P85 if not filing Self Assessment; if filing, use the return’s SA109 residence pages. A full tax year of overseas work for a UK employer is HMRC’s stated case for **both** P85 and the return/SA109. HMRC’s own online return does not handle these residence pages; use the supported filing route.
- Make the Italian election in the applicable return and pay on the actual balance timetable. Retain any interpello and eligible family/country selections. Arrange anagrafe and immigration administration separately; neither replaces the fiscal tests.
- Keep UK property reporting/NRLS, Italian foreign-asset obligations where applicable, the two five-year clocks, UK IHT status and each regime’s expiry in the diary. A missed payment has different consequences under24-bis and24-ter.
