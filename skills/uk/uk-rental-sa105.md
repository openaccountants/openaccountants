# UK property income on the SA105 pages

## Scope and who this is for

Ordinary individual UK property businesses, tax year 2026/27 (6 April 2026 to 5 April 2027), with the published SA105 **2026** boxes used only for the **2025/26** return. Historical examples retain their own years. A bank description or title deed alone does not establish tax treatment. Collect the facts below before computing or filing. [SA105 notes](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/69cbb66fa60a12ca3913c62a/UK_property_notes.pdf).

## Ask the client first

Obtain residence and usual place of abode; all properties and their use; actual receipt/entitlement and ownership evidence; spouse/civil-partner living arrangements and Form 17 dates; gross rent and service receipts before agent fees; accounting basis; loan purpose, interest and capital split; invoices and private use; furniture replacements and disposal receipts; losses and finance costs brought forward; previous FHL computations; room-sharing receipts; connected company/partnership/employer income; MTD qualifying income for each relevant year, exemptions and HMRC notices; prior returns, payments and notice-to-file dates. Missing facts mean a hold, not an assumed deduction or credit.

## The method, step by step

1. Establish the person receiving or entitled to the profits; ownership shares alone do not settle a disputed receipt/entitlement arrangement. For non-spouses, confirm the actual allocation and its evidence. For uncertain or conflicting arrangements: Check this point with one of our partner accountants. [PIM1030](https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/property-income-manual/pim1030).
2. Combine ordinary UK lettings into one property business; keep overseas property separate. Joint personal lettings are not automatically a partnership. [HMRC rental income](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/income-tax-when-you-rent-out-a-property-working-out-your-rental-income).
3. Establish cash basis or GAAP before classifying timing. Cash basis counts money received and paid, with statutory adjustments. GAAP is required above the prorated receipts limit and for companies, LLPs, trustees or partnerships with a non-individual member. Spouses/civil partners taxed equally under section 836 must use the same basis; this condition does not apply with a section 837 declaration. An individual can elect GAAP within one year of the filing date. Former BPRA balancing events, basis changes and advance rents needing allocation: Check this point with one of our partner accountants. [PIM1092](https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/property-income-manual/pim1092).
4. Test Rent-a-Room and property allowance eligibility before choosing the computation. Record the election rather than treating a lower arithmetic result as an automatic entitlement.
5. Deduct eligible costs, adjust private use and apply same-business losses. Compute the residential finance reduction separately. Reconcile the result to the appropriate dated return, credits and payments; do not treat a calculated liability as the cash balance due.

## Expenses and the tax base

Costs must satisfy the wholly-and-exclusively business test and relevant capital restrictions. Deduct an identifiable business part of a mixed expense; where full costs were entered in boxes 24–29, add the private amount back in box 30. These are the **2025/26** expense categories, subject to that test. [HMRC rental income](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/income-tax-when-you-rent-out-a-property-working-out-your-rental-income), [SA105 notes](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/69cbb66fa60a12ca3913c62a/UK_property_notes.pdf).

A nearest modern equivalent can be a repair if the improvement is incidental: replacing single glazing with double glazing is HMRC's example. Buying, selling, extensions and improvements are capital, rather than ordinary revenue deductions. Whether a run-down property's initial works are capital depends on the facts: Check this point with one of our partner accountants. Where insurance or a retained deposit covers repair costs, HMRC allows only costs exceeding that reimbursement. [HMRC rental income](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/income-tax-when-you-rent-out-a-property-working-out-your-rental-income).

Replacement of domestic items relief is for a property business letting a dwelling-house: a new domestic item replaces an old one, is solely for the tenant's use, and the old one is no longer available. Expenditure must satisfy the business test, otherwise be capital, and have no capital allowances claimed on the new item. A first purchase does not qualify; Rent-a-Room-relieved income cannot also use this deduction. Use box 36. Deduction = equivalent replacement cost plus incidental buying/disposal costs less disposal proceeds. A reasonable modern equivalent is not an improvement. HMRC's £550 sofa bed replacing a sofa with a £400 equivalent allows £400, excluding the £150 improvement (assuming no other costs/proceeds). [HMRC](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/income-tax-when-you-rent-out-a-property-working-out-your-rental-income), [notes](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/69cbb66fa60a12ca3913c62a/UK_property_notes.pdf).

### Property allowance

Eligible gross property income of £1,000 or less across property businesses is normally exempt; each joint owner tests their own gross share. Above that, an eligible claim replaces expenses and other allowances, cannot create a loss and is limited to £1,000 in total across property businesses, including UK and overseas. If used for one property business, actual expenses cannot be deducted in another. A notice to file still needs to be dealt with. [HMRC allowances](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/tax-free-allowances-on-property-and-trading-income).

The allowances are barred in a tax year with trade or property income from a company the individual or connected person owns/controls, a partnership in which either is a partner, or the individual's or spouse/civil partner's employer. The property allowance cannot be used with the residential finance-cost reducer, on Rent-a-Room income, or if expenses are deducted from letting a room at home instead of using Rent-a-Room. Election timing, mixed-business allocation or an uncertain exclusion: Check this point with one of our partner accountants. [HMRC allowances](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/tax-free-allowances-on-property-and-trading-income).

### Rent-a-Room

Owner-occupiers and tenants providing furnished accommodation in their only or main home can qualify. Excluded accommodation includes unfurnished accommodation, accommodation outside the main home or in the UK home while living abroad, and office/business use (HMRC permits incidental evening/weekend home-working by a lodger or student study facilities). Separate self-contained flats need specialist classification: Check this point with one of our partner accountants. [HS223](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/rent-a-room-for-traders-hs223-self-assessment-helpsheet/hs223-rent-a-room-scheme-2026).

Gross receipts include rent, meals/services and balancing charges. Not more than the applicable limit gives automatic exemption unless elected out. Above the limit, Method A (default) taxes actual profit; an election for Method B taxes receipts less the limit with no expenses/capital allowances. Exemption and Method B cannot create a loss. For a non-trading letting on the 2025/26 pages, Method B uses boxes 20 and 37; ordinary profit uses expense boxes and leaves 37 blank. If exempt and this is the only letting, box 4 suffices. [HS223](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/rent-a-room-for-traders-hs223-self-assessment-helpsheet/hs223-rent-a-room-scheme-2026), [notes](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/69cbb66fa60a12ca3913c62a/UK_property_notes.pdf).

ITTOIA 2005 sections 799(3) and 800(5) set the election/withdrawal limit at the first anniversary of the normal Self Assessment filing date: **31 January 2028 for 2025/26**. Section 800 elections continue unless withdrawn; if receipts do not exceed the limit, the election is treated as withdrawn for that and subsequent years. HMRC's HS223 example instead says 31 January 2027; the statute controls. Act early where possible, and obtain help for a late or disputed election: Check this point with one of our partner accountants. [Section 799](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2005/5/section/799), [section 800](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2005/5/section/800).

### Residential finance costs

For ordinary individual residential lettings, otherwise allowable interest on mortgages, furnishings loans and overdrafts, finance-arrangement costs and alternative-finance returns are subject to the restriction. Mortgage principal is excluded. Mixed business/private or residential/commercial borrowing requires a supported apportionment; loan security alone does not establish eligible use. Companies, trusts, estates, partnerships and multiple-business allocation: Check this point with one of our partner accountants. [HMRC rental income](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/income-tax-when-you-rent-out-a-property-working-out-your-rental-income), [section 274A](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2005/5/section/274A).

For one ordinary individual property business in 2025/26 or 2026/27, none of these restricted costs is deducted from profit. Reduction = **20% of the lowest** of eligible current plus brought-forward finance costs, property profits after brought-forward losses, and adjusted total income (net income after losses/reliefs, excluding savings/dividends, less applicable personal allowances). It cannot create a refund; do not assume the full interest amount generates relief. Costs exceeding the profit/income cap carry forward for the same business. Current costs go in box 44; prior unused costs in box 45. [HMRC computation](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/changes-to-tax-relief-for-residential-landlords-how-its-worked-out-including-case-studies), [notes](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/69cbb66fa60a12ca3913c62a/UK_property_notes.pdf).

### Joint ownership, losses and former FHLs

For ordinary jointly held property in the names of spouses/civil partners living together, section 836 normally taxes income equally. The statutory exceptions include income beneficially belonging to neither, partnerships, a valid section 837 declaration and income attributed to someone else under other tax provisions. Exceptional ownership/settlement arrangements: Check this point with one of our partner accountants. [Section 836](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2007/3/section/836).

Form 17 requires unequal beneficial income shares matching property shares (or sole entitlement), a joint declaration stating both, evidence and HMRC notice within 60 days beginning with the declaration date. It affects income from that date only and ends on a change in either interest; the living-together gateway must still hold. Beneficial joint tenants cannot make this declaration. For otherwise ordinary 90/10 tenants in common without a valid declaration, the default is 50/50; receipt on day 61 does not validate a declaration. [Section 837](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2007/3/section/837), [TSEM9846](https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/trusts-settlements-and-estates-manual/tsem9846), [TSEM9850](https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/trusts-settlements-and-estates-manual/tsem9850).

Ordinary property losses automatically carry forward against the first available profits of the same continuing business, indefinitely; UK, overseas and partnership businesses remain separate. Use box 39 for losses used and 43 for unused losses. Cash-basis property losses cannot be set against general income. Cessation, a restart within 3 years, agricultural expenses, excess capital allowances or a box 42 sideways claim (including the relief cap): Check this point with one of our partner accountants. [PIM4210](https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/property-income-manual/pim4210), [PIM1092](https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/property-income-manual/pim1092), [notes](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/69cbb66fa60a12ca3913c62a/UK_property_notes.pdf).

The FHL regime ended for individuals from **6 April 2025**. Former FHL income joins the appropriate UK or overseas property business, and individual residential finance-cost restrictions apply. Existing qualifying capital-allowance pools at 5 April 2025 can continue; prior FHL losses become losses of the appropriate ongoing property business. Do not turn a historical qualifying FHL into an ordinary letting retrospectively. Historical FHL qualification (availability, occupation, longer lets, commerciality, elections), pool calculations, overlap-relief assertions, pension earnings, CGT/BADR/rollover/gifts, cessation and anti-forestalling contracts from 6 March 2024: Check this point with one of our partner accountants. [PIM1092](https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/property-income-manual/pim1092), [HMRC clarification](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/furnished-holiday-lettings-tax-regime-abolition/clarification-on-abolition-of-the-furnished-holiday-lettings-tax-regime).

## Deadlines and filing

For a person outside Self Assessment, HMRC says to contact it for gross property income over £1,000 up to £2,500. HMRC says to report on a return if income is more than £2,500 after allowable expenses or more than £10,000 before expenses; existing filing notices and other return obligations still matter. Boundary or reporting-route uncertainty: Check this point with one of our partner accountants. [Allowances](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/tax-free-allowances-on-property-and-trading-income), [paying tax](https://www.gov.uk/renting-out-a-property/paying-tax).

For the ordinary 2025/26 return, notification is due 5 October 2026 if a return is needed and the individual is a first filer or did not need one for 2024/25. Normal receipt deadlines: paper 31 October 2026, online 31 January 2027; the ordinary balance is due 31 January 2027. A notice issued after 31 July can change filing dates, and a timely notification followed by a notice after 31 October can change the balance-payment date. Late notices, tax-code collection (ordinary online cutoff 30 December 2026), amendments or a missed deadline: Check this point with one of our partner accountants. [Deadlines](https://www.gov.uk/self-assessment-tax-returns/deadlines), [TMA section 8](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1970/9/section/8), [section 59B](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1970/9/section/59B).

Payments on account are normally two halves of the preceding relevant tax bill, due 31 January and 31 July. Exemption applies if that amount was under £1,000 or **more than 80%** of tax was paid outside Self Assessment. Exact base, reductions and credit reconciliation: Check this point with one of our partner accountants. Keep property records at least 5 years after the relevant January filing deadline, longer where the statutory enquiry window/enquiry requires it. [Payments](https://www.gov.uk/understand-self-assessment-bill/payments-on-account), [TMA section 12B](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1970/9/section/12B).

### MTD and non-resident landlords

For a registered individual landlord/sole trader with qualifying self-employment plus property income before expenses, and no exemption, HMRC gives these entry dates. Employment income does not count; joint property uses the individual's share. Threshold alone is not the complete exemption/eligibility test. [Qualifying income](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/work-out-your-qualifying-income-for-making-tax-digital-for-income-tax).

| Relevant-year qualifying income | Start date | Official source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Over £50,000 in 2024/25 | 6 April 2026; exactly £50,000 does not meet this trigger | [HMRC entry](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/find-out-if-and-when-you-need-to-use-making-tax-digital-for-income-tax) |
| Over £30,000 in 2025/26 | 6 April 2027 | [HMRC entry](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/find-out-if-and-when-you-need-to-use-making-tax-digital-for-income-tax) |
| Over £20,000 in 2026/27 | 6 April 2028 | [HMRC entry](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/find-out-if-and-when-you-need-to-use-making-tax-digital-for-income-tax) |

Digital records, software, exemptions, quarterly periods/updates/deadlines, annual return submission and the separate MTD penalty regime: Check this point with one of our partner accountants. Do not infer exemption from the absence of an HMRC letter or assume a single year's receipts settle continuing MTD obligations.

NRLS concerns usual place of abode abroad, which is not the same test as tax residence. A letting agent receiving or controlling rent normally operates it regardless of rent; a tenant paying the landlord directly (or a UK representative who is not a letting agent) normally operates it above £100 a week. HMRC written authority to receive rent gross removes the deduction requirement. Exactly £100 is not over the tenant threshold, but HMRC can direct operation below it. Non-standard agents/tenant-finders, gross-payment applications, written authorisation, registration, annual NRLY/NRL6 reporting and NRLS liability/credit entitlement: Check this point with one of our partner accountants. For a case where deduction is required, HMRC's basic computation is quarter's gross rent (including payments to others at the landlord's request) less deductible expenses paid that quarter, times the basic rate. Payment is due within 30 days after quarter ends 30 June, 30 September, 31 December and 31 March. [NRLS](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/paying-tax-on-rent-to-landlords-abroad), [scheme guidance](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/non-resident-landord-guidance-notes-for-letting-agents-and-tenants-non-resident-landlords-scheme-guidance-notes/what-the-non-resident-landlords-scheme-is).

## Worked cases

All tax illustrations assume eligible, evidenced expenses/reliefs and no other charges, losses or band adjustments unless stated. Missing eligibility or income facts prevent a client liability conclusion: Check this point with one of our partner accountants.

### Case A: finance costs above profit

Original Year 1: rents £20,000, non-finance costs £7,000, interest £15,000; profit £13,000. Assuming adjusted total income is at least £13,000 and sufficient tax liability, reduction = £13,000 × 20% = £2,600; unused finance costs £2,000. Year 2: profit £22,000; interest £15,000 plus £2,000 brought forward = £17,000; assuming income/liability limits do not bind, reduction = £3,400. These retain the Guide's Year 1/Year 2 inputs and fully restricted mechanics. HMRC's full Example 4 uses 2020/21 and 2021/22 labels and separate salary/allowance assumptions; do not reuse its old illustrative tax bands for a current liability. [Finance computation](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/changes-to-tax-relief-for-residential-landlords-how-its-worked-out-including-case-studies).

### Case B: Chris, 2025/26

Rent £10,400 plus heating/light £200 gives receipts £10,600; eligible expenses £9,000. Method A: £1,600 profit. Method B, assuming sole receipt of the property's letting income and a valid election: £10,600 − £7,500 = £3,100. Method A gives the lower tax base. A previous Method B election must be withdrawn; the statutory ordinary 2025/26 deadline is 31 January 2028 as explained above, despite the contrary HS223 example's 31 January 2027. Method A uses box 20 and expense boxes, leaving 37 blank. [HS223](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/rent-a-room-for-traders-hs223-self-assessment-helpsheet/hs223-rent-a-room-scheme-2026), [section 800](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2005/5/section/800).

### Case C: garage, 2026/27

Rent £2,400; expenses £300; no mortgage or connected-party income. Assuming the allowance exclusions do not apply (including other businesses/room income) and a valid claim, allowance basis gives £1,400 versus £2,100 with expenses. The allowance lowers the tax base; it does not by itself prove an actual tax saving. Outside Self Assessment, contact HMRC for this gross-income range, subject to other obligations/notices. [Allowances](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/tax-free-allowances-on-property-and-trading-income).

### Case D: England, 2026/27

Salary £45,000; rents £20,000; non-finance expenses £4,000; eligible residential interest £9,000; no other income. Assume entitlement to the full £12,570 allowance, no band extensions or other reliefs. Property profit £16,000; total £61,000; taxable £48,430. Tax = £37,700 × 20% + £10,730 × 40% = £7,540 + £4,292 = £11,832. Reduction = min(£9,000, £16,000, £48,430) × 20% = £1,800. Liability £10,032 before PAYE/payments/credits. The allowance cannot accompany the finance reducer. £20,000 of current rents alone does not establish MTD status: check the prescribed earlier year's qualifying income and continuing obligations. [Rates](https://www.gov.uk/income-tax-rates), [finance computation](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/changes-to-tax-relief-for-residential-landlords-how-its-worked-out-including-case-studies).

### Case E: spouses' 75/25 interests, 2026

Original facts: tenants in common, property and income shares 75/25 by trust declaration; Form 17 signed 1 September 2026, received 20 September 2026. Assuming the couple live together throughout, no statutory exception applies, and valid evidence/notice is supplied, income up to 31 August is split 50/50, from 1 September 75/25. Each reports their share and ticks box 3. Receipt after the 60-day period would not validate that declaration. The dates alone do not prove all conditions. [Sections 836](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2007/3/section/836) and [837](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2007/3/section/837).

### Case F: Julie and NRLS

HMRC's example has £1,500 for the quarter: £200 plumbing, £100 landlord loan principal and £1,200 direct to the landlord. In a case subject to withholding and with the plumbing deductible, base = £1,500 − £200 = £1,300; 20% = £260, due within 30 days of quarter end. Loan principal stays in the base. Box 21 records evidenced tax deducted; do not invent credit from this arithmetic if no tax was actually deducted. [HMRC NRLS](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/paying-tax-on-rent-to-landlords-abroad), [notes](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/69cbb66fa60a12ca3913c62a/UK_property_notes.pdf).

### Historical Examples 1–3: same buy-to-let, 2024/25, 2025/26 and 2026/27

Each retains rent £12,000, interest £4,000, agent fees £1,200, insurance £300, repairs £800, no other property income and a basic-rate taxpayer. For an ordinary residential letting, profit = £12,000 − £1,200 − £300 − £800 = £9,700 in each year. **Only if other income has already used the allowance, all this profit remains in the basic band, and finance-cost income/liability limits permit the full credit**, incremental tax = £1,940 − £800 = £1,140. Without those facts the case cannot determine tax. Correct the old 2026/27 forecast of £2,134 tax, £880 credit, £1,254 net and £114 increase: the conditional result is £1,140, with no rate-driven increase on unchanged facts. Residential interest belongs in box 44, not 26. For a qualifying 2024/25 FHL, use a separate historical review: Check this point with one of our partner accountants. [Previous rates](https://www.gov.uk/income-tax-rates/previous-tax-years), [finance computation](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/changes-to-tax-relief-for-residential-landlords-how-its-worked-out-including-case-studies).

### Historical Example 4 (original Example 2): furnished spare room

Original £6,000 receipts, only/main home, covering 2024/25, 2025/26 and 2026/27: assuming the £7,500 limit applies, sole receipt, all Rent-a-Room conditions and no election out, income is exempt. Shared receipt can reduce the limit to £3,750 and change that result. Box 4 alone suffices only where this is the sole letting income on the applicable return. [Rent-a-Room](https://www.gov.uk/rent-room-in-your-home/the-rent-a-room-scheme), [HS223](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/rent-a-room-for-traders-hs223-self-assessment-helpsheet/hs223-rent-a-room-scheme-2026).

### Historical Example 5 (original Example 3): spare room above the limit

Original receipts £10,000 and expenses £4,000, including the 2026/27 comparison: with all Rent-a-Room conditions, sole receipt and a valid Method B election, the tax base is £10,000 − £7,500 = £2,500, compared with ordinary profit £6,000. This compares taxable bases; the person's total circumstances determine any actual saving. No expense deduction accompanies Method B. [HS223](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/rent-a-room-for-traders-hs223-self-assessment-helpsheet/hs223-rent-a-room-scheme-2026).

### Historical Example 6 (original Example 4): higher-rate landlord, 2025/26

Employment £80,000, rent £15,000, residential interest £8,000, other expenses £3,000. Assuming full allowance, no other income/relief, ordinary non-FHL property and full credit eligibility: profit £12,000; incremental tax £4,800 − £1,600 = £3,200, or £3,200 / £15,000 = 21.3% of rent (rounded). The former unrestricted-interest comparison (£4,000 × 40% = £1,600; difference £1,600) is a counterfactual, not an available election or promised saving. For 2026/27 the same conditional rates apply. [Rates](https://www.gov.uk/income-tax-rates), [finance computation](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/changes-to-tax-relief-for-residential-landlords-how-its-worked-out-including-case-studies).
