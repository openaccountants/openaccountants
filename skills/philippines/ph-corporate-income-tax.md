# Philippines Corporate Income Tax

2026 rules as at 22 November 2026. Not yet approved, pending Philippine CPA review. Amounts are in Philippine pesos (PHP) unless stated otherwise.

## Scope

Compute ordinary corporate income tax under the NIRC, as amended by CREATE, CREATE MORE and CMEPA. Establish whether the company is domestic, a resident foreign corporation doing business in the Philippines, or a nonresident foreign corporation. A domestic corporation's ordinary base includes worldwide income; a foreign corporation's Philippine tax base is limited to Philippine-source income, subject to the relevant rules and treaty. Incorporation in the Philippines and a shareholder's nationality are different questions. [NIRC, sections 22(B)-(D) and 23(E)-(F)](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/2/3896); [CREATE, sections 6-7](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf//showdocs/2/93191).

This method retains the original ordinary-tax, dividend, interest, royalty, branch-remittance and return topics. It does not determine eligibility for an income tax holiday, special corporate income tax, sector-specific regime, foreign tax credit or a particular treaty reduction. Separate registered-project income from other income before using an incentive. [BIR RR 7-2025, sections 2-3](https://bir-cdn.bir.gov.ph/BIR/pdf/RR%20No.%207-2025.pdf).

## Ask the client first

- Where is the company incorporated, what Philippine activities does it conduct and what are its BIR registration date and year-end? For domestic MCIT, establish the taxable year containing the BIR registration date.
- What are the gross sales, returns/discounts, cost of goods or services, other income, allowable deductions, prior losses and tax credits? Obtain the accounts and tax reconciliation.
- For the smaller-company rate, what are net taxable income and total assets after excluding only the land on which the business's office, plant and equipment are situated?
- Has an OSD election been made for this taxable year, or do itemised deductions apply? Obtain earlier quarterly returns and the election evidence.
- Which receipts are interest, royalties, domestic/foreign dividends or other separately taxed items? For older financial instruments, obtain the original agreement, issue date, maturity and changes/renewals.
- For a payment abroad or branch remittance, identify the beneficial recipient, legal form, residence, source, tax-sparing/treaty documents and amount earmarked before tax. Obtain any registered-project incentive evidence.

## The method, step by step

### 1. Select the regular rate and preserve the base distinction

| Company or test | 2026 rate, amount and conditions | Official source |
|---|---|---|
| Domestic corporation, ordinary rate | **25% of taxable income**, subject to a qualifying reduced or special regime. | [NIRC 27(A); RR 7-2025, section 2(A)](https://bir-cdn.bir.gov.ph/BIR/pdf/RR%20No.%207-2025.pdf) |
| Smaller domestic corporation | **20%** only when net taxable income is **not over PHP 5,000,000 AND total assets not over PHP 100,000,000**, excluding the specified office/plant/equipment land. Both conditions must hold for the taxable year. | [NIRC 27(A); RR 7-2025, section 2(A)](https://bir-cdn.bir.gov.ph/BIR/pdf/RR%20No.%207-2025.pdf) |
| Resident foreign corporation | Ordinarily **25% of Philippine-source taxable income**; the smaller-domestic-company test is not available merely because a branch is small. | [NIRC 28(A)(1); RR 7-2025, section 2(B)](https://bir-cdn.bir.gov.ph/BIR/pdf/RR%20No.%207-2025.pdf) |
| RBE under the enhanced deductions regime | **20%** for taxable income from its **registered projects or activities**, effective **28 November 2024**. This is a separate route, not a waiver of the smaller-company asset test for unregistered income. | [RR 7-2025, sections 2-3](https://bir-cdn.bir.gov.ph/BIR/pdf/RR%20No.%207-2025.pdf) |
| Nonresident foreign corporation | Ordinarily **25% of gross Philippine-source income**, subject to specific exemptions/rates and substantiated treaty relief. Do not deduct expenses as if this were resident-corporation net income. | [NIRC 28(B)(1), as amended by CMEPA section 7](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/2/99213) |
| MCIT | **2% of the statutory gross-income base**, beginning in the **fourth taxable year immediately following the commencement year**, when greater than ordinary income tax; applies to the ordinary domestic/resident-foreign regime. The temporary **1%** period ended **30 June 2023**. | [NIRC 27(E), 28(A)(2); CREATE sections 6-7](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf//showdocs/2/93191) |
| Corporate OSD | **40% of gross income**, in lieu of itemised deductions, for a corporation taxed under **27(A) or 28(A)(1)**; elect in the return and keep gross-income records. It is not 40% of sales. | [NIRC 34(L), amended by RA 9504 section 3](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/2/8724) |

“Assets” is not capital or annual revenue. Reconcile the asset total to the accounts and identify the excluded land; do not exclude every investment property or all fixed assets. The statutory smaller-company thresholds are supported by the BIR regulation's words and figures. A numerical typo in an online CREATE MORE transcription does not change the BIR's **PHP 100,000,000** asset ceiling. [RR 7-2025, first-page table](https://bir-cdn.bir.gov.ph/BIR/pdf/RR%20No.%207-2025.pdf).

### 2. Reconcile the ordinary taxable-income base

Start with the company's gross income within its geographical tax scope, then deduct the legally allowed expenses or the elected OSD. Reconcile accounting profit to taxable income and separate exempt and final-taxed amounts. Taxable income is not automatically bank receipts, accounting profit or total sales. [NIRC sections 31-34](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/2/3896); [RA 9504, section 3, NIRC 34(L)](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/2/8724).

For corporate OSD, deduct up to 40% of gross income in place of itemised deductions. Elect the method on the first-quarter return. A valid election is irrevocable for the taxable year. Keep gross-income records and meet the applicable financial-statement requirements. [NIRC 34(L)](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/2/8724); [BIR RR 2-2010](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/10/50806).

For MCIT, start with sales net of returns, discounts and allowances, less cost of goods sold. For services, start with gross service revenue less the corresponding adjustments and direct cost of services. Add other gross income subject to ordinary corporate tax, and exclude exempt and final-taxed income. Compare MCIT with ordinary tax once the age test and regime apply. [NIRC 27(E)(1), (4)](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/2/3896); [BIR RR 12-2007, section 2](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/10/47521).

For a domestic corporation, the commencement year for MCIT is the taxable year in which it registered with the BIR. For a calendar-year company registered in **2022**, the 4 following taxable years are 2023, 2024, 2025 and **2026**, the first MCIT year. Excess MCIT over ordinary tax can be carried forward against ordinary income tax for the **3 immediately succeeding taxable years**, subject to the available ordinary tax and remaining credit. Relief for specified labour disputes, force majeure or legitimate business reverses requires the authorised relief process. [NIRC 27(E)(1)-(4)](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/2/3896); [CREATE section 6](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf//showdocs/2/93191); [BIR RR 9-98, section 2.27(E)(5)](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/10/43525).

### 3. Separate passive income and dividends before applying ordinary tax

| Receipt or payment | Domestic-law treatment and conditions | Official source |
|---|---|---|
| Dividend received by a domestic or resident foreign corporation from a domestic corporation | **Exempt from income tax** under the intercorporate rule. Identify the payer as domestic; this is not an automatic foreign-dividend exemption. | [CMEPA NIRC 27(D)(3), 28(A)(1); RR 21-2025 official digest, corporations table](https://bir-cdn.bir.gov.ph/BIR/pdf/RR%20No.%2021-2025%20Digest.pdf) |
| Dividend paid by a domestic corporation to an NRFC | **25%** ordinary final withholding, or **15%** where tax-sparing conditions are established; a qualifying treaty rate may apply. Current tax-sparing deemed credit is the **10-percentage-point** difference between ordinary and reduced rates, or the home country does not impose tax on the dividends. | [NIRC 28(B)(1), 28(B)(5)(b); RR 21-2025 digest, NRFC table](https://bir-cdn.bir.gov.ph/BIR/pdf/RR%20No.%2021-2025%20Digest.pdf); [CREATE section 7](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf//showdocs/2/93191) |
| Dividend paid to a nonresident alien individual | **20% of the total** for one engaged in Philippine trade/business; **25%** for one not so engaged, subject to applicable treaty relief. Do not substitute the resident-individual dividend rate. | [CMEPA, section 5, NIRC 25(A)(2), (B)](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/2/99213) |
| Domestic/RFC bank-deposit, deposit-substitute, trust or similar interest/yield | **20% final tax**, including foreign-currency deposits, under the rule effective **1 July 2025**; check the grandfathered-instrument conditions below before overwriting an older preferential rate. | [CMEPA sections 6-7; RR 21-2025 digest, corporate table and transition](https://bir-cdn.bir.gov.ph/BIR/pdf/RR%20No.%2021-2025%20Digest.pdf) |
| Domestic/RFC royalties earned as passive income | **20% final tax**. Classify the receipt as passive; do not mechanically final-tax active business service revenue as a royalty. | [NIRC 27(D)(6), 28(A)(1), as amended by CMEPA](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/2/99213); [RR 21-2025 digest](https://bir-cdn.bir.gov.ph/BIR/pdf/RR%20No.%2021-2025%20Digest.pdf) |
| Branch profits applied or earmarked for remittance to its foreign head office | **15%** of the total remittable profits **without deducting the tax component**; assess the statutory PEZA exception, effective connection and applicable treaty before applying a concession. This is separate from the branch's ordinary corporate income tax. | [Current NIRC 28(A)(4), recited in CREATE section 7](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf//showdocs/2/93191) |

**Corrected by Jonathan I. Ruiz (CPA, Philippines)**: the interest rule includes FCDU deposits. The former preferential **15%** interest rate is not the general current rate for newly issued/transacted instruments. The grandfather rule is narrower than “all accounts opened before the change”: the instrument must have been issued or transacted **before 1 July 2025**, have an evidenced maturity extending beyond that date, and retain its original maturity/coverage without renewal or a replacement instrument from that date. Read the actual agreement and bank certificate before applying an old exemption or rate for its remaining maturity. [CMEPA section 28](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/2/99213); [RR 21-2025 digest, final transition conditions](https://bir-cdn.bir.gov.ph/BIR/pdf/RR%20No.%2021-2025%20Digest.pdf).

**Corrected by Jonathan I. Ruiz (CPA, Philippines)**: the branch-remittance domestic-law rate remains the one shown above; it was not replaced by the ordinary corporate rate. A lower treaty rate cannot be assumed from the country name alone. Obtain the applicable treaty, residence/entitlement documents and Philippine relief procedure; no particular treaty reduction is computed here. [Current NIRC 28(A)(4), recited in CREATE section 7](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf//showdocs/2/93191).

For NRFC tax sparing, establish the recipient's home-country law and supporting documents before selecting the lower rate. The BIR's official RMO 46-2020 digest describes an ITAD confirmation request within **90 days from remittance or the foreign authority's determination of deemed credit/non-imposition, whichever is later**. Apply that procedure with the current CREATE/CMEPA rate difference; the digest's old waived-tax percentage reflects its pre-CREATE context and is not the current difference. A reduced dividend rate and treaty entitlement are separate legal routes. [BIR official RMO 46-2020 digest](https://bir-cdn.bir.gov.ph/local/pdf/RMO%20No.%2046-2020.pdf); [CREATE section 7](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf//showdocs/2/93191); [RR 21-2025 NRFC table](https://bir-cdn.bir.gov.ph/BIR/pdf/RR%20No.%2021-2025%20Digest.pdf).

### 4. Reconcile cumulative payments and select the return

Compute quarterly income on a cumulative basis for the first **3 quarters**, crediting tax previously paid and supported creditable withholding. Use **BIR Form 1702Q**, with the appropriate ordinary-tax/MCIT schedules for the company's facts. At year-end prepare the final adjustment against quarterly payments, valid withholding certificates and other allowable credits; an MCIT carryforward needs its own year-by-year record. [NIRC sections 75-77](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/2/3896); [RA 11976 section 11](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/2/96948); [BIR Form 1702Q, schedules and instructions](https://bir-cdn.bir.gov.ph/local/pdf/1702Q%202018ENCS%20final2.pdf). The historical form confirms the return structure; its pre-CREATE ordinary rate is superseded by the current rate table above.

Choose **1702-RT** for the ordinary corporate return route, **1702-MX** where the applicable mixed/special treatment requires it, or **1702-EX** for the relevant exempt non-individual route. Match the actual form to the entity's registration, income and incentive certificates rather than selecting EX merely because the net amount payable is zero. The BIR's 2026 filing circular lists these forms and the electronic channels; it covers **calendar year 2025 filed in 2026**, so it is not a new deadline for the 2026 accounts. [BIR RMC 20-2026 official digest](https://bir-cdn.bir.gov.ph/BIR/pdf/RMC%20No.%2020-2026%20Digest.pdf).

| Return/payment | Statutory timing and practical completion | Official source |
|---|---|---|
| Quarterly corporate income tax | Within **60 days after each of the first 3 quarters**, whether calendar or fiscal year. File and pay using the applicable authorised channel; retain both confirmations. | [NIRC 75, 77, as amended by EOPT section 11](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/2/96948) |
| Annual final adjustment return | **15 April following the calendar year**, or the **15th day of the fourth month after fiscal year-end**. Payment accompanies filing. Check a specific governing extension before varying the statutory date. | [NIRC 77(B)-(C), EOPT section 11](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/2/96948) |
| Electronic filing/payment and attachments | Use mandated/valid eFPS, the appropriate eBIRForms route or an authorised provider; submit required accounts/certificates and retain acknowledgements. Portal-version and attachment instructions must match the actual filing cycle. | [RMC 20-2026 digest, eFPS/eBIRForms/provider and evidence provisions](https://bir-cdn.bir.gov.ph/BIR/pdf/RMC%20No.%2020-2026%20Digest.pdf) |

A return receipt is not proof that tax has been paid or that an attachment was accepted. Reconcile the filed amount, payment, withholding certificates and accounts. Identify late or missing filings for the CPA's current penalty computation; do not use a historical tax rate or assume an annual filing cured omitted quarterly returns.

## Worked cases

### A. Both smaller-company conditions matter

A domestic corporation registered with the BIR in **2024** and has **PHP 4,000,000 taxable income** for calendar **2026**, with **PHP 99,000,000 assets after the permitted land exclusion**. It meets both smaller-company tests, giving **PHP 800,000 ordinary tax**. At **PHP 101,000,000 relevant assets**, it fails the asset test and ordinary tax rises to **PHP 1,000,000**, a **PHP 200,000** difference. Assume no separate incentive. The first MCIT year is 2028. [RR 7-2025](https://bir-cdn.bir.gov.ph/BIR/pdf/RR%20No.%207-2025.pdf); [NIRC 27(E) as amended by CREATE](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf//showdocs/2/93191); [BIR RR 9-98](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/10/43525).

```oa-check
name: Domestic reduced-rate asset-boundary decision
inputs:
  taxable_income: 4000000
  qualifying_assets: 99000000
  alternative_assets: 101000000
  income_limit: 5000000
  asset_limit: 100000000
steps:
  income_headroom: income_limit - taxable_income
  asset_headroom: asset_limit - qualifying_assets
  alternative_excess: alternative_assets - asset_limit
  qualifying_tax: taxable_income * 0.20
  alternative_tax: taxable_income * 0.25
  tax_difference: alternative_tax - qualifying_tax
expected:
  income_headroom: 1000000
  asset_headroom: 1000000
  alternative_excess: 1000000
  qualifying_tax: 800000
  alternative_tax: 1000000
  tax_difference: 200000
```

### B. Low taxable profit does not eliminate MCIT

An ordinary domestic corporation registered with the BIR in **2022** and keeps calendar years. In **2026** it has **PHP 150,000,000 relevant assets**, **PHP 7,000,000 gross income from its core business** after direct costs, **PHP 1,000,000 other gross income subject to ordinary corporate tax** and **PHP 400,000 ordinary taxable income** after allowable deductions. It fails the smaller-company asset test, so ordinary tax is **PHP 100,000**. MCIT is **PHP 160,000** on total gross income of PHP 8,000,000. The liability before payment credits is the higher **PHP 160,000**. Record the **PHP 60,000 excess MCIT** for potential ordinary-tax credit in **2027-2029**. [RR 7-2025](https://bir-cdn.bir.gov.ph/BIR/pdf/RR%20No.%207-2025.pdf); [NIRC 27(E), read with CREATE section 6](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf//showdocs/2/93191); [BIR RR 12-2007](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/10/47521).

```oa-check
name: MCIT age and higher-of-tax computation
inputs:
  bir_registration_year: 2022
  taxable_year: 2026
  core_gross_income: 7000000
  other_ordinary_gross_income: 1000000
  taxable_income: 400000
steps:
  first_mcit_year: bir_registration_year + 4
  following_year_count: taxable_year - bir_registration_year
  ordinary_tax: taxable_income * 0.25
  mcit_gross_income: core_gross_income + other_ordinary_gross_income
  mcit: mcit_gross_income * 0.02
  tax_before_credits: max(ordinary_tax, mcit)
  excess_mcit: max(0, mcit - ordinary_tax)
  last_carryforward_year: taxable_year + 3
expected:
  first_mcit_year: 2026
  following_year_count: 4
  ordinary_tax: 100000
  mcit_gross_income: 8000000
  mcit: 160000
  tax_before_credits: 160000
  excess_mcit: 60000
  last_carryforward_year: 2029
```

## When to refuse or refer

- Do not finalise a computation without the company's legal tax classification, accounts, source reconciliation, deduction election, commencement year and supported credits.
- Do not apply the smaller-company rate when either statutory ceiling fails, or extend it to a resident foreign corporation on that basis.
- Do not apply a gross-income rate to net taxable income, use sales as the corporate OSD base, or carry excess MCIT against another MCIT liability without legal authority.
- Do not grant tax sparing, a treaty reduction, foreign-dividend exemption or a registered-project incentive without the actual conditions and documents. Obtain a Philippine international-tax review for a disputed foreign classification or relief claim.
- Do not treat an old bank-account opening date as proof of the CMEPA grandfathering conditions, or use the new general rate to erase a demonstrably qualifying instrument's remaining preferential period.
- Do not use the last filing cycle's portal instructions as a promise about the next release. Obtain the actual return, account and filing-channel confirmation before submission.
