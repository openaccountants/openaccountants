# Jersey GST and imports: 2026

## Scope and period

This is a partial reference for Jersey GST, with hypothetical arithmetic. It does not authorise an arbitrary supply classification, customs assessment or complete GST engagement output. Load the universal workflow base and the cited source conditions; unresolved facts or law hold the dependent amount and complete financial package. The complete operational companion remains unfinished.

The current [*Goods and Services Tax (Jersey) Law 2007*](https://www.jerseylaw.je/laws/current/l_27_2007) capture applies from 6 April 2026. The personal import direction below applies from 11 May 2026. Earlier events require the corresponding version and instruments. A current capture does not establish every subsidiary direction or relief.

## Registration and classification

The [business GST guidance](https://www.gov.je/Industry/BusinessAdviceHub/BusinessTaxesSocialSecurity/Pages/GoodsServicesTaxBusinesses.aspx) states a 5% standard rate and mandatory registration when taxable turnover reaches or is expected to reach GBP 300,000 in a 12-month period. Voluntary registration is available below that level. A business making zero-rated supplies may apply for exemption from registration; zero rating does not automatically remove registration obligations.

Determine place of supply, supplier/customer location, registration, tax point and statutory classification. The [place-of-supply guidance](https://www.gov.je/taxesmoney/gst/businesses/introduction/pages/placesupply.aspx) distinguishes goods, exports, land-related services and other services. Schedule 3 received services can affect registration and reverse charging. Overseas services are not uniformly outside GST.

[Articles 48 and 49 and Schedules 5 and 6](https://www.jerseylaw.je/laws/current/l_27_2007) distinguish exemption from a taxable supply at nil. A customer's location, invoice without GST or business description alone does not settle classification.

| Statutory classification | Conditions to establish |
|---|---|
| Schedule 5 finance | The specified transaction or service; separate/disclosed credit and qualifying security/fund rules, including the dwelling-share exception |
| Schedule 5 insurance | Permitted insurance/reinsurance, arrangements or claims handling; specified legal/assessment services are excluded from claims-handling exemption |
| Schedule 5 postal services | Qualifying postal operator and conveyance/connected service; hire of goods is excluded |
| Schedule 5 medical/paramedical | Specified practitioner, regulated occupation, premises/activity or contractor and the relevant patient/service conditions |
| Schedule 5 charities | The statutory income-tax-exempt charity definition |
| Schedule 5 child care | Parental-responsibility recipient, registered day-care accommodation and approval, cumulatively |
| Schedule 5 education | Named/approved schools, qualifying nursery provision or specified/approved higher-education college |
| Schedule 5 burial/cremation | Specified disposal/arrangement/approved services and coffin/approved goods conditions |
| Schedule 6 dwellings and land/construction | Qualifying interest/use, occupation and term; planning, certificates, connected parking shares and the particular grant/construction conditions |
| Schedule 6 pharmaceutical benefit | The recipient is an insured person entitled to pharmaceutical benefit for that supply under the Health Insurance Law |
| Schedule 6 exports/international services | The precise export/service category, required evidence and applicable directions/regulations |

Each row is a source route, not a blanket exemption for the sector. Schedule 6 paragraph 3 makes the qualifying pharmaceutical-benefit supply **zero-rated**, despite the [customer guidance](https://www.gov.je/TaxesMoney/GST/GSTCustomers/Pages/PayingGSTJersey.aspx) grouping prescription medicines with exemptions. Not every pharmacy sale meets the statutory conditions.

For dwelling interests/licences, Schedule 6 paragraph 1 excludes specified discontinuous occupation, terms below 3 months and restrictions on principal-private-residence use. Food, linen and cleaning are not absorbed into the dwelling supply. Paragraph 2 distinguishes construction from ordinary repairs/alterations, contains specified conversion rules, excludes named professional services and certain furniture/appliances/materials, and requires the relevant charitable/residential certificate. Do not classify an entire mixed property project from its address or label.

Schedule 6 paragraph 5 requires the actual international-service category. Its overseas-contract branch also requires direct overseas benefit and excludes specified Jersey land/goods services. Ship/aircraft definitions and charter exclusions matter. Export-linked financial services require prescribed Regulations, not merely an overseas customer. Paragraph 4 export zero rating depends on Comptroller conditions; [Article 50](https://www.jerseylaw.je/laws/current/l_27_2007) can impose joint and several GST liability if export/conditions fail. The complete subsidiary-instrument chain remains open.

## Input tax and ordinary return

[Articles 33 to 39A](https://www.jerseylaw.je/laws/current/l_27_2007) define input/output tax and allowable credit. Identify business use, attribution, evidence and any disallowance before entering a credit. Directors' domestic accommodation and nonbusiness use are restricted; goods incorporated in qualifying dwellings can have a separate non-building-material restriction. Articles 34 and 35 provide for excess credits but permit specified postponement, withholding and conditions. An excess is not proof of an immediately payable cash refund.

Article 36(2) includes taxable supplies, specified services outside Jersey that would be taxable domestically, and qualifying exempt cross-border/export-linked and intermediary services within paragraphs (c) and (d). Verify the applicable direction and conditions before including the latter. Do not reject or recover their inputs solely because the output is described as exempt.

Use the [GST return instructions](https://www.gov.je/TaxesMoney/GST/Businesses/SubmittingReturn/Pages/Return.aspx):

1. Reconcile sales, zero-rated/remitted sales, domestic purchases and imports to the GST account.
2. Calculate actual output tax using the established tax point. At the ordinary rate, tax-exclusive value x 5% gives GST; an inclusive value contains gross x 5 / 105.
3. Establish allowable input credit, supported by invoices or import evidence, after attribution and adjustments. Purchase value is not input tax.
4. Net allowable credit against output tax, retaining any credit/postponement conditions.
5. A Customs-approved trader does not claim import GST it has not paid, but still reports the import value in the appropriate box. Check any partial-exemption import adjustment separately.

[Article 36A](https://www.jerseylaw.je/laws/current/l_27_2007) removes credit attributable to monetary consideration still unpaid at the end of 6 months after the later of supply and payment-due dates. Part payment, allocation, subsequent restoration and supplier bad-debt relief need the operative directions; they are not interchangeable with a general accounting write-off.

[GST Regulations 14 to 16](https://www.jerseylaw.je/laws/current/ro_133_2007), in the consolidation from 1 January 2025, generally exclude a credit not claimed within three years after the accounting period in which GST became chargeable, subject to exceptional Comptroller extension to avoid injustice. Pre-registration recovery is discretionary and conditional: goods/imports ordinarily look back no more than three years and services six months, with consumption, business use and relevant pre-incorporation reimbursement/person conditions. Claims normally belong on the first return, with required stock/service records. A purchase inside the time limit alone does not establish recovery.

## Partial exemption

[Direction 2008/15](https://www.gov.je/TaxesMoney/GST/Businesses/LawRegulationDirection/Pages/PartialExemptionsGSTDirection.aspx), made under Article 36(3), requires attribution using Revenue Jersey's GST Partial Exemption booklet and states no expiry. The [current explanatory page](https://www.gov.je/TaxesMoney/GST/Businesses/Introduction/pages/partialexemptions.aspx) describes the following tests. Apply them only after establishing eligible inputs and the correct supply values.

| Test or amount | Published boundary |
|---|---|
| Test A | Exempt sales value no more than 5% of total sales for the specified rolling/initial period |
| Test B, after failing A | Exempt input tax from steps 2 and 3 no more than GBP 250 a month on average **and** no more than 25% of all input tax |
| Annual-method turnover | Below GBP 10 million including taxable and exempt supplies, for GST years after the first; GBP 10 million or more uses each return period |
| Capital-purchase regime | Qualifying purchase value more than GBP 1 million excluding GST; purchase year and four following GST years |

The guidance defines the first GST year from registration to the end of the first return more than 12 months later. Different years require approval. Test A's initial projections/first-three-year adjustment and connected-person rules, and Test B's first-year/connected-person exclusions, must be checked. Passing a numerical limit alone does not establish full recovery.

For ordinary domestic supplies, attribute wholly taxable input directly, identify wholly exempt input, then split residual input by eligible taxable supplies divided by all relevant supplies. The HTML says round the percentage to the nearest whole number. The linked [2010 booklet](https://www.gov.je/sitecollectiondocuments/tax%20and%20your%20money/id%20gstpartialexemptionv3%202010%20jc.pdf), page 8, says round up but changes 58.77% to 58%; a later example uses 78.82% to 79%. It also retains historical 3% examples. These sources do not establish an unqualified current rounding algorithm. **A nonintegral percentage remains Needs Input until the operative method is reconciled.** The exact-integer example below does not resolve that conflict. Article 36's specific service categories also qualify the guidance's broad outside-Jersey wording.

The guidance's import/Schedule 3 step 4 adjusts for exempt or mixed use, including a possible output-tax addition when the adjustment exceeds taxable input credit. Do not apply it without establishing how the import/received-service tax was accounted for. The annual true-up changes input or output tax on the specified following return; provisional recovery is not final. For a qualifying mixed-use capital purchase, compare 20% of its GST using the current annual deductible proportion with 20% of the original deduction in each of the following four years; disposal/destruction ends adjustments for subsequent years. Changes of use, connection, nonbusiness use and the original deduction require evidence. An alternative attribution method needs written approval before use.

## Bad debts, unpaid inputs and special provision

[Article 54](https://www.jerseylaw.je/laws/current/l_27_2007) requires the original supplier's monetary supply, accounted-and-paid GST, a bad-debt write-off, at least six months since supply, the open-market-value limit and transfer of goods where relevant. Claim within the approved time or, by default, twelve months after the **last writing-off** of the consideration, in the approved/sufficient form. A dispute or unpaid invoice alone is insufficient. [Direction 2008/24](https://www.gov.je/taxesmoney/gst/businesses/lawregulationdirection/pages/gstreliefbaddebtsdirection.aspx) adopts the evidential guide; [bad-debt guidance](https://www.gov.je/TaxesMoney/GST/Businesses/SubmittingReturn/Pages/ReliefBadDebts.aspx) uses return box 7 and retains claim records for six years from claim. Partial payments, enforceable security, setoff, factoring, group changes and subsequent money/goods recovery can change relief. A credit note requires an actual agreed consideration change, not mere nonpayment.

[Direction 2013/01](https://www.gov.je/taxesmoney/gst/businesses/lawregulationdirection/pages/baddebtreliefadjustmentsgstdirection.aspx) supplies these separate adjustments, with its definitions and period conditions. Its linked [January 2013 PDF](https://www.gov.je/SiteCollectionDocuments/Government%20and%20administration/ID%20GST%20Relief%20on%20Bad%20Debts%20January%202013%2020130318%20JB.pdf) supplies the complete formula arrangements:

- Unpaid-input reversal: deduction actually claimed D x unpaid consideration UC / total consideration TC, entered negatively in allowable input tax for the period in which the Article 36A relevant period ends.
- Later restoration: earlier input repayment R x later payment PC / consideration unpaid at that relevant-period end C. The original adjustment return and any GST payable must have been submitted/paid; restore in the later payment period.
- Multiple-supply payment allocation: earliest supply first; for supplies on the same day, payment P x outstanding amount for that supply OS / all that day's outstanding consideration TOS. An identified full payment for a particular supply has its stated exception.
- Applicable hire-purchase/credit-sale allocation: payment P x total agreement interest A / total payable less amounts on which interest is not charged B goes to credit; the balance goes to goods, subject to the direction's trigger.

Do not substitute the supplier's relief for the purchaser's input reversal or reverse more input than was claimed. The direction's cash-accounting exception refers to historical Direction 2010/02; the [directions register](https://www.gov.je/TaxesMoney/GST/Businesses/LawRegulationDirection/Pages/GSTDirectionsSummary.aspx) lists 2020/03 as its replacement. Cash-scheme application requires that operative instrument, not the old reference alone.

The [retail guidance](https://www.gov.je/TaxesMoney/GST/Businesses/Industry/pages/retailsbusinesses.aspx) describes eligibility where at least 50% of sales are to the general public, ordinary use without prior registration, and permission for a bespoke method. Keep sales to registered businesses individually accounted for and separate zero-rated/exempt classifications before applying the current 5/105 fraction to eligible standard-rated inclusive takings. [Direction 2008/09](https://www.gov.je/taxesmoney/gst/businesses/lawregulationdirection/pages/gstretailschemeinvoices.aspx) conditions the ordinary simplified-invoice easement at a supply value no more than GBP 250, with registered-customer-request and programmable-till exceptions. Required receipt details include supplier name/address/GST number, supply date, inclusive value and an indication of GST at 5%. Higher regular values require the stated full-invoice or approved-ceiling route. The guidance's shorter list does not replace those conditions. Full retail accounting and cash-scheme mechanics remain unverified here.

[GST Regulations 23 and 25 to 27](https://www.jerseylaw.je/laws/current/ro_133_2007) extend the conditional second-hand margin scheme to motor vehicles, pedal cycles and electrically assisted pedal cycles. Eligible acquisition routes, invoice/hire exclusions and agent/auction prices must be established; the scheme is not available for every used item. Regulations 28 and 28A separately condition charity and reciprocal overseas-business refunds on qualifying use/supplies, actual payment, records and the prescribed claims. An exempt output or overseas address alone establishes no refund.

[Articles 56A to 60](https://www.jerseylaw.je/laws/current/l_27_2007) and [ISE Regulations 2 and 8](https://www.jerseylaw.je/laws/current/ro_39_2008) distinguish listing, relieved supplies and refunds. Article 57 relief needs the relevant business purpose and prescribed conditions; it excludes specified unchanged onward goods for Jersey persons and specified special-provision taxable supplies valued below GBP 1,000. Regulation 8 provides a separate conditional refund route for qualifying supplies below GBP 1,000 after actual GST payment and with required status, business-use, records and application. Under Article 58, relief does not change the underlying taxable/exempt/zero-rated classification. Listing fees and eligibility require their actual category rules; no universal ISE fee or blanket purchase exemption is given.

## Imports and customs boundaries

[GST Law Article 32](https://www.jerseylaw.je/laws/current/l_27_2007) values imports using the customs-law value plus relevant taxes/duties/charges not already included, excluding Jersey GST, and commission, packing, transport and insurance to importation. Foreign VAT is expressly included. Avoid double-counting amounts already included in customs value. A registered overseas retailer collects GST at purchase regardless of order value; [import guidance](https://www.gov.je/taxesmoney/gst/gstcustomers/pages/declaringpaying.aspx) explains how to prevent duplicate collection.

[Direction 2026/03](https://www.gov.je/TaxesMoney/GST/Businesses/LawRegulationDirection/Pages/GSTDirection202304MovementofGoods.aspx), effective 11 May 2026, gives a GBP 60 de minimis for qualifying unaccompanied personal-use imports from unregistered retailers. It excludes business imports and artificial splitting. At or below GBP 60 qualifying relief applies; above it GST applies to the whole value, subject to other applicable relief. Accompanied luggage has separate rules.

GST, customs tariff and excise are separate liabilities. The [customs procedures](https://www.gov.je/Travel/InformationAdvice/ImportsExports/CustomsTradersAgents/Pages/ImportExportAndPostalProcedures.aspx) require classification, valuation and origin evidence; the [classification guidance](https://www.gov.je/travel/informationadvice/importsexports/customstradersagents/pages/classifications.aspx) warns that tariff codes can change during a year. Within-union duty relief under [Tariff Order Article 4](https://www.jerseylaw.je/laws/current/ro_117_2019) has origin/duty-discharge conditions and its own cessation clause for equivalent Article 33A(4)(c) Regulations. Article 7's incorporation of UK tariffs separately ceases on Article 33A Regulations unless they expressly continue it. Both cessation questions, full excise/vehicle mechanics and applicable periods remain unverified here; do not infer duty-free status or a tariff amount from a UK shipping address.

## Filing and records

The registration letter establishes return periods, normally quarterly. The [return instructions](https://www.gov.je/TaxesMoney/GST/Businesses/SubmittingReturn/Pages/Return.aspx) put an ordinary return and payment on the last day of the following month; January to March ordinarily ends with a 30 April deadline. Confirm notices and applicable date rules for an actual filing.

[GST Regulation 18](https://www.jerseylaw.je/laws/current/ro_133_2007) requires the relevant supply records for six years after the supply date and import records for six years after importation, with production on demand and paper/electronic retention permitted. Keep the GST account, invoices, adjustments and customs evidence described in the [recordkeeping guidance](https://www.gov.je/TaxesMoney/GST/Businesses/SubmittingReturn/pages/keepingbooksrecords.aspx). Bad-debt claim records have their separate claim-based period, and company accounting records can require longer retention; see je-company-formation.

## Hypothetical worked positions

These positions use stated eligible inputs, domestic supply classifications and the captured rules. They are reference arithmetic, not evidence of consumer execution or a complete return.

1. **Ordinary sale and net tax.** A 2026 domestic standard-rated sale of GBP 100 excluding GST has GBP 5 GST and GBP 105 gross. Separately, GBP 1,000 output less GBP 400 established deductible input gives GBP 600 before other adjustments. Sources: business guidance and return instructions above.
2. **Test A equality.** In an established qualifying test period, domestic exempt sales GBP 5,000 / total sales GBP 100,000 = 5%. With all other conditions established, this passes the no-more-than-5% test. GBP 5,001 exempt sales fails A; it does not by itself fail B. Source: partial-exemption guidance.
3. **Both B limits.** After A fails, assume a qualifying 12-month calculation with total eligible input GBP 12,000, exempt-attributed input GBP 2,400 and no other adjustment/exclusion. GBP 2,400 / 12 = GBP 200 monthly; GBP 2,400 / GBP 12,000 = 20%. Both limits pass, allowing GBP 12,000 recovery under the stated conditions. GBP 3,000 exempt input instead gives GBP 250 monthly and 25%, passing both at equality. Source: partial-exemption guidance.
4. **Failure of one B limit.** In the same qualifying annual method, GBP 3,600 exempt input / 12 = GBP 300 monthly, against GBP 14,400 total input. The ratio is 25%, but the GBP 250 monthly limit fails. Full recovery is unavailable; the ordinarily attributed taxable input is GBP 10,800 before other adjustments. Source: partial-exemption guidance.
5. **Exact residual proportion.** A qualifying later GST year below the turnover limit has GBP 800,000 wholly domestic taxable sales and GBP 200,000 ordinary exempt sales. Residual input GBP 10,000 x 80% = GBP 8,000 attributable to taxable supplies. Add GBP 8,000 wholly taxable input and GBP 2,000 wholly exempt input: exempt input is GBP 4,000 annually, above GBP 250 a month on average. B fails; recovery is GBP 16,000 before other adjustments. There are no imports, connected persons, capital adjustments or special directions, and no percentage rounding is needed. Source: partial-exemption guidance.
6. **Personal relief does not cover business imports.** An established qualifying personal import valued at GBP 60 passes Direction 2026/03's value limit. The same GBP 60 value imported for business fails that personal relief. Once ordinary import classification/value and no other relief are established, 5% x GBP 60 = GBP 3 GST. This supplies no customs/excise amount. Sources: Direction 2026/03, Article 32 and business/import guidance.

7. **Unpaid-input reversal and later restoration.** Assume the full GBP 50 input deduction on an ordinary GBP 1,050 inclusive supply was claimed, GBP 630 was paid before the established relevant-period end, and no special scheme or other adjustment applies. Unpaid consideration is GBP 420; reversal is GBP 50 x GBP 420 / GBP 1,050 = GBP 20. After the required adjustment return/payment, a later GBP 210 payment restores GBP 20 x GBP 210 / GBP 420 = GBP 10 in that payment period. Supply/due dates, allocation and all eligibility conditions are established assumptions. Sources: Article 36A and Direction 2013/01.

## Intake, holds and reviewer checks

Obtain registration/notices, tax points, invoices and payment ageing, supply-by-supply classification/location, recovery evidence, rolling/year sales values, taxable/exempt/residual inputs, first-year projections, connections, imports, capital purchases and approved methods. Missing facts remain unknown; an absent invoice, import payment or approval does not become a credit or zero liability.

The reviewer brief must show source/version, classification, value/tax point, input attribution, adjustments, held items and return-box reconciliation. Recheck both B limits, all exclusions, unpaid-input timing, import treatment, excess-credit conditions and the same figures across outputs.
