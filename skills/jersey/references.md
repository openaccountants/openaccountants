## Material source decisions

- Annual parish rates use the original 2026 committee table cells and selected parish minutes. Nine located decisions agree; St Brelade, Grouville and St Ouen records are labelled drafts. Original St Clement, St John and Trinity decisions remain unlocated. St Helier percentage descriptions differ, so only the supported absolute rate is used. The blocked R.105/2026 PDF is not treated as inspected merely because generated HTML was accessible.
- Rates Law Articles 16–19 distinguish assessed quarters, owner/occupier roles and consequent island-wide components. Furnished letting and failure to identify an occupier have separate recovery conditions; the latter excludes surcharge. Actual/deemed service under Article 3 controls over the June 2025 guide's date-sent shorthand. The corporate forty-eight-hour condition in Article 23 concerns Assembly membership, not every representation under Article 29.
- Article 1(2) permits nearest-thousandth-penny rounding; no general final GBP method is selected. Supplements, exempt roles, hardship awards, revised demands and refund-interest instruments need their own evidence. The official current title search found the Rates Law and Apportionment Regulations, without establishing complete coverage of differently named instruments.

- GST classification uses Articles 48/49 and complete Schedules 5/6, with their conditions. Qualifying pharmaceutical-benefit supplies are zero-rated under Schedule 6 paragraph 3; the customer page's exempt classification is not used. Article 36(2) also contains specific exempt cross-border/intermediary recovery branches, whose required directions remain unverified.
- Direction 2008/15 adopts the partial-exemption booklet. The 2010 booklet says round up but gives inconsistent examples and historical 3% figures; current HTML says nearest whole number. Nonintegral percentage recovery is held. Exact-integer examples establish neither a general rounding rule nor all import/capital/connected-person cases.
- Article 36A uses six months after the later of supply and payment-due date; Article 54 supplier relief instead has its own six-month-from-supply and last-write-off claim conditions. Direction 2013/01 supplies reversal/restoration/allocation formulas, but its old cash-scheme reference requires the current replacement. Regulations 14/18 distinguish three-year credit claims, supply/import record dates and other claim-based retention. Retail and ISE summaries do not replace the operative direction/regulatory conditions.
- MCIT uses the 2026 Law plus its separate 2025 version, Threshold Order and selected Model Rules. Condition B uses Article 12(1)(d) before loss relief and a strict GBP 100,000 boundary; annual election does not remove registration. Condition A averages current and two preceding fiscal years with the statutory modifications, distinct from the two-of-four global revenue scope test. Credits reduce tax after the income/loss base; the blended-CFC cap is group-level.
- The MCIT registration page omits the sole-intermediate-parent qualification in one summary, and its IIR summary is narrower than the complete statutory qualifying-entity branches. The Laws control. The final payment is actual liability less instalment paid, and nominal calendar examples do not establish a holiday-adjusted deadline. IIR notification/GIR/return periods remain separate from MCIT. Foreign GIR relief requires actual conforming filing and an effective qualifying exchange agreement for the reporting year.
- The technical page retains the older securitisation definition; the 2026 statutory reference is used. Its JPUT concession ends before 31 December 2026 and proposed 2027 legislation is not treated as enacted. The employee-benefit-trust easement is conditional. The OECD record establishes only dated transitional IIR recognition current to 1 May 2026. No unverified interest rate, QRTC instrument or safe-harbour computation is supplied.
- Formation uses the JFSC current process and final 2026 fee notice. Positioned native page 3 text verifies the annual fee/levy row associations; the undefined ordinarily administered base-fee category is not resolved from the levy. Disclosure Regulation 7's class O-only exception applies to the levy. Fees are not projected into 2027.
- CHWL Order Article 5(4) requires the resident sole-trader/one-member-company, eligible status, sole-worker and less-than-eight-hours conditions cumulatively. Detailed guidance omits the one-member condition. Status Regulation 24 replaces Registered with Standard from 14 September 2026; Standard does not satisfy that exemption. Other licence/staffing/exemption branches remain separate.
- The 15 October corporate additions use Income Tax Law Articles 3AF, 70, 70E, 70F, 106A to 106C, 107, 107A, 108, 110B, 123F, 123P and 123S. The regulatory-compliance enhancement is restricted to the Article 3AF eligible entity and activities; it is not an ordinary-business IT allowance. - Company payments use Articles 39 and 41AB. The current guidance describes a strict GBP 500,000 threshold and a different look-back year; the statute includes equality and refers to the assessment year in which the instalment is payable. Its second historical example changes the final assessment-year label and is not used for period mapping.

- Income-tax compliance uses Articles 17A, 23 to 29, 36 to 41AB and 41I within the cited scopes. - Corporate residence uses Article 123. Raw turnover or tax profit does not establish every Schedule 2 income component, and the guide blocks that unsupported mapping.
- ITIS uses the employer guidance's valid-notice rule, 22% missing-rate default and inclusion of LTC. The payroll example's 12% is an assumed valid notice, not a published statutory rate.
- GST retains the earlier domestic business/return/place-of-supply guidance and Direction 2026/03. The personal GBP 60 relief excludes business imports. New S13 sources expand the selected mechanics, with the remaining holds above.
- Accounts use Companies Law Articles 103 to 105, 108 and 113 in the snapshot effective from 30 September 2026. The substance outline uses Articles 3 to 5A; published specialist guidance and partnership law still need investigation.
- Non-resident categories use Income Tax Law Article 118B. Broader generic non-resident wording does not override the statutory dividend-profit conditions or short-term visitor test. - Property v1.1 adds complete ordinary transfer bands and temporary 2026 higher bands, with GBP 100-or-part units and separate fixed fees. - Crypto classification uses the published tax guidance; it does not settle staking, DeFi or licensing. The CARF page combines a 2026 reportable list with conditional implementation wording. Enacted rules must be checked before an operational reporting instruction.
