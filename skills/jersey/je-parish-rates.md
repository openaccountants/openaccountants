# Jersey annual parish and island-wide rates: 2026

## Scope, period and sources

Load workflow-base and read this whole guide before using its rates. It covers annual rates on Jersey land for the rateable year beginning 1 January 2026. A rateable quarter is the statutory measure of assessed value, not GBP market value or a quarter of a calendar year. The parish financial year runs from 1 May to 30 April; it does not replace the January rateable year. Sources were captured on 15 October 2026 UTC, with editing dated 16 October in Australia/Sydney. Establish operative law, any supplementary levy and the actual assessment/demand for the requested year before an engagement calculation.

Do not treat its approximate aggregate quarters as an exact reconciliation or project its figures into 2027.

This guide does not compute transfer stamp duty, LTT, EPTT, probate, income tax or GST. Use je-property-transactions for its supported transaction and estate branches. It does not decide a disputed assessment, value land from a sale price or floor area, determine a court-lease/share-transfer/estate entitlement, agree contractual apportionment or execute a payment.

## Establish the liable person and assessed use

Under Articles 1 and 16–19, separate the foncier (owner) and occupier positions. The persons liable for those rates each bear the assessed amount; one person who is both owner and occupier bears both amounts. The consequent island-wide component follows the applicable owner/occupier liability. Retain each role separately in the working paper.

The statutory definitions control:

- For unlet land, the owner definition includes the person entitled to occupy and use it as owner, usufructuary or under the specified rights. For unlet share-transfer property it names the company owning the land.
- For let land, an occupier who is also lessee under a lease passed before the Royal Court can be the statutory owner. Otherwise the definition names the occupier's immediate landlord. Ordinary title-holder shorthand is insufficient.
- An occupier is a person entitled to occupy/use as owner or under a lease or tenancy, excluding a landlord of the occupier. Check the separate share-transfer definition, including its licence branch. A generic licence-holder statement does not settle every share-transfer case.

Articles 17 and 18 establish liability by the position at the start of the rateable year. The government guidance confirms that a later sale or move does not apportion the parish's annual charge between successive parties. A private sale/lease agreement may allocate costs between them; record that separately from statutory liability.

For a furnished letting, Article 18(3) makes the person who let the premises liable for the occupier's rate, including consequent island-wide liability. The letting terms may allow recovery of all or part from the occupier. Under Article 18(4), an owner's failure to supply the occupier's name/address as required by Article 3(1)(a) makes the owner liable for that component; the recoverable civil debt excludes any surcharge. Reconcile liability with the actual demand without inferring that the occupier's liability is extinguished or charging the same occupier component twice. Establish which branch applies before charging or allocating a recovery.

Use the Rates List's assessed quarters and domestic/non-domestic split. Article 1 defines domestic purposes as wholly or mainly use as a private dwelling; Articles 2, 5, 6 and 19 allow assessment of parts. The June 2025 guidance distinguishes an incidental home study from a separately identifiable business area. Do not invent a floor-area percentage or use an income-tax residence category to replace the actual rates assessment. Unresolved use or specialist assessment requires Needs Input.

Articles 16 and 19 give, for each liable role and assessed part: parish amount = assessed quarters × parish pence rate ÷ 100; island-wide amount = assessed quarters × applicable island-wide pence rate ÷ 100. The results are GBP. Sum the supported parts and then the liable roles, preserving parish and island-wide components. An owner-occupier has one owner position and one occupier position.

Articles 20–21 distinguish the Parish Assembly's rate approval from the Supervisory Committee's island-wide calculation. The Apportionment Regulations allocate 55% of the Annual Island-wide Rates Figure to domestic rates and 45% to non-domestic rates. Those are aggregate funding proportions, not discounts, ownership percentages or multipliers to apply again to an individual bill. Article 24 allows a supplementary parish rate. Check the actual year/demand for one; the table does not establish that none exists.

Article 1(2) permits a figure expressed in pence to be rounded to the nearest thousandth of a penny. It does not prescribe a general final GBP two-decimal method. Preserve exact intermediate values and the supported stage, precision and method. Where final presentation, component rounding or an amended demand cannot be reconciled to established authority, use R-JE-RATES-METHOD. Do not silently round up, choose the higher tax or copy a display format as a legal rule.

## Exemptions, demands and corrections

Articles 17(2)–(3) and 18(2)–(2A) have different exemption lists. Foncier branches include specified religious premises/houses, Crown/UK service land, particular public/parochial/education uses and Don Baudains land. Occupier branches are narrower. The States' buildings and predominantly parking land have express exclusions from specified public-use exemptions. A charity label alone does not establish exemption. Record the exact subparagraph and each ownership, occupation and use condition separately. Article 19 consequent liability follows the applicable foncier/occupier liability; do not apply one role's exemption automatically to the other.

Under Article 25, rates are payable on demand. A pending rateable-value appeal does not suspend payment. Gazette publication can prevent non-receipt being an excuse. Instalment arrangements do not themselves remove the Article 27 surcharge: its stated rule adds 10% of the unpaid amount after three months from demand, subject to applicable Regulations and the actual demand. Check service, any amendment, payment allocation and outstanding balance. Do not call the three-month surcharge interval an interest-free statutory due date.

Article 25(6) permits hardship reduction or remission by the Connétable; a request does not establish an award. If the Supervisory Committee amends the assessed value on the Approved Rates List, Article 25(7) requires an amended demand or, where paid, an overpayment refund with interest. The Law states 5% annually or another rate prescribed by Regulations. This guide does not compute refund interest without establishing the applicable rate, dates and method. It does not apply a nonexistent remission or net a pending refund against a demand without authority.

## Returns, notifications, review and appeal

| Obligation or step | Statutory rule and source |
|---|---|
| Annual information notice | Article 3(1): served in December/January for land owned at the start of the rateable year and required attributes/use/occupier details. Article 3(3) deems service on the Gazette publication date. Article 3(6) uses 15 days from actual/deemed service; for the annual paragraph (1) notice, paragraph (7) allows 15 January if later |
| Additional information notice | Articles 3(4)–(6): notice may be served on an owner or occupier at any time; the annual 15 January extension does not automatically apply |
| Reassessment request | Article 5(1)(d): owner requests by the end of January, with reasons. Article 5(2) requires relevant assessments before the end of May or a later date the Supervisory Committee approves |
| Inspection and ordinary review | Articles 7 and 9: Rates List available for fourteen consecutive days; advertised ordinary review closing date is fourteen days after first availability. Use the approved/provided form. The Committee may allow a later date for an exceptional reason |
| Rateable-value review ground | Article 9(3)–(5): comparison with land of similar attributes; claimed difference at least the greater of 10% of the subject land's rateable value and 500 quarters. The Article 6(3) specialist-land exception is separate. Article 9(6) allows factual/typographical/similar corrections at any time |
| Decision and appeal | Article 10(8): fourteen days from receipt of the decision notice, or a longer period allowed for an exceptional reason by the specified Board authority. Article 45 requires the preceding review route, approved appeal form and grounds, with the appeal notice sent to the Supervisory Committee; it is not an unrestricted initial appeal |
| Board process | Articles 46–48: at least seven days' written hearing notice to interested persons; the Board can confirm/alter the review and its decision is final under Article 46(12). Do not infer that every other legal remedy is available or excluded |
| Ownership transfer | Article 12: both transferor and acquirer give the Connétable written identifying land/party/address particulars within seven days |
| Name or notice-address change | Article 13: a start-of-year owner/occupier gives the specified written particulars within seven days of the change |

Retain the actual notice, publication, first-inspection and receipt dates; verify service and time-computation rules before assigning an engagement deadline. The June 2025 guide's annual-return summary refers to the date sent; Article 3's actual/deemed-service rules control. Factual-error correction, reassessment, ordinary review and Board appeal are distinct procedures.

Under Articles 29–30, keep body-corporate and joint-ratepayer representation evidence. A joint-representative notice received after approval takes effect in the next rateable year; joint and several liability remains. Article 23(1)(d)'s forty-eight-hour corporate-list condition concerns Parish Assembly membership; do not add it as a general delay to every Article 29 representation. This guide does not submit a notice or act as a representative.

## Hypothetical worked positions

These are fabricated calendar-2026 facts, not taxpayer records or a finding that an actual property qualifies. In every monetary example, the reviewer has established the stated assessed quarters/use, liable role(s), year, published ordinary rates and absence of an applicable exemption, supplementary rate, remission or different operative provision. The amounts end in exact GBP pennies unless expressly held. No example selects an unsupported rounding policy.

| Position | Fully worked components and result | Required distinction |
|---|---|---|
| St Helier, 10,000 domestic quarters, one owner role | Parish 10,000 × 1.37p = GBP 137; island-wide 10,000 × 0.96p = GBP 96; **GBP 233** | One role; a separate liable occupier has a separate position |
| Same property, one person is owner and occupier | Owner 137 + 96 = 233; occupier 137 + 96 = 233; **GBP 466** | Both roles, with parish/island-wide components retained |
| St Saviour, 10,000 non-domestic quarters, one liable role | Parish 10,000 × 1.59p = 159; island-wide 10,000 × 1.41p = 141; **GBP 300** | Non-domestic island-wide rate, not the domestic rate |
| St Clement, assessed parts 5,000 domestic and 3,000 non-domestic quarters, one role | Parish 8,000 × 1.31p = 104.80; island-wide 5,000 × 0.96p + 3,000 × 1.41p = 48 + 42.30 = 90.30; **GBP 195.10** | Use the evidenced assessed split; do not allocate market value |
| St Brelade, 10,000 domestic quarters; ordinary owner lets furnished premises and is the person liable under Article 18(3) | Foncier 149 + 96 = 245; occupier component payable by that landlord 149 + 96 = 245; **GBP 490** to parish. If the evidenced terms allow recovery of the whole occupier component, that recovery is **GBP 245** | Contractual recovery is separate from statutory payment; it is not an automatic recovery of the total 490 |
| St Lawrence, 8,000 domestic quarters; January owner-occupier sells in July | Per role: 8,000 × (1.30p + 0.96p) = 104 + 76.80 = 180.80; both roles **GBP 361.60** for the year | Sale does not halve the annual statutory charge; any private apportionment is separate |
| Established demand GBP 466, paid GBP 346; the applicable three-month surcharge interval has expired, no remission or different applicable rule | Unpaid 466 − 346 = 120; surcharge 120 × 10% = 12; outstanding **GBP 132** | Apply to the verified unpaid balance, not the original 466 |
| St Mary, one domestic quarter, one role; final monetary method not established | Exact parish 1.95p plus island-wide 0.96p = 2.91p = GBP 0.0291; **no final payable amount** | R-JE-RATES-METHOD; do not silently select GBP 0.03 |

The first seven positions follow Articles 16–19 and 27 with the cited 2026 rates. The last is a method hold, not a rounded liability. Synthetic arithmetic checks do not establish the facts, eligibility, portal results, consumer behaviour or a recalculated financial workbook.

| Ambiguity | Permitted factual default |
|---|---|

| Unknown rounding, disputed valuation/allocation, refund-interest method or private cost apportionment | None; R-JE-RATES-METHOD |

No cash floor permits a factual default. Follow the base's confirmation and question limits. More than ten required ambiguity questions triggers R-JE-RATES-INPUTS; obtain a consolidated assessment/demand evidence set. When a refusal fires, follow the base stop and scope-trace rules. A refusal before computation does not produce a partial return or require completed normal outputs.
