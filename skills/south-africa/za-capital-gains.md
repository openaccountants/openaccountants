## Who this applies to

This reference covers South African capital gains tax (CGT) under the Eighth Schedule to the Income Tax Act 58 of 1962, in rand, for natural persons, special trusts, other trusts, companies and non-residents. CGT is not a separate tax at a separate rate: a percentage of the gain, the inclusion rate, enters taxable income and is taxed at the taxpayer's income tax rate (section 26A and paragraph 10).

It assumes the amount is capital. It does not decide whether an amount is capital or revenue, and trading stock and other revenue amounts are outside it. SARS years of assessment for natural persons and special trusts run from 1 March to the last day of February, and SARS names a year by the year it ends in: the 2027 year of assessment is 1 March 2026 to 28 February 2027, written 2026/27 here. This guide's coverage year is 2026/27, and 2025/26 is shown wherever the amounts applied for that year need comparing.

### How to read the 2026/27 amounts

Treat every 2026/27 amount in this guide as **held**, using the status words below. A held amount may be used only in a conditional working, side by side with the enacted amount, and only with a reviewer.

- **Enacted** means the amount is in the Eighth Schedule text as read.
- **Announced** means Budget Review 2026, tabled on 25 February 2026, proposes the amount.
- **Billed** means the Rates and Monetary Amounts and Amendment of Revenue Laws Bill, B34-2026, introduced on 21 October 2026 (proposed section 77), contains the amount. A Bill is not law and its text can change.
- **Provisionally effective** is a reading, not a settled fact. Paragraphs 5(3), 45(1A) and 57(7) let an alteration announced in the national annual budget take effect on the date the announcement gives, for 12 months, subject to Parliament passing legislation giving effect to it within those 12 months. If Table 4.6 of Budget Review 2026 is such an announcement, the amounts run from 1 March 2026 to about 28 February 2027 on that condition.

Do not write 'the law is R50,000' or any equivalent for any 2026/27 amount.

## The base

### Order of calculation

Work in this order. The legacy formula here omitted the assessed capital loss and misnamed the result.

1. **Each disposal.** A capital gain is proceeds above base cost; a capital loss is base cost above proceeds (paras 3 and 4). Apply exclusions that disregard a gain or loss first: primary residence (para 45), small business (para 57) and the others in Part VIII.
2. **Aggregate capital gain or aggregate capital loss.** Capital gains less capital losses less the annual exclusion for a natural person or special trust (paras 6 and 7). The annual exclusion applies to gains and losses, and any unused part is not carried forward.
3. **Net capital gain.** Aggregate capital gain less the assessed capital loss brought forward from the previous year, plus any amount brought back by para 64B(3) (para 8). If the assessed capital loss is the larger, the balance carries forward (para 9).
4. **Taxable capital gain.** Net capital gain × the para 10 inclusion rate.
5. **Tax.** Section 26A includes the taxable capital gain in taxable income, taxed at the taxpayer's rate: up to 45% for natural persons, giving 18% at most.

### Disposal and timing

An event is a disposal under para 11, and SARS's examples include sale, donation, exchange, loss, death and emigration. Para 12 deems some disposals at market value, such as when a person starts to be a resident or an asset moves into trading stock.

Time of disposal decides the year, and it may decide which amount applies. Under para 13(1)(a):

- a change of ownership under an unconditional agreement falls when the agreement is concluded;
- under a suspensive condition, it falls when the condition is satisfied; and
- a donation falls when all legal requirements for a valid donation are met, and other events have their own dates in para 13.

SARS's Budget 2026 questions page says the liability on property arises when the sale agreement is signed. That is correct only for an unconditional agreement. Read the agreement for any suspensive condition and the date it was met.

### Annual exclusion and year of death

Each natural person and special trust gets the annual exclusion: R40,000 enacted, R50,000 held for 2026/27. The legacy text said 'individual'; para 5 also gives it to a special trust within paragraph (a) of the section 1 definition (para 1). In the year a person dies, the R300,000 amount replaces it (R440,000 held for 2026/27). The deemed disposal on death (section 9HA(1)) and disposals by a deceased estate (para 40(3)), both seen only through SARS's comprehensive guide, are not covered here.

Spouses each have their own annual exclusion. For spouses married in community of property, para 14 treats a disposal of a joint estate asset as made in equal shares. SARS's ITR12 guide says full amounts are declared and SARS applies the 50% apportionment, excluding the annual exclusion.

### Primary residence exclusion

A natural person or special trust disregards the gain or loss up to the limit, or the whole gain if proceeds are within the limit. See the two limbs in the table. In this Schedule a special trust means only a trust within paragraph (a) of the section 1 definition (para 1), so it is narrower than the 40% inclusion rate, which covers a special trust as section 1 defines it. All of these conditions apply.

- **Interest held by the person or special trust.** 'An interest' means a real or statutory right, a direct share in a share block company or similar non-resident entity, or a right of use or occupation. It excludes a right in a trust or its assets, other than the right of a lessee who is not a connected person in relation to that trust. So someone whose only holding is ordinary company shares, or a right in an ordinary trust, holds no interest for this purpose. A direct share in a share block company, or a share or interest in a similar non-resident entity, does count (para 44).
- **Ordinary residence.** The person, a beneficiary of the special trust or a spouse of either must ordinarily reside there as a main residence and use it mainly for domestic purposes (para 44). 'Residence' means any structure, including a boat, caravan or mobile home, used as a place of residence.
- **One residence at a time.** Only one residence counts as primary for a person or special trust for any period, subject to para 48 (para 45(3)). The legacy line 'per married couple' is not in the text: the rule is per person, and where more than one person jointly holds an interest the amount is apportioned to each interest (para 45(2)). Two spouses each owning half of one home share one limit, half each.
- **Location.** The legacy condition 'Property must be in South Africa' is not in paras 44 to 50. It has been removed as a condition. How the exclusion works for a home abroad, including interaction with foreign tax, is held.
- **Land.** The exclusion covers land, including unconsolidated adjacent land, only so far as it does not exceed 2 hectares, is used mainly for domestic or private purposes with the residence, and is disposed of at the same time and to the same person as the residence (para 46).
- **Proceeds limb off-switches.** Para 45(1)(b) does not apply if the person, a beneficiary or a spouse of either was not ordinarily resident throughout the period from the valuation date, or if any part was used for trade (para 45(4)).
- **Periods away.** Para 45(1)(a) applies only to the part of the gain or loss attributable to periods on or after the valuation date in which the person, beneficiary or spouse ordinarily resided there (para 47). Para 48 treats a continuous period of up to 2 years as ordinary residence in listed cases: offered for sale and vacated for a new primary residence, while being built, accidentally uninhabitable, or on death.
- **Trade use.** If all or part was used for a trade, para 45(1)(a) applies only to the part of the gain or loss attributable to domestic use, by time and by extent (para 49). The legacy line 'partly used for business' stays, with this limit. Para 50 treats a continuous absence of up to 5 years whilst the residence is let as domestic use, if the person resided there for at least a year before and after, no other residence was treated as primary, and the person was temporarily absent from South Africa or working in South Africa more than 250 km away.

Paras 51 and 51A offered time-limited transfers out of companies and trusts, and expired on 30 September 2010 and 31 December 2012. Do not apply them now.

### Small business disposal relief

A natural person disregards a gain on the disposal of any of these (para 57(2)):

- an active business asset of a small business held as a sole proprietor;
- an interest in each active business asset of a partnership's small business, on withdrawal; or
- an entire direct interest of at least 10% of a company's equity, to the extent it relates to active business assets of its small business.

All of these conditions apply. The legacy phrase 'in contemplation of retirement' is not in the text and has been replaced.

- The person held the asset or interest for own benefit for a continuous period of at least 5 years before the disposal, and was substantially involved in the business's operations for that period.
- The person has attained age 55, **or** the disposal is in consequence of ill-health, other infirmity, superannuation or death. So age 55 is not always required.
- 'Small business' tests the market value of **all the assets of the business** at the disposal date, not the asset sold.
- 'Active business asset' means immovable property to the extent used for business, or another asset used or held wholly and exclusively for business. It excludes financial instruments and assets held mainly for annuity, rental, foreign exchange gain, royalty or similar income.
- All qualifying gains must be realised within 24 months from the first disposal (para 57(4)).
- Several businesses may be included (para 57(5)), but relief is lost if total market value across them exceeds the para 57(6) limit.
- The relief disregards gains only, not losses, and it is separate from the annual exclusion, which still applies.

A registered micro business under the Sixth Schedule disregards a gain or loss on an asset used mainly for business (para 57A). Eligibility was not reviewed.

### Capital losses

A capital loss reduces capital gains only. An assessed capital loss cannot be set off against ordinary income. SARS's comprehensive guide says it carries forward and 'in theory' can do so indefinitely, and that section 103(2) should act as a deterrent to trafficking in such losses, so the legacy word 'indefinitely' now carries that condition.

Some losses are limited or ignored:

- **Clogged losses.** A loss on a disposal to a connected person can be used only against gains from disposals to the same person, in that or a later year, whilst that person still qualifies as a connected person (para 39, seen only through SARS's comprehensive guide).
- **Personal-use assets.** A capital loss, but not a gain, is disregarded to the extent the asset is used otherwise than in a trade, for an aircraft above 450 kg, a boat over 10 metres, a fiduciary, usufructuary or similar interest that declines in value, a lease of immovable property, a fixed-life time-sharing interest or share block share, and rights in any of these (para 15).
- **Other limits.** Paras 16 to 19 limit losses on certain intangible assets, forfeited deposits, options and shares.
- **Ring-fenced assessed losses.** SARS's ITR12 guide says section 20A(6)(b) of the Income Tax Act permits a ring-fenced assessed loss to be set off against the taxable capital gain once the trade has ceased and the asset used in it is disposed of. Primary text was not read.

### Base cost

The legacy line 'generally the cost of acquisition including incidental costs' is correct only as a start. Para 20(1) lists the expenditure actually incurred that counts:

- acquisition or creation cost, and the cost of valuing the asset for this purpose;
- listed incidental costs, such as professional fees, transfer costs, stamp duty, transfer duty, securities transfer tax, advertising, moving and installation costs;
- amounts to establish, maintain or defend legal title, and improvement expenditure; and
- one-third of interest on money used to buy listed shares or collective investment scheme interests, and other listed items.

Para 20(2) excludes borrowing costs (other than that one-third item), repairs, maintenance, protection, insurance, rates and taxes and similar expenditure. Para 20(3) reduces the para 20(1)(a) to (g) expenditure by three kinds of amount. First, amounts allowable or deemed allowed as a deduction, unless included in taxable income under section 9C(5). Second, amounts reduced, recovered, recoverable or paid by another person, unless taken into account as a recoupment under section 8(4)(a) or paragraph (j) of the gross income definition, reduced under section 12P, or applied under section 19(3) or para 12A(3). Third, amounts exempt under section 10(1)(yA) and granted or paid to acquire the asset. Paras 24, 25 and 32 can also change the result.

### Assets held before 1 October 2001

Base cost of a pre-valuation date asset is the valuation date value plus para 20 expenditure on or after that date (para 25). The legacy phrase 'valuation date election' is shorthand: the methods depend on the facts, and a person does not always have a free choice.

- **Proceeds exceed total para 20 expenditure:** adopt any of market value under para 29, 20% of proceeds after deducting post-valuation expenditure, or time-apportionment under para 30 (para 26(1)).
- **Pre-valuation expenditure unknown:** only market value or the 20% method (para 26(2)).
- **Market value adopted and proceeds do not exceed it:** valuation date value is proceeds less post-valuation expenditure (para 26(3)).
- **Proceeds do not exceed expenditure:** para 27 prescribes the outcome, including higher-of and lower-of rules where a market value was determined. There is no free choice.
- **Excluded assets:** paras 26(1) and 27 do not apply to an asset under para 28, which gives its own valuation date value for an instrument as defined in section 24J, or to an asset on which para 32(3A) has been applied.

Market value is available only if the person valued the asset on or before 30 September 2004, or the price was gazetted, or it came from a spouse under section 9HB who adopted it (para 29(4)(a)). For a valuation date after 1 October 2001, para 29(4)(b) sets its own conditions, including a valuation within 2 years. A person must also have furnished proof with the first return after that period for an asset above R10 million, an intangible above R1 million, or unlisted shares above R10 million in the same company (para 29(5)). Retain proof of every valuation (para 29(6)). SARS may adjust a value it does not accept (para 29(7)).

Time-apportionment with no post-valuation expenditure: Y = B + [(P − B) × N ÷ (N + T)]. B is the pre-valuation expenditure and P the proceeds. N is years from acquisition to the day before the valuation date, limited to 20 if expenditure was incurred in more than one year of assessment before the valuation date. T is years from the valuation date to disposal. A part of a year counts as a full year. Para 30(2) to (5) change P for post-valuation expenditure, allowances and selling costs.

### Collective investment schemes

For distributions on or after 1 March 2026, a distribution by a collective investment scheme portfolio that is not gross income is treated as a capital gain for the holder in the year it accrues (para 82A, inserted by Act 5 of 2026 section 34; enacted). Para 61(1) was made subject to it, for disposals on or after that date. Para 82A is new to this guide and was read for that wording only.

## Penalties and interest

No general CGT penalty rate is stated here, because the Tax Administration Act chapters were not read.

- **Section 35A.** SARS's guide says a purchaser who pays the withheld amount late owes interest at the prescribed rate from the day after the last payment date, and a penalty equal to 10% of that amount, in addition to any other penalty. No provision waives the interest.
- **Interest on late or underpaid tax.** SARS's Budget 2026 questions page gave 10.25% a year from 2 March 2026. Later rate changes were not checked.
- **Provisional taxpayers.** A capital gain feeds the provisional tax estimate. See [za-provisional-tax](za-provisional-tax.md).

## Cross-border points

**Residents** are taxed on the disposal of any asset, wherever it is (para 2(1)(a)). Foreign disposals go in the ITR12 under the foreign codes, and a foreign tax credit is claimed through the return (code 4114). Currency conversion (para 43) and section 6quat were not reviewed here. Becoming or ceasing to be a resident has its own deemed disposal rules: use [za-tax-residency](za-tax-residency.md) and [leaving-south-africa-tax-emigration](leaving-south-africa-tax-emigration.md), which cover section 9H.

**Non-residents** are taxed only on (para 2(1)(b)):

- immovable property in South Africa, or an interest or right in it, including rights to minerals; and
- any asset effectively connected with a permanent establishment in South Africa.

**Indirect interests in South African property.** The legacy statement that non-residents are 'generally NOT taxed' on South African shares needs this condition. Equity shares in a company, ownership in another entity or a vested interest in trust assets count as an interest in immovable property if these tests are met at disposal (para 2(2)):

- 80% or more of the market value of the shares or interest is attributable, directly or indirectly, to South African immovable property or an interest or right in it, including mineral rights; and
- for a company or other entity, the person, alone or with connected persons, directly or indirectly holds at least 20% of the equity or ownership. The 20% test is not written for a vested trust interest.

Shares that fail either test, and other movable assets not connected with a permanent establishment, stay outside para 2(1)(b). Para 2 applies subject to para 97, which was not read, and a double tax agreement may change the answer. No agreement was read.

**Section 35A withholding.** A purchaser from a non-resident seller of South African immovable property withholds at the rates in the table and pays SARS within 14 days if the purchaser is resident, or 28 days if not, using declaration NR02. The seller may ask SARS for a directive (NR03). The amount is an advance against the seller's normal tax. It applies to the full price once the aggregate exceeds R2 million. A deposit paid to secure the disposal until the agreement becomes unconditional is not withheld from; what would have been withheld comes off the first later payments. Whether it reaches a share sale under para 2(2) was not stated in SARS's guide; held.

## Two worked examples that recompute

All examples are hypothetical. Examples 2 and 3 show enacted and held outcomes side by side, and a held case is not a statement of law. Both assume other taxable income above R1,878,600, which is above the top threshold in Act 3 and in B34-2026, so 45% applies either way. A lower income gives a lower rate.

### Example 1: enacted amounts, 2025/26, loss order

A resident natural person has capital gains of R300,000 and capital losses of R20,000, with an assessed capital loss of R30,000 brought forward. Other taxable income is above R1,817,000. No exclusion applies other than the annual exclusion.

```oa-check
name: ZA 2025/26 enacted calculation order with assessed capital loss
inputs:
  gains: 300000
  losses: 20000
  annual_exclusion: 40000
  assessed_loss_brought_forward: 30000
  inclusion_percent: 40
  marginal_percent: 45
steps:
  - aggregate_gain: gains - losses - annual_exclusion
  - net_gain: aggregate_gain - assessed_loss_brought_forward
  - taxable_gain: pct(net_gain, inclusion_percent)
  - tax: pct(taxable_gain, marginal_percent)
expected:
  aggregate_gain: 240000
  net_gain: 210000
  taxable_gain: 84000
  tax: 37800
```

Decision point: the annual exclusion comes before the assessed capital loss. If gains are R60,000 with no current losses, the aggregate gain is R20,000, net capital gain is nil, and R10,000 of assessed capital loss carries forward.

```oa-check
name: ZA 2025/26 assessed capital loss carried forward after annual exclusion
inputs:
  gains: 60000
  annual_exclusion: 40000
  assessed_loss_brought_forward: 30000
steps:
  - aggregate_gain: gains - annual_exclusion
  - net_gain: max(aggregate_gain - assessed_loss_brought_forward, 0)
  - loss_carried_forward: max(assessed_loss_brought_forward - aggregate_gain, 0)
expected:
  aggregate_gain: 20000
  net_gain: 0
  loss_carried_forward: 10000
```

### Example 2: primary residence, timing and the held amount

One owner sells a home for R6,000,000 with a base cost of R2,500,000. They ordinarily resided there throughout, with no trade use, and have no other gains.

- **Case A, enacted amounts:** the agreement was unconditional and concluded before 1 March 2026, or the alteration does not apply.
- **Case B, held amounts:** disposal falls in 2026/27 and the alterations apply, provisionally or after enactment.

```oa-check
name: ZA primary residence enacted case A and held case B
inputs:
  proceeds: 6000000
  base_cost: 2500000
  residence_limit_a: 2000000
  residence_limit_b: 3000000
  annual_exclusion_a: 40000
  annual_exclusion_b: 50000
  inclusion_percent: 40
  marginal_percent: 45
steps:
  - gain: proceeds - base_cost
  - remaining_a: gain - min(gain, residence_limit_a)
  - aggregate_a: max(remaining_a - annual_exclusion_a, 0)
  - tax_a: pct(pct(aggregate_a, inclusion_percent), marginal_percent)
  - remaining_b: gain - min(gain, residence_limit_b)
  - aggregate_b: max(remaining_b - annual_exclusion_b, 0)
  - tax_b: pct(pct(aggregate_b, inclusion_percent), marginal_percent)
  - difference: tax_a - tax_b
expected:
  gain: 3500000
  aggregate_a: 1460000
  tax_a: 262800
  aggregate_b: 450000
  tax_b: 81000
  difference: 181800
```

Decision point: R181,800 turns on the time of disposal under para 13 and on the status of the held amounts. Get the agreement and the date any suspensive condition was met. Do not report Case B as the answer. Show both until a reviewer signs off.

### Example 3: small business above the enacted cap

A sole proprietor aged 57 disposes of qualifying active business assets at a gain of R3,000,000. They held them for at least 5 years, were substantially involved, have never used para 57, and the business's assets have a market value of R12,000,000.

- **Case A, enacted amounts:** the disposal falls before 1 March 2026, or the alteration does not apply. R12,000,000 exceeds R10 million, so this is not a small business and para 57 gives nothing.
- **Case B, held amounts:** the disposal falls in 2026/27 and the alterations apply, provisionally or after enactment. R12,000,000 is within R15 million, and R2,700,000 is disregarded.

```oa-check
name: ZA small business enacted case A and held case B
inputs:
  gain: 3000000
  disregarded_a: 0
  disregarded_b: 2700000
  annual_exclusion_a: 40000
  annual_exclusion_b: 50000
  inclusion_percent: 40
  marginal_percent: 45
steps:
  - aggregate_a: gain - disregarded_a - annual_exclusion_a
  - tax_a: pct(pct(aggregate_a, inclusion_percent), marginal_percent)
  - aggregate_b: gain - disregarded_b - annual_exclusion_b
  - tax_b: pct(pct(aggregate_b, inclusion_percent), marginal_percent)
  - difference: tax_a - tax_b
expected:
  aggregate_a: 2960000
  tax_a: 532800
  aggregate_b: 250000
  tax_b: 45000
  difference: 487800
```

Decision point: the whole relief depends on a held amount here. At a market value of R10 million or less, para 57 applies on enacted law, up to R1.8 million.

### Example 4: asset bought in 1991, sold in 2026

A person bought land on 1 October 1991 for R400,000, with one amount of expenditure and none after. They sell in June 2026 for R5,000,000, so N is 10 and T is 25 (part-year counted in full). A valuation of R1,100,000 was made by 30 September 2004. Para 26(1) applies because proceeds exceed expenditure, and base cost does not depend on any held amount.

```oa-check
name: ZA pre-valuation date methods under para 26
inputs:
  proceeds: 5000000
  pre_valuation_expenditure: 400000
  years_before: 10
  years_after: 25
  market_value: 1100000
steps:
  - time_apportioned: pre_valuation_expenditure + (proceeds - pre_valuation_expenditure) * years_before / (years_before + years_after)
  - twenty_percent: pct(proceeds, 20)
  - highest: max(time_apportioned, twenty_percent, market_value)
  - gain_highest: proceeds - highest
  - gain_if_expenditure_unknown: proceeds - max(twenty_percent, market_value)
  - gain_if_also_no_valuation: proceeds - twenty_percent
expected:
  time_apportioned: 1714285.71
  twenty_percent: 1000000
  highest: 1714285.71
  gain_highest: 3285714.29
  gain_if_expenditure_unknown: 3900000
  gain_if_also_no_valuation: 4000000
```

Decision point: the evidence decides the method. Without proof of the 1991 cost, para 26(2) removes time-apportionment, which adds R614,285.71 of gain. Without a valuation by 30 September 2004 as well, only 20% is left.

### Example 5: non-resident scope and withholding

A non-resident natural person sells land in South Africa directly for R5,000,000. Separately, another non-resident holds 25% of a company's equity shares. Those shares have a market value of R10,000,000, of which R8,500,000 is attributable to South African immovable property.

```oa-check
name: ZA non-resident withholding and indirect interest test values
inputs:
  price: 5000000
  property_value: 8500000
  company_value: 10000000
steps:
  - natural_person_withholding: pct(price, 7.5)
  - company_seller_withholding: pct(price, 10)
  - trust_seller_withholding: pct(price, 15)
  - property_percent: property_value / company_value * 100
expected:
  natural_person_withholding: 375000
  company_seller_withholding: 500000
  trust_seller_withholding: 750000
  property_percent: 85
```

Decision point: the land sale is in scope, and R375,000 is an advance, not the tax. The shares meet the 80% and 20% tests, so para 2(1)(b)(i) reaches them, subject to para 97 and any agreement. Para 2(2)(b) counts the holder alone or together with connected persons, directly or indirectly. If they held 15% in total, the 20% test would fail and the shares would fall outside. If the holder had 15% and connected persons another 10%, the combined 25% would pass.

## Ask the client first

Ask these before any calculation. Do not assume an answer.

1. **Who and what.** Is the person disposing a natural person, a special trust (paragraph (a) or (b) of the section 1 definition), another trust, a company or a non-resident? What was disposed of, and is it capital rather than trading stock or revenue?
2. **Dates.** What are the dates of the agreement, any suspensive condition and its fulfilment, and transfer? Does the year of assessment end on the last day of February, or on a substituted date?
3. **Residence.** Was the taxpayer resident at disposal, and throughout the holding period?
4. **Numbers.** What are proceeds and each para 20 cost, with evidence? Are there current losses, connected-person losses or an assessed capital loss brought forward? What other taxable income is there, so the actual rate can be used rather than the maximum?
5. **Older assets.** Was the asset acquired before 1 October 2001? Is there a valuation done by 30 September 2004, and was proof furnished if para 29(5) applies?
6. **Relief facts.** For a home: who holds it, is it held jointly, was it the ordinary residence throughout, was it ever let, was any part used for trade, how much land, and where is it? For a business: market value of all assets of every business, active business asset analysis, holding period, involvement, age or ill-health, other infirmity, superannuation or death, prior para 57 use, and the date of the first disposal. For a non-resident: is it immovable property, a permanent establishment asset or shares; what are the 80% and 20% tests; is there a directive; which double tax agreement applies? For a death: date of death, and how the estate deals with the assets.
