# Morocco transfer pricing v0.1

> **General reference only.** This skill is general tax/accounting reference material for AI-assisted workflows. ## Who this applies to

Any company in Morocco with direct or indirect dependency links with companies in or outside Morocco can have profits adjusted (Art. 213-II). The documentation duty covers only transactions with related companies outside Morocco, and only above the thresholds below (Art. 214-III-A). The country by country report covers multinational groups at the level in Art. 154 ter.

## The base

**Adjustment.** Profits transferred indirectly, through higher or lower purchase or sale prices or by any other means, are added back to the taxable result and or declared turnover (Art. 213-II). The amount is set by comparison with similar companies, or by direct assessment on information the administration holds. The CGI names these two routes; it does not list OECD methods. A foreign company with permanent activity in Morocco can have foreign costs limited where their size is not justified (Art. 213-III). Adjustments follow the procedures in Art. 220 or 221 (Art. 213-IV).

**Documentation.** The file has two parts (Art. 214-III-A): a master file on all related companies' activities, the overall transfer pricing policy and the worldwide split of profits and activities; and a local file on the audited company's transactions with those related companies. It goes to the administration electronically. The list of contents and the terms are set by regulation.

**When.** The documentation is due at the start of the audit: it must be available on the date the accounting audit begins (Art. 210). A related company with no file on that date gets the Art. 219 request and 30 days, then the fine (Art. 185-IV, 214-III-A). For operations with companies outside Morocco, the administration can also ask for further information (Art. 214-III-B). It can also request information from tax authorities of states with an exchange agreement (Art. 214-II).

**Country by country report: who files** (Art. 154 ter). A Moroccan company subject to IS files if it (a) holds stakes abroad that require consolidated accounts, or would if its shares were listed in Morocco; (b) meets the turnover threshold; and (c) has no parent in or outside Morocco. A Moroccan subsidiary files in place of a foreign parent if the parent's state does not require the report, or has no exchange agreement with Morocco, or if the group designates it and it has told the administration (II). It also files when the administration tells it that the parent state has failed on exchange (III). Where several Moroccan group companies are caught under II or III, the group designates one, which tells the administration first (IV).

**Advance agreement** (Art. 234 bis, 234 ter). The administration cannot challenge an agreed method. The agreement is void from its start date if facts were misstated, information hidden, errors or omissions are the company's, the method or duties are not kept, or fraud is used. The administration can use those grounds only through the Art. 220 or 221 procedures.

**Abuse of law** (Art. 213-V). Fictitious operations, or those aimed only at tax benefits against the law's purpose, or at avoiding or reducing tax, can be set aside.

## Deadlines and filing

| Duty | When | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Master and local file | Available on the first day of the audit; missing items within 30 days of the Art. 219 request | CGI Art. 210, 214-III-A |
| Country by country report | Within 12 months of year end, electronically | CGI Art. 154 ter |
| Advance agreement | On request; terms set by regulation | CGI Art. 234 bis |

Keep accounting documents for 10 years (Art. 211; Art. 185 bis sets a MAD 50,000 fine per year for not doing so).

## Penalties and interest

Missing file: 0.5% of the transactions concerned, minimum MAD 200,000 per year (Art. 185-IV). Missing or incomplete country by country report: MAD 500,000 (Art. 199 bis). Tax on an adjustment bears the general surcharges and interest in [company tax](morocco-corporate-income-tax.md).

## Cross-border points

Withholding on payments to non-residents, including related ones, is in [cross-border](ma-cross-border.md). Treaty relief on a Moroccan adjustment is not covered here: see Not yet confirmed.

## Two worked examples that recompute

**Example 1: the minimum fine decides.** A company with turnover of MAD 80 million buys MAD 30 million from its French parent and produces no file after the 30 days. 0.5% gives MAD 150,000, below the minimum, so the fine is MAD 200,000 for that year. With MAD 60 million of purchases the fine would be MAD 300,000.

```oa-check
name: Art. 185-IV fine on MAD 30 million
inputs:
  transactions: 30000000
steps:
  - percent: transactions * 0.005
  - fine: max(percent, 200000)
expected:
  percent: 150000.00
  fine: 200000.00
```

**Example 2: should the company document at all?** Turnover is MAD 40 million but gross assets are MAD 55 million, with related purchases abroad of MAD 12 million. Turnover misses the test; assets pass it, so the file is required. An adjustment of MAD 2 million to the price at the 20% normal rate adds MAD 400,000 of tax before surcharges, and the missing file costs MAD 200,000 more (0.5% of 12 million is 60,000, below the minimum). Assume the year is taxed at 20% ([company tax](morocco-corporate-income-tax.md)).

```oa-check
name: Adjustment tax and missing-file fine
inputs:
  adjustment: 2000000
  transactions: 12000000
steps:
  - tax: adjustment * 0.20
  - fine: max(transactions * 0.005, 200000)
  - cost: tax + fine
expected:
  tax: 400000.00
  fine: 200000.00
  cost: 600000.00
```

## Ask the client first

1. Turnover excl. VAT and gross assets at year end.
2. Which related companies, in which countries, and direct or indirect links.
3. Value of each transaction type with related companies outside Morocco.
4. Is an audit notified, and has an Art. 219 request arrived?
5. Group consolidated turnover, who the ultimate parent is, and whether its state requires the report.
6. Is an advance agreement in place or wanted?
