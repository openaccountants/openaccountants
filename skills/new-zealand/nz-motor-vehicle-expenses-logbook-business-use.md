# Motor vehicle expenses, logbooks and shareholder-employee vehicles in New Zealand

## Scope

This skill covers how a New Zealand business claims the cost of a motor vehicle that is used for both business and private travel. It is written for the 2026-27 tax year (1 April 2026 to 31 March 2027), with a dated section for 2025-26 returns, which are the ones being filed now.

It covers sole traders, partners and companies (including close companies with shareholder-employees): actual costs with a logbook versus Inland Revenue's (IRD's) kilometre rates, the no-logbook limit, depreciation, GST, and how fringe benefit tax (FBT) applies to company vehicles.

It does not cover: working out the FBT payable (the FBT rates and the return itself), an employer paying an employee for using the employee's own car (IRD's OS 19/04B), charities, or vehicles held as trading stock.

**Who pays FBT and who adjusts instead.** IRD says: "If you're a sole trader or a partner of a partnership, you do not pay FBT on business vehicles that you use privately. Instead, you can keep a logbook and make adjustments for private use in your income tax and GST returns" ([IRD: motor vehicles and FBT](https://www.ird.govt.nz/employing-staff/deductions-from-other-payments/fringe-benefit-tax/types/motor-vehicles/about)). A company that makes a vehicle available to an employee, including a shareholder-employee, pays FBT for "every day that a motor vehicle is available for an employee's private use, even if they do not actually use it", unless an exemption applies or a close company opts out (same page).

**Home to work is private.** IRD: "Travelling from home to work is a personal trip" ([IRD: vehicle expenses](https://www.ird.govt.nz/income-tax/income-tax-for-businesses-and-organisations/types-of-business-expenses/claiming-vehicle-expenses)).

## Ask the client first

- Who owns the vehicle: the individual, the partnership, a company or a trust? Whose name is it registered in? Is it owned outright, on hire purchase or leased?
- Is it used only for business? If so, IRD allows the full running costs ([IRD: vehicle expenses](https://www.ird.govt.nz/income-tax/income-tax-for-businesses-and-organisations/types-of-business-expenses/claiming-vehicle-expenses)) and most of this skill is not needed.
- What does it run on: petrol, diesel, hybrid, electric, or something else? Anything else must use actual costs.
- Which method has been used for this vehicle in earlier years (kilometre rates or actual costs)? IRD requires one method for as long as the vehicle is owned.
- Do actual journey records show the business-use proportion, or is there a current logbook? When did the logbook period start, and has any month's business use dropped by at least 20 percentage points?
- Total kilometres for the year (odometer at the start and end of the income year) and business kilometres.
- For actual costs: fuel or charging, road user charges, registration, insurance, Warrant of Fitness, repairs, tyres, parking, finance interest, and the vehicle's cost, the date it was bought and whether it was new or new to New Zealand.
- Is the business GST-registered? What did the vehicle cost excluding GST, and how was GST claimed when it was bought?
- For a company: how many vehicles can shareholder-employees use privately? Does the company give any other fringe benefit? Is it registered for FBT, and how often does it file?
- Was an FBT opt-out already made for this vehicle, and was it made by the due date for the return of the year the vehicle was bought or first used for business?
- Is the vehicle a ute, van or other goods vehicle with permanent signage (possible work-related vehicle exemption)?

## The method, step by step

1. **Decide the regime from the owner.** A sole trader or partnership claims the business share of the vehicle and makes private use adjustments in income tax and GST; no FBT ([IRD: motor vehicles and FBT](https://www.ird.govt.nz/employing-staff/deductions-from-other-payments/fringe-benefit-tax/types/motor-vehicles/about)). A company that lets an employee or shareholder-employee use a vehicle privately is in the FBT rules (step 8) unless it is a close company that validly opts out (step 9).
2. **Check vehicle type and fuel.** Use published kilometre rates only for covered vehicles and fuel types. Inland Revenue publishes rates for petrol, diesel, petrol hybrid and battery electric cars, vans and similar vehicles. Motorcycles and scooters are outside the rates. Use eligible actual costs if no rate applies. Check the election made in the first relevant income tax return and continue with that method for the vehicle. [Inland Revenue OS 19/04a](https://www.taxtechnical.ird.govt.nz/operational-statements/os-1904-a-commissioner-s-statement-on-using-a-kilometre-rate-for-business-running-of-a-motor-vehicle).
3. **Find the business share.** Use actual journey records ([Income Tax Act s DE 5](https://www.legislation.govt.nz/act/public/2007/97/en/latest/sections/DLM1513864/)) or a current representative logbook. Otherwise, check the statutory limit for actual costs.
   - Logbook: keep it "for at least 90 consecutive days", recording the start date and odometer reading, "the date, distance and reason for each business journey", and the end date and odometer reading. Add up business distance and work out its proportion of total distance ([IRD: use a logbook](https://www.ird.govt.nz/income-tax/income-tax-for-businesses-and-organisations/types-of-business-expenses/claiming-vehicle-expenses/use-a-logbook)).
   - A logbook term can run for up to 3 years, measured from the first day of the income year in which the term starts. It ends on the last day of any month when business use is at least 20 percentage points lower than the logbook proportion and the logbook no longer represents average business use. The Commissioner can also require a fresh test. [Income Tax Act s DE 8](https://www.legislation.govt.nz/act/public/2007/97/en/latest/sections/DLM1513871/) and [Income Tax Act s DE 10](https://www.legislation.govt.nz/act/public/2007/97/en/latest/sections/DLM1513876/).
   - If there are no actual records, no current logbook term and no kilometre-rate election, use the lower of demonstrated actual business use and 25% for eligible running costs and depreciation. [Income Tax Act s DE 4](https://www.legislation.govt.nz/act/public/2007/97/en/latest/sections/DLM1513861/)
4. **Actual cost method.** Apply the business share to all vehicle costs, "things like buying petrol, getting a Warrant of Fitness, paying for maintenance and repairs, insurance and parking" ([IRD: use a logbook](https://www.ird.govt.nz/income-tax/income-tax-for-businesses-and-organisations/types-of-business-expenses/claiming-vehicle-expenses/use-a-logbook)). Records must "show the reasons for all business travel, and the distances of all journeys". Add depreciation on the business share: "You can also claim a deduction on any depreciation loss for the business use of your vehicle" ([IRD: vehicle expenses](https://www.ird.govt.nz/income-tax/income-tax-for-businesses-and-organisations/types-of-business-expenses/claiming-vehicle-expenses)). See step 6.
5. **Kilometre rate method.** Multiply business kilometres by IRD's rate for the income year being claimed ("Use the rates for the year you're claiming", [IRD: vehicle expenses](https://www.ird.govt.nz/income-tax/income-tax-for-businesses-and-organisations/types-of-business-expenses/claiming-vehicle-expenses)).
   - Tier 1 covers "the business portion of the first 14,000 kilometres travelled by the vehicle in an income year. This includes private use travel." Tier 2 is "for running costs only" and applies to the business portion "when overall vehicle travel (both business and private) exceeds 14,000 kilometres" ([IRD: kilometre rates 2025-2026](https://www.ird.govt.nz/income-tax/income-tax-for-businesses-and-organisations/types-of-business-expenses/claiming-vehicle-expenses/kilometre-rates-2025-2026)).
   - So: Tier 1 kilometres = business share x the smaller of total kilometres and 14,000; Tier 2 kilometres = business share x total kilometres above 14,000.
   - "Kilometre rates include depreciation. If you use this method, you will not claim a separate depreciation deduction or recovery of depreciation for the vehicle" ([IRD: vehicle expenses](https://www.ird.govt.nz/income-tax/income-tax-for-businesses-and-organisations/types-of-business-expenses/claiming-vehicle-expenses)).
   - The rates for a year are published only after that year ends (see "Figures, by year").
6. **Depreciation (actual cost method only).** Depreciate the vehicle at IRD's general rate, then deduct the business share. For a GST-registered business the depreciation base is the price "less the GST charged"; if not registered, it is "the total price of the asset, including GST" ([IRD: claiming depreciation](https://www.ird.govt.nz/income-tax/income-tax-for-businesses-and-organisations/types-of-business-expenses/depreciation/claiming-depreciation)). A new (or new to New Zealand) vehicle first available on or after 22 May 2025 may qualify for Investment Boost (see the figures). On sale, depreciation recovery is generally the lesser of the excess over adjusted tax value and the recoverable prior deductions, including Investment Boost if applicable ([Income Tax Act s EE 48](https://www.legislation.govt.nz/act/public/2007/97/en/latest/sections/DLM1514660/)), and for a vehicle with private use "you must divide any losses or gains between those uses, in proportion to their use" ([IRD: managing depreciation](https://www.ird.govt.nz/income-tax/income-tax-for-businesses-and-organisations/types-of-business-expenses/depreciation/managing-depreciation)).
7. **GST.** If registered, apply the purchase and running-cost input tax rules. For apportionment, use the taxable-use share, which a logbook can help establish. For eligible purchases costing $10,000 or less excluding GST, the principal-purpose method is also available. Review changes in use and make any required adjustments. Kilometre rates are GST-inclusive. A GST claim must be based on eligible actual costs and supported by taxable supply information. The kilometre-rate calculation itself gives no GST input deduction. [GST Act s 20](https://www.legislation.govt.nz/act/public/1985/141/en/latest/sections/DLM83479/) and [Inland Revenue OS 19/04a](https://www.taxtechnical.ird.govt.nz/operational-statements/os-1904-a-commissioner-s-statement-on-using-a-kilometre-rate-for-business-running-of-a-motor-vehicle).
8. **Company vehicles under FBT.** The company pays FBT on each day the vehicle is available for private use. IRD's close-company page treats FBT and private use adjustments as alternatives: a company that opts out of FBT "must make private use adjustments for income tax and GST" instead ([IRD: close companies and FBT](https://www.ird.govt.nz/employing-staff/deductions-from-other-payments/fringe-benefit-tax/close-companies)). Test the exemptions first (work-related vehicle, emergency call-outs, business travel, unavailable days) using [IRD: FBT exemptions for motor vehicles](https://www.ird.govt.nz/employing-staff/deductions-from-other-payments/fringe-benefit-tax/types/motor-vehicles/exemptions). Value the vehicle by cost price or tax book value, and apply the percentage for the return frequency ([IRD: calculating taxable value](https://www.ird.govt.nz/employing-staff/deductions-from-other-payments/fringe-benefit-tax/types/motor-vehicles/calculating-taxable-value)). A close company may also elect to file income-year FBT returns (see the figures).
9. **Close company opt-out.** A close company with "only 1 or 2 motor vehicles available for private use of shareholder-employees" that does "not provide any other fringe benefits" can opt out and "use the income tax rules for vehicle expenditure instead", making "private use adjustments for income tax and GST" ([IRD: close companies and FBT](https://www.ird.govt.nz/employing-staff/deductions-from-other-payments/fringe-benefit-tax/close-companies)). Tell IRD by "sending us a message in myIR" or "including a note with your paper return". The election "is only valid if it's made by the due date for filing the income tax return in the year a vehicle is acquired or first used for business". After that the company "cannot return to using the FBT rules for that vehicle unless the vehicle is disposed of, or you stop using the vehicle for business use". The election continues until the end of that income year. Cancel any FBT registration if the election is in time. [Income Tax Act s CX 17](https://www.legislation.govt.nz/act/public/2007/97/en/latest/sections/DLM1513367/). Then apply steps 2 to 7 to the vehicle.

## Worked cases

The people and amounts are made up. Rates come from the sources linked in each table.

**Case 1: sole trader, petrol car, kilometre rates, 2025-26 return.** A sole trader drove 18,000 km in 2025-26. A current 90-day logbook started in 2024 shows 60% business use. The logbook still represents average use and monthly business use has stayed above 40% ([IRD: use a logbook](https://www.ird.govt.nz/income-tax/income-tax-for-businesses-and-organisations/types-of-business-expenses/claiming-vehicle-expenses/use-a-logbook)).

| Step | Working | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tier 1 km | 60% x 14,000 = 8,400 km | [IRD: kilometre rates 2025-2026](https://www.ird.govt.nz/income-tax/income-tax-for-businesses-and-organisations/types-of-business-expenses/claiming-vehicle-expenses/kilometre-rates-2025-2026) |
| Tier 2 km | 60% x (18,000 minus 14,000) = 2,400 km | same page |
| Tier 1 claim | 8,400 x $1.20 = $10,080 | [IRD Tax Technical: OS 19/04 (KM 2026)](https://www.taxtechnical.ird.govt.nz/operational-statements/2026/os-19-04-km-2026) |
| Tier 2 claim | 2,400 x $0.37 = $888 | same instrument |
| Deduction | $10,968, with no separate depreciation | [IRD: vehicle expenses](https://www.ird.govt.nz/income-tax/income-tax-for-businesses-and-organisations/types-of-business-expenses/claiming-vehicle-expenses) |

```oa-check
name: NZ kilometre rates, petrol car, 2025-26
inputs:
  total_km: 18000
  business_share: 0.60
  tier1_cap: 14000
  tier1_rate: 1.20
  tier2_rate: 0.37
steps:
  - tier1_km: business_share * min(total_km, tier1_cap)
  - tier2_km: business_share * max(total_km - tier1_cap, 0)
  - tier1_claim: tier1_km * tier1_rate
  - tier2_claim: tier2_km * tier2_rate
  - deduction: tier1_claim + tier2_claim
expected:
  tier1_km: 8400
  tier2_km: 2400
  tier1_claim: 10080
  tier2_claim: 888
  deduction: 10968
```

For 2026-27 the sole trader must wait for Inland Revenue to publish its 2026-27 rates.

**Case 2: no logbook, 2026-27.** A sole trader has neither actual journey records nor a current logbook term and uses actual costs. The 2026-27 running costs are $6,400 and other evidence supports at least 25% business use ([IRD: vehicle expenses](https://www.ird.govt.nz/income-tax/income-tax-for-businesses-and-organisations/types-of-business-expenses/claiming-vehicle-expenses)).

| Step | Working | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Limit | lower of actual business use and 25% | [Income Tax Act s DE 4](https://www.legislation.govt.nz/act/public/2007/97/en/latest/sections/DLM1513861/) |
| Deduction | 25% x $6,400 = $1,600 | same page |

Actual journey records or a current logbook of at least 90 consecutive days can support a higher claim. Include eligible depreciation where applicable.

**Case 3: electric car, kilometre rates, 2025-26 return.** The vehicle travelled 9,000 km, with 70% business use from a current logbook ([IRD: use a logbook](https://www.ird.govt.nz/income-tax/income-tax-for-businesses-and-organisations/types-of-business-expenses/claiming-vehicle-expenses/use-a-logbook)).

| Step | Working | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| All km are within the first 14,000 | 70% x 9,000 = 6,300 km at Tier 1 | [IRD: kilometre rates 2025-2026](https://www.ird.govt.nz/income-tax/income-tax-for-businesses-and-organisations/types-of-business-expenses/claiming-vehicle-expenses/kilometre-rates-2025-2026) |
| Deduction | 6,300 x $1.22 = $7,686 | [IRD Tax Technical: OS 19/04 (KM 2026)](https://www.taxtechnical.ird.govt.nz/operational-statements/2026/os-19-04-km-2026) |

**Case 4: actual costs and depreciation, not GST-registered, 2026-27.** A sole trader bought a car previously used in New Zealand on 1 April 2026 for $23,000 including GST and owns it for the whole 2026-27 income year. A logbook shows 65% business use. It was bought second-hand in New Zealand, so no Investment Boost ([IRD: what you can claim with Investment Boost](https://www.ird.govt.nz/income-tax/income-tax-for-businesses-and-organisations/types-of-business-expenses/new-assets---investment-boost/what--you-can-claim-with-investment-boost)).

| Step | Working | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Depreciation base | not GST-registered, so the full price including GST: $23,000 | [IRD: claiming depreciation](https://www.ird.govt.nz/income-tax/income-tax-for-businesses-and-organisations/types-of-business-expenses/depreciation/claiming-depreciation) |
| Depreciation for the year | 30% diminishing value x $23,000 = $6,900 | [IRD: IR265 March 2026](https://www.ird.govt.nz/-/media/project/ir/home/documents/forms-and-guides/ir200---ir299/ir265/ir265-march-2026.pdf) |
| Business share | 65% x $6,900 = $4,485 | [IRD: vehicle expenses](https://www.ird.govt.nz/income-tax/income-tax-for-businesses-and-organisations/types-of-business-expenses/claiming-vehicle-expenses) |
| Running costs | 65% of fuel, insurance, Warrant of Fitness, repairs and parking | [IRD: use a logbook](https://www.ird.govt.nz/income-tax/income-tax-for-businesses-and-organisations/types-of-business-expenses/claiming-vehicle-expenses/use-a-logbook) |

**Decision: logbook or no logbook.** With running costs of $4,000, the logbook supports a 65% deduction for both running costs and depreciation. With no actual journey records or current logbook term, assume other evidence supports at least 25% business use. The 25% cap applies to both amounts.

```oa-check
name: NZ actual costs with logbook vs 25 percent no-logbook limit
inputs:
  price: 23000
  dv_rate: 0.30
  business_share: 0.65
  running_costs: 4000
  no_logbook_share: 0.25
steps:
  - depreciation: price * dv_rate
  - dep_business: depreciation * business_share
  - running_business: running_costs * business_share
  - with_logbook: dep_business + running_business
  - without_logbook: (running_costs + depreciation) * no_logbook_share
  - logbook_gain: with_logbook - without_logbook
expected:
  depreciation: 6900
  dep_business: 4485
  running_business: 2600
  with_logbook: 7085
  without_logbook: 2725
  logbook_gain: 4360
```

The actual-cost limit includes eligible depreciation. [Income Tax Act s DE 4](https://www.legislation.govt.nz/act/public/2007/97/en/latest/sections/DLM1513861/)

**Case 5: GST on buying and selling, GST-registered.** A GST-registered business buys a car for $34,500 including $4,500 GST ($30,000 excluding GST). A logbook supports 60% taxable use ([IRD: GST adjustments](https://www.ird.govt.nz/gst/gst-adjustments)).

| Step | Working | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Method | over $10,000 excluding GST, so claim by business-use percentage | [IRD: GST adjustments](https://www.ird.govt.nz/gst/gst-adjustments) |
| GST claimed | 60% x $4,500 = $2,700 | same page |
| Adjustment periods | cost is over $20,000 and at most $500,000: default maximum of 5 | [IRD: change-in-use adjustments](https://www.ird.govt.nz/gst/gst-adjustments/change-in-use-adjustments-for-gst) |
| Later taxable sale for $23,000 including GST, previous taxable-use share 60% | $3,000 output GST and a separate final deduction of 3/23 × $23,000 × (1 − 0.60) = $1,200. Net GST on the sale is $1,800 before other entries. | [GST Act s 21F](https://www.legislation.govt.nz/act/public/1985/141/en/latest/sections/DLM83944/) |

**Case 6: close company car under FBT, income-year return.** Kōwhai Ltd provides one car, bought new for $57,500 including GST, to its shareholder-employee. It is available for private use on all 365 days, no exemption applies, and the company files income-year FBT returns on cost price including GST ([IRD: calculating taxable value](https://www.ird.govt.nz/employing-staff/deductions-from-other-payments/fringe-benefit-tax/types/motor-vehicles/calculating-taxable-value)).

| Step | Working | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Formula | (private use days x cost price ÷ 365) x percentage | [IRD: calculating taxable value](https://www.ird.govt.nz/employing-staff/deductions-from-other-payments/fringe-benefit-tax/types/motor-vehicles/calculating-taxable-value) |
| Taxable value | (365 x $57,500 ÷ 365) x 20% = $11,500 | same page |
| FBT payable | apply the FBT rate to the taxable value: outside this skill, refer | |

Had Kōwhai Ltd opted out in time, it would instead claim only the business share of the car's costs and GST, using the applicable evidence and GST methods (steps 2 to 7).

**Case 7: opt-out not available.** Tui Ltd lets its two shareholder-employees use one car each and also gives one of them an employment-related loan that produces a taxable fringe benefit, so the company cannot opt out of FBT for the cars ([IRD: close companies and FBT](https://www.ird.govt.nz/employing-staff/deductions-from-other-payments/fringe-benefit-tax/close-companies)).

## When to refuse or refer

- A 2026-27 kilometre-rate claim before IRD publishes the 2026-27 rates. Do not substitute the 2025-26 rates.
- Working out the FBT payable: FBT rates, the multi-rate or alternate-rate options, and filling in FBT returns.
- A client who wants to switch between kilometre rates and actual costs on the same vehicle, or who claimed GST on the purchase and now wants the kilometre method.
- Investment Boost on a vehicle with private use, or on a vehicle whose "new" status is unclear (imported, demonstrator, ex-trading stock).
- FBT periods before 1 April 2026 where Investment Boost was claimed.
- A late opt-out election, or a company wanting to go back into FBT for an opted-out vehicle while it still owns and uses it.
- Employer reimbursement of an employee's own vehicle (IRD's OS 19/04B), and any vehicle held as trading stock or pooled.
- Charities, and businesses outside charitable purposes run by a charity.
- Disputes over whether a vehicle meets the work-related vehicle test, and vehicles over 3,500 kg gross laden weight, which IRD treats as unclassified benefits ([IRD: FBT exemptions](https://www.ird.govt.nz/employing-staff/deductions-from-other-payments/fringe-benefit-tax/types/motor-vehicles/exemptions)).
- A leased vehicle (operating or finance lease) or one bought on hire purchase: how the lease payments or instalments are deducted, and who depreciates the vehicle, is outside this skill. A lessee under an operating lease does not own the vehicle and cannot use the depreciation steps here; refer.
- Any IRD review, audit or dispute about the business-use percentage.

## Filing and payment

- **Sole traders and partners:** the vehicle claim goes into the income tax return for the year (for individuals, the IR3; see the separate OpenAccountants skill on the IR3 for due dates). GST adjustments go into the GST return for the period.
- **Companies under FBT:** file FBT returns at the chosen frequency. For a close company on income-year returns, the return period matches its income tax return period and "the due date for filing and paying FBT is the same as your company's end of year income tax payment due date"; a weekend or public holiday moves it to the next working day ([IRD: close companies and FBT](https://www.ird.govt.nz/employing-staff/deductions-from-other-payments/fringe-benefit-tax/close-companies)).
- **Elections:** income-year FBT election in myIR (or in writing) by the last day of the first FBT quarter, or the last day of the quarter the company first employed staff if it had no employees in the preceding income year; opt-out by myIR message or a note with a paper return, by the income tax return due date for the year the vehicle is acquired or first used for business (same page).
- **Records:** keep the logbook and vehicle cost records. IRD requires depreciation records (purchase value, depreciation each year, adjusted tax value and depreciation recovered on sale) for at least 7 years ([IRD: managing depreciation](https://www.ird.govt.nz/income-tax/income-tax-for-businesses-and-organisations/types-of-business-expenses/depreciation/managing-depreciation)).
- **Sale of the vehicle:** under actual costs, report any depreciation gain or loss in the year of sale, split between business and private use; no depreciation is claimed in the year of disposal (same page). Under kilometre rates there is no separate depreciation recovery. Make any GST final adjustment in the GST return for the period of sale.

## Completion checklist

- [ ] Owner identified; regime chosen (private use adjustment, FBT, or close company opt-out).
- [ ] Fuel type confirmed; method matches the one used in earlier years for this vehicle.
- [ ] Business share supported by actual records or a current logbook of at least 90 consecutive days. If the default method applies, cap eligible costs and depreciation at the lower of demonstrated business use and 25%.
- [ ] Kilometre rates used are for the income year being claimed; for 2026-27, IRD's rates have been published.
- [ ] Apply Tier 1 and Tier 2 using the kilometre bands for the claim year and the substantiated business-use share.
- [ ] No separate depreciation claimed under kilometre rates.
- [ ] Under actual costs: depreciation rate from IR265, correct GST-inclusive or exclusive base, business share applied, Investment Boost only for a new vehicle.
- [ ] GST claimed using the applicable purchase method and taxable use; adjustment periods noted; change-in-use and sale adjustments diarised.
- [ ] For FBT: exemptions tested, valuation basis and percentage match the return frequency and GST basis, minimum tax book value checked.
- [ ] For an opt-out: 1 or 2 vehicles only, no other fringe benefits, election made in time, FBT registration cancelled if needed.
- [ ] Records kept for at least 7 years.
