# France: VAT and the VAT return (TVA, CA3 and CA12)

This skill covers French VAT for 2026: who is in and who is out, the rates, the two filing regimes and their limits, how to fill the CA3 and the CA12, self-assessment on cross-border purchases, the EU statements, the e-invoicing timetable, the deduction restrictions, and penalties and interest. Every figure was read on an official French page on 24 October 2026, unless the Sources table says otherwise. Load `vat-workflow-base` for the workflow architecture and `eu-vat-directive` for the EU directive content alongside it.

Two facts about the law itself in 2026:

- **The VAT articles are still in the Code général des impôts (CGI) for all of 2026.** Ordonnance n° 2025-1247 of 17 December 2025 moves them into book II of the Code des impositions sur les biens et services (CIBS). Ordonnance n° 2026-671 of 27 July 2026 put that move back from 1 September 2026 to 1 January 2027, and the old CGI references can still be used until 30 June 2028 (BOFiP, actualité of 7 October 2026). This skill cites the CGI article in force in 2026.
- **The 2026 finance law is loi n° 2026-103 of 19 February 2026.** For VAT it changed rate rules at the edges (waste services and cold networks to 5.5%, forestry work kept at 10%, among others), ended the exclusion of deduction for alcohol advertising from 21 February 2026, and amended the e-invoicing and e-reporting fines (CGI art. 1737 and 1788 D). It did not touch the franchise thresholds or the 20%, 10%, 5.5% and 2.1% rates.

## Who this applies to

- **Taxable persons established in France** making supplies for consideration in the French VAT scope. Charge the applicable French VAT where the supplier is liable, subject to the actual franchise, exemption and recipient-liability rules; establish the supply and place first.
- **The franchise en base (CGI art. 293 B).** An eligible business does not charge VAT on its franchised supplies and cannot deduct tax on costs used for those operations (art. 293 E). No ordinary CA3/CA12 output return is required for those franchised sales/services; separately test foreign purchases and other operations for which the business itself owes VAT. The franchise does not cancel those liabilities. Its domestic franchise invoices state "TVA non applicable, art. 293 B du code général des impôts"; art. 293 E gives the applicable alternative reference for art. 293 B bis claims. It may opt for VAT under art. 293 F.
- **The single EUR 25,000 threshold never applied.** The 2025 finance law (loi n° 2025-127, art. 32) set one threshold of EUR 25,000 from 1 March 2025. Loi n° 2025-1044 of 3 November 2025, art. 1, restored the separate thresholds with effect from 1 March 2025 itself. So for 2025 and 2026 the thresholds are the ones in the table below. Any page or skill that prints EUR 25,000 as the French franchise threshold is wrong.
- **Businesses established in another EU state** may use the French franchise only if they meet the French art. 293 B conditions as well as the cumulative art. 293 B bis requirements: EU-wide turnover at or below EUR 100,000 in BOTH the previous and current year and the prescribed home-state notification/identification process. A French business using another state's franchise notifies the French administration and reports quarterly under art. 293 B ter.
- **Régime réel normal.** Monthly CA3, or quarterly where the VAT due each year is below EUR 4,000 (CGI art. 287-2).
- **Régime simplifié (régime simplifié de déclaration).** One annual CA12 plus two instalments (CGI art. 287-3), for businesses inside the turnover band in the table and with VAT due of EUR 15,000 or less for the previous year (CIBS art. L. 162-4, L. 162-5, L. 162-7).
- **Out of this skill:** the overseas departments (their own rates, listed for recognition only; VAT is provisionally not applicable in Guyane and Mayotte), Corsica's special rates beyond the CA3 lines, the margin scheme, property VAT (TVA immobilière), VAT groups (CGI art. 256 C), and non-residents registering through a fiscal representative. Refuse or refer these (see Ask the client first).

**Apply the reduced-rate conditions to the actual supply.** Food at 5.5% excludes confectionery, the specified chocolate products, margarine/vegetable fats, caviar and alcohol; the statutory chocolate exceptions still matter. The title of a product or supplier does not decide the rate. For ordinary 10% dwelling works, verify improvement, conversion, fitting-out or maintenance, domestic use and completion more than two years ago; exclude the specified household/furniture and large-equipment supply components. Cleaning, green-space works and supply/installation of a fossil-fuel-capable boiler are at the standard rate. Works producing a new building or increasing floor area by more than 10% over the statutory two-year period also use the standard rate. Obtain the customer's certification on the quote or invoice and keep the supporting documents (CGI art. 279-0 bis).

For 5.5% energy renovation, CGI art. 278-0 bis A requires residential use and premises completed at least two years ago, specified insulation, heating/ventilation or hot-water energy-saving/renewable works, and the delegated technical/performance conditions. Apply its new-building/floor-increase and fossil-boiler exclusions and customer certification requirements. The customer keeps the supporting copy and invoices through 31 December of the fifth year after the invoice; these gates do not replace checking the delegated technical list for the actual work.

The 2.1% performance treatment is limited to the qualifying works and first 140 performances under CGI art. 281 quater and annexe III art. 89 ter, with the statutory exclusions; it is not a rate for every new show. Press must meet the annexed publication conditions or the recognised digital-press conditions. Human-blood treatment is also conditional: the defined labile products for human research and in-vitro diagnostic devices prepared from blood/components fall within CGI art. 281 octies; whole blood and derivatives intended for direct therapeutic use instead fall within the exemption in CGI art. 261, 4-2° (BOI-TVA-LIQ-40-10§350).

**Other retained rate examples.** Qualifying theatres, chansonniers, circuses, concerts and variety shows fall within CGI art. 278-0 bis, F at 5.5%, subject to its exclusion for establishments where consumption during performances is customary and the stated Centre national de la musique affiliation exception. Its separate F-2 ticket category requires an original musical performance, a physically present remunerated artist and the specified operator licence where refreshments are optional. Check those actual conditions and the distinct 2.1% first-performance rules above; a generic “live performance” label does not decide the rate.

**How the franchise thresholds bite.** The basic threshold is tested on the previous calendar year: crossing it brings VAT from 1 January of the next year. The higher threshold is tested on the current year: crossing it brings VAT from the day of the overrun itself (CGI art. 293 B, II), so invoices issued that day already carry VAT. For a start-up, service-public F21746 annualises turnover as actual turnover × calendar-year days / actual active days expressly to test the following year. It does not establish a universal annualised-turnover test for the current year. The conflicting first-year sources are set out below.

**Start-up franchise: literal law and administrative conflict.** CGI art. 293 D, III literally prorates the ceilings named in art. 293 B, I and I bis, by the fraction of the year remaining at the activity start. On that literal worksheet, a goods business starting on 1 July 2026 has 184/365 of the year: the EUR 93,500 current-year ceiling becomes EUR 47,134.246575... before any assessment rounding, and EUR 50,000 turnover exceeds it. The retained DGFiP regimes page instead expressly grants the first-year franchise up to the unscaled EUR 93,500 / EUR 41,250, so its worksheet does not find that overrun. It then prorates those higher ceilings to test the next year. Service-public F21746 expressly annualises for the following year; its printed example exceeds both the basic and higher limits and cannot distinguish them. With EUR 45,000 from that same start date, annualised turnover is EUR 89,266.304347..., above EUR 85,000 but below EUR 93,500. The statute and those administrative statements have not been reconciled here. Keep both calculations and the actual start date, qualifying turnover and dated sales; this Guide does not decide the first-year exemption, next-year basic-versus-higher test or exact VAT start date where those readings disagree. A year-end total alone cannot identify the overrun transaction. The turnover composition and capital-asset-disposal exclusion are in art. 293 D, I.

**Where two official pages disagree.** The impots.gouv.fr page "Les régimes d'imposition à la TVA" (last modified 21 May 2026) still prints EUR 840,000 and EUR 254,000 as the régime simplifié limits. Those are the 2023-2025 values. The law settles it: CIBS art. L. 162-4 sets base amounts of EUR 818,000 and EUR 247,000 for 2020 to 2022 and indexes them on inflation every three years, rounded to the nearest thousand euros, and CIBS art. A. 162-7, as amended by the arrêté of 27 January 2026 (in force from 29 January 2026), fixes the 2026 amounts at EUR 945,000 / 1,040,000 and EUR 286,000 / 323,000. The BOFiP barème of 1 July 2026 and service-public F23566 give the same figures. Use the 2026 values.

**Numeric identifiers on the retained 2026 CA3 form.** These identify printed form fields, not additional tax tests: A1 0979; A2 0981; A3 0044; A4 0056; A5 0051; B1 0048; B2 0031; B3 0030; B4 0040; B5 0036; E1 0032; E2 0033; E3 0047; E4 0052; F1 0055; F2 0034; F6 0037; F8 0039; 15 0600; 17 0035; 18 0038; 19 0703; 20 0702; 21 0059; 22 8001; 24 0710; 2E 0711; 25 0705; TD 8900; 26 8002; 27 8003; 28 8901; 29 9979; 32 9992. Use the visible line labels and the applicable current form when transferring data; the identifiers do not replace the ordinary line-selection and adjustment rules.

Line 18 is a Monaco memo line, not the line for self-assessed EU services. A credit at line 25 does not excuse paying the assimilated taxes at line 29.

**CA3 completion, rounding and refunds.** The 2026 notice requires one return for each reporting period, including a “Néant” return when there were no operations; do not combine several months or quarters on one return. Keep precise invoice values in the working paper. Round reported taxable bases, corresponding VAT and the deductible total for each of lines 19 and 20 to the nearest euro: below EUR 0.50 is discarded, EUR 0.50 or more becomes EUR 1. Thus EUR 101.49 becomes EUR 101 and EUR 101.50 becomes EUR 102; do not assume a per-invoice rounding method from that return-stage rule. For a requested line-26 refund, the notice gives a EUR 760 minimum for monthly or quarterly requests for January–November or the first three quarters, and EUR 150 for December or the fourth quarter. These are amount and period tests, not automatic approval of every claim: establish ordinary refund conditions and file the request on 3519. Carry only the unrequested credit to line 27 and the next line 22; do not both refund and carry the same amount. See the [2026 notice](https://www.impots.gouv.fr/sites/default/files/formulaires/3310-ca3-sd/2026/3310-ca3-sd_5426.pdf), sections I and IV.

**The CA12 (form 3517-S-SD, 2026)** has its own numbering. Lines 5A, 06, 6C and 07 carry the standard, reduced, intermediate and overseas standard rates; line 19 is total gross VAT; line 20 deductions on invoices, 21 the flat-rate deduction, 22 their total; line 23 VAT on fixed assets; line 24 the credit brought in; line 26 total deductible; line 28 VAT due; line 29 credit; line 30 instalments. Line 57 is next year's instalment base: line 16 less lines 11, 12 and 22, which is before VAT on fixed assets. Below EUR 150 a year-end credit is refundable only on a disposal, death or entry into a VAT group; otherwise carry it to next year (line 51) or set it against the next instalment (line 52). The selected 2026 completion notice is now accessible and was read for these ordinary entries. For line 30, distinguish instalments actually paid (column 1) from those still payable (column 2), using the instalments exigible within the covered VAT year. If the expected instalment was below EUR 550 for July or EUR 400 for December, show only what was actually paid in column 1; do not enter the unpaid balance in column 2. Keep credit used against instalments at AE separate from an unrefunded, unimputed opening credit at line 24; do not carry or refund the same amount twice. See the [selected 2026 CA12 notice](https://www.impots.gouv.fr/sites/default/files/formulaires/3517-s-sd/2026/3517-s-sd_5424.pdf), lines 24, AE, 30 and 57 and frame VII.

**Self-assessment (autoliquidation).**

- Ordinary general-rule B2B services taxable in France from a supplier not established there, with no participating French establishment: use A3, the actual output rate block and the eligible deduction at line 20 or 19 according to actual expensing/capitalisation (CGI arts. 259-1° and 283-2). First check the service and any special place rules. Net nil requires full ordinary deduction entitlement. VAT wrongly charged abroad is not French input tax; obtain the supplier correction and assess the lawful foreign-recovery route separately.
- Intra-EU acquisitions of goods: base on B2, tax in the rate block and again as a memo at line 17, deduction at line 20 or 19.
- Imports: import VAT is self-assessed on the CA3 (base on A4, tax at I1 to I6, deduction at line 20 or 19 and memo at line 24). The return is pre-filled with customs data: reconcile it with the client's import records.
- Domestic reverse charges: construction subcontracting (art. 283-2 nonies), scrap and waste (283-2 sexies), emission quotas (283-2 septies), electronic communications services (283-2 octies), gas and electricity (283-2 quinquies). Flag these for the reviewer.

**Exempt and zero-rated.** Exports go to E1 with export evidence. Intra-EU supplies of goods go to F2: check the customer's VAT number on VIES before invoicing without VAT, keep transport proof, and file the état récapitulatif. General-rule B2B services actually located abroad go to E2; first check supply classification and the special place rules, and apply the DES conditions below rather than treating every foreign business customer as enough. Where an exemption without input credit actually applies, charge no output VAT and exclude the related input tax to that extent. Establish the precise medical/education conditions under CGI art. 261, financial/insurance conditions and eligible options under arts. 261 C/260 B/260 C, and rental conditions/options under arts. 261 D/260; a profession or supplier label does not establish exemption. A mixed business applies the actual deduction-coefficient rules in CGI annexe II arts. 205–207. Before applying a proportional business-use deduction, test art. 206, IV-2-1°: use of the good or service more than 90% for purposes foreign to the business makes its admission coefficient zero, so none of its input VAT is deductible. Exactly 90% does not trigger that exclusion; the remaining coefficient and ordinary invoice/eligibility conditions still apply. Refer the detailed partial-exemption calculation to an expert-comptable.

**Questions to raise when a line is ambiguous.** Fuel and vehicle costs (was the vehicle designed for people or goods, and which fuel?); restaurants (who was present and why?); SaaS (legal entity, country, any French TVA charged?); round transfers from the owner's name (sale, capital or loan?); incoming money from individuals or foreign accounts (sale? business or consumer? which country?); large one-off purchases near EUR 500 (asset or expense?); mixed-use phone or home costs (business share?); outgoing transfers to individuals (contractor with invoice, wages, refund or private?); foreign hotels (foreign VAT is not French input VAT; a refund may be claimed in that state); Airbnb income (furnished letting falls in the services band); construction subcontractors (domestic reverse charge?); platform sales on Amazon, Etsy or eBay (apply the qualified current-and-previous-year EUR 10,000 destination selector below, including establishment and goods-dispatch facts; use `eu-vat-directive` R-EU-5 for any applicable OSS route).

**Invoices.** At EUR 150 excluding VAT or less, the ordinary simplification in CGI annexe II art. 242 nonies A, II permits omission of the supplier's VAT identification number (I-2°) and the exemption reference (I-12°), subject to the actual exclusions and particulars independently imposed for the operation. It does not generally waive the customer's VAT number or the reverse-charge wording. The statutory distance-goods, exempt intra-EU/new-transport and specified cross-border branches have their own exclusions or simplifications: check the actual operation under II-a to c and BOFiP BOI-TVA-DECLA-30-20-20-20, §20–60, before omitting a particular. Reverse-charge invoices say "Autoliquidation". F31808's supplier/customer-number summary is not the selected statutory rule. From 1 September 2026 (large companies and ETI) and 1 September 2027 (SMEs and micro-enterprises), invoices add four new particulars: the customer's SIREN, the delivery address if different, whether the invoice is for goods, services or both, and "Option pour le paiement de la taxe d'après les débits" where that option was made (service-public F31808 and the actual article's commencement notes).

**Qualitative review checks (OpenAccountants working practice).** Flag unusually large transactions relative to the period, a classification uncertainty that materially changes tax, concentration in one counterparty, repeated unresolved facts, and a large payment/refund position. No statute-derived monetary trip point is asserted. Resolve the supporting facts; an internal review flag is not itself a VAT classification.

**Input pack (OpenAccountants working practice).** Obtain a bank statement covering the full reporting period, in CSV, PDF or readable text. Add sales and purchase invoices, the legal entity and trading name, written VAT identification, SIREN/SIRET, the prior CA3/CA12 and the invoice register/FEC-compatible export when available. Confirm the regime and full reporting period from the taxpayer record; the first and last bank transactions do not establish that period. Ask what the business does, whether it employs staff, whether it makes exempt supplies, whether sales are cross-border, and whether construction subcontracting is involved. Names, company suffixes, payroll-looking debits and IBANs suggest questions; they do not establish those facts or the VAT result. Reconcile the previous line27 credit to current line22.

**Bank statement only (OpenAccountants working practice, not a legal rule).** If there is no bank statement at all, stop. With a bank statement but no invoices, prepare the return and tell the reviewer in writing that every input VAT claim must be checked against a compliant invoice before the return is approved.

**CA3 credit-note corrections.** For an ordinary supported credit note reducing a previously taxed customer sale, enter the positive correction base on B5; recover the previously paid output VAT through line 21, with the required credit note or corrected invoice and its separate collected-VAT-correction identification. Do not put negative VAT in the gross-tax block or also reduce the other frame-A amounts for that same correction. For example, a qualifying EUR 2,000 excluding-VAT reduction with EUR 400 VAT is B5 EUR 2,000 and line 21 EUR 400, not a second reduction of current A1 plus another EUR 400 deduction. A supplier credit note reverses the earlier input deduction through line 15 to its supported extent. Non-taxed customer corrections go as positive amounts on F8 without inventing output VAT. The 2026 notice expressly prohibits negative B5/F8 amounts and retains special rules for foreign customers already refunded incorrectly invoiced VAT; do not override those with this ordinary method. Keep the original invoice, its declared tax and the correction evidence together. See the [2026 notice](https://www.impots.gouv.fr/sites/default/files/formulaires/3310-ca3-sd/2026/3310-ca3-sd_5426.pdf), B5/F8 and lines 15/21.

In the Transactions sheet the line-code column accepts only 2026 CA3 codes, blank for excluded lines, and "A3/20" style for self-assessed lines. The Line Summary sheet sums A1, E1, E2, E3, F2, A3, A4, B2, B4, then the rate block 08, 09, 9B, I1 to I6, line 16, memo 17, lines 19, 20, 22, 23, memo 24, then 25 or TD, 26, 27, 28 and 32. If line 23 exceeds line 16, line 25 is the credit and line 27 = 25 less 26 carries to the next return; otherwise TD = 16 less 23. This ordinary sheet assumes no energy-excise credit/levy or group-payment transfer. Where applicable, use the actual form’s X5/Z5/AB adjustments: line 28 = TD − X5 (X5 cannot exceed TD), and line 32 = 28 + 29 + Z5 − AB; do not silently set those actual liabilities or credits to zero. Keep the base Transactions columns and a separate Line Summary and Return Form. ## Cross-border points

- **B2B services sold to EU customers:** establish the general CGI art. 259-1° place-of-supply rule first; a foreign customer alone does not displace the special place rules in art. 259 A–D. For the ordinary service supplied from the relevant French business establishment to an eligible business customer in another member state, invoice without French VAT, mark "Auto-liquidation", put the amount on E2 and check the customer's number on VIES. DES applies where the service is located there under directive art. 44 and the customer owes the tax under art. 196; a service exempt in that state is not included in DES. Keep the establishment, customer status, supply classification and destination-liability/exemption facts; apply the ten-working-day deadline above only to an operation actually requiring DES.
- **Goods sold to EU businesses:** zero-rated on F2 with VIES check and transport proof; état récapitulatif within ten working days after the month.
- **Distance sales and B2C digital services to other EU states:** the ordinary origin-tax exception applies to a seller established only in France, with qualifying goods dispatched from France, where the combined eligible intra-EU distance-goods sales and cross-border telecommunications, broadcasting and electronic services do not exceed EUR 10,000 excluding VAT in either the current or previous calendar year and no destination-tax option applies. Domestic digital sales are not part of that cross-border total. Destination tax applies from the transaction crossing the limit, where either year fails, or under the valid option. A prior-year EUR 11,000 / current-year EUR 1,000 seller fails the amount condition; EUR 10,000 in each year meets only the amount test, not all the other conditions. See CGI art. 259 D / 258 A and https://bofip.impots.gouv.fr/bofip/11964-PGP.html, §220 and 240–270. OSS can provide the declaration route where eligible, rather than being an unconditional duty created by this Guide. On the French CA3, destination-taxable distance goods go to E3; the stated cross-border B2C telecommunications, broadcasting and electronic services taxable in the consumption state go to E2 under the 2026 notice. Establish any applicable OSS declaration route separately. This is separate from the EUR 10,000 intra-EU acquisition threshold; detailed destination filing is in `eu-vat-directive`, R-EU-5.
- **Purchases from abroad:** self-assess as set out in The taxable base (A3, B2, A4, B4).
- **Non-established businesses selling in France:** where a French taxable customer buys, the customer is liable under art. 283-1 and 283-2 in the cases those articles name. A non-EU business that must register may need a fiscal representative: refer.
- **The EU franchise:** an EU-established claimant must meet the French art. 293 B conditions and the cumulative art. 293 B bis EU-wide EUR 100,000 limit in BOTH years plus the prescribed home-state process. A French business using another state's franchise must tell the French administration within 15 working days after its EU-wide annual turnover exceeds EUR 100,000 and supply the required partial-quarter figures (art. 293 B ter).
- **Monaco:** tax on operations to Monaco is shown at memo line 18 of the CA3.
- Income tax on cross-border activity is in `fr-cross-border` and `fr-corporate-tax`.

- **3 April 2026, NOTION LABS INC, subscription EUR 14.68.** Stipulate that the actual invoice is for a general B2B service taxable in France from a non-established supplier without a participating French establishment, with no VAT charged and full ordinary deduction entitlement. Record EUR 14.68 at A3; EUR 14.68 × 20% = EUR 2.936, or EUR 2.94 in the decimal working paper, goes through the output rate block and the matching deductible amount at line 20. The two-sided self-assessment has nil net effect on those stipulated facts. A brand alone does not establish this branch.
- **10 April 2026, GOOGLE IRELAND LIMITED, Ads EUR 850.** On the same stipulated general-service and full-deduction facts, A3 is EUR 850 and the output and deductible tax are each EUR 170. Both sides are required; the first main example below retains the same purchase within an aggregate return. Verify the actual billing establishment and invoice rather than assuming them from the description.
- **15 April 2026, BRASSERIE LIPP PARIS, meal EUR 220.** Obtain the invoice, actual VAT breakdown, attendees and business purpose. A restaurant name does not by itself block or establish recovery. If the ordinary use, lawful invoice and deduction conditions are met, put the supported deductible tax at line 20; if those facts are missing, hold the claim for evidence rather than inventing a permanent zero-tax invoice or denial. The EUR 220 debit alone does not prove the rate or deductible amount.
- **18 April 2026, DELL FRANCE SAS, laptop invoice DEL2026-0041, EUR 1,595 including 20% VAT.** On those stipulated invoice facts, EUR 1,595 ÷ 1.20 = EUR 1,329.166… net, or EUR 1,329.17, and the working-paper VAT is EUR 265.83. If the supported accounting treatment records this durable laptop as a fixed asset and the ordinary deduction conditions hold, claim the deductible tax at line 19. The optional EUR 500 equipment tolerance does not make every purchase above or below it automatically an asset or expense; retain the actual classification and linked-component facts.
- **22 April 2026, STUDIO KREBS GMBH, IT consultancy receipt EUR 3,500.** If the actual transaction is an ordinary general B2B service supplied from the relevant French establishment to an eligible German business customer, taxable there with recipient liability and no destination exemption, invoice without French VAT, state the required reverse charge, record EUR 3,500 at E2 and submit the required DES. Verify customer status/number and the supply/establishment facts; the German name or IBAN does not establish them. Apply the stated DES deadline only if its actual conditions hold.
- **28 April 2026, ARVAL FRANCE, lease debit EUR 650.** If the actual leased vehicle is designed for passengers or mixed use and no qualifying exception applies, the vehicle-related input VAT is excluded under the admission rule. Retain the lease invoice and design/exception evidence. The bank debit alone does not establish its net price or VAT breakdown, so do not label all EUR 650 as net merely because nothing is claimed. Fuel and an independently qualifying vehicle exception follow their own stated rules.

**Example 1: monthly CA3 for a Paris consultancy (régime réel normal).** In April 2026 the company sells EUR 50,000 of consulting at 20%, and its in-house café sells EUR 8,000 of food and soft drinks for consumption on the premises at 10% (CGI art. 279, m; alcoholic drinks would be 20%). It buys Google Ads from Google Ireland for EUR 850 (no VAT on the invoice), other French purchases of EUR 12,000 plus 20% VAT, and a server for EUR 1,500 plus 20% VAT that it records as a fixed asset. Sales: A1 is 58,000; line 08 base 50,000 and tax 10,000; line 9B base 8,000 and tax 800. Google Ads: A3 is 850, tax 170 joins line 08, and the same 170 is deducted at line 20. Deductions: line 20 is 2,400 plus 170, line 19 is 300. Line 16 is 10,970, line 23 is 2,870, so TD and line 28 are 8,100.

**Example 2: régime simplifié, instalments and the fixed-asset edge.** A shop on the régime simplifié had tax of EUR 9,000 for 2025 before the VAT on fixed assets. Its 2026 instalments are 55% in July (4,950) and 40% in December (3,600). On the 2026 CA12 its gross VAT is EUR 18,000, deductions on other goods and services EUR 6,500, and VAT on fixed assets EUR 1,200. Net VAT for 2026 is 10,300, so the balance due with the CA12 by the second working day after 1 May 2027 is 10,300 less 8,550, or 1,750. The edge: next year's instalment base (line 57) is struck before the fixed-asset VAT, so it is 18,000 less 6,500, or 11,500, not 10,300. The 2027 instalments are 6,325 in July and 4,600 in December.

## Ask the client first

- For a start-up, what are the exact start date and dated qualifying sales? For the EUR 10,000 cross-border test, provide both calendar years, all establishments, goods-dispatch countries, combined eligible sales and any destination-tax option. For currency, identify the actual VAT tax point and conversion evidence; for assets and lodging, provide capitalisation/expense and beneficiary/use evidence.

- Which regime are you in, and what were last year's turnover (by activity: goods and catering, or services) and last year's VAT due? Both numbers decide the regime.
- What filing date does your espace professionnel show for the CA3? It depends on department, category and SIREN.
- Do you sell outside France? For each customer: business or consumer, goods or services, which country. That decides E1, E2, E3, F2 and the EU statements.
- Do you make intra-EU acquisitions, imports or exits from a suspensive regime? These trigger the CGI art. 287-3 ter CA3 route from 2025 even where the turnover remains within RSI limits; apply monthly filing or the statutory quarterly exception where annual VAT due is below EUR 4,000.
- Any credit at line 27 of your last return, any exempt income, any construction subcontracting, any company cars (designed to carry people or goods)?
- Refuse or refer: a business inside the franchise that has not opted; partial exemption above de minimis; margin scheme; property VAT; VAT groups; fiscal representatives; overseas departments; anyone wanting income tax (use `fr-income-tax` or `fr-personal-income-tax`); any refusal in `eu-vat-directive` R-EU-1 to R-EU-12.
