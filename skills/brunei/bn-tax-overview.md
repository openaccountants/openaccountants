## The base

- **Companies** are taxed on income accruing in, derived from or received in Brunei, under the Income Tax Act (Chapter 35), on a preceding-year basis: YA 2026 taxes the basis period ending in 2025. The Revenue Division excludes dividends received from companies taxable in Brunei. Check the source and statutory treatment of the particular distribution. Capital gains are not taxed.
- **Individuals** have no ordinary Brunei personal income tax return. An individual receiving a specified Brunei-source payment as a non-resident may still be subject to withholding.
- **Payroll** carries no ordinary PAYE. SPK applies to eligible Brunei citizens and permanent residents below 60. Check the transitional rules for workers who deferred SPK.
- **Goods** imported bear customs duty and, for specified goods, excise.

## Deadlines and filing

- Company: submit returns and ECI through OCP. The return and related payment are due by 30 June. ECI and related payment are due within 3 months after the accounting period end if the return has not already been furnished. An additional assessment is normally payable within 30 days after service.
- Withholding: normally remit within 14 days of payment to the non-resident. A longer period may be authorised for financial institutions.
- SPK: by the 15th of the following month, through e-Amanah.
- Stamp duty: normally stamp before signing in Brunei. Section 39 generally allows stamping within 14 days after first execution in Brunei without penalty. The ordinary period for a covered instrument executed wholly abroad is 30 days from first receipt in Brunei. Bills, cheques and promissory notes drawn abroad have a separate rule.

## Penalties and interest

Late ordinary company tax carries a 5% penalty. If tax remains unpaid 60 days after that penalty is imposed, an additional 1% per completed month applies, capped at 12% of outstanding tax. Late withholding carries 5% if unpaid within 14 days of the underlying payment, then an additional 1% per completed month if unpaid within 30 days of that payment, capped at an additional 15%. Failure to furnish a required return without reasonable excuse can lead on conviction to a BND 10,000 fine and up to 12 months' imprisonment in default of payment. Detail in [bn-corporate-income-tax](bn-corporate-income-tax.md).

## Cross-border points

Brunei has avoidance of double taxation agreements with 19 jurisdictions listed by the Revenue Division (including Singapore, Malaysia, Indonesia, China, Japan, the United Kingdom, Hong Kong and Cambodia). A company is non-resident if its control and management is not exercised in Brunei. Treaty use and withholding: [bn-corporate-income-tax](bn-corporate-income-tax.md).

## Two worked examples that recompute

**Example 1. Existing company with BND 1,200,000 turnover and BND 180,000 chargeable income.** The company is outside the turnover exemption. BND 100,000 x 18.5% x 25% = BND 4,625; BND 80,000 x 18.5% x 50% = BND 7,400. Tax BND 12,025.

```oa-check
name: Brunei company, CI BND 180,000
inputs:
  ci: 180000
  rate: 0.185
steps:
  - band1: min(ci, 100000) * 0.25 * rate
  - band2: clamp(ci - 100000, 0, 150000) * 0.5 * rate
  - band3: max(ci - 250000, 0) * rate
  - tax: band1 + band2 + band3
expected:
  tax: 12025.00
```

**Example 2 (decision). A Singapore treaty-resident individual bills a Brunei client BND 50,000 for independent technical consultancy.** Assume the fee is for services only, is Brunei-source under domestic law, and the consultant has no fixed base regularly available in Brunei and is present there for no more than 183 days in any 12-month period. Domestic withholding is 10%, or BND 5,000. Article 14 assigns taxing rights on these fees to Singapore under those facts, so the final Brunei tax after treaty relief is nil. Follow the Revenue Division's claim procedure, including evidence of treaty residence. A separate Brunei-resident sole proprietor would ordinarily pay no Brunei income tax on the fee. Registration for that business structure is limited to Brunei citizens and permanent residents.

```oa-check
name: Brunei technical fee before and after Singapore treaty relief
inputs:
  fee: 50000
steps:
  - domestic_wht: fee * 0.10
  - tax_after_treaty_relief: fee * 0
  - treaty_relief: domestic_wht - tax_after_treaty_relief
expected:
  domestic_wht: 5000.00
  tax_after_treaty_relief: 0.00
  treaty_relief: 5000.00
```

## Ask the client first

1. Individual, sole proprietor, company or foreign business?
2. What is the transaction: profit, salary, payment abroad, import, property or share transfer?
3. Is the payee or payer resident in Brunei?
4. Is the business in oil and gas?
5. Are staff Brunei citizens or permanent residents?
