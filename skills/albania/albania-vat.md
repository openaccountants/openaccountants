# Albania VAT (TVSH), tax year 2026

> **Built from the law. Not yet approved.** The official consolidated texts and the operative sections named below were read on 27 November 2026. The 2026 rules below use Law 92/2014 through Law 80/2025, VKM 953/2014 through VKM 612/2026, Law 87/2019 through Law 83/2025 and Law 9920/2008 through Law 79/2025. These are the consolidated rules read on that date, not permission to backdate a later amendment to an earlier 2026 event. Changed-provision outcomes before an established commencement date remain non-usable: Check this point with one of our partner accountants. Specific non-usable holds are identified explicitly.

Tax period: the calendar month (Law 92/2014 art. 107(2)). Currency: Albanian lek (ALL). Authority: Drejtoria e Përgjithshme e Tatimeve (General Directorate of Taxes), tatime.gov.al; returns go through e-Filing. Use Albanian domestic/import/export rules for EU counterparties too; do not treat these transactions as intra-Community supplies or use EU OSS or an EC Sales List for Albanian VAT. Article numbers below are Law 92/2014 unless another act is named.

See also: `albania-income-tax`, `al-corporate-tax`, `al-cross-border` (tax representative, withholding), `albania-payroll`, `albania-social-contributions`, `albania-tax-optimization`.

Before classifying, obtain the entity and NUIS/NIPT, actual VAT registration, month, current-year and rolling turnover, supply and customer location, invoices/customs records, business use, exemption mix, prior credit and any tourism certification. A bank description, foreign IBAN or request for a VAT return does not establish those facts.

## Who this applies to

**Taxable person.** Anyone who independently carries on an economic activity, whatever its form, place, purpose or result (art. 3). VAT is charged on supplies of goods and services for consideration made in Albania by a taxable person acting as such, and on all imports of goods (art. 2).

**2026 applicability.** The newer [official VKM consolidation](https://tatime.gov.al/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/Vendimi-nr-953-date-29.12.2014.pdf) incorporates VKM 822 of 28 October 2026, but its opening footnote expressly gives those amendments effect from 1 January 2027. Do not substitute its new threshold for the 2026 threshold below. The earlier consolidated VKM copy linked below preserves the operative 2026 wording.

**Registration threshold.** A resident taxable person whose turnover is below the minimum registration threshold is in the small business regime: no VAT is charged on sales and no input VAT is deducted (arts. 117, 121). The threshold is set by the Council of Ministers: **ALL 10,000,000 of turnover in a calendar year** (VKM 953/2014 art. 11(1), as amended by VKM 576/2020). The General Directorate of Taxes states the same figure: registration is mandatory for turnover above ALL 10 million.

**Turnover for the test** is all taxable supplies, including 0% exports and international transport (arts. 57, 59 to 62), measured excluding VAT (art. 119(1)). Sales of capital assets, incidental financial transactions and incidental real estate transactions are left out (art. 119(2)). Real estate and financial supplies that are the main business count, even though exempt (art. 119(3)). A new business pro-rates the threshold from its registration date to the year end (art. 119(4)).

**Crossing the threshold.** A small business whose turnover in any 12 consecutive months exceeds ALL 10,000,000 must apply to register within 15 days of the date of crossing. The date of crossing is the last day of the calendar month in which turnover went over the line (art. 122(1)). A person can go back to the small business regime if turnover in the last 12 calendar months falls below the threshold; deregistration then takes effect 12 months after the request (art. 122(2)).

**Voluntary registration.** A person below the threshold may opt into the normal VAT regime only if annual turnover is above **ALL 5,000,000** (VKM 953/2014 art. 11(1); art. 120(1)). Once in, the person must stay in for at least 2 calendar years. A later request to leave takes effect 6 months after it is filed (art. 120(2), (3)). ALL 5,000,000 is the floor for opting in, not a registration threshold.

**Zero threshold.** Notaries and state and private bailiffs register for VAT whatever their turnover, even if they also make exempt supplies (VKM 953/2014 art. 11(2), added by VKM 128/2022). The text says "professions such as" these two; no other profession is named.

**Who cannot use the small business regime**: incidental supplies of immovable property, activities in the agricultural producers' compensation scheme, and services supplied by a non-established taxable person where Albanian VAT is due (art. 118). Persons whose activity is tax evasion under Law 9920/2008 art. 116 are also excluded (art. 117(3)).

**Non-established suppliers.** A taxable person not established in Albania that makes supplies where Albanian VAT is due, other than services reverse-charged by an Albanian business customer, must register through a tax representative (arts. 87, 88, 91(2)). See Cross-border points.

**Every taxable person**, VAT-registered or not, must declare the start of its economic activity and register within 15 days of starting (art. 91(1)). The tax administration can register a person who should have registered (art. 91(4)).

**Special regimes and historical R-AL refusals.** Travel agents/package tours (arts. 123–128), second-hand goods/art/antiques margin schemes (arts. 129–137), auctions (arts. 138–144), investment gold (arts. 151–155) and free-zone transactions (including the former Spitalla/Koplik/Vlora examples) need their own established conditions. This Guide supplies no usable special-scheme result for them: Check this point with one of our partner accountants. The same hold applies to implementing the electricity-exchange scheme: arts. 155/2–155/3 distinguish imports/supplies destined for exchange sale from purchases from the exchange, with buyer reverse charge through 31 December 2026; the operative implementing decision is not established here. Check this point with one of our partner accountants.

The agricultural compensation scheme is distinct from ordinary VAT and the 10% agricultural-input rate. Arts. 145–150 require eligible own-production supplies, turnover not exceeding the registration limit, an identified producer and a non-scheme taxable buyer; art. 150(1) identifies VAT-registered collectors/processors/certified agrotourism buyers issuing the invoice. Under the Law 80/2025 amendment the compensation rate is 10%, paid by the administration to the producer, not a deemed buyer input credit. Its art. 4 commences 15 days after Gazette publication, not simply on the date of enactment; no pre-commencement 2026 scheme result is supplied here. Detailed scheme application remains non-usable here: Check this point with one of our partner accountants. R-AL-1 small-business and R-AL-4 partial-exemption cases use the ordinary rules above; R-AL-6 income-tax questions go to the companion income-tax Guide.

**Which 6% rule applies matters.** The art. 49(3) rate covers only the sleeping service (and breakfast sold inside the room price). Food, drinks and other services in the same hotel are at 20%, and the accommodation must be shown separately on the invoice (VKM 953/2014 art. 11/1(1)). A taxpayer that runs other activities as well must register a separate taxable person for the accommodation business to use the 6% rate (VKM 953/2014 art. 11/1(1), (2)). The art. 49(4) branded 5-star special-status hotels are the exception: every service inside them is at 6%. Confirm that special status and registered internationally recognised brand; an ordinary 5-star rating alone is insufficient.

**Exempt supplies.** No output VAT; ordinarily no deduction on related inputs. Art. 70(c) preserves deduction for the listed financial/insurance services to customers outside Albania or directly linked to exported goods. The main exemptions (arts. 51–53; related supplies under art. 51 also need art. 52 necessity/non-competition conditions):

- medicines, active implantable medical devices and implantable medical devices; health services by recognised institutions and licensed medical, paramedical and dental professionals;
- education by recognised public and private institutions, and private school or university teaching by teachers;
- insurance and reinsurance, including brokers and agents; granting, negotiating and managing credit; deposit, payment, transfer, currency and securities transactions (debt collection is taxable); management of investment funds;
- supply of buildings or parts of buildings and the land they stand on, other than the construction process itself; supply of undeveloped land;
- letting of immovable property, except lettings of buildings up to two months, hotel and holiday accommodation, parking and vehicle storage, fixed equipment and machinery, and safe deposit boxes (these are taxable);
- printed newspapers and magazines, printing of newspapers, and advertising in print media;
- betting, lotteries and games of chance; postal universal service; agricultural machinery on the Council of Ministers list (VKM 953/2014 art. 8(3), Annex 1/A); veterinary services except for pets; new electric-only vehicles never registered before (other than the art. 49(7) buses).

The Minister of Finance may allow an option to tax the art. 53(b), (e), (g), (gj), (h) financial and property supplies under prescribed conditions (art. 55); commercial rent is not automatically taxable. For an unconfirmed option, or the older cultural/social-welfare/burial-services claims without established conditions, do not select an exemption or deduction: Check this point with one of our partner accountants.

## The taxable base

**Taxable amount.** Everything the supplier receives or will receive from the customer or a third party for the supply, including subsidies linked directly to the price, excluding the VAT itself (art. 37). Other taxes and charges, and ancillary costs charged to the customer (commission, packing, transport, insurance), are included (art. 39(1)). Early payment discounts, discounts given at the time of supply, and disbursements recorded as suspense accounts are excluded (art. 40). On imports, the base is the customs value plus taxes and duties due on import other than VAT, and ancillary costs up to entry into Albania (art. 44).

**Tax point.** VAT arises when the goods or services are supplied (art. 32). An advance payment creates a tax point when it is received, and an invoice issued before supply creates one when issued (art. 33(3), (4)). Continuous supplies, including construction, are treated as made in the month the invoice is issued (art. 33(2)). Imports are taxed on import or on release from a suspensive regime (arts. 34, 35).

**Input VAT.** A registered person deducts VAT on purchases and imports used for taxable supplies, including 0% supplies and foreign supplies that would carry deduction if performed domestically (arts. 68 to 70). Private use is excluded and mixed business/private use is proportioned under art. 69(2), (3). The deduction needs a valid invoice under arts. 96 to 105, a customs import document naming the person as importer, or the reverse charge formalities (art. 75). Ordinarily the invoice must be fiscalised and carry the NIVF fiscal number. Law 87/2019 art. 20(5) expressly permits invoices without NIVF under arts. 29–31 (internet interruption, equipment breakdown or verified unconnected areas); do not reject a compliant exception solely for initial absence of NIVF. Verify the exception and subsequent reporting, or hold the claim. Unused input VAT can still be claimed within 12 tax periods, counting the period the right arose (art. 76(4)).

**Blocked input VAT** (art. 74(1)):

- fuel, unless the business sells fuel or uses it for technological needs within ministerial norms;
- fuel of any kind for passenger cars (autoveturat);
- passenger cars and related services, unless the business resells cars or the cars are its only purpose (rental, taxi, ambulance);
- travel and per diem, hotel accommodation, recreation and entertainment, and luxury costs. Art. 74(1)(ç) separately links representation to the deductible income-tax limit; this Guide does not establish that limit or resolve a disputed dinner/representation distinction. For a claim relying on it: Check this point with one of our partner accountants. Do not use the former blanket representation block or unproved 0.3% figure.

**Partial exemption.** Attribute inputs to their actual use first. For common inputs supporting deductible and non-deductible transactions, the coefficient is annual turnover giving deduction under arts. 69–70 divided by total turnover, both excluding VAT; leave out business capital-asset sales and incidental financial/real-estate transactions (arts. 71–72; Instruction 6/2015 art. 56). The coefficient is set yearly, rounded up to the next whole percent, applied provisionally on the prior year's figures, and corrected in the first period of the next year. The provisional coefficient must be notified by 31 January in subsequent years, or by the deadline for the first return in which it applies; where prior-year transactions are absent or negligible, use a supervised provisional estimate (art. 73). Instruction 6/2015 art. 56(3) waives the annual correction where the change in deductible VAT is less than ALL 20,000; the correction otherwise belongs in the first period of the next year, not December.

**Capital goods.** Input VAT on capital goods is adjusted when their use changes between taxable and non-deductible activities: one fifth a year over 5 years for movable goods, one tenth a year over 10 years for immovable goods (art. 80).

**Credit notes and losses.** Reduce the taxable amount for post-supply cancellation, withdrawal, termination or price reduction (art. 41). Adjust deductions when purchases are cancelled or prices reduced (arts. 78–79); an invoice correction must specifically reference the original invoice and meet the invoice rules (Law 87/2019 art. 9(3)). Do not treat a bank reversal alone as a credit note. Proven destruction/loss/theft has the art. 79(2) exception, subject to the ministerial rules in art. 79(3); disputed loss/theft treatment is non-usable here: Check this point with one of our partner accountants.

**Bad debts.** VAT on an unpaid amount can be reduced once the debt is declared uncollectable by the courts, and not earlier than six months after the end of the period in which the VAT was charged. The seller issues an invoice marked "Borxh i keq" referencing the original invoice; the buyer reduces its input VAT by the same amount (art. 84). A later recovery is invoiced as a new supply marked “Borxh i keq i paguar”, referencing the bad-debt invoice (art. 84(4)).

**Excess credit** is carried forward to the next period (art. 76(2)) or refunded under art. 77 (see Deadlines and filing).

**The return.** The tax administration pre-fills the monthly return from the electronic sales and purchase books (libri i shitjes, libri i blerjes) built from fiscalised invoices. The taxpayer checks it, corrects or completes it against its books, confirms it and submits it on e-Filing by the deadline (arts. 106(4), 109(2)). The return is filed electronically from the taxpayer's own account; if the deadline falls on a non-working day, it moves to the next working day (Law 9920/2008 art. 65(1), (3)). An active registered person files every month, including nil months; registration/cessation periods follow art. 107(3), (4), and cessation of filing follows Law 9920/2008 art. 65(7). Small business regime taxpayers do not file VAT returns but still issue fiscalised invoices (art. 110(1)).

**Invoicing and fiscalisation (Law 87/2019).** Every corporate and personal business income taxpayer and every VAT taxpayer must issue an invoice for every supply, whatever its turnover (Law 87/2019 arts. 4, 6). Each invoice is electronically signed and sent to the tax administration over the internet before it is given to the buyer. The administration returns a fiscal number (NIVF), and ordinarily only then is the invoice valid for tax purposes, subject to the express arts. 29–31 exceptions (art. 20(1) to (5)). Ordinary cash sales get a printed fiscalised invoice at supply; art. 23(3), (4) allows electronic delivery for electronic/mobile/online sales, with consent where required, and art. 22 permits its qualifying simplified route. Non-cash business-to-business invoices between the taxpayers listed in art. 24(1), and invoices to public bodies, must be exchanged through the central platform (arts. 24–26); art. 25(2) excepts qualifying secret/security public procurements. For goods transported under art. 24(1), fiscalise the e-invoice before transport and give the transporter the printed QR document to retain throughout transport (art. 24(4)); cash-goods transport instead carries the invoice under art. 23(2). Invoices may be in any currency, but the total, the taxable value and the VAT must also be shown in lek at the Bank of Albania rate on the day of issue (art. 6(6)). Use registered/certified software; premises, devices and operators require the applicable advance registration, normally at least 24 hours before first invoicing (Law 87/2019 arts. 12(4), 14–16; Law 9920/2008 art. 122). Law 87/2019 art. 6(6) excepts imports from its invoice-date exchange rule: use the customs legislation for imports. For the VAT taxable amount generally, Law 92/2014 art. 39(3) uses the Bank of Albania rate when VAT becomes chargeable; the old bank-posting/payment-date defaults are not a substitute.

**Refunds.** A registered person can claim a refund of excess credit only if it has carried the excess forward for 3 consecutive months and the amount claimed is above ALL 400,000 (art. 77(1)). Exporters can claim once their excess is above ALL 400,000 (art. 77(3)). The claim goes on the form "Kërkesë për rimbursimin e TVSH-së" to the VAT Refund Directorate (Law 9920/2008 art. 75/1(1)). The administration decides within 60 days, or 30 days for exporters, and pays through the treasury within five days of approval; if it does not, the taxpayer may withhold payment of other taxes up to the claimed amount (Law 9920/2008 art. 75/1(1)). For exporters (VKM 953/2014 art. 10):

- exports above 70% of total sales in the claim period, verified against customs reports, active status, more than 1 year exporting and no unpaid social or health contributions: zero risk, refunded automatically within 30 days;
- exports above 50% up to 70% (or above 70% without all the other conditions): refunded within 30 days after risk analysis, including any audit; passive-status claimants instead wait until the audit concludes;
- other exporters: 60 days after risk analysis.

These are the VKM 10 categories implementing the statutory exporter rule, not a promise that every exporter receives payment in 30 days. Claims are suspended while a registered criminal investigation for VAT fraud schemes or concealment of income is open (Law 9920/2008 art. 75/1(1/3)). Before refunding, the administration can require proof that the business aims to make taxable supplies (art. 77(4)).

## Cross-border points

**Services: general rule.** For business customers (B2B) the place of supply is where the customer is established (art. 24(1)). For private customers (B2C) it is where the supplier is established (art. 24(2)). Special rules override this for:

- services connected with immovable property in Albania (art. 25(a));
- passenger transport, by distance (art. 25(b));
- restaurant and catering, where performed (art. 25(c));
- short-term hire of means of transport, where made available: no more than 30 continuous days, or 90 for vessels/aircraft (art. 25(d));
- onboard restaurant/catering during passenger transport, at the place of departure, treating the return leg separately (art. 25(ç));
- B2B admission to events and admission-related ancillary services, where held (art. 26).

For B2C, intermediary services follow the underlying transaction (art. 27); freight follows distance, events/transport support/work on movable goods follow actual performance, and non-short-term transport hire generally follows the customer, with the art. 28(d) yacht availability/supplier-establishment exception (art. 28). Electronic/telecom/broadcast services and the listed intellectual-property, advertising, professional, financial, staff-supply, movable-equipment and energy-network services follow the consumer location under arts. 29–30. Email contact alone does not make a service electronic.

**Services from abroad (reverse charge).** When a non-established supplier provides services whose place of supply is Albania under art. 24(1) to an Albanian taxable person or VAT-identified legal person, the customer pays the VAT (art. 86(2)). The customer self-assesses output VAT and, if the purchase is for taxable supplies, deducts the same amount as input VAT. The Albanian buyer must also issue and fiscalise a self-invoice by the 10th of the following month, unless the foreign supplier issued an e-invoice through the Albanian central platform (Law 87/2019 arts. 7(2)(b), 8(3), 20(7), (10)). Reverse charge is not automatically a full input credit: apply actual use, deduction restrictions and the required formalities (Instruction 6/2015 art. 54(6)). A VAT-identified non-taxable legal person that must self-assess files a return only to pay the VAT and has no deduction (Instruction 6/2015 art. 67(3)).

**Non-established suppliers to non-registered customers** (consumers, small businesses, unregistered public bodies) must appoint an Albanian tax representative to account for the VAT (arts. 87, 88; Instruction 6/2015 art. 27). The representative must have a place of business in Albania, registers separately under its own number, and is personally liable for the VAT and penalties (art. 88). If no representative is appointed, the Albanian customer pays the VAT and penalties (art. 88(3)). This covers telecommunications, broadcasting and electronically supplied services to Albanian consumers (art. 29), and the art. 30 list of services supplied to non-taxable persons in Albania, which includes consulting, legal, accounting, advertising, intellectual property, staff supply and movable equipment hire. Use this Albanian representative route rather than an EU OSS registration for Albanian VAT.

**Selling services abroad.** Under the general B2B rule an Albanian business supplying a foreign business establishment charges no Albanian VAT because the place is outside Albania (art. 24(1)); test the special-place rules first. This is outside Albanian VAT, not a 0% goods export. Related inputs can qualify under art. 70(a). Keep evidence that the customer is a business established abroad. The invoice is still issued and fiscalised (Law 87/2019 arts. 4, 20(1), 24(6)).

**Goods.** Imports pay VAT at customs at the domestic rate (arts. 50, 86(5), 90). Some imports are exempt under art. 56(38), for example machinery and equipment directly linked to investment contracts of ALL 500,000,000 or more, or to inward processing or agribusiness investment whatever its value (VKM 953/2014 art. 8(1)), with the eligible categories in VKM 953/2014 art. 9. This needs a directly linked listed machine, investment/user importer (not resale) and customs exemption authorisation under art. 8(2)–(5); the value threshold alone does not grant exemption. VKM No. 612/2026 added to art. 8(3) a separate machinery list (Annex 6) for investment contracts of ALL 500,000,000 or more by or for entities licensed by the defence industry authority under Law No. 87/2024, for licensed activities, requiring the valid licence copy, direct-use declaration and relationship explanation. Do not apply the 2026 Annex 6 addition to a pre-commencement import without establishing its operative date. Item-level eligibility must match the applicable annex and description; an unresolved machinery code or authorisation is non-usable: Check this point with one of our partner accountants. Exports of goods transported outside Albania by or for the seller, or by or for a non-established buyer, are 0% (art. 57). For travellers’ personal-luggage exports, the traveller must prove residence outside Albania, export within the three months following the month of supply and obtain customs validation of the purchase invoice (art. 58). Instruction 6/2015 art. 49 sets a total including VAT of at least ALL 120,000 and uses a refund procedure, not an automatic VAT-free checkout: present the original invoice, payment receipt, identification and refund form for customs validation, then send the stamped documents and ID copy to DPT from abroad within 30 days of departure. DPT verifies and orders a qualifying refund within 30 days of receiving the application. The instruction’s wording “3 months following the date” differs from art. 58’s following-month rule; the statute controls the export deadline.

**Withholding and treaties** on payments to non-residents are income tax, not VAT: see `al-cross-border`.

### Example 2: exporter with a refund claim

Shkodra Textile SHPK has been exporting for three years, has active status and owes no social or health contributions. In each of April, May and June 2026 it exports goods for ALL 4,500,000 (0%) and sells in Albania for ALL 1,500,000 net at 20% (output VAT ALL 300,000). Its deductible input VAT is ALL 480,000 a month. Each month leaves an excess credit of ALL 180,000, carried forward.

After three consecutive months the carried excess is ALL 540,000. That is above the ALL 400,000 floor, so a refund can be claimed (art. 77(1), (3)). Exports are 4,500,000 ÷ 6,000,000 = 75% of total sales, above 70%. With its other facts this puts the claim in the zero-risk category, refunded automatically within 30 days (VKM 953/2014 art. 10(2)). Had the export share been 60%, the claim would still be paid within 30 days, but only after risk analysis (VKM 953/2014 art. 10(3)).

## Ask the client first

1. **Turnover in the last 12 months and this calendar year**, measured excluding VAT and including exports. Above ALL 10,000,000 registration is mandatory within 15 days of the end of the crossing month; between ALL 5,000,000 and ALL 10,000,000 registration is optional with a 2-year lock-in.
2. **What exactly is sold.** Accommodation, agrotourism, books, audiovisual advertising, agricultural inputs and sports construction can be 6% or 10%; health, education, finance, insurance, land and long lets are exempt and limit input recovery.
3. **Who the customers are, the receiving establishment and where services occur.** Apply the general B2B/B2C rules and their exceptions; a foreign address alone does not prove the VAT treatment.
4. **Purchases from abroad.** Foreign services trigger reverse charge and a self-invoice by the 10th; imported goods pay VAT at customs and must be fiscalised within 7 days.
5. **Fiscalisation set-up.** Whether invoicing software, premises and operators are registered and every invoice gets an NIVF. Check any arts. 29–31 NIVF exception before denying deduction; ordinary non-compliant invoices expose the seller to the art. 123 fines.
6. **Carried-forward credit and export share**, to see whether a refund claim is open and in which category.
