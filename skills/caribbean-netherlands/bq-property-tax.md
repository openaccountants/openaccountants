# Bonaire, Sint Eustatius and Saba: national property tax in 2026

Establish the island, legal right, taxable object and applicable valuation
decision before calculating *vastgoedbelasting*. The tax uses statutory deemed
benefits and an inspector's assessment. It is distinct from local
*grondbelasting*, transfer taxes and tax on actual rental or business income.
This Tier 2 reference has no accountant sign-off and does not yet support a
complete operational property-tax engagement.

Load `workflow-base`. Preserve its profile confirmation, source conditions,
refusals, four outputs, recalculation and professional review. Labelled examples
below establish component arithmetic under stipulated facts; they do not clear
the completion conditions for an actual engagement.

## Scope, period and completion conditions

This reference covers selected national liability, object and exemption rules,
valuation, deemed benefits, ordinary and hotel rates, investment relief,
Bonaire opcenten and selected notification, collection and objection interfaces.
It does not determine actual title, individual property value, complete income
tax treatment, every designated-body exemption, customs-entrepot status,
mandatory representative cases, local land tax or other property levies.

The source comparison covers the Belastingwet BES consolidations for
[1 January to 20 February][B2], [21 February to 10 April][B3] and
[from 11 April 2026][B1]. Chapter IV is textually unchanged across those
captured versions. The [2025 amendment][H1] and its [commencement amendment][H2]
support the current hotel rate and investment-relief transition. The separate
[2024 notification amendment][H3] supports article 8.83a's commencement.

Sources were inspected on 16 and 17 October 2026. Verify the requested period,
applicable amendments and delegated rules before reliance. Neither this editing
date nor a current guidance page establishes the complete applicable law.

Amounts are United States dollars (USD). Apply each figure only after
establishing its legal conditions. [B1, articles 1.3 and 4.1–4.11]

Ordinary national tax is algebraically 4% × 17.5% = 0.7% of its established
value base; the qualifying hotel product is 4% × 11% = 0.44%. Adding Bonaire's
30% surcharge gives respective products of 0.91% and 0.572%. These products do
not establish eligibility, an assessed value, a fractional-cent convention or
a final payable amount. [B1; L1]

Current BCN guidance says Saba and Sint Eustatius charge no opcenten. This is an
attributed administrative statement; a complete local legislative absence check
has not been established. Reverify applicable island law before operational
reliance. [G1; G3, question 28]

## Establish liability, object and exemptions

1. Identify the person enjoying the property through ownership, possession or a
   limited right at the calendar year's start. Cadastral registration is
   presumptive evidence and can be rebutted by the actual right. A mid-year
   transfer does not itself authorise ordinary monthly proration. [B1,
   article 4.2(1)–(2)]
2. Establish the statutory object: built or unbuilt property, an independently
   usable part, or a qualifying combination used by the same taxpayer and
   belonging together in the circumstances. A parcel number alone does not
   settle every object boundary. [B1, article 4.3]
3. Check every applicable exemption in article 4.4 before selecting a rate.
   The principal own-home and income-tax business-property categories depend
   on their Income Tax BES definitions. Worship, water/public-utility works,
   agricultural land, nature property, public holdings and designated
   non-profit institutions have distinct use, ownership and residential
   carve-outs. A designated institution must also meet the property's own-use
   and competition conditions. [B1, article 4.4(a)–(i); R1, article 4.2]
4. Establish any article 5.2 Netherlands-deemed entity status separately.
   Incorporation in BES does not itself settle that classification or the
   article 4.4(j) property exemption. [B1]
5. Apply the vacant-land branch only with its natural-person, BES-residence
   and value-composition conditions. Land under construction can qualify only
   with the statutory own-home condition within 24 months after building
   begins. A bare plot or construction invoice alone is insufficient. [B1,
   article 4.4(k)]

National exemption does not establish freedom from local grondbelasting.
FinBES separately enables local land tax for the specified principal-home and
income-tax business-property categories, and opcenten on national property tax.
Establish the actual local enactment and its conditions. [L2, article 43]

### Main home, business assets and bare land

For article 4.4(a), establish the *Wet inkomstenbelasting BES* article 4
definition. The dwelling must be available to the taxpayer as a main residence
other than temporarily, with one of these legal interests: ownership of the
dwelling; the taxpayer holding its land in *erfpacht* or *huur*; or usufruct
acquired under inheritance law. The land-holding branch does not establish an
exemption for every rented dwelling. Article 4.4(a) also expressly requires
main-residence availability. Ownership alone does not settle the exemption.
[B1, article 4.4(a); I1, article 4]

For article 4.4(b), establish both that the property belongs to an enterprise's
assets and that an income-tax taxpayer obtains article 6 income through that
enterprise. Commercial use, company ownership or rent received alone does not
establish both conditions. Article 6 covers a wider range of business and work
income; it does not classify every property producing receipts as an enterprise
asset. The exemption has no express BES-residence condition. Establish the
taxpayer's applicable income-tax status and asset attribution before applying
it. Passive letting, mixed use and changes between private and business assets
remain classification conditions for the professional reviewer. [B1,
article 4.4(b); I1, articles 1 and 6]

Article 4.4(k) separately requires the property's value to be determined
exclusively or almost exclusively by its bare land. Preserve its 'voor zover'
('to the extent') qualification for the holder or holders under ownership,
possession or limited right being natural persons resident in BES. This
reference does not determine apportionment for mixed holders.
Do not assign an unsupported percentage to 'almost exclusively'. Land on which
a dwelling is being built can qualify only if that dwelling becomes an article
4 own home for those holders within 24 months after construction starts.
Establish each holder's residence and the construction dates; a national
exemption still leaves any applicable local levy to be determined. [B1,
articles 1.5 and 4.4(k); I1, article 4; L2, article 43]

The captured 2025 and 2026 article 4 and 6 texts match. The official article
histories identify the 2025 amendment as their latest displayed change. That
amendment added article 4's main-residence words from 1 January 2025. The
original 2010 commencement instruments were not read for this reference.
[H1, article II(A)–(B); H2; I1–I3]

The separately listed own-home policy is now read. It uses the Belastingwet BES
article 8.19 hardship power for two distinct income-tax approvals. Section 2.1
treats a home under construction or a building plot as an own home once concrete
construction steps have been taken. It identifies the new-build purchase and
construction agreement and, once building work has started on a plot, a
presumption that concrete steps began at least six months earlier. [O1–O2,
section 2.1]

Section 3.1 concerns personal deductions where the own home is empty or under
construction. It requires evidence of exclusive intended main-residence use in
the year first treated as an own home or one of the following two years, plus
the other article 16(1)(h) conditions. That deduction window has a different
starting event from article 4.4(k)'s 24 months after construction starts.
[O1–O2, section 3.1]

This 2020 policy predates the 2025 article 4 amendment. Its income-tax approvals
do not themselves establish how the amended definition and separate property
conditions apply to an actual object. Article 4.4(a) expressly requires
main-residence availability; article 4.4(k) has its own land and construction
conditions. For a case relying on the policy, keep the post-amendment
interaction as a source condition rather than importing a vacant-home,
former-home or construction exemption. [B1, article 4.4(a), (k); H1,
article II(A); O1–O3]

## Determine the value and relief components

The statutory valuation premise assumes full, unencumbered ownership can be
transferred and the acquirer can use the property immediately and fully in its
existing state. Use replacement value if it is higher, considering the nature
and use, technical and functional obsolescence, and later changes. Purchase
cost, accounting carrying amount, actual rent or an imported mainland WOZ
shortcut cannot replace the applicable valuation. [B1, article 4.5]

The inspector establishes value by an objectionable decision. The ordinary
reference date is the start of the five-year valuation period. BCN's
[28 July 2025 announcement][G2] identifies a national 2026–2030 programme with
1 January 2026 as reference date. This general programme does not supply an
individual valuation decision or establish every property's taxable amount.
[B1, article 4.6]

Specified construction, renovation, improvement, demolition or destruction
changes lead to valuation by the property's state at the start of the following
calendar year. For a qualifying increase caused by the listed construction
activities, article 4.8(2) delays its inclusion. It does not exempt the property's
whole existing value. The trade/services-warehouse exception must be checked.
[B1, article 4.8(1)–(2)]

Select the relief text by **valuation establishment date**. Article 4.8(3)
applies the new rule to establishments on or after 1 January 2025 and preserves
the former rule for establishments on or before 31 December 2024. The year of
the investment alone does not settle this transition. Record the change year,
determination date, established increase, applicable text and first inclusion
year for each component. [B1; B4; H1–H2]

Only inspector-established increases qualify under R1, article 4.1. Notify
within one year **after the end of the calendar year of the change**, using the
inspector-issued form requested by the taxpayer. Preserve the supporting
construction records. The inspector's determination has the prescribed
retrospective effect from the first following calendar year. Its two-month
decision and two-year information-based revision provisions remain distinct.
This relief notification does not replace article 8.83a's separate four-month
notifications. [R1, article 4.1]

## Calculate the national component and island surcharge

For an established taxable ordinary object, determine the applicable value
after verified relief, compute the statutory deemed benefits and apply the
national rate. For an independent dwelling other than the exempt own home,
apply the USD 70,000 branch to the value exceeding that amount. Do not repeat
the allowance for each person or extend it automatically to every commercial
or hotel object. The text does not limit the independent-dwelling allowance
to natural-person owners alone. Preserve article 4.10(1)'s additional
co-holder condition where all owners are natural persons. [B1]

For the 11% hotel rate, verify both non-natural-person enjoyment of the legal
right and commercial operation by a non-natural person. The property or
complex must be wholly or almost wholly directed at short tourist stays and
have full communal hotel facilities. An online listing, holiday let or the
word hotel in an invoice does not prove these conditions. [B1, article 4.9]

For joint holders under article 4.2(3), the Act provides a joint assessment
and article 8.70 makes each holder jointly liable for the whole tax. The
inspector can address the assessment to one holder and collection follows
the named assessed person under article 4.7. Do not treat an internal share
allocation as a limitation on that liability. [B1]

Time-share rights under article 4.2(4) have the separate article 4.10(2)
benefit calculation. Multiply by permitted-use days divided by calendar-year
days. If aggregate permitted days are less than the year, apply the stated
vacancy factor of year days divided by aggregate permitted days. Establish
the actual year, rights and statutory base first. Zero denominators, overlapping
rights and an unresolved dwelling/object classification require further
evidence; no allocation method is invented here. Article 8.70's express
cross-reference is to article 4.2(3), not a blanket statement about every
time-share holder. [B1]

Apply any established island surcharge to the national principal as its
ordinance requires. Bonaire's 30% opcenten are equal for all national taxable
objects and follow the national tax's assessment and collection method.
Keep national principal and island surcharge separate in the schedule. [L1]

## Notifications, collection and challenges

The inspector assesses annual national property tax without a preceding
return under article 4.7. This does not remove notification duties. Article
8.83a separately addresses acquisitions, physical changes and a start-of-year
holder who has received no assessment within one year after the tax calendar
year ends. Preserve that provision's further four-month notification rule;
establish the relevant year and actual deadline before an engagement date is
computed. A representative may notify for co-holders. A failure causing no or
too-low assessment can attract a penalty up to 100% of the assessment under
the statutory conditions. [B1; H3]

An ordinary assessment is collectible two months after its bill date.
Provisional assessments dated before 1 November use the statutory remaining
monthly instalments; additional assessments have a one-month period. Article
8.43(4) excludes the General Term Limits Act for those collection periods.
Do not import a generic annual due date, local land-tax instalments or
unverified weekend extensions. [B1, article 8.43]

Challenge the valuation in the objectionable valuation decision. An assessment
objection cannot be based on an incorrect determination of value. Identify
which decision, issue and deadline is being challenged; a later assessment is
not a substitute opportunity to reopen the value. [B1, articles 4.6–4.7 and
8.92; G4]

Objection or appeal does not itself suspend payment. A deferral request is a
separate written request to the receiver, who decides it under the statutory
and delegated conditions. Disputed-assessment deferral can concern the
contested portion and security can be required. Preserve the separate rules
for collection while a request is pending and the exception where state
interests may be harmed. [B1, articles 8.57, 8.96 and 8.102; R1,
articles 8.10–8.17]

Article 8.44 states 6% simple annual interest, a USD 1 minimum and further
upward rounding to a USD 0.50 multiple, with its specified timing and refund
conditions. Those are **interest** rules. This reference has not established
principal-tax rounding, a complete interest day-count method or every
minimum/zero-interest interaction. Do not reuse interest rounding or ABB
whole-dollar return presentation for national property-tax principal. [B1]

A fiscal representative must be resident or established in BES and can take
the statutory place of the taxpayer. Article 4.11 also enables mandatory
cases and treatment of a property manager as representative. This reference
does not establish that every non-resident must appoint one or supply the
complete delegated cases. Resolve the actual representation requirement.

## Hypothetical component positions

These are fictional 2026 reference examples. Eligibility, legal rights,
classification and values are stipulated as independently established. No
unresolved principal-rounding rule or actual payable assessment is selected.

1. **Independent secondary dwelling in Bonaire.** A taxable dwelling has
   established value 200,000 and qualifies for the article 4.10 allowance.
   Value base = 200,000 − 70,000 = 130,000; benefits = 130,000 × 4% = 5,200;
   national component = 5,200 × 17.5% = 910. Bonaire opcenten = 910 × 30% =
   273; combined unrounded components = 1,183. [B1; L1]
2. **Qualifying commercial hotel.** An established qualifying hotel in
   Bonaire has value 1,000,000 and no independent-dwelling allowance.
   Benefits = 40,000; national component = 40,000 × 11% = 4,400; opcenten =
   1,320; combined components = 5,720. Using the superseded 10% hotel rate
   would produce 4,000 national tax and understate that component by 400.
   This example does not establish that an actual rental qualifies. [B1;
   H1–H2; L1]
3. **Allowance boundary and fractional precision.** Qualifying independent
   dwellings valued at 69,999 or 70,000 have no value above the 70,000 branch.
   At 70,050, the excess is 50 and deemed benefits are 2. National tax is
   exactly 0.35; Bonaire opcenten are exactly 0.105; their unrounded sum is
   0.455. Retain those components. This is not an instruction to pay 0.45,
   0.46 or a whole-dollar amount; principal rounding remains a source hold.
   [B1; L1]
4. **Joint independent dwelling.** Two natural persons jointly hold one
   established qualifying independent dwelling valued at 200,000. Apply the
   stipulated joint-object allowance once: national component 910 as in
   example 1. Computing two separate 100,000 values with two 70,000
   allowances would yield 420 and understate that component by 490. An
   internal sharing arrangement does not replace the statutory assessment
   and liability rules. [B1, articles 4.2(3), 4.10(1) and 8.70]
5. **Commercial time-share arithmetic.** A taxable commercial object, not
   an independent dwelling or hotel, has value 250,000 and total benefits
   10,000. Two established non-overlapping rights allow 120 and 180 days in
   the 365-day year. Apply use factors and the vacancy factor 365/300:
   first benefits = 10,000 × 120/365 × 365/300 = 4,000; second = 6,000.
   National components are 700 and 1,050; Bonaire opcenten 210 and 315;
   combined components 910 and 1,365, totalling 2,275. This verifies the
   stipulated statutory fractions, not actual rights or a collection
   allocation. [B1, article 4.10(2); L1]
6. **Relief transition.** A qualifying increase occurs in 2026 and its
   valuation is established in 2027 under the new text: the sixth following
   calendar year starts on 1 January 2032. A 2023 increase established in
   2024 under the preserved old text reaches the eleventh following year
   on 1 January 2034. A 2024 increase established in 2025 instead reaches
   the sixth following year on 1 January 2030 under the stipulated new-text
   determination. The change year alone cannot choose the transition.
   Actual relief decisions and applicable later law remain necessary.
   [B1, article 4.8; B4; H1–H2]
7. **Transfer during the tax year.** The holder in example 1 enjoyed the
   qualifying right on 1 January 2026 and transfers it in June. The
   stipulated national annual component remains 910 under the year-start
   rule; no monthly ownership proration is supplied by that rule. The
   acquirer's separate article 8.83a notification and any private contractual
   allocation require separate treatment. [B1, articles 4.2 and 8.83a]
