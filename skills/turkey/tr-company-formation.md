# Turkey Company Formation & Entity Choice

> **General reference only.** This skill is general tax/accounting reference material for AI-assisted workflows. It has not been reviewed for any specific person's facts, documents, elections, deadlines, residency, filing status, or local procedures. Do not rely on it to file, pay, amend, or take a tax position without review by a qualified professional in the relevant jurisdiction.

**Quick answer.** A limited company needs TRY 50,000 of capital; a joint stock company TRY 250,000, with 25% of cash capital paid into a blocked bank account before registration. The articles are signed before the trade registry (or a notary for a joint stock company), filed through MERSIS, registered and announced within 30 days; the registry passes the file to the tax office. 2026 registry fees for a capital company are TRY 35,354.50 plus TRY 8,598.80 per representative, and 0.04% of capital goes to the Competition Authority. Every figure was read on 2026-11-14 in the consolidated laws on mevzuat.gov.tr or the Official Gazette.

## Scope

- **Who this is for:** founders, foreign parents and advisers forming a limited company (limited şirket, Ltd. Şti.) or a joint stock company (anonim şirket, A.Ş.) in Turkey in 2026, or opening a branch or liaison office of a foreign company, and existing companies that must raise their capital to the new minimums.
- **The vehicles.** The Turkish Commercial Code No. 6102 (TTK) has five kinds of commercial company: collective, commandite, joint stock, limited and cooperative (art. 124(1)). The joint stock company, the limited company and the commandite company with capital divided into shares are capital companies (art. 124(2)). This skill covers the limited and the joint stock company.
- **Limited company.** Formed by one or more natural or legal persons under a trade name, with a fixed capital made up of capital shares (TTK art. 573(1)); at most 50 partners (art. 574(1)). Partners are not liable for the company's debts; they owe the capital shares they took up and any additional payment and side duties set in the company contract (art. 573(2)). Any lawful business purpose (art. 573(3)).
- **Joint stock company.** Fixed capital divided into shares, liable for its debts only with its own assets (art. 329(1)); shareholders are liable only to the company and only for the capital they subscribed (art. 329(2)). One founder is enough (art. 338(1)). Any lawful economic purpose (art. 331). Some fields of activity named by Ministry communiqué need the Ministry's permission to incorporate (art. 333(1)). Public (halka açık) and non-public companies are treated separately, for example for registered capital (art. 332(1)).
- **Foreign owners.** Foreign investors may invest freely and are treated like local investors unless a treaty or special law says otherwise (Foreign Direct Investment Law No. 4875, art. 3(a)), so foreign persons can own 100% of a limited or joint stock company.
- **Out of scope:** which entity to choose (see [turkey-company-formation-entity-choice-2](turkey-company-formation-entity-choice-2.md)); corporate tax, VAT and payroll after formation ([tr-corporate-income-tax](tr-corporate-income-tax.md), [turkey-vat](turkey-vat.md), [tr-payroll-social](tr-payroll-social.md)); the headline tax figures and dates ([tr-tax-overview](tr-tax-overview.md)); collective, commandite and cooperative companies; regulated sectors (banking, insurance, capital markets).

## Ask the client first

1. Limited company or joint stock company? If joint stock: will it adopt the registered-capital system, and is it public or non-public?
2. How much capital, and how much in cash versus in kind?
3. How many founders and how many people authorised to represent the company (each pays a registry fee)?
4. Is the business in a field that needs Ministry permission to form a joint stock company (art. 333)?
5. Foreign founders: which documents need an apostille or consular legalisation and a sworn translation, and do they have a Turkish potential tax number?
6. For an existing company: is its capital below TRY 50,000 (limited) or TRY 250,000 (joint stock)?

## The method, step by step

1. **Choose the vehicle and the capital.** Check the minimums in the rates table below. A non-public joint stock company in the registered-capital system needs TRY 500,000 initial capital (başlangıç sermayesi), the capital it must hold at formation or on first entering the system (TTK art. 332(1), (2)).
2. **Check for permission.** If the field of activity is on the Ministry of Trade's art. 333 list, get the permission before signing.
3. **Draft the articles (esas sözleşme / şirket sözleşmesi).** For a joint stock company the founders state their will to form it and unconditionally undertake to pay the whole capital (art. 335(1)). A limited company contract must be in writing (art. 575(1)). No valuable-paper charge is taken on the papers containing the articles (arts. 335(1), 575(1)).
4. **Prepare the application in MERSIS.** MERSIS (Merkezi Sicil Kayıt Sistemi) is the Ministry of Trade's central registry system; the application is opened there and taken to the trade registry office where the company has its seat.
5. **Sign.** Joint stock company: founders' signatures certified by a notary, or signed before the trade registry director or deputy (art. 335(1)). Limited company: signed by the founders before authorised trade registry staff (arts. 575(1), 585(1)).
6. **Pay in the capital.**
   - Joint stock company: at least 25% of the nominal value of shares subscribed in cash before registration, the rest within 24 months after registration; any share premium in full before registration (art. 344(1)). Cash goes into a special account at a bank in the name of the company being formed, usable only by the company; the bank releases it once the registry confirms legal personality (art. 345(1)).
   - Limited company: the 25%-before-registration rule does not apply (art. 585(1)); the other payment rules apply by analogy, so cash capital is paid within 24 months after registration (arts. 344(1), 585(1)).
7. **Pay the fees.** Registry fees (2026 amounts below) are collected in advance by the chamber of commerce (or commerce and industry) against a receipt (Fees Law No. 492, art. 132). The Competition Authority share is 0.04% of capital (Law No. 4054, art. 39(1)(c)).
8. **Register and announce.** The articles are registered at the trade registry of the seat and announced in the Turkish Trade Registry Gazette within 30 days of formation (TTK art. 354(1)).
9. **Tax registration follows.** The trade registry sends a copy of a corporate taxpayer's registration file to the tax office, and the company's duty to notify the start of business is then treated as met (Tax Procedure Law No. 213, VUK, art. 153).
10. **Set up the first-year calendar** (see Filing and payment).

## Worked cases

**A foreign parent forms a joint stock company with the minimum TRY 250,000, all in cash, and one representative.** Before registration it pays at least 25% into the blocked account: TRY 62,500. The Competition Authority share is 0.04% of 250,000: TRY 100. Registry fees: TRY 35,354.50 for the business and trade name plus TRY 8,598.80 for the one representative: TRY 43,953.30. Fees in total: TRY 44,053.30. Notary, Trade Registry Gazette announcement and chamber registration charges come on top and are not in this example.

## When to refuse or refer

- Banks, insurers, capital markets institutions and other regulated sectors: refer; they need sector licences outside this skill.
- A field on the art. 333 permission list: do not file until the Ministry's permission is in hand.
- An existing company that may already be treated as dissolved, or that misses the 31 December 2027 deadline: refer to a Turkish lawyer.
- Contributions in kind (valuation by expert), mergers, conversions and share transfers: refer.
- Do not quote notary, Gazette announcement or chamber fees, or a typical formation time: none was confirmed from an official source.

## Completion checklist

- [ ] Vehicle and capital meet the 2026 minimum.
- [ ] Art. 333 permission checked.
- [ ] Articles signed before the registry (or a notary for a joint stock company).
- [ ] 25% of cash capital in the blocked account (joint stock company).
- [ ] Registry fees and the 0.04% Competition Authority share paid.
- [ ] Registered and announced within 30 days.
- [ ] Tax office record confirmed after registration.
- [ ] Remaining capital diarised for 24 months after registration; first general meeting within 3 months of year-end.
