# Saint Lucia VAT

## Scope and source version

Source-cited draft checked 22 October 2026. The Attorney General Chambers publishes the Revised Laws (2023). The sections below were directly read; a complete post-2023 amendment audit remains outstanding. Older registration brochures still referring to EC$180,000 are not used for the threshold.

## Registration

Under [section 12](https://attorneygeneralchambers.com/laws-of-saint-lucia/value-added-tax-act/section-12), apply within ten working days after a period of twelve or fewer months in which taxable supplies **exceed EC$400,000**, or from the beginning of a 365-day period where supplies are reasonably expected to exceed that amount. A separate test uses supplies above EC$100,000 in three months and an expectation above EC$400,000 over those months plus the next nine; application is within ten working days after the third month.

Special rules cover auctioneers, public entertainment and government bodies. Related persons acting together can be considered by the Comptroller. Do not treat the ordinary threshold as universal.

Example: EC$120,000 of taxable supplies over three months plus a supported EC$300,000 forecast for the next nine meets both three-month tests: 120,000 > 100,000 and 420,000 > 400,000. Exactly EC$400,000 does not exceed the ordinary threshold, but other registration triggers must still be checked.

## Rates and arithmetic

[Section 10](https://attorneygeneralchambers.com/laws-of-saint-lucia/value-added-tax-act/section-10) specifies the standard rate of 12.5%, subject to zero-rating and special rates. For a supply established as standard-rated and valued at EC$8,000 before VAT, tax is EC$1,000 and the invoice total EC$9,000. Conversely, EC$9,000 inclusive of 12.5% VAT contains EC$1,000 VAT: 9,000 × 12.5/112.5.

The [tourism order, S.I. 188/2020, paragraph 3](https://attorneygeneralchambers.com/laws-of-saint-lucia/value-added-tax-act/section-3-01) specifies 7% for tourism accommodation and 10% for its listed restaurant, water-sports, tour and attraction supplies. Confirm the statutory definitions and classification; do not apply an accommodation rate to every item supplied by a hotel.

## Returns

[Section 37](https://attorneygeneralchambers.com/laws-of-saint-lucia/value-added-tax-act/section-37) requires a return for each tax period within 21 calendar days after period-end, including periods with no tax payable, subject to section 38. Keep return and payment obligations separate; filing a return does not prove payment.

## Working-paper controls and exclusions

Retain registration-trigger evidence, supply classification and separate tax calculations. Reconcile output VAT to invoices, and support every input deduction independently. This Guide does not establish recoverability for all purchases, refund entitlement or a zero-rating/exemption list.

Payment mechanics, invoices, mixed supplies, input apportionment, imports, current sector amendments and the Health and Citizen Security Levy need separate checks. The examples calculate VAT only, not the total of every tax or levy. Independent review and amendment verification remain merge requirements.
