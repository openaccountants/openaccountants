## Commonwealth and applied Western Australian law

Christmas Island is an Australian external territory. Under section 8E of
the Christmas Island Act 1958, Commonwealth Acts extend of their own force
unless that Act or another Act expressly provides otherwise. The territory's
own ordinances and a federal Act's specific definitions therefore matter.
[GOVERNANCE]; [TERRITORY, section 8E][TERRITORY]

Section 8A applies Western Australian laws subject to its conditions,
section 8G and the relevant court provisions. An ordinance can incorporate,
amend, repeal or suspend an applied law; inconsistent applied provisions
have no effect to the extent specified. Service delivery or a Western
Australian postal address does not establish an unmodified Western
Australian revenue regime. [TERRITORY, section 8A][TERRITORY]

For a state-type tax, duty, land transaction, rates notice or service charge,
identify the applied law, current territory modification, responsible body,
charge period and exemptions. This guide has not verified a complete
territorial revenue-law inventory. Record the unresolved charge instead of
asserting either mainland liability or a blanket exemption.

Use `cx-income-tax` for selected 2026-27 ordinary personal rates, LITO
and Medicare screening. Its incomplete income-tax slots still prevent a
complete individual-return result.

Use `cx-super-guarantee` with the social contributions base for selected
2026-27 employer SG, annual-base and fund-receipt rules. It does not
complete the payroll or all-programme social contribution slots.

## Income tax, residence, period and Medicare

The 1936 Act's Australia definition adopts the 1997 Act's meaning. Section
960-505(1)(d)-(e) expressly includes Christmas Island and the Cocos (Keeling)
Islands in geographical Australia. Section 995-1 connects Australian-resident
and foreign-resident status to the 1936 Act. Geographical inclusion does not
by itself decide tax residence or the source of a particular receipt.
[IT36, section 6(1)][IT36]; [DICTIONARY, sections 960-505 and 995-1][DICTIONARY]

For an individual, section 6(1) includes ordinary residence and the separate
domicile, presence and specified Commonwealth-superannuation branches,
with their conditions. The presence branch refers to more than half the
income year and its usual-abode and intention exception; it is not a
universal day-count rule. The company test separately considers Australian
incorporation or Australian business with central management and control
or resident-controlled voting power. Confirm trust and partnership treatment
under their own rules. This guide does not decide an actual person's,
company's or trust's residence. [IT36, section 6(1)][IT36]

Sections 6-5 and 6-10 distinguish residents' worldwide ordinary/statutory
income from foreign residents' Australian-source income and other statutory
inclusions. Section 6-15 preserves exempt and non-assessable non-exempt
branches. Income character, source, derivation, temporary-resident treatment,
treaties and specific exclusions need their own authority and facts; island
residence is not a blanket offshore-income exemption. [IT97, sections
6-5, 6-10 and 6-15][IT97]

The Commissioner of Taxation administers the income-tax Act. Keep the
financial year, income year and any Commissioner-approved substituted
accounting period distinct. Do not infer a return or payment date from the
ordinary year end. [IT97, sections 1-7 and 4-10][IT97]; [IT36, sections
6(1) and 18][IT36]

Current section 251S generally imposes Medicare levy by reference to the
specified income and taxpayer branches, including an individual resident
at any time in the income year. Sections 251T and 251U contain conditional
prescribed-person rules, including dependant conditions, and distinguish
ordinary levy from surcharge treatment. Neither a Christmas Island address,
health-service access nor historical territorial guidance proves a current
levy exemption. Establish tax residence, benefit entitlement and any required
certificate, dependants, exempt periods and the current Levy Acts before
calculating ordinary levy or surcharge. No levy or surcharge amount is
computed here. [MEDICARE, sections 251S-251VA][MEDICARE]

## GST: island activity and other connections

The GST Act section 195-1 indirect-tax-zone definition excludes external
territories. ATO LCR 2016/1 paragraphs 25-27 identifies both Christmas Island
and Cocos within that exclusion. The ruling's captured HTML is guidance;
its page identifies the PDF as the authorised consolidated version. The
statutory definition supplies the territorial rule here. [GST2]; [GST-RULING]

A taxable supply must meet section 9-5, including connection with the
indirect tax zone and registration or required registration, and must not
be GST-free or input taxed to that extent. Section 9-25 distinguishes goods,
real property and other things. Goods wholly delivered and made available
on the island do not meet the wholly-within-zone goods branch merely
because the supplier is Australian-resident or registered. Establish every
potential connection and applicable special rule before a conclusion.
[GST1, sections 9-5 and 9-25][GST1]

Mainland goods delivery, exports from the zone, supplier importation,
services done through a mainland enterprise, digital supplies and mixed
transactions require separate connection and exemption analysis. The
Australian-consumer definition has a specific exception for residence
arising solely from the external-territory geographical definition; do not
apply it to every island resident without testing the condition. Registration
alone neither taxes every island transaction nor proves every mainland
transaction is GST-free. [GST1, section 9-25, particularly (5) and (7)][GST1]

ABF guidance says normal export requirements apply to goods sent from
Australia to Christmas, Cocos and Norfolk. A territorial destination is
not proof that every GST export condition is met. Actual registration,
export timing, evidence, credits, returns and refunds require the complete
operative rules, including applicable regulations. [EXPORT]

## Customs controls, excise and direction of movement

Federal Customs Act 1901 section 6 generally excludes external territories,
with its specified Ashmore and Cartier regulation power. Read that with
the local Customs Ordinance 1993 (CI): section 4 reapplies the listed
Commonwealth customs laws as territory laws, as in force from time to time
and subject to modifications, **except to the extent they deal with customs
duties**. The ordinance also preserves tariff classification and territorial
form conditions. This excludes duty provisions from that reapplication; it
does not abolish customs controls, permits, declarations or other laws.
[CUSTOMS-FED, section 6][CUSTOMS-FED]; [CUSTOMS-LOCAL, sections 4-5 and
Schedules 1-3][CUSTOMS-LOCAL]

Schedule 1 item 1 inserts an application provision under which movements
between Christmas Island and the Cocos (Keeling) Islands are not imports
or exports to which that applied Act applies. Keep that specific exception
separate from mainland or third-country movements and from biosecurity,
transport, licensing and local service requirements. [CUSTOMS-LOCAL,
Schedule 1 item 1][CUSTOMS-LOCAL]

Goods moving from this territory into Australia have a distinct conditional
customs-duty exemption under Christmas Island Act 1958 section 21: they must
be the territory's produce or manufacture, shipped there for export to
Australia, and not goods which, if made or produced in Australia, would be
subject to excise. Mere storage, purchase or transshipment on the island
establishes none of those conditions. The exemption does not decide GST,
clearance or other charges. [TERRITORY, section 21][TERRITORY]

Excise Act section 4 expressly excludes this territory from that Act's
Australia definition. Verify any mainland manufacture, removal, export,
remission and other charging law separately. This guide does not calculate
excise, wine equalisation tax, luxury car tax, fuel credits, biosecurity
charges, freight, port fees or an overall landed cost. [EXCISE, section 4][EXCISE]

## Keep other territories' asset transitions separate

Section 102-25 of the Income Tax (Transitional Provisions) Act 1997 contains
conditional Cocos and Norfolk asset transitions. Subsection (1) addresses
historical prescribed status because of **Cocos residence** and assets
acquired on or before **30 June 1991**; subsection (2) addresses Norfolk
status and its separate October 2015 conditions. Neither grants rebasing
simply because an asset or its owner is on Christmas Island. An entity with
relevant historical Cocos or Norfolk facts needs the full applicable branch;
this guide does not reject that branch merely because its current address
is on Christmas Island. [TRANS, section 102-25][TRANS]

For an actual disposal, establish the taxpayer and residence history,
acquisition and event dates, asset character, proceeds, cost base, losses,
choices and relief under operative CGT law. This guide supplies no universal
Christmas Island exemption, market-value reset, net capital gain, discount
or withholding computation.
