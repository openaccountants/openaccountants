# Canada: CPP, EI and QPP not withheld, and the PIER

> **General reference only.** This skill is general tax/accounting reference material for AI-assisted workflows. It has not been reviewed for any specific person's facts, documents, elections, deadlines, residency, filing status, or local procedures. Do not rely on it to file, pay, amend, or take a tax position without review by a qualified professional in the relevant jurisdiction.

## Scope

Use this Guide when an employer discovers missed or insufficient employee Canada Pension Plan (CPP), Employment Insurance (EI) or Quebec Pension Plan (QPP) deductions, or receives a CRA pensionable and insurable earnings review (PIER). It addresses correction and recovery, rather than calculating the original payroll deduction. Keep the actual payroll year, pay dates and that year's forms: the first case concerns 2025 and the second concerns 2026.

## Ask the client first

Collect the payroll records, contribution type, employees, pay dates, original deduction amounts, amounts actually remitted, T4/RL-1 slips, and any PIER or assessment with its reply date. Identify any remaining remuneration payments and the date of each missed deduction.

Review province of employment from the reporting and employer-establishment facts rather than the employee's home address. Remote, foreign or mixed-province cases need the referral below. [CRA province-of-employment guidance](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/payroll/set-up-new-employee/determine-province-employment.html)

Confirm pensionable/insurable employment, age, elections and exemptions before accepting a shortfall. The examples assume that the given shortfalls are correctly established; they do not establish these missing eligibility facts.

## The method, step by step

### 1. Establish the employer's liability

After confirming that deductions were required and that no written-ruling exception applies, an employer that under-deducted CPP or EI is responsible for remitting the unpaid balance of both employee and employer shares, even if it cannot recover the employee share. Do not add a share already remitted. [CRA corrections](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/payroll/calculating-deductions/making-corrections.html); [CPP s. 21(2)](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/C-8/section-21.html); [EI Act s. 82(4)](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/E-5.6/section-82.html)

| Employer contribution | Amount for the ordinary case | Official source |
|---|---|---|
| CPP | Same amount as the employee's required CPP contribution | [CRA](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/digital-services-businesses/business-account/respond-notices.html) |
| EI | 1.4 times the employee's required premium, unless a reduced employer rate is approved | [CRA](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/digital-services-businesses/business-account/respond-notices.html) |

The employer's own EI premium cannot be recovered from the employee by withholding or otherwise, despite any contract to the contrary. [EI Act s. 84](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/E-5.6/section-84.html)

### 2. Recover missed CPP or EI only within the payroll limits

For CPP, recovery may be deducted from a subsequent remuneration payment made within 12 months after the payment from which the deduction was missed. On each subsequent pay, in addition to the normal deduction, recover no more than one previously missed amount. EI has the same limits. Track each missed payment separately; discovery of the error does not restart the clock. [CPP s. 21(4)](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/C-8/section-21.html); [EI Act s. 82(6)](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/E-5.6/section-82.html)

The recovered amount must not exceed the contribution or premium that should have been deducted from the original payment. Do not adjust the employee's income-tax deduction to cover the shortfall. A prior-year CPP or EI amount recovered in the current year is not reported on the current year's T4. [CRA corrections](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/payroll/calculating-deductions/making-corrections.html)

Leaving employment does not by itself prove that no subsequent remuneration is payable. If no later remuneration payment is available within the applicable period, there is no recovery through this payroll route. Other recovery rights and the tax treatment of an amount the employer absorbs are referred below.

### 3. Answer a PIER

The CRA compares CPP and EI required from the pensionable and insurable earnings reported on T4 slips with the reported contributions and premiums. The PIER package lists affected employees and the amounts used, a summary balance and a remittance voucher. Check those earnings and deductions against the payroll records, exemptions, age, elections and province facts before agreeing. [CRA PIER](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/payroll/file-information-returns-slip-summaries/receiving-payroll/earnings-review.html)

| Timeline | CRA process | Official source |
|---|---|---|
| Day 1 | Initial package requests a reply within 30 calendar days | [CRA](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/payroll/file-information-returns-slip-summaries/receiving-payroll/earnings-review.html) |
| Day 45 | Assessment issued if neither a reply nor full payment is made; follow the reply date on the PIER | [CRA](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/payroll/file-information-returns-slip-summaries/receiving-payroll/earnings-review.html) |
| Day 65 | CRA issues amended T4 slips to the employer | [CRA](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/payroll/file-information-returns-slip-summaries/receiving-payroll/earnings-review.html) |

- **Agree and pay:** no reply is required if you agree with the CRA's calculation and will pay the exact PIER amount by its deadline.
- **Disagree:** return the PIER with corrected information and an explanation through My Business Account or Represent a Client, by mail to the PIER return address, or by fax to the relevant National Verification and Collection Centre.
- **Amended T4s:** do not submit amended T4 slips because of the PIER. If amendments are required, the CRA prepares two copies and sends them to you: keep one and give the other to the employee.
- **Several payroll accounts:** the CRA compares all T4 information returns under the business number and contacts the employer if it finds CPP or EI deficiencies.

Source for these response instructions: [CRA PIER](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/payroll/file-information-returns-slip-summaries/receiving-payroll/earnings-review.html).

### 4. Interest and penalties

Unremitted CPP and EI amounts bear interest at the prescribed rate from the remittance due date until remittance. [CPP s. 21(6)](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/C-8/section-21.html); [EI Act s. 82(8)](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/E-5.6/section-82.html)

A PIER assessment includes applicable penalties or interest, or both. This Guide does not calculate them or treat the late-remittance table as the penalty for every failure to deduct. For the applicable penalty, relief request or dispute of an assessment: Check this point with one of our partner accountants. [CRA PIER](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/payroll/file-information-returns-slip-summaries/receiving-payroll/earnings-review.html)

### 5. Quebec QPP correction

If employee QPP contributions were insufficient, remit the missing employee amounts to Revenu Québec together with the employer contributions. [Revenu Québec, insufficient contributions](https://www.revenuquebec.ca/fr/entreprises/retenues-a-la-source-et-cotisations-de-lemployeur/calcul-des-retenues-et-des-cotisations/cotisations-au-rrq/total-des-cotisations-au-regime-de-rentes-du-quebec-payees-dans-lannee/)

After paying the employee contributions from the employer's funds, recovery can be made from pensionable salary or wages paid in the 12 months following the date the deduction should have been made. Recover the equivalent of only one missed contribution on each payment, alongside the normal contribution. [Revenu Québec](https://www.revenuquebec.ca/fr/entreprises/retenues-a-la-source-et-cotisations-de-lemployeur/calcul-des-retenues-et-des-cotisations/cotisations-au-rrq/total-des-cotisations-au-regime-de-rentes-du-quebec-payees-dans-lannee/)

If the employee does not reimburse the employer, the amount becomes a taxable benefit reported in boxes A and L of the employee's RL-1. If a prior-year missed amount is recovered this year, do not put the recovered contribution on this year's RL-1: amend the RL-1 for the previous year instead. Detailed benefit calculations and any RLZ-1.S correction are referred below. [Revenu Québec](https://www.revenuquebec.ca/fr/entreprises/retenues-a-la-source-et-cotisations-de-lemployeur/calcul-des-retenues-et-des-cotisations/cotisations-au-rrq/total-des-cotisations-au-regime-de-rentes-du-quebec-payees-dans-lannee/)

Quebec has its own review process. The **2026** RL-1 summary guide, RLZ-1.S.G (2026-10), §3.2 says Revenu Québec makes a summary examination of the RL-1 slips and summary. If contributions are insufficient, it may send an assessment with revised amounts or forms to complete and return, including LMU-141 or LMU-141.1 for employee and employer QPP contributions. Follow the actual notice; do not apply the CRA PIER timetable to it. The same guide identifies TP-1015.G as the employer guide for calculating and periodically paying source deductions and employer contributions. [Revenu Québec, §3.1–3.2](https://www.revenuquebec.ca/fr/services-en-ligne/formulaires-et-publications/rlz-1-s-g/guide-du-sommaire-1-sommaire-des-retenues-et-des-cotisations-de-lemployeur/)

## Worked cases

The shortfalls are hypothetical inputs from an agreed PIER or verified payroll records, not deductions calculated from gross wages here. Assume pensionable/insurable employment, no ruling exception, matching CPP employer amounts, no employer amounts already remitted, and the ordinary EI employer multiplier where relevant. Confirm these facts for a real case.

“Employer cost” in the check blocks means **cash funded by the employer after the illustrated payroll recoveries**, before penalties, interest, any further recovery, benefit-related charges or income-tax treatment. It is not a final accounting expense or tax deduction.

### PIER shows CPP and EI shortfalls for one employee who has left

A 2025 PIER shows employee CPP short by $400 and EI short by $120; the employer agrees. The employee left in December 2025. For this illustration, assume no further remuneration is payable and no employee reimbursement is received: recoverable pays are zero. Employer CPP is $400; standard employer EI is $120 × 1.4 = $168. Total unpaid contributions and premiums are $400 + $400 + $120 + $168 = **$1,088**, all funded by the employer under those assumptions. The reduced-EI-rate and other recovery cases are outside the illustration.

Paying the exact PIER amount by its deadline requires no reply. Penalties and interest are outside the $1,088. [CPP/EI matching](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/digital-services-businesses/business-account/respond-notices.html); [recovery limits](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/C-8/section-21.html); [PIER](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/payroll/file-information-returns-slip-summaries/receiving-payroll/earnings-review.html)

```oa-check
name: PIER deficiency, employee and employer shares, no recovery possible
inputs:
  cpp_employee_short: 400
  ei_employee_short: 120
  recoverable_pays: 0
steps:
  cpp_employer: cpp_employee_short
  ei_employer: ei_employee_short * 1.4
  total_due: cpp_employee_short + cpp_employer + ei_employee_short + ei_employer
  employee_share_recovered: min(cpp_employee_short + ei_employee_short, recoverable_pays)
  employer_cost: total_due - employee_share_recovered
expected:
  ei_employer: 168
  total_due: 1088
  employer_cost: 1088
```

### Shortfall found in time: recover from later pay, or absorb it

A current employee is paid every two weeks. Four pays in October and November 2026 each missed a required $100 CPP deduction. The employer discovers the error in December 2026. Assume each of the next four remuneration payments occurs within 12 months of the respective missed payment and supports the normal deduction plus one missed $100. Recover $400 across those four pays; remit the employee $400 and employer $400 shortfalls, for **$800** total. The employer funds **$400** after recovery.

If the employee is about to leave and only two such pays remain, assume no other subsequent remuneration or reimbursement: recover **$200**, leaving **$600** funded by the employer. Apply the time limit to each missed payment separately; do not delay the employer's remittance while waiting to recover. [CPP liability and recovery, s. 21](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/C-8/section-21.html); [matching](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/e-services/digital-services-businesses/business-account/respond-notices.html)

```oa-check
name: Recovery from later pay within the 12-month and one-per-pay limits
inputs:
  missed_pays: 4
  missed_per_pay: 100
  pays_left_if_staying: 4
  pays_left_if_leaving: 2
steps:
  employee_short: missed_pays * missed_per_pay
  employer_share: employee_short
  total_remitted: employee_short + employer_share
  recovered_if_staying: min(missed_pays, pays_left_if_staying) * missed_per_pay
  recovered_if_leaving: min(missed_pays, pays_left_if_leaving) * missed_per_pay
  employer_cost_staying: total_remitted - recovered_if_staying
  employer_cost_leaving: total_remitted - recovered_if_leaving
expected:
  total_remitted: 800
  recovered_if_staying: 400
  recovered_if_leaving: 200
  employer_cost_staying: 400
  employer_cost_leaving: 600
```

## When to refuse or refer

For each situation below: **Check this point with one of our partner accountants.**

- A written CRA ruling that no deduction was required was later reversed; worker status, exemptions, age, elections or province of employment are disputed.
- Money was deducted but not remitted; the employer is insolvent; directors' liability or prosecution is in issue.
- The employee's pension contribution record, deemed contributions or notification deadlines need correction.
- Recovery outside the permitted payroll route, an employer-absorbed employee contribution, a taxable-benefit calculation, or the final accounting/tax cost needs determining.
- Over-deductions, PD24 refunds, slip corrections outside a PIER, or a Quebec RLZ-1.S amendment are needed.
- An approved reduced EI employer rate or an unusual payment-timing case affects the employer share.
- The applicable federal or Quebec penalty, Quebec interest, an offence, penalty/interest relief (including RC4288), or a formal objection/appeal needs determining.
- Original CPP/EI/QPP deduction calculations, self-employed CPP, or a large or multi-year shortfall needs resolving.

This Guide does not file a reply, amend a slip or make a payment.
