# Australian residential rental property tax

Prepare a separate reconciliation for each property and owner. This Guide covers individuals' Australian residential investment property for 2025–26 and 2026–27, with a separate enacted-law note for 2027–28 onwards. Sources were read on 27 November 2026; the approval date above is unchanged.

## Required inputs

Establish the income year, title and ownership shares, rent and agent statements, rental and private-use periods, advertisements and rental terms, loan drawdowns and repayments, invoices and the condition when acquired. For capital claims obtain asset costs, first-use dates, effective lives and methods, construction commencement/completion dates and eligible construction expenditure. For a sale obtain the contract, proceeds, cost-base records, main-residence history and tax residency.

## Reconcile gross income and ownership

Declare gross rent paid to you or your agent, before agent fees. Include retained bonds for unpaid rent or damage requiring repairs, lost-rent insurance and reimbursements of deductible expenses; record the deductible expense separately. Compensation for damage to a capital asset needs its own classification: do not automatically treat every insurance receipt as rent. [ATO rental income](https://www.ato.gov.au/individuals-and-families/investments-and-assets/property-and-land/residential-rental-properties/rental-income-you-must-declare).

For co-owners outside a rental business, allocate shared income and expenses by legal interest, regardless of who pays the bills or a private allocation agreement. Interest and borrowing expenses incurred by an owner to acquire their own interest are claimed by that owner, rather than divided again. If beneficial ownership differs from title, or the activity constitutes a partnership business, the ordinary allocation is not a concluded answer here. Check this point with one of our partner accountants. [ATO rental properties guide 2026, co-ownership](https://www.ato.gov.au/law/view/document?PiT=20260701000001&docid=SAV%2FRENTAL%2F00001).

Distinguish domestic contributions to shared household costs from payment for a lease or licence. Commercial letting to family or friends follows the ordinary rules; below-market letting has a private element requiring fair apportionment. The ATO accepts limiting otherwise deductible expenses to rental income as fair and reasonable where those expenses exceed that rent. [ATO rental properties guide 2026, domestic arrangements](https://www.ato.gov.au/law/view/document?PiT=20260701000001&docid=SAV%2FRENTAL%2F00001).

## Establish the deductible use

Keep advertisements, agent agreements, bookings, lease terms and evidence of market rent, realistic access and actual efforts to obtain tenants. Apportion mixed expenses fairly for private use, the area rented and relevant periods. Costs solely attributable to obtaining tenants can be fully attributable to rent even when shared holding costs need apportionment. Vacancy alone does not deny a deduction where the property is genuinely held to earn rent; advertising with unrealistic restrictions does not establish that use. [ATO rental properties guide 2026, apportionment](https://www.ato.gov.au/law/view/document?PiT=20260701000001&docid=SAV%2FRENTAL%2F00001).

Some pre-tenant interest, rates and holding costs qualify where the property is held to produce assessable rental income, with evidence of that purpose. First apply section 26-102: vacant-land holding costs are generally denied for an ordinary individual investor; newly constructed or substantially renovated residential premises must be lawfully occupiable and leased or available for lease before they stop being disregarded as a structure. Construction/renovation costs and associated construction borrowing must be distinguished from holding the vacant land. Business, entity and exceptional-circumstance exceptions require separate facts; do not claim an exception on the basis of this Guide. Check this point with one of our partner accountants. [ATO vacant land and pre-rental expenses](https://www.ato.gov.au/law/view/document?PiT=20260701000001&docid=SAV%2FRENTAL%2F00001).

### Holiday homes: section 26-50

A property used or held for the owner's holidays or recreation, or family/friends' free or discounted holidays, can be a leisure facility. Ownership and use expenses are denied unless an exception applies. For the ordinary investor, the rental exception requires use or holding mainly to produce assessable rents at all times in the year; a definite change of main use can permit a reasonable deduction for the qualifying part of the year. Advertising for a few weeks, or counting days alone, does not establish the exception: consider actual use, reserved private use and access during peak demand. Where the exception applies, still exclude private use under the ordinary deduction rules. Booking, tenant advertising and rental cleaning costs outside the ownership/use category remain subject to their own income-producing nexus. [TR 2026/1, paragraphs 31–47](https://www.ato.gov.au/law/view/document?DocID=TXR%2FTR20261%2FNAT%2FATO%2F00001&PiT=99991231235958).

The ATO's transitional compliance approach does not devote resources to reviewing section 26-50 for holiday-home expenses incurred before 1 July 2026, except avoidance, fraud, evasion or inappropriate advantage. This is an administrative approach, not a repeal or permission to deduct private expenses; requested assessments, amendments and rulings follow the ruling's view. [TR 2026/1, paragraphs 137–140](https://www.ato.gov.au/law/view/document?DocID=TXR%2FTR20261%2FNAT%2FATO%2F00001&PiT=99991231235958).

Private redraws create a private loan component. Repayments of a mixed-purpose loan generally reduce rental and private components proportionately; they cannot all be directed to private debt by choice. Keep the running allocation: interest follows use of the borrowed funds. [ATO interest](https://www.ato.gov.au/individuals-and-families/investments-and-assets/property-and-land/residential-rental-properties/rental-expenses/interest-expenses).

For repairs, identify the asset or entirety, condition at purchase, cause of deterioration and nature of the change. Modern materials can restore an asset without making the work an improvement. Separate repair and improvement costs on itemised invoices; a capital component does not become deductible because it shares an invoice with a repair. [ATO repairs](https://www.ato.gov.au/individuals-and-families/investments-and-assets/property-and-land/residential-rental-properties/rental-expenses/repair-and-maintenance-expenses), [ATO rental guide, roof repair](https://www.ato.gov.au/law/view/document?PiT=20260701000001&docid=SAV%2FRENTAL%2F00001).

## Separate plant from capital works

Division 40 concerns depreciating assets such as appliances. Record cost, start time, effective life, method and taxable-use proportion for each asset. Start time is first use or installation ready for any use; deductions are reduced for non-taxable use. Use a supportable effective life, not a building rate for all contents. [ITAA 1997 ss 40-25, 40-60 and 40-65](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2004A05138/2026-08-27/2026-08-27/text/original/epub/OEBPS/document_2/document_2.html).

Second-hand asset restrictions generally deny the residential rental portion of an individual's Division 40 deduction outside a business. They cover prior use by another holder or the owner's prior private/non-taxable use (subject to the statutory occasional-use distinction). They apply from 2017–18 to relevant acquisitions/contracts at or after 7:30 pm ACT time on 9 May 2017, and to older assets whose start time was in 2016–17 or earlier for which no deduction was available in 2016–17. A genuinely new appliance bought for the rental does not become second-hand merely because the building is old. [ITAA 1997 s 40-27](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2004A05138/2026-08-27/2026-08-27/text/original/epub/OEBPS/document_2/document_2.html), [2017 Act, Schedule 2 item 13](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2017A00126/asmade/text).

Business and specified-entity exceptions exist. The new-residential-premises exception also requires the asset to be supplied with the new premises, used or installed there (or associated supplied property), no previous holder's depreciation entitlement, and the occupancy conditions in section 40-27(4)–(5). It is not a general exception for buying an unused-looking appliance in an established home. Do not use a low-value pool to bypass a denied deduction. For an exception not established by the evidence, no deduction is concluded here. Check this point with one of our partner accountants. [ITAA 1997 s 40-27](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2004A05138/2026-08-27/2026-08-27/text/original/epub/OEBPS/document_2/document_2.html), [ATO second-hand assets](https://www.ato.gov.au/individuals-and-families/investments-and-assets/property-and-land/residential-rental-properties/rental-expenses/depreciating-assets-in-rental-properties/second-hand-depreciating-assets).

Division 43 applies to eligible construction expenditure, not the property's purchase price. Obtain actual costs or an appropriately qualified estimate. Claim only after construction is complete and for eligible income-producing use, within the remaining construction-expenditure entitlement. Record deductions claimed or claimable for the later CGT cost-base adjustment. [ATO capital works](https://www.ato.gov.au/individuals-and-families/investments-and-assets/property-and-land/residential-rental-properties/rental-expenses/capital-expenses/work-out-your-capital-works-deductions).

## Calculate the result and check a sale

For 2025–26 and 2026–27, subtract eligible deductions from gross rent, allocate shared amounts and owner-specific costs correctly, and reconcile each owner's net rental result. An eligible rental loss can generally reduce other assessable income, subject to specific restrictions and tax-loss rules. A tax reduction is not necessarily an immediate refund; depreciation does not itself represent current cash expenditure. [ATO negative gearing](https://www.ato.gov.au/law/view/document?PiT=20260701000001&docid=SAV%2FRENTAL%2F00001), [ITAA 1997 s 8-1](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2004A05138/2026-08-27/2026-08-27/text/original/epub/OEBPS/document_1/document_1.html).

### Enacted reform: 2027–28 and later

The Treasury Laws Amendment (Tax Reform No. 1) Act 2026 is enacted. Schedule 2 commenced on 27 June 2026 but applies to 2027–28 and later income years, not the two earlier years above. Section 26-155 quarantines otherwise deductible residential-dwelling expenditure exceeding relevant assessable residential income: the excess is not deductible that year, may reduce qualifying capital gains under section 102-5, and any remainder carries forward as residential expenditure. It is not automatically a CGT cost-base addition. [Act No. 49, s 2 and Schedule 2](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2026A00049/asmade/text).

The calculation excludes relevant interests last acquired before 7:30 pm ACT time on 12 May 2026 (contract time applies) and qualifying new residential dwellings, among statutory exceptions. New-dwelling status depends on requirements under section 26-160, not simply the seller calling it new. The computation also accounts for net income from non-quarantined dwellings and relevant revenue-asset gains. [Schedule 2, ss 26-155–26-160](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2026A00049/asmade/text).

The enacted No. 2 Act, Schedule 4, also applies from 2027–28: it addresses the section 118-192 first-income-use deemed acquisition and extends exceptions in specified death and relationship-breakdown situations. These are conditional extensions, not universal grandfathering of transferred property. For an actual later-year exception, new-dwelling instrument, carry-forward or CGT interaction not established here, do not issue a calculated entitlement. Check this point with one of our partner accountants. [Act No. 71, Schedule 4](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2026A00071/asmade/text).

### Sale reconciliation

Use the contract date for the ordinary CGT event, reconcile proceeds against the adjusted cost base, and allocate each owner's interest. Track capital works claimed or claimable. Main-residence periods and foreign-resident restrictions require separate analysis; no exemption or discount is established by this Guide. Check this point with one of our partner accountants. [ATO rental guide, CGT](https://www.ato.gov.au/law/view/document?PiT=20260701000001&docid=SAV%2FRENTAL%2F00001).

| --- | --- | --- |
| Australian property contracts signed from 1 January 2025 | FRCGW is 15% of the relevant market value with no value threshold; resident vendors provide an ATO clearance certificate to the purchaser before settlement to avoid withholding. Foreign vendors provide an ATO variation notice by settlement to obtain a reduced rate. Amounts withheld and paid to the ATO can be claimed as a tax credit; withholding is not final CGT. | [ATO FRCGW](https://www.ato.gov.au/individuals-and-families/investments-and-assets/capital-gains-tax/foreign-residents-and-capital-gains-tax/foreign-resident-capital-gains-withholding/foreign-resident-capital-gains-withholding-overview) |

For the credit, retain the ATO payment confirmation. [ATO 18 Capital gains 2026](https://www.ato.gov.au/forms-and-instructions/tax-return-for-individuals-2026-supplementary-section/income-questions-13-24-supplementary-tax-return-2026/18-capital-gains-2026).

## Worked cases

These arithmetic illustrations use expressly assumed eligible expenditure; they do not establish eligibility for an actual expense.

### A. Ordinary rental reconciliation, 2025–26

Assume an investment property outside a business, no private use, equal legal shares, gross rent AUD 32,000, and AUD 40,000 of already verified shared deductions. There are no owner-specific expenses. The property loss is AUD 8,000; each owner reports AUD 16,000 income and AUD 20,000 deductions, giving an AUD 4,000 loss before applying any personal restrictions.

```oa-check
name: Equal owners rental reconciliation 2025-26
inputs:
  rent: 32000
  eligible_shared_expenses: 40000
  share: 0.5
steps:
  - property_result: rent - eligible_shared_expenses
  - owner_income: rent * share
  - owner_deductions: eligible_shared_expenses * share
  - owner_result: owner_income - owner_deductions
expected:
  property_result: -8000
  owner_income: 16000
  owner_deductions: 20000
  owner_result: -4000
```

### B. Ordinary capital works, 2026–27

Assume eligible residential construction begun and completed in 2005, eligible construction expenditure AUD 200,000, sufficient undeducted expenditure, sole ownership and qualifying rental use for the full 365 days. At 2.5%, the deduction is AUD 5,000. The purchase price is not the calculation base.

```oa-check
name: Eligible residential construction 2026-27
inputs:
  eligible_construction: 200000
  rate: 0.025
  qualifying_days: 365
steps:
  - capital_works: eligible_construction * rate * qualifying_days / 365
expected:
  capital_works: 5000
```
