# Colombia: corporate income tax, año gravable 2026 v1.0

> **General reference only.** This skill is general tax/accounting reference material for AI-assisted workflows. It has not been reviewed for any specific person's facts, documents, elections, deadlines, residency, filing status, or local procedures. Do not rely on it to file, pay, amend, or take a tax position without review by a qualified professional in the relevant jurisdiction.

**Tax year.** Año gravable 2026 (calendar year), declared on Form 110 in 2027. One UVT is **COP 52,374** for 2026 (DIAN Resolución 000238 of 15 December 2025).

**Which text of the law.** The Estatuto Tributario (ET) as consolidated on the Senate's official site, updated there to 15 October 2026. Art. 240 is in the text given by Ley 2277 de 2022. The 2026 emergency decrees (Decretos Legislativos 150, 173 and 240 de 2026) changed the wealth tax and some IVA items; they do not change art. 240. Deadlines: Decreto Único Reglamentario 1625 de 2016 (DUR), arts. 1.6.1.13.2.11 and 1.6.1.13.2.12, substituted by Decreto 2229 de 2023.

Related Colombia skills: [colombia-income-tax](colombia-income-tax.md) (shareholders who are individuals, and how they are taxed on dividends), [co-vat-return](co-vat-return.md) (IVA), [formalizing-small-informal-businesses-in-colombia](formalizing-small-informal-businesses-in-colombia.md) (RUT, registration and Régimen Simple sign-up, approved by Mauro Escudero; this skill only adds the tax comparison and does not repeat it).

## Who this applies to

- **National companies** (art. 12-1): companies with their effective place of management in Colombia, their main domicile in Colombia, or incorporated under Colombian law (SAS, SA, Ltda and others). Taxed on worldwide income.
- **Foreign companies**: on Colombian-source income, through a branch or permanent establishment, or by withholding when they do not file (art. 408). Foreign legal persons without residence also use the 35% rate when they must file (art. 240).
- **Free-zone users** (art. 240-1) and the special-rate groups listed below.
- **Out of scope**: entities of the Régimen Tributario Especial (non-profits), transfer pricing documentation, the wealth tax (impuesto al patrimonio), ICA and other municipal taxes.

## The base

**From income to taxable income (Form 110).**

1. Gross income from ordinary activities, financial income, dividends and other income (boxes 47 to 58).
2. Subtract returns and discounts and ingresos no constitutivos de renta (boxes 59, 60) to reach net income (box 61).
3. Subtract deductible costs and expenses (boxes 62 to 67). An expense must be linked to producing income, necessary and proportionate (art. 107), and backed by an invoice meeting arts. 617 and 618 (art. 771-2). Income tax is never deductible; other taxes linked to the activity are, and 50% of GMF (art. 115). Interest to related parties is limited by the 2 x equity thin-cap rule (art. 118-1).
4. The result is ordinary taxable income (box 72) or a loss (box 73). Offset losses of the previous 12 years (art. 147, box 74). Shareholders cannot use the company's losses.
5. Renta presuntiva is 0% (art. 188), so box 76 is nil; subtract exempt income (box 77) and add special taxable items (box 78) to reach taxable income (box 79).
6. Tax at 35% (box 84), plus extra points where they apply (box 85) and tax on taxed dividends received (boxes 86 to 90). Subtract discounts (box 93): foreign tax paid (art. 254), donations (art. 257) and others, subject to the 25% cap in art. 258. That is net tax without IA (box 94).
7. Minimum tax test (box 95). Add any IA to reach box 96.
8. Add ganancias ocasionales tax (box 97) at 15% (art. 313) for total tax (box 99), then the anticipo and settlement (boxes 103 to 114).

**Minimum tax (TTD), art. 240 par. 6.** It applies to companies taxed under arts. 240 and 240-1, except foreign legal persons without residence.

- Impuesto depurado: ID = INR + DTC minus IRP, where INR is net income tax, DTC is the treaty and art. 254 foreign tax credits, and IRP is the tax on passive income of controlled foreign entities (passive income x 35%).
- Utilidad depurada: UD = UC + DPARL minus INCRNGO minus VIMPP minus VNGO minus RE minus C, where UC is accounting profit before tax, DPARL permanent differences that increase taxable income, INCRNGO non-taxable income in the accounting profit, VIMPP equity-method income, VNGO net occasional gains, RE income exempt under treaties, CAN Decision 578 or the CHC holding regime and the art. 235-2(4)(a),(b) and (7) income, and C losses offset that did not affect the accounting profit.
- TTD = ID / UD. If under 15%, the company pays IA = UD x 15% minus ID. A company with UD of zero or less pays no IA. For groups consolidated in Colombia the test runs on the group totals and the group IA is shared among members with positive UD in proportion to their UD.
- Not subject to it: ZESE companies while at 0%, ZOMAC companies, the 15% hotel and publisher companies (unless they file country-by-country reports), the 9% state monopolies, and taxpayers under art. 32 (concession contracts).

The usual cause of a top-up is a timing difference (tax depreciation or deductions ahead of the books) or a large discount: they cut the tax without cutting the accounting profit. See Example 1.

**Dividends received.** Dividends from a national company out of profits taxed at company level are ingreso no constitutivo for the receiving company but carry the 10% withholding of art. 242-1, which the receiving company passes on to its final individual or foreign shareholder. Dividends from untaxed profits (art. 49 par. 2) and from foreign companies are taxed at the art. 240 rate.

**Ordinary regime or Régimen Simple.** A company whose shareholders are all resident individuals and whose prior-year gross income was under 100,000 UVT may join the Régimen Simple (art. 905). It then pays one consolidated rate on gross income (art. 908), which already includes the municipal ICA, instead of 35% on profit. It suits a business with a high profit margin; a low-margin business can pay less under the ordinary regime. See Example 2. How and when to opt in is in the approved formalising skill.

Pay each instalment by its date.

## Penalties and interest

The same procedural rules apply as for individuals (ET arts. 634 to 648):

- **Late filing (art. 641):** 5% of the tax per month or part month, up to 100%; with no tax, 0.5% of gross income per month within the stated caps.
- **Not filing after a summons (art. 643(1)):** 20% of the higher of bank deposits or gross income found by DIAN, or gross income in the last return.
- **Own correction (art. 644):** 10% of the extra tax before a DIAN summons, 20% after it.
- **Inaccuracy (art. 648):** 100% of the difference; 200% for omitted assets or false debts.
- **Minimum penalty (art. 639):** 10 UVT (COP 523,740).
- **Late payment interest (arts. 634, 635):** daily, at the consumer-credit usury rate minus 2 points.

## Cross-border points

- **Foreign branches and PEs** pay 35% on income attributable to them.
- **Foreign tax credit** (art. 254): foreign tax on foreign-source income, capped at the Colombian tax on that income; for foreign dividends, the underlying tax under the art. 254 rules.
- **Withholding on outbound payments** (art. 408): 20% general, 15% on loans of a year or more and leasing, 5% for 8-year infrastructure loans, 33% on management fees, 10% for significant economic presence, 35% to non-cooperative or preferential regimes. Dividends 20% (art. 245), with 35% first on untaxed profits. Treaties can lower these rates; apply the treaty with a tax residence certificate.
- **Related-party debt** is limited by art. 118-1 whatever the lender's country. Transfer pricing (arts. 260-1 ff.) is out of scope.

## Two worked examples that recompute

### Example 1: SAS with a minimum tax top-up

Andina Logística SAS (not a gran contribuyente, files for many years) has 2026 accounting profit before tax of COP 1,000,000,000, after a COP 80,000,000 donation to a qualifying non-profit. Tax depreciation exceeds book depreciation by COP 600,000,000 (a timing difference). There are no other adjustments. Self-withholding and withholding total COP 90,000,000; last year's anticipo was COP 25,000,000.

- Taxable income: 1,000,000,000 + 80,000,000 (donation not deductible, art. 257) minus 600,000,000 = COP 480,000,000. Tax at 35%: COP 168,000,000.
- Donation discount: 25% x 80,000,000 = 20,000,000, under the cap of 25% x 168,000,000 (art. 258). Net tax without IA (INR, box 94): COP 148,000,000.
- UD: 1,000,000,000 + 80,000,000 (permanent difference) = COP 1,080,000,000. ID = 148,000,000. TTD = 13.7%, under 15%.
- IA = 15% x 1,080,000,000 minus 148,000,000 = **COP 14,000,000** (box 95). Net tax (box 96): **COP 162,000,000**.
- Anticipo for 2027: 75% x 162,000,000 minus 90,000,000 = COP 31,500,000.
- Balance: 162,000,000 + 31,500,000 minus 25,000,000 minus 90,000,000 = COP 78,500,000, paid in two equal parts of COP 39,250,000 in May and July 2027 on its NIT days.

### Example 2: consulting SAS, ordinary regime or Régimen Simple (a decision)

Two Colombian resident engineers own Ingeniería Clara SAS. In 2025 its gross income was under 100,000 UVT, so it may choose the Régimen Simple for 2026. Expected 2026 gross income is COP 300,000,000 (5,728 UVT, the bracket under 6,000 UVT) with deductible costs of COP 180,000,000.

- **Régimen Simple:** professional services rate 5.9% x 300,000,000 = **COP 17,700,000**, which already includes the municipal ICA component.
- **Ordinary regime:** profit 120,000,000 x 35% = **COP 42,000,000**, plus municipal ICA on top.
- The figure that decides: the Simple tax equals the ordinary tax at a profit of 17,700,000 / 35% = COP 50,571,429, a 16.9% margin. Above that margin the Régimen Simple costs less; below it, the ordinary regime may cost less (before ICA, which only widens the gap in favour of the Simple). At 40% margin, the Simple saves COP 24,300,000.

## Ask the client first

1. Is the company national (domicile, incorporation, effective management) or a branch, and is it a gran contribuyente? The last digit of its NIT.
2. Its activity (CIIU) and taxable income: does it fall in a surcharge group (financial, coal, oil, hydro) or a special rate (free zone, hotel, publisher, ZESE, ZOMAC)?
3. The accounting profit before tax and the reconciliation to taxable income: permanent and timing differences, non-deductible items, losses brought forward, exempt income. These drive the TTD test.
4. Related-party debt and its average balance against last year's net equity; payments abroad and their kind.
5. Withholding and self-withholding in 2026, last year's anticipo and credit balance, and how many years it has filed.
6. For the Régimen Simple question: shareholders (all resident individuals?), prior-year gross income, activity group and expected margin.
