# Federated States of Micronesia National Tax and Social Security Reference, 2026 (v0.5)

> **General reference only.** This skill is general tax/accounting reference material for AI-assisted workflows. It has not been reviewed for any specific person's facts, documents, elections, deadlines, residency, filing status, or local procedures. Do not rely on it to file, pay, amend, or take a tax position without review by a qualified professional in the relevant jurisdiction.

## Who this applies to

Load this on top of the base files above. It answers their slots so far as the sources allow. **It is incomplete as a country skill.** The Slot coverage table shows what is missing. Use this file for rates, due dates, treatment and briefing a local adviser. HOLD means the sources read do not establish the point, and no figure may be supplied for it from memory.

**Citations.** 'T54' is Title 54 and 'T53' is Title 53 of the Code of the Federated States of Micronesia, 2014 edition ('Code'), unless a Public Law ('PL') is named. 'Regs' is FSMSSA Regulations No. 03, effective 1 May 2012. 'FSMSSA' is the FSM Social Security Administration. 'Secretary' is the Secretary of the Department of Finance and Administration (T54 s 112(12)). 'CTA' is the Customs and Tax Administration, the name used in the Department's contractor guideline.

**Reading limits that affect every section.**

- The Code is a 2014 text. The Department's page dated 10 July 2025 links a reproduction of it, not a later consolidation. No official consolidated T53 was found.
- T54 Ch 1, T54 Ch 9, PL 15-73, PL 16-10, PL 14-86, PL 18-118 and PL 21-250 were read as native PDF text. T53 Chs 6 and 9 were read from fsmlaw.org HTML, a non-official host. T54 ss 141 to 144 were also read in fsmlaw.org HTML, and no difference in those sections' operative wording was seen. Page images were checked only for PL 18-118 pages 6 and 9 and PL 21-250 page 6. Every other page rests on extracted text.
- Later law was checked by Act titles in Congress indices for the 18th to 24th Congresses and by a non-official fsmlaw.org amendment list. Neither shows a later Act on T54 ss 121 to 123, 131 to 139, 141 to 144, 155 or 902, or on T53 ss 603, 605 or 901 to 905. That is not proof that none exists.
- Regulations under T54 s 114 were not found. Forms under T54 s 115 were not found. HOLD anything that depends on them.
- The historical Chapter 8 codifier notes and the official dated RMS news item were read separately. They do not establish the complete current Chapter 8 chain or current filing operation. The Taxpayers' Charter was captured and extracted natively, but its column layout was not visually checked; none of its additional obligations is adopted here.
- Case notes are the Code's annotations. The judgments themselves were not read.

All amounts are stated in dollars. The sources read do not separately name the currency: HOLD. T54 has no rounding rule in Chs 1 or 9: HOLD. T53 s 603(15) says remuneration 'shall, as may be prescribed by regulations, be computed to the nearest dollar', and no such regulation was found in the Regs. The cents shown here are a drafting choice.

### National VAT: none found in the selected sources

No national VAT was found. T54 Chs 1 to 9 as listed in the Code contain none, and the Act titles read add no VAT. The national tax on business is the gross revenue tax, which the Code's annotation says 'is distinguishable from a sales tax in several ways' (*Ponape Fed'n of Coop. Ass'ns v. FSM*, 2 FSM R. 124, 127). The only VAT text read is in PL 17-50 (see Chapter 8 below), where the VAT is a condition about state legislation. Treat absence as established only on these sources.

### State taxes

The 4 states have unlike taxes, in separate instruments, and this guide does not cover them. A national result here does not mean no state tax is due. See R-FM-2.

### Chapter 8 replacement revenue law: do not use

- PL 17-50 (1 May 2012) enacted a Revenue Administration Act of 2012 in T54 Ch 8. Its s 934 made the Act 'null and void' if any of the 4 states had not passed VAT legislation by midnight 19 April 2013.
- PL 18-16 moved that date to 31 March 2014. PL 18-51 (31 March 2014) moved it to 'midnight May 31, 2014' and read: 'this act is null and void'. PL 17-83 and PL 18-06 also amended s 934 and were not read.
- The Code's title contents list Ch 8 twice: 'Revenue Administration (§§ 801-805)' and 'Revenue Administration (§§ 801-896) [FOR REFERENCE ONLY]'.
- Later law treats the old chapter as live (inference). PL 22-190 (1 May 2023) adds new ss 806 and 807, and its chain runs from PL 18-107 through PLs 21-152, 22-57, 22-139 and 22-158, not from PL 17-50. Those numbers sit inside the reference-only range.
- By its title only, PL 19-01 repeals PL 16-75, the Unified Revenue Authority law on whose commencement date PL 17-50 s 933 relied.

The 2014 codifier's historical Chapter 8 note, newly read on the non-official fsmlaw.org host, expressly says that the PL 17-50 version is included for reference only and was 'null and void' at codification under s 934 as amended by PL 17-83. Its s 802 editor's note says that all 4 states did not adopt VAT. The note quotes a 19 July 2013 deadline, whilst the later amendments above address 31 May 2014. This is positive historical codifier evidence, not an independent reconciliation of every amendment or proof of the current legal position. The full active Chapter 8 chain and state enactments remain unverified. Do not import the replacement's procedures: R-FM-4.

PL 22-190 ss 806 and 807 stand apart. The Secretary may require a Taxpayer Identification Number and may establish an Electronic Notice System, with a physical copy still allowed for a taxpayer with no access to the equipment. No implementing regulation was found: HOLD its commencement, phasing and current requirements.

**Dated RMS evidence.** An official Department news item published on 4 November 2025 describes a Pohnpei Revenue Management System workshop held on 14 to 16 October 2025, covering employee withholding, gross revenue tax, return verification and submission. It describes online registration, filing, payment and record access. This establishes dated rollout evidence. It does not establish current 2026 access, mandatory taxpayer phasing, approved return boxes, calculation methods or that RMS is the statutory Electronic Notice System. Keep those points on HOLD.

### Tax year and basis

**Calendar year: HOLD.** Code s 112(14) said 'Year' means calendar year. PL 18-118 s 1 and PL 21-250 s 1 each re-enact s 112 'to read as follows', and each text runs from (1) to (13)(l), then Section 2. Paragraph (14) is not there. Nothing in either Act says why.

Text still pointing to a calendar year:

- s 122 ('per year', 'in the year in which the deduction is claimed');
- s 123(1)(c) ('calendar year');
- s 133 ('succeeding year', 'calendar year');
- the fixed quarter dates in ss 132 and 143.

Section 121 attaches no period to its $11,000. Inference only: work on a calendar year. Get a regulation or the CTA's confirmation before relying on it. The examples below assume it.

**Filing unit:** each employee for wage tax, each employer for withholding, each business for gross revenue tax. No general annual individual return was found. The annual statement in s 133 is an employer duty.

**Residence:** no residency test appears in ss 121 or 141. Wages for work both inside and outside FSM are presumed wholly earned in FSM (s 124). Gross revenue from both is presumed wholly from FSM sources, and the business may file for apportionment on a prescribed form (s 142). The form was not found.

**Employee or business:** 'usual common law rules' (s 112(3)). One who qualifies as an employee is not a business (s 112(1)). The annotation to s 121 looks to the nature of the services and the person performing them, not the contract's label (*Heston v. FSM*, 2 FSM R. 61, 64). The Department's contractor guideline gives criteria for FSM Government contracts: it is guidance for that setting, not law.

### Wage tax (T54 ss 121 to 124)

**Charge:** 'a tax of six percent upon the first $11,000 and ten percent upon the amount over the first $11,000 of all wages and salaries received by every employee, as defined, except as provided in section 122' (s 121).

**Per employer or per year: the text settles neither.**

- It says 'all wages and salaries received by every employee'. It does not say per employer.
- Inference: the band attaches to the employee, across employers.
- It does not say per year. See Tax year above.
- No mechanism was found for tax that remains short where 2 employers each apply the 6% band. Section 123 refunds excess only, and s 137 covers only employers with no place of business or agent in FSM. HOLD.

**Quarterly withholding brackets: the text is silent.** Tax is withheld 'on any wages and salaries as and when paid or credited' (s 131(1)). Nothing allocates the $11,000 to a pay period or quarter, and no table or s 114 regulation was found. HOLD the method. Neither method below is in the text:

- **Year-to-date method:** the first $11,000 paid in the calendar year bears 6%, and later wages bear 10%.
- **Even quarter method:** `11,000 ÷ 4 = 2,750` per quarter bears 6%, and the rest bears 10%.

Both give the same year for level pay, but they differ by quarter and when pay is uneven.

**Wages include** commissions, fees, compensation, emoluments, bonuses and 'every and all other kinds of compensation paid for, or credited or attributable to, personal services' as an employee (s 112(13)). The exclusions, in short:

- (a) US military payments
- (b) reasonable per diem and travel allowances up to comparable FSM Government rates
- (c) rental value of a home furnished to an employee, or a reasonable rental allowance to the extent used by the employee to rent or provide a home
- (d) sickness or accident disability and medical payments, but not normal wages during sick leave
- (e) trust or annuity payments
- (f) non-cash pay for service outside the ordinary course of the trade
- (g) casual labour outside the ordinary course and for not more than one week in each calendar month
- (h) scholarship, fellowship or stipend to a full-time bona fide student
- (i) payments to clergy from a religious group
- (j) domestic or household employee of a private individual or family
- (k) non-citizens under foreign aid agreement terms (R-FM-6)
- (l) foreign service premium

Employee social security is not among them, so wage tax falls on wages before it (inference).

**Section 122 deduction.** 'Every employee, as defined, except those whose gross annual wages and salaries are $5,000 or more, shall be allowed a deduction of $1,000 per year from all wages and salaries subject to tax'. It 'shall be claimed by the employee filing for a refund' under s 123. The heading says 'Deduction from tax', but the text deducts from wages. Follow the text: it is not a $1,000 credit and not a payroll exemption. Withhold in full and let the employee claim. Assuming 6% withholding and all wages in the 6% band:

| Gross annual wages | Withheld | Tax after s 122 | Refund |
|---|---|---|---|
| $900 | `900 × 6% = 54.00` | nil; the deduction exceeds the wages (inference) | `54 - 0 = 54.00`  https://dofa.gov.fm/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/FSMCode-Taxation-of-Wages-Salaries-Gross-Revenues.pdf |
| $4,800 | `4,800 × 6% = 288.00` | `(4,800 - 1,000) × 6% = 228.00` | `288 - 228 = 60.00`  https://dofa.gov.fm/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/FSMCode-Taxation-of-Wages-Salaries-Gross-Revenues.pdf |
| $4,999 | `4,999 × 6% = 299.94` | `(4,999 - 1,000) × 6% = 239.94` | `299.94 - 239.94 = 60.00`  https://dofa.gov.fm/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/FSMCode-Taxation-of-Wages-Salaries-Gross-Revenues.pdf |
| Exactly $5,000 | `5,000 × 6% = 300.00` | `300.00`; no deduction, as $5,000 is 'or more' | `300 - 300 = 0.00`  https://dofa.gov.fm/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/FSMCode-Taxation-of-Wages-Salaries-Gross-Revenues.pdf |

HOLD whether 'gross annual wages' counts amounts excluded by s 112(13).

**Refund (s 123):** on application, if the Secretary is satisfied that the amount was paid to the Government, was not used as a credit, and the application 'was filed within one year after the end of the calendar year in which the amount to be refunded was withheld or paid'. For an amount paid in 2026, that is by 31 December 2027 (inference from the wording). A decision is due within 90 days of submission. The application form was not found. The CIT-Refund-Request-Form listed on the Department's site was not read, and its name points to another tax.

## The base

### Gross revenue tax (T54 ss 141 to 144 and s 112)

**Gross revenue** means gross receipts, 'cash or accrued', from services, trade, business and sales, and receipts by reason of capital including interest, rentals, royalties and fees, 'without any deductions on account of the cost of property sold, the cost of materials used, taxes, royalties, or interest paid or any other expenses whatsoever' (s 112(5)). Cost of goods therefore does not reduce it.

**Exclusions (s 112(5)), in short:**

- refunds and rebates
- fiduciary money
- wages taxed under the chapter
- matched aircraft sale and rental payments
- cash discounts and returns
- the sale price of an article accepted as part payment for a new article, if the new article's full sale price is included in gross revenue
- foreign aid funds under agreement terms (R-FM-6)
- export sales of goods produced or made in FSM
- proceeds of sales of products of a processing facility in FSM that are subsequently exported from, and not used in, FSM
- proceeds of sales of fish by foreign or domestic fishing vessels to processing facilities within FSM
- proceeds of sales of bait fish to foreign or domestic fishing vessels
- recycling, removal and collection of waste, as listed in (l)

**Business** excludes casual sales 'as determined by the Secretary', an employee, and unincorporated copra production (s 112(1)). No determination on casual sales was found.

**Taxable gross revenue** means gross revenue less only the listed expenses of the business for the period, subject to every condition in s 112(8). Wages, salaries and benefits must be reasonably paid for employee services relating to producing gross revenues, and wages and salaries must be reported under T54 Ch 1 subchapter III. Apply the other expense conditions in the table above. Inference: the list is closed, so rent, cost of goods, depreciation and interest are not deducted. No loss relief rule appears in Ch 1. HOLD a period where deductions exceed gross revenue.

**Charge:**

- s 141(1): 'a tax of $80 per year upon that portion of the amount of taxable gross revenues ... which does not exceed $10,000 per year'. It is a charge on the band, not a rate.
- s 141(2): 'three percent per year upon that portion ... which is in excess of $10,000 per year'.
- s 141(3): 'Businesses which earn gross revenues of not more than $2,000 per year are exempt from taxation under this section. The deduction shall be claimed by the business by filing for a refund under the provisions of sections 122 and 123'.

**Annual boundaries**, on taxable gross revenue (T) and gross revenue (G):

| Case | Result |
|---|---|
| G $2,000 exactly | Exempt ('not more than'), by refund: `80 - 80 = 0.00` after a refund of $80 paid |
| G $2,001, T $1,000 | Not exempt. The test uses gross, not taxable, revenue: $80.00 |
| T $8,000 | $80.00. The text prorates nothing inside the band |
| T $10,000 exactly | $80.00. None is 'in excess' |
| T $10,001 | `80 + (10,001 - 10,000) × 3% = 80.03` |
| T $11,000 | `80 + (11,000 - 10,000) × 3% = 110.00` |
| T $156,000 | `80 + (156,000 - 10,000) × 3% = 4,460.00` |

**HOLD:**

- Section 141(3) says 'exempt', then 'deduction', then refund. Do not assume the exemption applies automatically. Inference: pay as due and claim under s 123. Whether a business that expects to qualify must still file quarterly is not stated.
- Whether $80 is due where T is nil. The charge is 'upon that portion', and T of nil gives no portion (inference).
- A part-year business: no proration rule was found.
- 'Per year' depends on the Tax year hold.

**Quarterly mechanics.** 'Every business, on or before the last day of the month following the close of each quarter, to wit: on or before April 30, July 31, October 31, January 31, shall pay, based on its taxable gross revenue of the preceding quarter, the amount of tax imposed' to the National revenue officer in the State of its principal place of business, or to the Secretary (s 143(1)). A return shows gross revenue 'received, accrued, or earned', taxable gross revenue and the wages and social security claimed (s 143(2)). It does not name the later (c) to (e) deductions. The Secretary may extend for good cause, but not beyond the last day of the first month after the due date (s 143(3)). Section 115(7) requires revenue to be identified by the State or States in which it is earned. Section 141(4) separately requires returns for revenue collected in each State expressly 'For the purpose of section 805 of this title'. The active s 805 and its amendment chain were not qualified here: HOLD the current operation of that separate-return provision rather than treating it as unconditional.

**HOLD how an annual band fits a quarterly payment.** The text does not allocate $80 or $10,000 to quarters, and no regulation or current form was found. The worked examples show 2 inferences:

- **Even quarter method:** `80 ÷ 4 = 20.00` and `10,000 ÷ 4 = 2,500`.
- **Year-to-date method:** $80 in the first quarter with taxable revenue, and 3% once the year's T passes $10,000.

Uneven revenue can make the even method overpay against the annual result. Section 123 refunds a business's overpayment.

**Accounting basis:** the annotation to s 143 says the statute 'mandates that all businesses compute gross revenue tax liability using the accrual accounting method' (*NIH Corp. v. FSM*, 5 FSM R. 411, 413). It also records an estoppel on penalties and interest for those led to believe that cash basis was acceptable (at 415). The s 112(8) deductions speak of amounts 'paid', so HOLD timing where accrued revenue and paid expenses fall in different quarters.

**Major corporations:** T54 Ch 3 exists and is not computed here (R-FM-1).

### Social security (T53 Subtitle II)

**Rates:** 'with respect to wages paid on and after January 1, 2013, the rate shall be seven point five (7.5) percent', on the employee (s 901(1)(g)) and on the employer as an excise tax (s 902(7)). PL 16-10 ss 5 and 6 enact this. Its s 902 says 'employment' where the Code says 'covered employment', and the rate is the same. Regs § 100.41 still says 7% each and 14% combined. It predates the rise, so follow the statute. The employer withholds the employee share when wages are paid and pays both shares (Regs § 100.41; annotation, *Kingtex*, 7 FSM R. 280, 285).

**Wage base.** Current for all of 2026: $9,000 a quarter. Exact wording of s 603(15), as enacted in PL 15-73 s 1 and PL 16-10 s 1:

| Limb | $9,000 | $10,000 | Wording |
|---|---|---|---|
| Wage exclusion (a), employees | 'in excess of $9,000 beginning on January 1, 2023' | 'in excess of $10,000 beginning on January 1, 2028' | '... paid in a quarterly reporting period by one employer' |
| Self-employed with employees | '$9,000 on January 1, 2023' | '$10,000 on January 1, 2028' | 'maximum quarterly amount' |
| Self-employed with no covered employees | '$9,000 on January 1, 2023' | '$10,000 on October 1, 2028' | 'maximum quarterly amount' |

- The October 2028 date appears only in the third limb. The Code, PL 15-73 and PL 16-10 all print it. Whether it is a slip is HELD and does not affect 2026.
- FSMSSA's press release of 17 June 2022 says 'per Public Law No. 16-10, the quarterly taxable wage base is scheduled to increase from $8,000 to $9,000 on January 01, 2023'. Its notice of 24 November 2017 says $7,000 to $8,000 on 1 January 2018.
- **Conflict resolved against the FAQ:** FSMSSA's FAQ says 'Currently the maximum quarterly taxable wage base is $7,000'. The same page speaks of a rise 'scheduled ... effective January 01, 2013', so it appears stale. Follow s 603(15). FSMSSA's form link is named 'quarterly-tax-return-7k_7.5-FSMSS-401'. The form was not opened, so check that it handles $9,000.
- No Act changing the base was found. CB 24-51, in the 24th Congress bill index, would amend s 603 to discontinue the earnings test. A bill is not law, and it was not read.

**Per employer, per quarter:** the cap is per 'quarterly reporting period by one employer'. It does not carry between quarters, and there is no annual ceiling in the text. Section 904 then provides: where a covered employee is 'reported with earnings in excess of the quarterly maximum' and tax on the excess is withheld, the excess employee tax 'during the four quarters ending December 31st' is refunded within 90 days of that date if one dollar or more. No refund goes to 'the employer or employers'. Inference, supported by the FAQ's example of a government worker with a private business: an employee with 2 employers is refunded, and the employers are not. HOLD how the excess is computed across employers.

**Floor:** none found. A 'quarter of coverage' needs $300 in wages (s 603(14)). That affects benefits, not the tax (inference).

**Who is an employee (s 603(8)):**

- (a) any officer of a corporation;
- (b) a common law employee;
- (c) 'any self-employed person who has at least one employee for whom he is required to report in a given quarter';
- (d) 'any self-employed person who had more than $10,000 of annual gross revenue in the preceding calendar year'.

Family employment is excluded unless the Board determines otherwise (s 603(10), (12), (15)(f)). Covered employment is service for an employer 'incorporated or doing business' in FSM, wherever performed (s 603(10)).

**Who is an employer (s 603(9)):** the actual person responsible for the working relationship. FSMSSA may determine who that is. Partners are jointly and severally liable. **For a corporation that fails to pay, liability is joint and several between the president and 'all shareholders with greater than a 30% interest'** (s 603(9)(c)).

**Self-employed base (s 603(15)):**

- With employees: 'twice the amount paid to the highest paid employee reported ... in a quarter', up to the cap.
- With no covered employees: 'for each quarter of a year, be deemed to be' 5% of prior-year gross revenue, up to the cap. FSMSSA's example applies 15% to that base, so the person pays both shares.

**Section 603(15) wording conflict.** PL 14-86 reads '5 percent of the gross revenue of the business'. PL 15-73 s 1 and PL 16-10 s 1 read 'five (5) percent of the gross revenue of the business or gross revenue of all businesses for the previous calendar year'. The Code reads 'five percent of the gross revenue of the business', although its source note cites PLs 15-73 and 16-10. Inference: this is a codification slip, and the enacted words govern. FSMSSA's Employers page, which allows combining businesses to reach the 'over $10,000' amount, fits that reading. No corrigendum was searched.

**Exact $10,000 where no separate mandatory trigger applies: HOLD.** A self-employed person with at least one employee required to be reported in the quarter is independently covered by s 603(8)(c), regardless of revenue. For an otherwise uncovered person, s 603(8)(d) needs 'more than $10,000', whilst s 903(1) allows voluntary contributions with 'less than $10,000'. Exactly $10,000 meets neither revenue wording. The Regs' voluntary contributions passage (page 5) says 'If revenue received by a self-employed person is $10,000.00 or more, they are subject to the regular mandatory contribution provisions'. The text and the Regs differ, so ask FSMSSA.

**Partnerships: conflict.** Regs § 100.39 deems partners with no employees to receive '2.5% of the gross revenue', whilst s 603(15) says 5%. Refused: R-FM-7.

**Voluntary (s 903; Regs p 5, 'Contributions, Voluntary'):** the Regs describe self-employed sole proprietors with no employees in FSM and less than $10,000 annual revenue. That voluntary route does not replace mandatory coverage under s 603(8)(c) for a person with a reportable employee. For a qualifying voluntary contributor, deemed remuneration is $1,250 a quarter and both shares are payable. So may a citizen working as an employee outside FSM, Palau and the Marshall Islands, with a minimum of $1,250 a quarter. `1,250 × 7.5% = 93.75` each and `93.75 × 2 = 187.50` a quarter, which matches Regs.

**Wages for social security differ from wages for wage tax.** T53 wages include 'the cash value of all remuneration paid in any medium other than cash'. The annotation treats employer meals as wages (*Kingtex*, 7 FSM R. 280, 288). Exclusions (b) to (f) in s 603(15) are narrower:

Each employee gets a social security number and card (s 905). Employer card fee: $10 for government and private sectors, $5 for domestic employers (Forms page).

## Deadlines and filing

### Employer withholding (T54 ss 131 to 139)

- **Who:** 'Employer' is widely defined and includes governments (s 112(4)).
- **Where:** pay the National revenue officer of the State of the principal place of business, or the Secretary if there is no place of business in FSM (s 131(2)).
- **When:** 'on or before the last day of the month following the close of each quarter, to wit, on or before April 30, July 31, October 31, and January 31', with a return showing wages and tax (s 132(1)).
- **Frequency:** the Secretary may require more frequent returns but no more often than monthly. For good cause the Secretary may extend, but not beyond the last day of the first month after the due date (s 132(3)).
- **Employee statements:** written statement to each employee by 31 January of the succeeding year, or on the last payment if employment ends earlier. A duplicate goes with the final return or by 31 January. An extension of up to 60 days is possible (s 133).
- **Trust and liability:** withheld tax is held in trust. An employer who fails to withhold or pay is liable, and the amount is a lien with priority except as the Secured Transactions Act provides. The employer may recover from the employee an amount it paid from its own funds (s 135). The employee has no action for proper withholding (s 136).
- **Government pay:** the Secretary may set special procedures (s 132(2)).
- **Non-resident employers:** where the employer has no FSM place of business and no responsible agent, the individual files and pays (s 137). The same applies to US Government wages unless tax was withheld. The return follows ss 131 and 132 and states employer details, the last 3 months' pay and the tax (s 138). The Secretary may permit semiannual returns, and may extend for good cause but not beyond the twentieth day of the second month after the due date (s 139).

## Penalties and interest

### Penalties and interest, T54

| Item | Rule | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Late return | 1% of the tax for each 30 days or fraction; 'minimum penalty under this subsection shall be five dollars and the maximum penalty under this section shall be 25 percent of the tax due' | s 155(1) |
| Failure to furnish a statement | $5 per statement, if wilful | s 155(2) |
| Failure after demand | 10% of tax assessed, in addition to s 155(1) | s 155(3) |
| Fraud | 50% of the deficiency, in addition to s 155(1) to (3) | s 155(4) |
| Interest | 'on the unpaid balance of the tax principal at the rate of six percent per annum from its due date until the date it is paid' | s 155(5) |
| Late payment | 5% of principal if not more than one month, 'an additional one percent for each additional month or fraction thereof', 'not exceeding 25 percent in the aggregate, in addition to any penalties assessed under section 155' | s 902 |
| Criminal | Up to $1,000, or up to one year for a natural person, or both; separate from civil penalties | ss 154, 155 |
| Wilful violation | Up to one year, or up to $500, or both, unless another penalty is specified | s 901 |

- **Stacking:** s 902 stacks on s 155 in express words. Section 155(1) and s 902 can therefore both apply where a return and payment are both late. A return on time with late payment attracts s 902 and interest only. The annotations confirm s 902 reaches gross revenue tax (*FSM v. George*, 2 FSM R. 88, 94; *Setik v. FSM*, 5 FSM R. 407, 409).
- **Interest on penalties:** interest runs on tax principal, so none is shown on penalties (inference from s 155(5)).
- **HOLD:** no day count for interest. 'Month' means calendar month in Ch 1 (s 112(7)), but s 902 sits in Ch 9. Counting from the due date's day of the month is a drafting assumption.
- **HOLD, minimum against maximum:** on a small tax the $5 minimum can pass the 25% cap. For tax of $20, `20 × 1% = 0.20`, raised to $5.00, and `20 × 25% = 5.00`, so they agree. For tax of $10, `10 × 25% = 2.50` is below $5.00, and the text does not rank them.
- **Assessment:** on failure, the Secretary may demand. After 30 days from receipt, the Secretary may make the return, file a lien notice and assess, and the assessment is presumed correct (s 152). Taxes are a lien (s 153). Review lies to a court within one year of an adverse decision (s 156).
- **Waiver:** Code s 903 is a past waiver. PL 19-66 amends it for 'a specific period' on its title, and its text was not read. HOLD any waiver: R-FM-8.
- **Weekends and holidays:** no rule was found in T54 Chs 1 or 9. HOLD. Treat the printed date as the date.

## Two worked examples that recompute

### Worked examples (fictional)

Pohnpei Lagoon Traders Inc operates in Pohnpei only and is assumed to be outside T54 Ch 3. Its 2 employees are common law employees with level cash pay, one employer each, and no excluded items. A calendar year is assumed. **Each wage tax and gross revenue tax quarterly split is conditional on a method the text leaves open.**

**Social security, January to March 2026 (statutory quarterly rate/base illustration; all global holds apply):**

- Employee A, wages $6,000: `6,000 × 7.5% = 450.00` employee and `6,000 × 7.5% = 450.00` employer.
- Employee B, wages $12,000, at the cap: `12,000 - 9,000 = 3,000` excluded; `9,000 × 7.5% = 675.00` employee and `9,000 × 7.5% = 675.00` employer.
- Employee share total: `450 + 675 = 1,125.00`. Employer share total: `450 + 675 = 1,125.00`.
- Illustrated total for the quarter: `1,125 + 1,125 = 2,250.00`; the statutory filing/payment date is 10 April 2026, subject to the working-day rule. This is not an instruction to remit an unverified amount.

**Wage tax, year-to-date method:**

| Quarter | Employee A | Employee B |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | `6,000 × 6% = 360.00` | `11,000 × 6% + 1,000 × 10% = 760.00` |
| 2 | `5,000 × 6% + 1,000 × 10% = 400.00` | `12,000 × 10% = 1,200.00` |
| 3 | `6,000 × 10% = 600.00` | `12,000 × 10% = 1,200.00` |
| 4 | `6,000 × 10% = 600.00` | `12,000 × 10% = 1,200.00` |
| Year | `360 + 400 + 600 + 600 = 1,960.00` | `760 + 1,200 + 1,200 + 1,200 = 4,360.00` |

**Wage tax, even quarter method:**

- A: `2,750 × 6% + 3,250 × 10% = 490.00` a quarter; `490 × 4 = 1,960.00`.
- B: `2,750 × 6% + 9,250 × 10% = 1,090.00` a quarter; `1,090 × 4 = 4,360.00`.

**Check against s 121 for the year:**

- A: `11,000 × 6% + 13,000 × 10% = 1,960.00`.
- B: `11,000 × 6% + 37,000 × 10% = 4,360.00`.

**Illustrative net pay, first quarter, unverified year-to-date method:**

- A: `6,000 - 360 - 450 = 5,190.00`.
- B: `12,000 - 760 - 675 = 10,565.00`.
- Illustrative first-quarter withholding under 2 unverified methods: year-to-date `360 + 760 = 1,120.00`; even-quarter `490 + 1,090 = 1,580.00`. These are comparison figures only. No payable wage-tax amount is determined.
- The statutory first-quarter return/payment deadline is 30 April 2026, subject to any applicable non-working-day rule. The quarterly wage-withholding method remains on HOLD. Do not determine a remittance without confirming the method and applicable requirements with the CTA.

**Gross revenue tax, first quarter above $10,000.** Gross revenue is $60,000 and cost of goods is $25,000, which is not deductible.

- Assume all s 112(8) conditions are met: wages/benefits are reasonably paid for the employees' services relating to producing gross revenues, wages and salaries are reported under subchapter III, social contributions are pursuant to T53 s 902 and utilities, communications and MiCare payments meet their stated conditions. Deductions: wages `6,000 + 12,000 = 18,000`; employer social security $1,125; directly related utilities $1,200; communications $300; MiCare $375.
- Taxable gross revenue: `60,000 - 18,000 - 1,125 - 1,200 - 300 - 375 = 39,000`.
- Level year: `39,000 × 4 = 156,000`; annual tax `80 + 146,000 × 3% = 4,460.00`.
- Even quarter method: `20 + (39,000 - 2,500) × 3% = 1,115.00`; `1,115 × 4 = 4,460.00`.
- Year-to-date method: first quarter `80 + (39,000 - 10,000) × 3% = 950.00`; later quarters `39,000 × 3% = 1,170.00`; year `950 + 1,170 + 1,170 + 1,170 = 4,460.00`.

**Gross revenue tax at the $80 band:**

- T of $2,500 a quarter: `2,500 × 4 = 10,000`, annual tax $80.00. Even method: `20 × 4 = 80.00`. Year-to-date method: $80.00 in the first quarter and $0.00 after that.
- T of $2,750 a quarter: `2,750 × 4 = 11,000`, annual tax `80 + 1,000 × 3% = 110.00`. Even method: `20 + 250 × 3% = 27.50`; `27.50 × 4 = 110.00`. Year-to-date method: $80.00 in the first quarter, $0.00 in the next 2, and `(11,000 - 10,000) × 3% = 30.00` in the last; `80 + 0 + 0 + 30 = 110.00`.

**Late example, wage tax (conditional).** Tax of $1,120 from the year-to-date method, due 30 April 2026. Return and payment on 15 July 2026, 76 days late.

- s 155(1): `76 ÷ 30 = 2.53`, so 3 periods; `1,120 × 1% × 3 = 33.60`, above the $5 minimum and below `1,120 × 25% = 280.00`.
- s 902: 30 May and 30 June complete 2 months and 15 July falls in a third; `5% + 1% + 1% = 7%`; `1,120 × 7% = 78.40`, below `1,120 × 25% = 280.00`.
- s 155(5): `1,120 × 6% × 76 ÷ 365 = 13.99`.
- Conditional total: `1,120 + 33.60 + 78.40 + 13.99 = 1,245.99`.

The stacking of s 155(1) and s 902 follows the words of s 902. The result is conditional on these assumptions:

- the withholding method;
- daily interest on a 365 day year;
- s 902 months counted from the due date;
- no extension and no demand under s 155(3);
- no waiver;
- assessment as stated.

It is not an assessed amount.

**Return on time, gross revenue tax paid 10 days late (conditional):**

- `1,115 × 5% = 55.75` under s 902;
- `1,115 × 6% × 10 ÷ 365 = 1.83` interest;
- s 155(1) is nil, as the return was on time.

**Late social security (conditional).** $2,250 due 10 April 2026, paid with the report on 15 July 2026. Interest runs from 11 April to 15 July, 96 days: `2,250 × 12% × 96 ÷ 365 = 71.01`. The s 605(4) penalty is discretionary, up to $1,000 for the quarter.

- Without penalty: `2,250 + 71.01 = 2,321.01`.
- With the maximum: `2,250 + 71.01 + 1,000 = 3,321.01`.

**Self-employed (a separate fictional trader, Sokehs Reef Supplies):**

- No covered employees, prior-year gross revenue $16,000: `16,000 × 5% = 800` a quarter; `800 × 15% = 120.00`; `120 × 4 = 480.00`. This matches FSMSSA's own example.
- Prior-year gross revenue $200,000: `200,000 × 5% = 10,000`, capped at $9,000; `9,000 × 15% = 1,350.00` a quarter.
- 2 businesses, gross $10,000 and $8,000, on the enacted wording: `(10,000 + 8,000) × 5% = 900` a quarter; `900 × 15% = 135.00`.
- Highest paid employee $3,000 in the quarter: `3,000 × 2 = 6,000` deemed wages; `6,000 × 15% = 900.00`.
- Highest paid employee $6,000: `6,000 × 2 = 12,000`, capped at $9,000; `9,000 × 15% = 1,350.00`.

**Below any coverage level:** wages $200 in a quarter: `200 × 7.5% = 15.00` each share, and no quarter of coverage (inference: no floor).

**2 employers; refund calculation held:** $9,000 from each in one quarter gives `9,000 × 7.5% = 675.00` withheld by each, a combined `675 + 675 = 1,350.00`. Section 904 provides an employee-only refund where its conditions are met, but the cross-employer excess/aggregation method remains unresolved. Do not compute a refundable amount from this illustration; no employer refund is allowed.
