# US federal income tax return, Form 1040

Form 1040 is the annual US individual income tax return. The IRS says Form 1040 is used by U.S. taxpayers to file an annual income tax return, and that Form 1040-SR is an optional alternative for taxpayers who are age 65 or older, using the same schedules and instructions ([About Form 1040](https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs/about-form-1040)). Most individuals use the calendar year. Figures are for tax year 2026. A tax year 2026 return is the one filed in 2027: the IRS says the tax year 2026 adjustments "generally apply to tax returns filed in 2027" ([IR-2025-103](https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/irs-releases-tax-inflation-adjustments-for-tax-year-2026-including-amendments-from-the-one-big-beautiful-bill)). Every table below names its year. Rows marked tax year 2025 belong to the 2025 return filed in 2026, and appear only where the IRS has not yet printed the 2026 figure or where a 2025 return is still being prepared.

## Scope

This skill is for preparers of a federal individual income tax return on Form 1040 or Form 1040-SR for tax year 2026. The IRS filing page says: "If you have wages, file Form 1040, U.S. Individual Income Tax Return or Form 1040-SR, U.S. Income Tax Return for Seniors", and that a person with a business or side income files Form 1040 with a Schedule C ([File your tax return](https://www.irs.gov/filing/individuals/how-to-file)). The IRS says "To file federal taxes, you need a taxpayer identification number (TIN)" ([Individual tax filing](https://www.irs.gov/individual-tax-filing)). It does not cover nonresident aliens, entity returns (partnership, corporation, trust or estate), amended returns, estimated tax payments, or state and local income tax returns.

## Ask the client first

- What is the filing status, citizenship and federal residence, and did marriage, separation, divorce, a death or household membership change? Can someone claim the client as a dependent? Obtain the taxpayer and spouse's ages, blindness status, identification numbers and any IP PIN.
- What income, withholding, estimated payments and carryovers are supported by records, including self-employment, foreign earnings, tips and overtime? For a new personal vehicle loan, obtain the interest statement, purchase/loan dates and VIN; collect modified AGI information for each deduction.
- What deductible mortgage interest, state and local taxes and charitable gifts were paid, and can they be substantiated? Compare the complete standard and itemized routes, including the non-itemizer charitable deduction.
- Is this a calendar or fiscal year, and was the client abroad, in a combat zone or affected by an IRS disaster postponement? Check each applicable filing and payment deadline.
- Are prior returns, prior-year AGI or Self-Select PIN, income documents, dependent records and bank details available? Identify missing records, notices, prior errors and any identity-theft issue.
- Can the balance be paid by the original due date? Determine whether an extension, extra withholding, estimated payments or a payment arrangement is needed; a self-employment return requirement alone does not establish an estimated-payment duty.

### Determine the tax year 2026 filing duty

For ordinary full-year US citizens/residents who cannot be claimed as dependents and are eligible for the standard deduction, the following gross-income filing thresholds are derived from [26 USC 6012(a)](https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/t26/s6012/), [section 151(d)(5)](https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/t26/s151/) and the 2026 basic/age amounts in [Rev. Proc. 2025-32, sections 4.14 and 4.23](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/rp-25-32.pdf). These are a statutory calculation, not a claim that final 2026 Form 1040 instructions have been published. File at or above the threshold.

| Filing status and age at year end | 2026 gross-income threshold | Derivation and source |
|---|---|---|
| Single, under 65 / 65 or older | USD 16,100 / USD 18,150 | 16,100 plus 2,050 if aged; [6012(a)(1)(A)-(B)](https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/t26/s6012/) and [2026 amounts](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/rp-25-32.pdf) |
| Head of household, under 65 / 65 or older | USD 24,150 / USD 26,200 | 24,150 plus 2,050 if aged; [6012(a)(1)(A)-(B)](https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/t26/s6012/) and [2026 amounts](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/rp-25-32.pdf) |
| Married filing jointly, neither / one / both 65 or older | USD 32,200 / USD 33,850 / USD 35,500 | 32,200 plus 1,650 per aged spouse; joint-return and same-household conditions apply; [6012(a)(1)(A)-(B)](https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/t26/s6012/) and [2026 amounts](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/rp-25-32.pdf) |
| Qualifying surviving spouse, under 65 / 65 or older | USD 32,200 / USD 33,850 | 32,200 plus 1,650 if aged; [6012(a)(1)(A)-(B)](https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/t26/s6012/) and [2026 amounts](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/rp-25-32.pdf) |
| Net earnings from self-employment | USD 400 or more requires a return | Includes exactly USD 400; [26 USC 6017](https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/t26/s6017/) |

Do not add the senior deduction to the gross-income filing threshold. Blindness affects deductions but does not increase these ordinary nondependent gross-income thresholds under section 6012(a)(1)(B). Married filing separately, spouses not living together, dependents and taxpayers with no standard deduction require their own statutory tests; do not treat the ordinary standard deduction as their filing exemption. The 2025 instructions' USD 5 married-filing-separately minimum is not carried forward here as a confirmed 2026 administrative threshold.

For a single dependent under 65 and not blind, the 2026 tests under [6012(a)(1)(C)](https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/t26/s6012/) and [63(c)(5)](https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/t26/s63/) require filing if unearned income exceeds USD 1,350, earned income exceeds USD 16,100, or gross income exceeds the larger of USD 1,350 and earned income plus USD 450 (with the ordinary basic-standard-deduction ceiling). Age/blindness and married-dependent cases need the additional statutory tests and final instructions. The 2026 qualifying-relative gross-income ceiling is less than USD 5,300; qualifying-child rules are separate. [Rev. Proc. 2025-32, sections 4.14 and 4.23](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/rp-25-32.pdf).

Also check special taxes, advance premium tax credit reconciliation, account distributions and other filing triggers even when income is below these amounts. Filing voluntarily may recover withholding or refundable credits. [Publication 501, Who Must File and Other Situations](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p501) supplies continuing procedures; its printed annual thresholds are for 2025.

**Estimated payments are a separate test.** For ordinary 2026 filers, [Publication 505, chapter 2](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p505) requires estimates when expected tax after withholding and credits is at least USD 1,000 and those payments/credits are below the smaller of 90% of 2026 tax or 100% of 2025 tax on a full-year return. The prior-year percentage becomes 110% for higher-income filers (prior-year AGI over USD 150,000, or USD 75,000 for 2026 married filing separately); farming/fishing and other exceptions need their special tests. Route the calculation and installments to the US quarterly-estimated-tax guide. USD 400 of self-employment income by itself does not require quarterly payments.

### Choose a filing route

Start through [IRS Free File](https://www.irs.gov/e-file-do-your-taxes-for-free), then verify the applicable filing-season income limit and the chosen partner's age, income and residency criteria. Free File Fillable Forms is available regardless of income and requires the taxpayer to do the tax work. IRS-certified volunteer programs, commercial software, a tax professional and paper filing are other routes. Do not use an income limit with an unconfirmed tax year to decide eligibility. [IRS how to file](https://www.irs.gov/filing/individuals/how-to-file).

### Prepare the return

1. Decide whether a return is required using the 2026 statutory tests above, then check special filing triggers and the final form instructions before submission. Net self-employment earnings of USD 400 or more independently require a return. Filing may also recover withholding or refundable credits.
2. Confirm the person has a taxpayer identification number, since "To file federal taxes, you need a taxpayer identification number (TIN)" ([Individual tax filing](https://www.irs.gov/individual-tax-filing)), and that the person files Form 1040 at all. A nonresident alien does not use this skill. A taxpayer 65 or older may use Form 1040-SR instead, with the same schedules ([About Form 1040](https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs/about-form-1040)).
3. Fix the filing status for the year: single, married filing jointly, married filing separately, head of household, or qualifying surviving spouse. Status picks the rate schedule, the standard deduction and most thresholds in the tables above. Married filing separately loses the senior, tips and overtime deductions, because the Act allows them to a married taxpayer only on a joint return ([Public Law 119-21](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/PLAW-119publ21/html/PLAW-119publ21.htm)).
4. Gather the documents and report income. Follow the IRS sequence at [How to file your taxes: step by step](https://www.irs.gov/filing/individuals/how-to-file). Business or side income goes on Schedule C. Use Schedule 1 for additional income (such as unemployment compensation, prize or award money, gambling winnings) and deductions (such as student loan interest, self-employment tax, educator expenses) ([About Form 1040](https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs/about-form-1040)).
5. Take the deductions. Compare the 2026 standard deduction, plus any additional amount for age or blindness, with itemized deductions on Schedule A (table from [Rev. Proc. 2025-32](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/rp-25-32.pdf)). For an itemizer in 2026, apply the state and local tax cap, the charitable floor and, in the 37% bracket, the § 68 reduction, all from the Act table above. For a non-itemizer in 2026, add the cash charitable deduction. Claim the senior, tips, overtime and vehicle loan interest deductions on Schedule 1-A, whether or not the taxpayer itemizes, applying each income reduction ([About Form 1040](https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs/about-form-1040)).
6. Compute the tax. Apply the 2026 rate schedule for the filing status to taxable income, from [Rev. Proc. 2025-32](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/rp-25-32.pdf). Where there are qualified dividends or long-term capital gains, apply the maximum zero rate and 15 percent rate amounts in the second table. Report other taxes on Schedule 2, which the IRS lists for self-employment tax, household employment taxes, additional tax on IRAs and other tax-favoured accounts, alternative minimum tax, and repayment of excess advance premium tax credit ([About Form 1040](https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs/about-form-1040)).
7. Take the credits. The child tax credit and earned income credit amounts are in the second table ([Rev. Proc. 2025-32](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/rp-25-32.pdf)). Use Schedule 3 for credits not claimed on Form 1040 itself, such as the foreign tax credit, education credits and the general business credit, and for other payments such as an amount paid with an extension request or excess social security tax withheld ([About Form 1040](https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs/about-form-1040)).
8. Assemble and sign a paper return as [Topic no. 301](https://www.irs.gov/taxtopics/tc301) says. Attach a copy of all Forms W-2 to the front. Attach Forms W-2G, 1099-R and any other income document to the front if federal income tax was withheld, plus any form the instructions say goes on the front (for example Form 2439). Put related schedules and forms behind the return in sequence-number order. If a Form W-2c was received, attach it and a copy of the original Form W-2. On a joint paper return both spouses sign; the page sets out how one spouse signs for the other in limited cases.
9. File by the due date in the deadline table. E-filing is timed by transmission; a paper return by postmark. A paper return goes to the address in the form instructions ([Topic no. 301](https://www.irs.gov/taxtopics/tc301)). Beyond the free options in the table above, the IRS lists two other ways to file: "Tax professional - Choose an IRS-approved tax professional" and "Paper forms - You can file with paper forms and mail them to the IRS" ([How to file](https://www.irs.gov/filing/individuals/how-to-file)).
10. If more time is needed, file Form 4868 by the original due date for the automatic extension, and still pay by the original due date ([When to file](https://www.irs.gov/filing/individuals/when-to-file)). The extended return is due October 15 ([Publication 509](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/p509.pdf)). Check first whether the client qualifies for the automatic 2-month rule for people abroad, combat zone relief, or a disaster postponement ([Topic no. 301](https://www.irs.gov/taxtopics/tc301)).
11. Pay any balance due. The IRS Make a payment page covers card, digital wallet, other electronic options and cash; if the client cannot pay in full, the IRS may agree a payment arrangement ([Topic no. 301](https://www.irs.gov/taxtopics/tc301)). The IRS tells those who cannot pay in full that they "should pay what they can now and apply for an online payment plan", and that "An extension to file is not an extension to pay" ([IR-2026-44](https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/act-now-to-file-pay-or-request-an-extension)).
12. If returns for earlier years are outstanding, the IRS says to file them as soon as possible ([When to file](https://www.irs.gov/filing/individuals/when-to-file)). Each earlier year uses that year's figures, not the 2026 tables.

### How to file Form 1040 and pay, step by step

Line numbers are from the 2025 Form 1040, the latest the IRS had printed when read. Check them against the 2026 form when it is issued.

**What you need first:** a taxpayer identification number; Forms W-2, 1099 and any other income records; last year's return and adjusted gross income; any Identity Protection PIN; and bank details for a refund or payment.

1. **Pick the filing route.** Use the current-season criteria in the filing-route section above, enter any partner through IRS.gov, and confirm the software supports the required forms.
2. **Complete Form 1040** in this order ([Form 1040](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/f1040.pdf), read 2026-10-31): income to line 9 (total income), adjustments, line 11a adjusted gross income, line 12e standard or itemized deduction, line 15 taxable income, then tax, credits and other taxes to line 24 (total tax). Payments total on line 33. The overpayment is line 34, with line 35a the part refunded; a balance owed goes on line 37. Attach Schedules 1, 1-A, 2, 3, A or C as the method above says.
3. **Enter the Identity Protection PIN if you have one.** A wrong or missing IP PIN gets an e-filed return rejected, and it delays a paper return ([IRS: IP PIN](https://www.irs.gov/identity-theft-fraud-scams/get-an-identity-protection-pin), read 2026-10-31).
For a self-prepared e-filed return, sign and validate using prior-year AGI or the prior-year Self-Select PIN. If issued an IP PIN, enter it as the software requests; the IRS says it verifies identity in place of prior-year AGI/PIN. Obtain missing AGI from the retained return, preparer or IRS account; do not reuse a zero-AGI instruction written for a different filing year. [IRS electronic-return validation](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/validating-your-electronically-filed-tax-return).

4. **Send it by April 15, 2027.** E-file is timed by transmission; paper by postmark, to the address in the form instructions ([Topic no. 301](https://www.irs.gov/taxtopics/tc301), read 2026-10-31). Need more time? There are 3 ways to get the extension to October 15, all requested by April 15, 2027 (or the applicable original due date): pay what you owe with an online payment option and tick the box saying the payment is for an extension (no Form 4868 needed); request it electronically through IRS Free File; or file Form 4868 by mail, online with an IRS e-filing partner, or through a tax professional. Tax owed is still due by the April date ([IRS: get an extension](https://www.irs.gov/filing/get-an-extension-to-file-your-tax-return), read 2026-10-31).
Save the transmitted return, electronic postmark and IRS **accepted** acknowledgment from the software/provider. Free File confirms acceptance by email; a transmission receipt alone is not acceptance. If rejected, read the rejection reason, correct it and resubmit. If a timely attempted electronic return must instead be filed on paper, the IRS gives the later of the applicable filing due date (including extensions) or 10 calendar days after its rejection notice; include the rejection explanation/history and the required notation. An acceptance does not establish that a scheduled payment cleared. [IRS Free File, How it works](https://www.irs.gov/e-file-do-your-taxes-for-free); [IRS e-file rejection FAQ](https://www.irs.gov/faqs/electronic-filing-e-file); [IRS payment help](https://www.irs.gov/filing/free-file-fillable-forms/free-file-fillable-forms-payment-help).

5. **Pay any balance by the due date.** Bank account (Direct Pay) is free and works as a guest. Debit or credit card and digital wallet carry fees. You can also use your online account, same-day wire, check or money order, or cash. Send Form 1040-V with any check or money order ([Form 1040-V](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/f1040v.pdf), read 2026-10-31). If you cannot pay in full, use a payment plan, set up in your Individual Account ([IRS: payments](https://www.irs.gov/payments), read 2026-10-31; [Amended return FAQ](https://www.irs.gov/filing/amended-return-frequently-asked-questions), read 2026-10-31).
6. **Track the refund and your account.** E-filing with direct deposit is the quickest and safest way to get a refund ([IRS: get ready to file](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/get-ready-to-file-your-taxes), read 2026-10-31). Use Where's My Refund? on IRS.gov, which updates once every 24 hours. Your [IRS Online Account](https://www.irs.gov/payments/online-account-for-individuals) (read 2026-10-31) shows refund status, balances by tax year, payment history, adjusted gross income, W-2 and certain 1099 documents, and notices. Check 24 hours after e-filing a current-year return, 3 days after e-filing a prior-year return, or 4 weeks after a paper return. Expect it about 3 weeks after e-filing, or 6 or more weeks after the IRS receives a mailed return. You need your SSN or ITIN, filing status, tax year and exact refund amount ([IRS: refunds](https://www.irs.gov/refunds), read 2026-10-31). Record your adjusted gross income for next year ([How to file](https://www.irs.gov/filing/individuals/how-to-file), read 2026-10-31).
7. **To correct it,** file Form 1040-X if filing status, income, deductions, credits, dependents or tax changes. You generally do not need to if the IRS already fixed the error. A refund claim is generally due within 3 years of filing the original or 2 years of paying the tax, whichever is later ([IRS: file an amended return](https://www.irs.gov/filing/file-an-amended-return), read 2026-10-31). E-file works for the current or two prior tax periods. File on paper if the return is older than the current or two prior tax periods, or if the original was filed on paper earlier this year for a prior tax year. A federal change can change your state tax: correct the state return with your state tax agency, and do not attach it to Form 1040-X. Allow 8 to 12 weeks, sometimes up to 16; it can take up to 3 weeks to appear in Where's My Amended Return ([Amended return FAQ](https://www.irs.gov/filing/amended-return-frequently-asked-questions), read 2026-10-31). Full method: `us-amended-return-form-1040-including-capital`.

**Stop and get a professional when:** anything under "When to refuse or refer" applies, or the IRS has sent a notice about the return.

## Worked cases

### Worked decision: which should I take, the standard deduction or itemized deductions? (tax year 2026)

The IRS rule is to itemize when allowable itemized deductions exceed the standard deduction, or when you cannot use the standard deduction ([Topic no. 501](https://www.irs.gov/taxtopics/tc501), read 2026-10-31). A married couple filing jointly, under 65, has adjusted gross income of USD 200,000 and pays USD 14,000 of deductible mortgage interest, USD 11,000 of state and local tax (under the USD 40,400 cap), and USD 6,000 of cash gifts to eligible section 170(b)(1)(A) charities, excluding supporting organizations and donor-advised funds. This uses adjusted gross income as the contribution base, with no carryover.

- Itemized: charity counts above 0.5% of 200,000 = USD 1,000, so USD 5,000. Total 14,000 + 11,000 + 5,000 = USD 30,000.
- Standard: USD 32,200, plus up to USD 2,000 of cash gifts for a joint non-itemizer = USD 34,200.
- The deciding figure: the couple needs itemized deductions above USD 34,200, so USD 4,200 more than they have. Standard is better. With USD 20,000 of mortgage interest, itemized is USD 36,000 and itemizing wins by USD 1,800.
- What the choice is worth: on the Table 1 schedule, taxable income of USD 165,800 on the standard route gives tax of USD 25,900; taxable income of USD 170,000 if itemized gives USD 26,824. Standard saves USD 924, which is USD 4,200 at 22%. This assumes no other income adjustments.

```oa-check
name: Standard or itemized, married filing jointly, 2026
inputs:
  agi: 200000
  mortgage: 14000
  salt: 11000
  cash_gifts: 6000
steps:
  - floor: agi * 0.005
  - itemized: mortgage + min(salt, 40400) + cash_gifts - floor
  - standard_route: 32200 + min(cash_gifts, 2000)
  - gap: standard_route - itemized
  - itemized_higher_mortgage: itemized + 6000
  - itemizing_wins_by: itemized_higher_mortgage - standard_route
expected:
  itemized: 30000
  standard_route: 34200
  gap: 4200
  itemized_higher_mortgage: 36000
  itemizing_wins_by: 1800
```

```oa-check
name: Tax on each route, married filing jointly, 2026 Table 1
inputs:
  agi: 200000
  standard_route: 34200
  itemized: 30000
  band_base: 11600
  band_start: 100800
  band_rate: 0.22
steps:
  - taxable_standard: agi - standard_route
  - taxable_itemized: agi - itemized
  - tax_standard: band_base + (taxable_standard - band_start) * band_rate
  - tax_itemized: band_base + (taxable_itemized - band_start) * band_rate
  - standard_saves: tax_itemized - tax_standard
expected:
  taxable_standard: 165800
  taxable_itemized: 170000
  tax_standard: 25900
  tax_itemized: 26824
  standard_saves: 924
```

## When to refuse or refer

- Nonresident aliens: they do not file Form 1040 under the pages cited here.
- Amended returns: the IRS uses Form 1040-X. See the skill `us-amended-return-form-1040-including-capital`.
- Estimated tax payments for the year: see the skill `us-quarterly-estimated-tax`.
- State or local income tax returns of any state.
- A decedent's final return, gift tax (Form 709), and expatriation filings (Form 8854): these involve rules not addressed here.
- Tax years other than 2026, except the rows marked 2025: each other year has its own figures.
- A disputed filing status, residency election, married-separate or dependent filing exception requiring interpretation beyond the statutory tests above.
- Disaster postponements: use the deadline the IRS sets for that disaster.
- A tips claim where it is unclear whether the occupation counts or whether the business is a specified service trade or business: the Act leaves the occupation list and further conditions to the Treasury, and those were not read for this skill.
- Excess business losses, the qualified business income deduction, passive activity losses and the alternative minimum tax computation itself: this skill gives the amounts only, not the method.
