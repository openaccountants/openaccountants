# Hiring your children and reimbursing owners through an accountable plan, tax year 2026

> **Built from the law. Not yet approved.** Federal tax reference for 2026, using the official provisions identified below. Hypothetical cases assume US-resident, unmarried dependent children eligible for the basic standard deduction, not blind, with no other income unless stated. This reference is not a filing opinion under Circular 230 §10.37; review the taxpayer’s actual facts and cited law before filing.

**Answer first.** A child **under 18** working in a parent's **sole proprietorship**, a partnership whose **only partners are the child's parents**, or a parent's **disregarded single-member LLC** pays no social security or Medicare tax on wages, and no FUTA applies **under 21**. A **corporation or S corporation never qualifies**, even if the parent owns 100%. Income tax rules apply at any age: a dependent child's standard deduction for 2026 is the greater of **USD 1,350** or earned income plus **USD 450**, capped at **USD 16,100**, so where wages are the only income and the child is an unmarried US resident eligible for this basic deduction, a reasonable wage up to USD 16,100 bears no federal income tax; the kiddie tax never reaches wages. Separately, an **accountable plan** (business connection, substantiation, return of excess) lets a business reimburse an employee, including an S corporation owner-employee, tax-free; the optional mileage allowance is **72.5 cents** a mile before July and **76 cents** for allowances paid on or after 1 July for costs incurred on or after that date, with business miles and purpose substantiated ([Announcement 2026-11](https://www.irs.gov/irb/2026-29_irb)).

## Ask the client first

1. **What legal entity employs the child, and how is it taxed:** sole proprietorship, disregarded single-member LLC, partnership (who are ALL the partners?), or a corporation including an LLC taxed as an S or C corporation?
2. **The child's date of birth**, and when in 2026 the child turns 18 or 21.
3. **The work:** what the child actually does, hours, rate, what the business would pay an unrelated person for the same work, and whether there are timesheets.
4. **The child's other income** (interest, dividends, capital gains) and who claims the child as a dependent.
5. **For reimbursements:** is the person on payroll as an employee, is there a written plan, what arrangement actually operates, and when were expenses paid, submitted and any excess returned? The 60/120-day rules below are safe harbours, not the only reasonable periods.
6. **For travel and home office:** what percentage of the corporation does the person own (directly and through family), and is any home office for the employer's convenience?

### Part A, hiring your own child

1. **Identify the legal employer and its tax classification.** A sole proprietor is the employer; a separate corporation or partnership may be the employer. An LLC label alone does not decide the result. The payroll tax result turns on this (IRS [Family employees](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/family-help); [26 C.F.R. 31.3121(b)(3)-1](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-26/section-31.3121%28b%29%283%29-1)).
2. **Confirm the child's age when the services were performed** and that the work is in the parent's trade or business. Social security and Medicare exemption ends at 18; FUTA at 21 ([26 U.S.C. 3306(c)(5)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title26/html/USCODE-2024-title26-subtitleC-chap23-sec3306.htm); [26 U.S.C. 3121(b)(3)(A)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title26/html/USCODE-2024-title26-subtitleC-chap21-subchapC-sec3121.htm)).
3. **Set a wage that is reasonable for work the child actually does.** The deduction is "a reasonable allowance for salaries or other compensation for personal services actually rendered", tested on "whether they are reasonable and are in fact payments purely for services", against what "would ordinarily be paid for like services by like enterprises under like circumstances" ([26 C.F.R. 1.162-7](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-26/section-1.162-7)).
4. **Run the child through payroll like any employee:** name and SSN, income tax withholding under the normal rules and the applicable Form W-2 reporting test below ([Pub. 15 (2026), sections 3, 4 and 9](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p15)).
5. **Check the child's own income tax:** the dependent standard deduction ([26 U.S.C. 63(c)(5)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title26/html/USCODE-2024-title26-subtitleA-chap1-subchapB-partI-sec63.htm)) and the kiddie tax, which does not reach wages ([26 U.S.C. 1(g)(4)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title26/html/USCODE-2024-title26-subtitleA-chap1-subchapA-partI-sec1.htm)).

### Part B, accountable plans

6. **Document and operate an accountable reimbursement arrangement** meeting all three tests of [26 C.F.R. 1.62-2](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-26/section-1.62-2): business connection, substantiation within a reasonable period, return of excess within a reasonable period.
7. **Choose a substantiation method.** Use substantiated actual allowable costs or an eligible mileage/per diem allowance; the allowance limits below are deemed-substantiation limits, not a ceiling on reimbursement of proven actual business costs ([Notice 2026-10, section 2](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/n-26-10.pdf)).
8. **Report correctly.** Qualifying reimbursements stay off Form W-2. Nonaccountable payments and retained/unsubstantiated excess are wages as explained below ([Treas. Reg. 1.62-2(c), (h)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-26/section-1.62-2)).

## Boundary and exception table

### Who gets the child payroll tax exemption

The exemption depends on WHO employs the child, not on whether the parent owns the business (IRS Family employees page).

| Employer | Child's pay | IRS wording |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Parent's sole proprietorship, or a partnership in which each partner is a parent of the child | Income tax withholding at any age; no social security or Medicare under 18; no FUTA under 21 | "Payments for the services of a child under the age 18 are not subject to social security and Medicare taxes." |
| Same, child aged 21 or older | FUTA applies | "If the child is 21 years or older, then payments for the services of a child are subject to FUTA taxes." |
| A corporation (C or S), a partnership unless each partner is a parent, or an estate | The family exemption does not apply: ordinary income tax withholding, social security, Medicare and FUTA coverage rules apply regardless of age | "subject to income tax withholding, social security taxes, Medicare taxes and FUTA taxes regardless of age" |
| Parent's single-member LLC disregarded for income tax | Treated as a corporation for employment taxes generally (Treas. Reg. 301.7701-2(c)(2)(iv)(B)), but for this family exemption the owner is treated as the employer, so it can qualify if the owner is the parent and the other conditions are met; FICA and FUTA look-through apply to wages paid on or after 1 November 2011 ([31.3121(b)(3)-1(d)–(e)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-26/section-31.3121%28b%29%283%29-1), [31.3306(c)(5)-1(d)–(e)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-26/section-31.3306%28c%29%285%29-1)) | |

- **Turning 18 mid-year.** The exemption is lost for service performed from the 18th birthday, not retroactively for the year: the statute exempts "service performed by a child under the age of 18" and the regulation says the exception "continues only during the time that" the child is under the limit. A payment mixing pre-birthday and post-birthday services needs allocation; no mixed-payment result is usable here. Check this point with one of our partner accountants.
- **The regulation's old age.** Treas. Reg. 31.3121(b)(3)-1(a)(3) still prints "under the age of 21". The statute as amended, and Pub. 15 (2026), say 18 for work in the parent's trade or business; the statute governs. Use the regulation for who the employer is (paragraphs (c) and (d)), not for the age.
- **A corporation never qualifies,** including an S corporation, a C corporation, and an LLC that elected either, even at 100% parent ownership: "Services performed in the employ of a corporation are not within the exception" (Treas. Reg. 31.3121(b)(3)-1(d)); Pub. 15 lists "A corporation, even if it is controlled by the child's parent".
- **A partnership qualifies only if EVERY partner is a parent of the child.** A parent and a grandparent, a sibling or an unrelated partner do not (Treas. Reg. 31.3121(b)(3)-1(c)).
- **Income tax withholding still applies.** A child with no income tax liability last year and none expected this year may claim exemption from withholding on Form W-4; the wages "are still subject to social security and Medicare taxes" where those apply (Pub. 15 (2026), section 9).
- **Domestic work in the parent's private home** (not the business) follows a different rule: no social security or Medicare until 21. Household employment is outside this business method. Check this point with one of our partner accountants.

### The child's own income tax

- For an unmarried US-resident dependent eligible for the basic standard deduction, not blind and with wages as the only income, the basic deduction is the lesser of USD 16,100 and the greater of USD 1,350 or wages plus USD 450 (section 63(c)(5) only limits the basic standard deduction; it never raises it above the single amount). Under those conditions, wages up to USD 16,100 leave no taxable income; above it, wages less the deduction enter the income-tax computation. Section 63(c)(6) excludes, among others, a nonresident alien and a married separate filer whose spouse itemizes; additional aged/blind deductions require a separate computation ([section 63(c)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title26/html/USCODE-2024-title26-subtitleA-chap1-subchapB-partI-sec63.htm), [P.L. 119-21 §70102](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/PLAW-119publ21/html/PLAW-119publ21.htm)).
- The kiddie tax taxes "net unearned income", the part of AGI "which is not attributable to earned income" (section 1(g)(4)(A)). Wages for work actually done are earned income, so they are outside it at any age (IRS [Topic no. 553](https://www.irs.gov/taxtopics/tc553)). If the child also has unearned income, the deduction is still built from earned income only and the unearned income may fall under the kiddie tax; do not use the wages-only result for the full return. Check this point with one of our partner accountants.
- For the child's IRA or Roth IRA eligibility, compensation test and annual limit, use `us-self-employed-retirement`; no contribution amount is determined here.

### Reasonable pay for real work

- The work must be real and done by the child: tasks the child can do at that age, for the business, not household chores.
- The rate should match what the business would pay an unrelated person of similar ability.
- Recommended proof of actual services and payment: contemporaneous timesheets or an hours log, duties, pay stubs and traceable payment to the child (an account in the child's name is useful evidence, not a statutory wage-deduction condition). Keep employment-tax records for at least four years ([Pub. 15, Recordkeeping](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p15)). Pay above the reasonable amount is not deductible as wages.
- Apply the Form W-2 reporting test below; the employer must get "each employee's name and SSN and to enter them on Form W-2" (Pub. 15 (2026), section 4).

### Accountable plan: the three requirements (all three)

| Requirement | What it means | Regulation wording |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Business connection, paragraph (d) | Pays only for deductible business expenses the employee pays or incurs performing services as an employee. Payment regardless of whether the employee incurs or is reasonably expected to incur expenses fails | "If a payor arranges to pay an amount to an employee regardless of whether the employee incurs" |
| 2. Substantiation, paragraph (e) | Each expense substantiated to the payor within a reasonable period; for travel, car use and other section 274(d) items: amount, time, place or use, business purpose | "requires each business expense to be substantiated to the payor" |
| 3. Return of excess, paragraph (f) | Employee returns within a reasonable period any amount beyond substantiated expenses | "requires the employee to return to the payor within a reasonable period of time" |
| Fixed date safe harbour (not mandatory deadlines), paragraph (g)(2)(i) | Advance within 30 days; substantiation within 60 days after the expense; excess returned within 120 days after the expense | "An advance made within 30 days of when an expense is paid or incurred" |
| Periodic statement safe harbour, paragraph (g)(2)(ii) | Statements at least quarterly; amounts substantiated or returned within 120 days of the statement are timely | "no less frequently than quarterly" |

Outside the safe harbours the reasonable period "will depend on the facts and circumstances" (paragraph (g)(1)). If wages and a reimbursement are paid together, the reimbursement must be paid or identified separately (paragraph (d)(1)). A pattern of abuse turns every payment under the arrangement into a nonaccountable payment (paragraph (k)); a practice of over-reimbursement to avoid reporting/withholding also bars the safe harbours (paragraph (g)(3)). A written policy helps document the arrangement; writing alone neither creates nor proves accountable treatment.

The plan covers expenses an EMPLOYEE pays "in connection with the performance of services as an employee of the employer" (paragraph (d)(1)). "Generally, an officer of a corporation is an employee of the corporation" ([26 C.F.R. 31.3121(d)-1(b)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-26/section-31.3121%28d%29-1)); an officer who performs no or only minor services and neither receives nor is entitled to any direct or indirect remuneration is not. So an S corporation shareholder who works as an officer and is on payroll can be reimbursed under the corporation's plan. A sole proprietor's own costs and a partner's costs are outside this Guide.

### What happens if the plan fails

- An arrangement failing a requirement is nonaccountable: payments are wages on Form W-2 and subject to the applicable employment-tax rules (paragraphs (c)(3), (c)(5), (h)(2)(ii)). Requirements apply employee by employee (paragraph (i)). A separate nonaccountable arrangement does not contaminate an otherwise qualifying one. Paragraph (d)(2) treats reimbursements of deductible expenses and other bona fide business-related nondeductible expenses as two arrangements; paragraph (h)(1) separately protects the nondeductible meal portion due solely to section 274(n).
- The employee cannot fix it afterwards: an employee "cannot compel the payor to treat the payments as paid under an accountable plan by voluntarily substantiating the expenses and returning any excess" (paragraph (c)(3)(i)).
- Under an otherwise qualifying plan, only the unsubstantiated or unreturned excess becomes wages; the substantiated amount remains accountable (paragraphs (c)(2)(ii), (c)(3)(ii)). Ordinary unsubstantiated/unreturned amounts enter payroll no later than the first payroll period after the reasonable period ends (paragraph (h)(2)(i)(A)); above-rate mileage/per diem reimbursements enter payroll when paid (paragraph (h)(2)(i)(B)(2)).
- For an ordinary S corporation shareholder-employee, unreimbursed employee expenses do not yield a miscellaneous itemized deduction. Public Law 119-21 section 70110 removed the sunset and redesignated the prohibition as section 67(h), effective for taxable years beginning after 31 December 2025 ([Public Law 119-21](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/PLAW-119publ21/html/PLAW-119publ21.htm)). Section 70110 also adds qualifying educator expenses to the non-miscellaneous list. Separate above-the-line exceptions for reservists, qualifying performing artists, fee-basis officials and eligible educators remain; do not extend the ordinary owner case to those categories ([Notice 2026-10 §3](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/n-26-10.pdf)).

### S corporation owner-employee: the usual items

Business miles in the owner's car, travel away from home, business costs on a personal card, and the home office, the item most often argued about:

- Under section 280A(c)(1), a home-office portion must be used exclusively and regularly as a principal place of business, a place for meeting patients/clients/customers in the normal course, or an unattached separate business structure. Principal place includes administrative/management use if no other fixed location carries substantial such activities. An employee must additionally use it "for the convenience of his employer" ([26 U.S.C. 280A(c)(1)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title26/html/USCODE-2024-title26-subtitleA-chap1-subchapB-partIX-sec280A.htm)).
- If that test fails, the cost is not the employee's business expense, so it fails the business connection test and a reimbursement is wages.
- Home-cost allocation and the section 280A(c)(5) limit are not computed here. No reimbursement amount is usable without that computation. Check this point with one of our partner accountants.

### Car use

For a non-FAVR allowance paid instead of actual car costs, the amount deemed substantiated is the **lesser of the allowance paid or the applicable rate multiplied by substantiated business miles**. The employee must also substantiate time, place/use and business purpose ([Rev. Proc. 2019-46 §§7.01–7.02](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/rp-19-46.pdf)). The 76-cent rate requires both payment of the allowance and incurring the transportation expense on/after 1 July; otherwise Notice 2026-10's 72.5-cent rate continues ([Announcement 2026-11](https://www.irs.gov/irb/2026-29_irb)).

An above-rate mileage allowance for substantiated miles is wages to the extent of the excess (§§7.05, 8.01). This does not prohibit a separate, fully substantiated actual allowable cost method. Do not reimburse the same cost twice. An allowance paid whether or not expenses are incurred or reasonably expected fails the business-connection test; a flat amount is not automatically wages if it is a reasonable advance under a qualifying arrangement and excess is substantiated/returned ([Treas. Reg. 1.62-2(d), (f)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-26/section-1.62-2)).

FAVR is a separate method: at least five employees must be covered at all times; control employees are barred under section 6.05(2), using 1.61-21(f)(5)–(6) **excluding the dollar limitation in (f)(5)(iii)**. A majority of covered employees cannot be management employees; substantiated employee mileage must meet the 5,000-mile/80%-of-plan-mileage test, subject to monthly proration. These are not all FAVR vehicle, depreciation and reporting conditions. No FAVR payment is determined here. Check this point with one of our partner accountants. [Rev. Proc. 2019-46 §6](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/rp-19-46.pdf).

### Travel: per diem, and the trap for owners of more than 10 percent

- For ordinary and necessary travel **away from home** as an employee, section 4.01 deems the lesser of the allowance paid or the applicable federal lodging/M&IE rate substantiated; time, place and business purpose must still be proved. Use the locality and travel day, prorating M&IE on partial travel days by three-fourths or another consistently applied reasonable business method (§6.04). GSA publishes CONUS rates; Defense/State publish the applicable non-CONUS rates. No destination rate or high-low computation is reproduced here. [Rev. Proc. 2019-48 §§3.02, 4.01, 6.04, 7.01](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/rp-19-48.pdf).
- Section 6.07 bars the **full lodging/M&IE** and **high-low** methods (§§4.01, 5) for a payor and employee related under section 267(b), substituting **10%** for section 267(b)(2)'s ownership percentage. The corporate test is **more than** 10% in value, directly or indirectly. Section 267(c) attributes family stock (siblings, spouse, ancestors and lineal descendants), so a child or parent can be related to the corporation through attribution. For these related employees, reimburse lodging using substantiated actual allowable costs and receipts. [Rev. Proc. 2019-48 §6.07](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/rp-19-48.pdf); [26 U.S.C. 267(b)–(c)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title26/html/USCODE-2024-title26-subtitleA-chap1-subchapB-partIX-sec267.htm).
- The related-party bar does **not** name §4.02's M&IE-only allowance. It can be used, including for a related employee, when that section's travel/other requirements hold: lodging is reimbursed at actual cost on receipts, provided in kind, paid directly to its provider, not reasonably expected to be incurred, or the allowance uses a compensation-like basis subject to §3.03(2): for an allowance computed like wages, it must have been separately identified by the payor or commonly used in the industry as of 12 December 1989. For the ordinary owner case, use receipted actual lodging plus the lesser of the M&IE allowance or applicable federal M&IE rate, with time/place/purpose and partial-day proration. Do not also reimburse the same meals or incidentals (§6.06). [Rev. Proc. 2019-48 §§4.02, 6.07, 7.01](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/rp-19-48.pdf).

### Defaults when a fact is missing

| Missing fact | Default, and flag it |
| --- | --- |
| Entity type of the employer | Do not claim a family exemption until the actual employer and tax classification are documented |
| Child's date of birth | Hold the age exemption until age at service is documented; do not guess |
| Timesheets | Obtain adequate evidence of actual services and reasonable pay; a missing particular form is not automatically disallowance |
| Written accountable plan | Inspect the actual arrangement and its operation; a missing written policy alone does not establish wages |
| Mileage dates | Hold the mileage allowance exclusion until business use, expense dates and allowance payment dates are substantiated |
| Owner's percentage | Verify direct/attributed ownership before using full/high-low per diem; use substantiated actual lodging while eligibility is unresolved |

### Red flags to raise

| Flag | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| Child paid by an S corporation with no payroll taxes | The exemption does not exist for corporations |
| Child's wage set at exactly USD 16,100 with no record of hours | Looks set by the deduction, not the work |
| Flat monthly allowance to an owner | Check expected business expenses, substantiation and excess return; an unconditional payment is wages |
| Reimbursements decided after year end | Test the actual arrangement and reasonable period; year end alone is not failure, and wages cannot simply be relabelled |
| Owner reimbursed on per diem for lodging | Full/high-low allowance is barred if the related-party test applies; actual receipted lodging remains possible |

## Worked cases

**Case 1 (historical example A), same child, two employers.** A 15-year-old works weekends in a parent's business, keeps a timesheet, and is paid a reasonable USD 12,000 for 2026 through payroll; no other income; claimed as a dependent. Case 1A, the parent's sole proprietorship: no social security or Medicare (under 18) and no FUTA (under 21). Case 1B, the parent's S corporation: USD 744 social security (6.2%) plus USD 174 Medicare (1.45%) = USD 918 withheld, the employer pays the same again, USD 1,836 in total, and FUTA applies. Income tax is the same in both: the child's standard deduction is the lesser of USD 16,100 and the greater of USD 1,350 or USD 12,450 (wages plus USD 450), so USD 12,450, and taxable income is nil. At wages of USD 17,000 instead, the deduction is capped at USD 16,100 and USD 900 is taxable. The business deducts reasonable wages and its own deductible payroll-tax costs; in 1A the wage deduction also cuts the parent's net self-employment income ([Treas. Reg. 1.162-7](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-26/section-1.162-7); [1.162-1(a)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-26/section-1.162-1); [26 U.S.C. 1402(a)](https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/t26/s1402/)).

**Case 2 (historical example B), a decision: what mileage rate to reimburse.** An S corporation's shareholder-employee drives 1,000 business miles in March 2026 and 1,000 in September 2026, logs each trip and submits the log within 60 days. Assume the corporation reimburses after each trip and timely submission, using an otherwise qualifying accountable plan and the optional mileage allowance method. Reimbursed at the right rates: USD 725 for March (72.5 cents) plus USD 760 for September (76 cents), USD 1,485, all under the accountable plan and off Form W-2. Paid at 72.5 cents throughout (USD 1,450), the September miles are under-reimbursed by USD 35, which this ordinary owner-employee cannot claim as a miscellaneous itemized deduction. Paid at 76 cents throughout (USD 1,520), the USD 35 excess on the March miles is wages. Without timely adequate substantiation of business miles, time, place/use and business purpose, the unsubstantiated allowance is wages; absence of one particular log format alone is not conclusive. Reimburse using the applicable expense-date and payment-date rate. Sources: [Rev. Proc. 2019-46 §§7–8](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/rp-19-46.pdf), [Announcement 2026-11](https://www.irs.gov/irb/2026-29_irb), [Treas. Reg. 1.62-2(h)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-26/section-1.62-2).

```oa-check
name: Mileage reimbursement across the two 2026 rates
inputs:
  miles_h1: 1000
  miles_h2: 1000
steps:
  - h1: miles_h1 * 0.725
  - h2: miles_h2 * 0.76
  - correct_total: h1 + h2
  - all_at_h1_rate: (miles_h1 + miles_h2) * 0.725
  - shortfall_if_h1_rate: correct_total - all_at_h1_rate
  - all_at_h2_rate: (miles_h1 + miles_h2) * 0.76
  - wages_if_h2_rate: all_at_h2_rate - correct_total
expected:
  h1: 725
  h2: 760
  correct_total: 1485
  all_at_h1_rate: 1450
  shortfall_if_h1_rate: 35
  all_at_h2_rate: 1520
  wages_if_h2_rate: 35
```

## Filing and payment

Apply the ordinary income-tax withholding rules and valid Form W-4. Form 941 is generally quarterly for wages subject to income-tax withholding or FICA, subject to Form 944 notification and other filing exceptions; use `us-form-941-940-payroll` for the employer's actual return/deposit obligations and Form 940/FUTA tests ([Pub. 15 §§9 and 12](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p15)). Do not assume a child exemption eliminates the employer's existing filing obligations.

For 2026 Form W-2, report when tax was withheld, would have been withheld absent the withholding exemption, or wages reach USD 2,000 even with no tax withheld. The USD 2,000 test is not the sole filing trigger ([2026 W-2/W-3 instructions, Who must file](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/iw2w3); [P.L. 119-21 §70433](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/PLAW-119publ21/html/PLAW-119publ21.htm)). Furnish required 2026 W-2 statements and file with the SSA by **1 February 2027**; 31 January 2027 is Sunday (ordinary deadline, absent applicable relief). Report income-tax wages in box 1 and only FICA-covered wages in boxes 3 and 5; exclude the family-exempt wages from those FICA boxes. Accountable payments are not W-2 wages; report nonaccountable amounts as applicable. See the [2026 W-2/W-3 instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/iw2w3) and [Treas. Reg. 1.62-2(c)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-26/section-1.62-2).

The child files their own Form 1040 if required, or to claim a refund of withheld tax; the wages-only basic deduction in this Guide is not a complete filing-requirement test. Special filing circumstances and nonresident/treaty questions are not resolved here. Check this point with one of our partner accountants.

## Completion checklist

- Employer entity and all partners identified; corporation cases use ordinary payroll coverage rather than the family exemption.
- Child's age at service checked; 18 and 21 birthdays noted, mixed payments held for allocation.
- Wage supported by timesheets, duties and an unrelated-person rate; paid through payroll; required W-2 reporting completed.
- Child's standard deduction worked from earned income; unearned income checked for the kiddie tax.
- Actual accountable arrangement documented and operated; reasonable periods assessed using safe harbours or facts and circumstances.
- Mileage split between 72.5 and 76 cents using expense and allowance-payment dates; related-party full/high-low lodging per diem barred, M&IE-only/actual-cost options tested.
- Nonaccountable/unsubstantiated excess handled in payroll under the applicable timing and coverage rules.
