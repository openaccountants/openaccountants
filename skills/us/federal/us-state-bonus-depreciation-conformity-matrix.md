# US state bonus depreciation and §179 conformity: 2026 matrix, with 2025 return notes

Use the state, taxpayer type, asset vintage and tax year together. A federal bonus deduction does not establish the state deduction. **HOLD means no usable deduction, addback, recovery or filing conclusion is supplied for that point. Check this point with one of our partner accountants.** A 2025 form reference identifies that year's form; it is not a statement that the same line number exists on the 2026 return.

## Scope and who this is for

Individuals with business or rental income, including sole proprietors and owners of partnerships and S corporations. This is a depreciation reconciliation, not a complete state return. C corporation, franchise, gross-receipts, business-activity and combined/unitary taxes require separate work. State §163(j), NOL and research-expensing outcomes are held separately below; do not infer their conformity from a depreciation row. The special §168(n) production-property election is also held; a row about §168(k) never establishes §168(n) treatment.

## Ask the client first

Obtain the return year; residence, part-year residence and every source state; entity classification and K-1s; acquisition and binding-contract dates; service date; cost, business use, asset class/life and convention; federal §179, bonus, regular depreciation and elections; prior state schedules, loss limitations, recoveries and disposals. Reconcile entity and owner adjustments before making another adjustment. These are information requests, not a finding that an asset qualifies or an election was made.

## The method, step by step

1. Establish the allowable federal deduction on Form 4562 before using any state percentage. Separate §179, bonus and regular depreciation. Section 179 is applied before bonus; regular depreciation uses the remaining basis. [IRS Publication 946, chapters 2–3](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p946).
2. Use the relevant state row and its express year restriction. Never carry a **HOLD** into a return as a zero or as federal conformity.
3. Distinguish a fixed percentage addback/recovery from a recomputation without federal bonus. For the latter, compare the **total** federal and state depreciation for the year, including any current-year state ordinary depreciation. An addback is not always the entire federal bonus or the excess federal §179 election. See California and Indiana below.
4. Maintain an asset-by-asset reconciliation and any fixed recovery schedule. Do not assume every addback changes state basis: North Carolina and Ohio expressly provide otherwise. Apply the state's disposition and transfer rules before releasing a remaining difference. [NC §105-153.6(d)–(g)](https://www.ncleg.gov/EnactedLegislation/Statutes/HTML/BySection/Chapter_105/GS_105-153.6.html); [Ohio §5747.01(A)(17)(b), (18)](https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-5747.01).
5. Determine sourcing, apportionment and entity/owner reporting for the actual year; residence alone does not establish a business-income allocation. Missing state basis records, a relocation, a basis-carryover transfer or an unresolved entity adjustment is a **HOLD**. Check this point with one of our partner accountants.
6. Reconcile the return to the state schedules, preserve the next year's recovery record and review estimated payments using the actual state income adjustment. Filing/payment deadlines and amendment entitlement are not supplied by this matrix.

### Ohio exceptions: apply these before the general fraction

Under [§5747.01(A)(17)–(18)](https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-5747.01):

- A qualifying withholding increase of at least 10% replaces 5/6 with 2/3; recovery is one-half of the addition in **each of two** succeeding years.
- A withholding increase at least equal to the statutory combined bonus/qualifying-§179 amount can remove the addition, subject to the NOL rule. Test the taxpayer and pass-through amounts under the statute.
- To the extent a current federal NOL results from these deductions, the addition is the **entire** applicable amount; recovery is one-sixth in each of six succeeding years.
- Ownership below 5% is **not an automatic exemption**: the commissioner may waive the pass-through addback under established procedures. Without an applicable waiver, do not omit it.
- A later-year subtraction is denied **to the extent** the specified depreciation creates/increases a federal NOL carryback/carryforward; that denied amount carries to the next year, continuing until recovered. Do not block the entire deduction merely because any NOL appears. Other unused-subtraction relief is **HOLD**. Check this point with one of our partner accountants.
- Allocate an addition attributable to nonbusiness property with that property; otherwise apportion. Later deductions use the recovery year's factors unless associated with an allocated addition. Do not freeze the purchase year's business allocation for every later year.
