# US sales tax economic nexus: 50 states and DC, 2026

Sources read on **27 November 2026**. This is a threshold decision matrix for remote sellers, not a rate or return-preparation table. It preserves 2025 transitions and earlier case inputs separately; do not apply the current row retrospectively.

## Scope

The matrix covers every state and DC, including Hawaii GET, New Mexico GRT, Arizona TPT and Alaska local taxes. Puerto Rico remains an explicit non-usable historical referral below. A threshold can create an economic-nexus obligation, but does not settle product taxability, an existing registration, physical/affiliate nexus or a marketplace exception.

## Ask the client first

Obtain state-by-state monthly receipts and transaction counts for 2025 and 2026, earlier years under review, direct versus facilitated sales, taxable/exempt/resale amounts, related entities, facilitator collection certificates, all inventory/employee/agent locations, existing registrations and any state contact. Preserve the underlying transactions so calendar years, rolling periods and tax quarters can be rebuilt.

## The method, step by step

1. Check physical, affiliate and existing-registration obligations before using an economic threshold as a safe harbor. Inventory is important, but “inventory always means register” is unsafe: marketplace and fulfillment exceptions differ. Connecticut expressly requires an out-of-state retailer with goods on a marketplace facilitator’s premises to register even below its thresholds ([DRS](https://portal.ct.gov/drs/businesses/new-business-resource-center/registering-with-drs)).
2. Build each row’s period and sales base. **Over/exceeds** excludes equality; **at least** includes it. An **OR** test needs one prong; Connecticut and New York require **both**. No count means transactions alone do not trigger that economic test.
3. Distinguish the seller from a facilitator. Excluding qualified facilitated sales needs evidence that the statutory facilitator conditions are satisfied; a payment processor or shopping-cart website alone is not proof. Aggregation is not limited to Arizona and California: Massachusetts and Virginia also have related-person conditions.
4. Use the commencement table after a test is met. Registration, first collection and first return are separate events. A seller already required to collect must not stop merely because a later period is below a threshold.
5. Use the state Guide for classification, sourcing, rates, registration forms, filing and cancellation. If an outcome is marked HOLD, it is non-usable: do not turn it into an estimated tax, registration date or automatic exemption.

## Worked cases

The five current illustrations assume no other nexus, existing collection duty or earlier qualifying period unless stated. Amounts are invented case inputs, not current rate lookups.

1. **Illinois:** 450 sales of goods, **$80,000**, 12 months ending **31 March 2026**. The transaction limb ended on 1 January 2026. $80,000 is $20,000 below $100,000; the economic test does not trigger on these facts. Retest at the quarter ends. Before 2026, 450 would have met the count limb; that comparison does not itself cancel an existing registration ([IDOR](https://tax.illinois.gov/research/publications/bulletins/fy-2026-12.html)).
2. **New York:** **$520,000 and 90 furniture orders** in the preceding four sales-tax quarters. Dollar limb met, count limb fails: 90 is not over 100. A later window with 101 sales triggers the presumption only if its receipts also remain over $500,000, subject to the statutory rebuttal; a 101st sale alone is insufficient ([Tax Law 1101(b)(8)(iv)](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/TAX/1101)).
3. **Connecticut:** **$150,000 and 150 retail sales**, 12 months ending **30 September 2025**. Dollar limb met, count 50 short: no economic trigger. If the seller has goods on a marketplace facilitator’s Connecticut premises, DRS requires registration even below the thresholds ([statute](https://www.cga.ct.gov/current/pub/chap_219.htm), [DRS](https://portal.ct.gov/drs/businesses/new-business-resource-center/registering-with-drs)).
4. **Georgia/South Carolina:** In **2025**, each state receives **$120,000** of the seller’s own goods: **$110,000** through a collecting facilitator and **$10,000, 50 orders** direct. Georgia’s qualified exclusion gives $120,000 − $110,000 = $10,000; neither >$100,000 nor ≥200 direct sales is met. South Carolina counts $120,000, exceeding $100,000; obtain the retail licence and remit on direct taxable sales, while the facilitator remits its sales ([Georgia](https://dor.georgia.gov/media/35306/download), [South Carolina](https://dor.sc.gov/sites/dor/files/Documents/Policy%20Manuals/Chapter%2013%20-%20Nexus.pdf)).
5. **Arizona:** Direct **2025 sales exactly $100,000**, with no earlier trigger, do not exceed the test. Each year is separate, but a **qualifying previous year does carry an obligation forward**. If **2026** direct sales first exceed $100,000 on **20 August 2026**, obtain the licence then and remit from **1 October 2026** (first month starting at least 30 days later), through the rest of **2026 and all of 2027**. Include affiliated persons before testing ([A.R.S. 42-5044](https://www.azleg.gov/ars/42/05044.htm)).

```oa-check
name: New York AND test, 520,000 over 90 sales (2026)
inputs:
  receipts: 520000
  sales_count: 90
steps:
  - receipts_over: receipts - 500000
  - sales_over: sales_count - 100
  - both_met: min(receipts_over, sales_over)
expected:
  receipts_over: 20000
  sales_over: -10
  both_met: -10
```

A negative `both_met` means at least one prong fails, so no registration from the economic test.

```oa-check
name: Same sales, Georgia excludes facilitated sales, South Carolina counts them (2025)
inputs:
  own_goods_sales: 120000
  facilitated: 110000
  threshold: 100000
steps:
  - georgia_measure: own_goods_sales - facilitated
  - georgia_margin: georgia_measure - threshold
  - sc_measure: own_goods_sales
  - sc_margin: sc_measure - threshold
expected:
  georgia_measure: 10000
  georgia_margin: -90000
  sc_measure: 120000
  sc_margin: 20000
```

Both New York prongs require a **strictly positive** margin. A zero margin is insufficient; the `min` diagnostic is not a general Boolean tax calculator.
