# Form 1040-X amended returns and capital loss carryforward corrections in the United States

Form 1040-X, Amended U.S. Individual Income Tax Return, corrects a Form 1040 series return that has already been filed, or changes amounts the IRS has already adjusted. A separate Form 1040-X is filed for each tax year being amended. A capital loss carryforward error usually touches more than one year, because the amount carried into a year depends on the net loss and the deduction allowed in the year before. Figures are for tax year 2026. An amended return corrects an earlier year, so each table below says which year its page speaks for, and the forms, schedules and instructions for the year being amended govern that year.

## Who this is for

This skill is for individuals amending a Form 1040, 1040-SR, 1040-NR or 1040-SS (and, for periods before 2023, Form 1040-PR), in particular where Schedule D or Form 8949 figures, the allowed capital loss deduction, or the capital loss carryforward were wrong on a return already filed.

It is not for errors the IRS fixes itself. The IRS may correct certain errors on a return and may accept returns without certain required forms or schedules; in those cases there is no need to amend. An amended return is filed if there is a change in filing status, income, deductions, credits or tax liability. It is not for corporate, partnership, estate or trust filers, who use their own amendment forms. It does not cover state amendments: a change on a federal return may change state tax liability, and the state tax agency says how to correct a state return.

### Capital loss deduction and carryforward (IRS topic page)

The page was last reviewed on 24 September 2026. It gives no year for the limit below, and the statute table that follows prints the same limit.

| What | Value | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Source | all figures below | https://www.irs.gov/taxtopics/tc409 |
| Excess capital loss allowed against other income | the lesser of USD 3,000 (USD 1,500 if married filing separately) or the total net loss on line 16 of Schedule D (Form 1040) | "the lesser of $3,000 ($1,500 if married filing separately) or your total net loss shown on line 16 of Schedule D (Form 1040)" |
| Where the allowed loss is claimed | line 7a of Form 1040, Form 1040-SR or Form 1040-NR; if Schedule D does not apply, the box on line 7b of Form 1040 | "Claim the loss on line 7a of your Form 1040, Form 1040-SR or Form 1040-NR" |
| Net capital loss above the limit | carried forward to later years | "If your net capital loss is more than this limit, you can carry the loss forward to later years." |
| Tool for the carryforward | Capital Loss Carryover Worksheet in Publication 550 or in the Instructions for Schedule D (Form 1040) | "You may use the Capital Loss Carryover Worksheet found in Publication 550 or in the Instructions for Schedule D (Form 1040)" |
| Personal-use property | a loss on it is not deductible, so it creates no carryforward | "Losses from the sale of personal-use property, such as your home or car, aren't tax deductible." |

### The statute: 26 U.S.C. 1211(b) and 1212(b)

Text from the Legal Information Institute (law.cornell.edu) mirror of the U.S. Code, as amended.

| What | Value | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Source | all figures below | https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/1211 |
| Loss limit for taxpayers other than corporations | losses allowed up to gains, plus the lower of USD 3,000 (USD 1,500 for a married individual filing a separate return) or the excess of losses over gains | "$3,000 ($1,500 in the case of a married individual filing a separate return), or (2) the excess of such losses over such gains." |

| What | Value | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Source | all rules below | https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/1212 |
| Character of the carryforward | the excess of the net short-term capital loss over the net long-term capital gain carries forward as a short-term capital loss, and the excess of the net long-term capital loss over the net short-term capital gain as a long-term capital loss, into the succeeding taxable year | "(A) the excess of the net short-term capital loss over the net long-term capital gain for such year shall be a short-term capital loss in the succeeding taxable year, and (B) the excess of the net long-term capital loss over the net short-term capital gain for such year shall be a long-term capital loss in the succeeding taxable year" |
| How much of the loss the deduction uses up | the lesser of the amount allowed under section 1211(b) or the adjusted taxable income for the year is treated as a short-term capital gain of that year, so it reduces the short-term loss first and only any remainder reduces the long-term loss | "there shall be treated as a short-term capital gain in the taxable year an amount equal to the lesser of" |
| Adjusted taxable income | taxable income plus the section 1211(b) amount plus the deduction under section 151 or any deduction in lieu of it; a negative figure is taken into account | "any excess of the deductions allowed for the taxable year over the gross income for such year shall be taken into account as negative taxable income" |

### Capital Loss Carryover Worksheet (Schedule D instructions)

This table reads the 2025 Instructions for Schedule D, which carry the worksheet for losses carried from 2024 to 2025. Each year's Schedule D instructions carry their own version; use the one for the year being amended.

| What | Value | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Source | all rules below | https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/i1040sd.pdf |
| When a carryover exists | the prior year's Schedule D line 21 is a loss, and either that loss is smaller than the loss on line 16, or the prior year's Form 1040 line 15 would be less than zero if a negative amount could be entered | "if your 2024 Schedule D, line 21, is a loss and (a) that loss is a smaller loss than the loss on your 2024 Schedule D, line 16; or (b) if the amount on your 2024 Form 1040, 1040-SR or 1040-NR, line 15 would be less than zero if you could enter a negative amount on that line." |
| Where the carryover goes | short-term carryover on Schedule D line 6, long-term on line 14 | "If more than zero, also enter this amount on Schedule D, line 6" |
| Joint return followed by separate returns | a carryover from the joint return is deducted only on the return of the spouse who had the loss | "any capital loss carryover from the joint return can be deducted only on the return of the spouse who actually had the loss" |

## The method, step by step

1. Confirm an amendment is needed. Per [Topic no. 308, Amended returns](https://www.irs.gov/taxtopics/tc308), the IRS may correct certain errors and may accept returns without certain required forms or schedules, and then no amendment is needed; file if filing status, income, deductions, credits or tax liability changes. The IRS Interactive Tax Assistant has a "Should I file an amended return?" tool, named on [When and how to amend a tax return](https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/when-and-how-to-amend-a-tax-return).
2. Start with the earliest wrong year. Each year's carryforward is built from the prior year's Schedule D result, so rebuild the chain from the first incorrect year forward. Pull the filed Schedule D (Form 1040) and Form 8949 for every year in the chain. Check each year against the IRS's own records, which the individual online account shows under tax records ([Where's My Amended Return?](https://www.irs.gov/filing/wheres-my-amended-return)). Under the [Instructions for Form 1040-X](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1040x), column A shows the original amounts unless the return was already amended, audited and changed, or changed by an IRS notice; in those cases column A shows the amounts as adjusted.
3. Test each affected year against the time limits in the first table before promising a refund. Apply the 3-year and 2-year rule, the early-filing and extension rules, and the lookback period, which caps the refund at tax paid within that period ([Instructions for Form 1040-X](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1040x)). [When and how to amend a tax return](https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/when-and-how-to-amend-a-tax-return) says special rules apply to net operating losses, foreign tax credits, bad debts and other issues, and that people in disaster relief situations or combat zone service, or with bad debts, foreign tax credits or loss or credit carrybacks, may have more time.
4. Recompute the gains and losses for the year. Per [Topic no. 409, Capital gains and losses](https://www.irs.gov/taxtopics/tc409), most sales are reported and the gain or loss figured on Form 8949, then summarised on Schedule D (Form 1040). The allowed excess loss, up to the limit in the capital loss table, goes on line 7a of Form 1040, 1040-SR or 1040-NR. Use the forms and instructions for the year being amended, as the [Instructions for Form 1040-X](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1040x) direct.
5. Recompute the carryforward with the Capital Loss Carryover Worksheet in the [Instructions for Schedule D](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/i1040sd.pdf) for that year, or in Publication 550. Keep short-term and long-term carryovers apart: under [26 U.S.C. 1212(b)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/1212) each keeps its character in the next year. The amount allowed under section 1211(b) is treated as a short-term capital gain of the loss year, so it comes out of the short-term loss first. Where the loss year's taxable income was low or negative, the deduction uses up only the lesser of the allowed amount or adjusted taxable income, so more of the loss carries forward. Feed each corrected year's result into the next year's Schedule D lines 6 and 14.
6. Follow the change through the rest of the return. The [Instructions for Form 1040-X](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1040x) warn that a change to adjusted gross income can change other amounts, such as credits that depend on AGI. Recompute those lines for each amended year.
7. Prepare one Form 1040-X for each corrected year under the [Instructions for Form 1040-X](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1040x). Enter the net change for each line in column B and explain each change in Part II. Attach the corrected Form 8949, Schedule D and any schedule or form relating to the change. For an electronic amendment, the [amended return FAQ](https://www.irs.gov/filing/amended-return-frequently-asked-questions) requires all necessary forms and schedules as if it were the original submission, even those with no adjustments. [When and how to amend a tax return](https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/when-and-how-to-amend-a-tax-return) adds a corrected Form 1040, 1040-SR or 1040-NR for the year, with supporting documents and updated forms or schedules. For a paper Form 1040-X this is mandatory under the [Instructions for Form 1040-X](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1040x): a completed and updated Form 1040, 1040-SR or 1040-NR with the changes goes behind the Form 1040-X, with new or changed schedules and forms behind it in Attachment Sequence No. order.
8. Choose the channel by year, using the filing table. The current or two prior tax periods may go electronically, up to three electronic amendments per tax year; older years, and a prior-year return first filed on paper during the current processing year, go on paper ([amended return FAQ](https://www.irs.gov/filing/amended-return-frequently-asked-questions)). The paper mailing addresses are under "Where To File" in the [Instructions for Form 1040-X](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1040x). Once the original due date has passed, do not send a second original return for the same year.
9. Handle the money. If tax is owed and that year's filing due date has not passed, filing and paying by the due date, without regard to any extension, avoids penalties and interest, and the return supersedes the original ([Topic no. 308](https://www.irs.gov/taxtopics/tc308)). If the due date has passed, leave interest and penalties off Form 1040-X. For a refund, [When and how to amend a tax return](https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/when-and-how-to-amend-a-tax-return) says that for tax years 2021 and later, direct deposit may be requested when filing electronically. Claim only the correct amount: an excessive claim carries the penalty in the first table unless there was reasonable cause.
10. Track it with [Where's My Amended Return?](https://www.irs.gov/filing/wheres-my-amended-return) or the phone line in the filing table, after the waiting and processing times shown there.
11. Deal with the state separately. [When and how to amend a tax return](https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/when-and-how-to-amend-a-tax-return) says to contact the state tax agency for guidance and not to attach state returns to the federal amended return.

## Ask the client first

- For each year in the carryforward chain: on what date was the original return filed, and on what dates was the tax paid (withholding and estimated tax count as paid on the due date)? These drive the time limits and the lookback period in the first table.
- Was any of those returns filed early, or filed during an extension but before it ended? The two rules give different start dates for the 3-year period.
- What was the filing status each year, and did a joint return year come before separate returns? Married filing separately changes the yearly loss limit, and a joint-year carryover follows only the spouse who had the loss.
- Was taxable income low or negative in any loss year? That changes how much of the loss the deduction used up and how much carried forward.
- Has the IRS already changed any of those years, by audit, notice or an earlier amendment? Column A must then show the adjusted amounts.
- Does the correction come from a bad debt or a worthless security, or from a loss on personal-use property? The first has its own longer limit; the second gives no deductible loss at all.

## When to refuse or refer

- Years past the time limits in the first table, where the question is whether a closed year's figures can still be corrected to fix a later year's carryforward. The IRS pages cited here set refund claim limits but give no method for carrying a corrected figure through a year that is closed to refund claims; refer this.
- Loss or credit carrybacks, including an election to carry back a net section 1256 contracts loss under [26 U.S.C. 1212(c)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/1212), net operating losses and foreign tax credits. The [Instructions for Form 1040-X](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1040x) give these their own time limits and attachments, and this skill does not set them out.
- Federally declared or State-declared disasters, combat zone or contingency operation service, the lookback extension under the Disaster Related Extension of Deadlines Act enacted December 26, 2025, and qualified wildfire relief payments. The [Instructions for Form 1040-X](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1040x) give each its own timing rule, not covered here.
- The section 174A domestic research or experimental expenditures election by amended return. Its deadline in the first table has passed, and Rev. Proc. 2025-28 governs the procedure; refer any question about it.
- A refund only of penalties, interest or an addition to tax already paid (Form 843), an injured spouse allocation (Form 8379), wage corrections needing Form W-2c and Form W-3c, and reportable transaction disclosures on Form 8886. The [Instructions for Form 1040-X](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1040x) name each as a separate filing or special case.
- Amendments to business entity, estate or trust returns, and any state amended return.
- Disputes over basis, wash sales or holding periods that decide whether the carryforward was wrong in the first place. The pages cited here say where the figures go, not how a contested basis is proved.
