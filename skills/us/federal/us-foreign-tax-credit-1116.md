# US foreign tax credit for individuals (Form 1116), tax year 2026

> **Source-cited draft.** Federal individual planning for US citizens and resident aliens. The form instructions read for this revision are the **2025** editions. Their line numbers are historical references, not a completed 2026 filing map. Do not use an unconfirmed 2026 field, threshold, country designation or relief assumption to file: Check this point with one of our partner accountants.

A qualifying foreign income tax may reduce US regular income tax, subject to creditability and a separate limit for each income category. Start with the tax legally owed and the income actually subject to US tax; withholding alone does not establish entitlement. [Form 1116 instructions, eligible taxes and line 24](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1116) (2025; read 2026-11-26).

## Records and routing

Collect foreign assessments, returns, payment/withholding dates and currencies; Forms 1099-DIV/INT and Schedules K-1/K-3; prior Forms 1116, Schedules B/C, credit-versus-deduction choices and accrual elections; Form 2555 history; share holding dates; and any disputes, refunds, CFC interests or treaty position. Missing records are **unknown**, not zero carryovers, an assumed holding period or an assumed treaty rate. Hold the affected result until resolved.

These are routing pointers, not a finding of eligibility.

## Ordinary individual method

### 1. Establish creditability and choose credit or deduction

Income, war-profits and excess-profits taxes, including qualifying taxes in lieu of them, can qualify. Amounts not legally owed or eligible for foreign refund do not: pursue available remedies. In the IRS treaty example only the legally owed amount qualifies, whether or not the refund is requested. Interest and penalties are not creditable. Subsidies, foreign tax credits, gross-basis levies and non-income taxes need individual analysis; use the referral below rather than treating every levy as creditable or noncreditable. [2025 Form 1116 instructions, eligible/not eligible taxes](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1116) (read 2026-11-26).

For ordinary corporate dividends, foreign withholding fails the holding-period test if stock was not held **at least 16 days within the 31-day period starting 15 days before the ex-dividend date**. Related-payment obligations can also disallow the credit. Preferred shares, hedged positions, exceptions and the corresponding test for other property income need the holding-period referral below; do not assume long-held fund units resolve every test. [2025 Form 1116 instructions, items 5–8](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1116) (read 2026-11-26).

Compare credit with an itemized deduction on Schedule A. The credit can be used with the standard deduction. Generally choose credit or deduction for **all qualified foreign taxes for that year**; special exceptions include certain noncreditable taxes and additional taxes relating to a prior deduction year for accrual taxpayers. Refer those exceptions before mixing the treatments. A change to credit generally has a **10-year** window from the regular unextended return due date of the paid/accrued year; a change to deduction generally has **3 years from filing or 2 years from payment, whichever is later**; agreement can extend the period. [Pub. 514, Choosing To Take Credit or Deduction](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p514) (2025; read 2026-11-26).

### 2. Test the no-Form-1116 election

An individual may elect only when **all** foreign-source gross income is passive category income, **all** that income and foreign tax are on qualified payee statements, and total creditable foreign tax is **not more than USD 300**, or **USD 600 on a joint return**. Exactly the cap passes; one dollar over fails. For this test passive income includes high-taxed income and certain export-financing interest. Statements include Forms 1099-DIV/INT, Schedule K-1 (Form 1041), Schedule K-3 (Form 1065/1120-S), or similar substitutes. Foreign wages or consulting income included in gross income prevent this election even without foreign tax. [2025 Form 1116 instructions, election](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1116) (read 2026-11-26).

The election removes the category limitation but keeps creditability and tax reductions. Enter the smaller of eligible foreign tax and regular tax on the foreign tax credit line (2025 Schedule 3, Part I, line 1). **No carryovers to or from the election year**; other years' carryovers remain subject to their original clock and are not deemed absorbed by the election year. The AMT credit equals the regular credit for an electing taxpayer, with no AMT carryover to/from that year. [2025 Form 1116 instructions, election and line 10](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1116); [2025 Form 6251 instructions, line 8](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i6251) (read 2026-11-26).

### 3. Source and categorize the income

Generally, services performed outside the US, interest from a payer abroad, and dividends from a foreign-incorporated corporation are foreign source. Mixed-location employee compensation other than fringe benefits generally uses time; fringe benefits use geographical sourcing, with possible alternative methods. Personal-property gains, tax-home exceptions and complex compensation sourcing use the specialist referral. [2025 Form 1116 instructions, Income From Sources Outside the United States](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1116) (read 2026-11-26).

Use a separate Form 1116 per category. The **2025** categories are: a section 951A; b foreign branch; c passive; d general; e section 901(j); f certain treaty-re-sourced income; g lump-sum distributions. Ordinary interest/dividends are generally passive; wages generally general. Active rents/royalties, export-financing interest, high-taxed income, financial-services income and CFC look-through can change classification. Foreign business income is not automatically general: test foreign-branch treatment. K-3 items needing partner-level sourcing must be analyzed rather than copied blindly. [2025 Form 1116 instructions, Categories of Income and Schedule K-3](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1116) (read 2026-11-26).

### 4. Allocate deductions and apply the limit

Reduce foreign gross income by definitely related deductions and a ratable share of other deductions. The **2025** instructions put the standard deduction on line 3a; lines 3d/e include excluded Form 2555 earned income, while line 1a excludes it. Interest has separate allocation rules. The USD 5,000 gross-foreign-income exception includes excluded income and permits allocation of all interest to US source for these individual taxpayers. The senior deduction actually claimed is added back in the worldwide limitation base; it is not an automatic full deduction for every person over 65. Qualified dividends/capital gains, loss adjustments/recapture and complex allocations use the referral below. [2025 Form 1116 instructions, lines 1a, 2–5 and 18](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1116) (read 2026-11-26).

For a simple positive-income case without special adjustments: **limit = regular US tax × min(category foreign-source taxable income / worldwide taxable income, 1)**; category credit is the smaller of available eligible foreign taxes and the limit. Use taxable amounts **after required adjustments**, not gross foreign receipts. If the worldwide base is zero, the 2025 line 19 ratio is zero. Regular tax excludes section 1411 net investment income tax; this computation is not a NIIT credit computation. Special section 960(c) increases are referred. [26 USC 904(a)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title26/html/USCODE-2024-title26-subtitleA-chap1-subchapN-partIII-subpartA-sec904.htm); [2025 Form 1116 instructions, lines 19–24](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1116) (read 2026-11-26).

On the **2025** form, Part IV is completed even with one Form 1116; multiple ordinary-category credits are summarized on the form with the largest line 24. Line 33 is the smaller of lines 20 and 32. Specialist categories e/g have summary exceptions and are referred. For 2026 filing, hold unconfirmed fields rather than copying the historical map. [2025 Form 1116 instructions, What's New and Part IV](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1116) (read 2026-11-26).

### 5. Cash, accrual and currency

A cash-method taxpayer generally claims tax in the payment year. Convert at the payment-date rate, or withholding-date rate for withheld tax; do not substitute an annual average. A cash taxpayer may elect accrual by checking the Accrued box on a timely original Form 1116, not an amended return; this binds later years and all qualifying foreign taxes. Do not change method just to obtain a convenient exchange rate. [Pub. 514, Credit for Taxes Paid or Accrued](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p514); [2025 Form 1116 instructions, Foreign Currency Conversion](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1116) (read 2026-11-26).

Accrued taxes generally use the relevant tax year's average exchange rate. Exceptions include tax paid before that year, more than two years after its end, and inflationary currency; paid taxes in those exceptions use payment-date rates, while unpaid inflationary-currency tax uses the last day of the US tax year. A separate election for nonfunctional currency, unpaid amounts and QBU rules needs specialist review. Tax already reported in dollars on a 1099-DIV/INT needs no conversion into foreign currency. [2025 Form 1116 instructions, Foreign Currency Conversion and Part II](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1116) (read 2026-11-26).

### 6. Carryovers and excluded income

Ordinary excess foreign taxes carry **back one year, then forward ten years**, within the category and the available limit. Apply the excess to the earliest available year first. A 2026 excess therefore goes first to 2025, with any remaining usable carryforward through 2036. A carryback uses an amended earlier return and revised Form 1116. The 2025 instructions require Schedule B for a prior-year carryover entered on line 10 or a current-year generated carryover; a carryback into the year alone does not require it. No carryover for section 951A category taxes. A deduction year cannot use a credit carryover and reduces it by what would have been used; an election year follows the different rule in step 2. [2025 Form 1116 instructions, line 10](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1116) (read 2026-11-26).

Tax attributable to foreign earned income/housing excluded on Form 2555 is removed (2025 line 12). With only wages, no housing amounts or deductions, multiply foreign wage tax by excluded wages / total foreign wages. Expenses, housing or tax covering inseparable other income change the fraction: refer. Do not treat tax left after this reduction as the final allowable credit; the category limitation still applies. Exclusion eligibility, stacking, revocation and re-election go to `us-foreign-earned-income-2555`: Check this point with one of our partner accountants. [2025 Form 1116 instructions, line 12](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1116) (read 2026-11-26).

## Worked cases

All original 2026 inputs and case years are retained. These are conditional illustrations, not eligibility findings or 2026 form-line approvals. Ordinary-rate arithmetic uses the unchanged 2026 tables in [Rev. Proc. 2025-32](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/rp-25-32.pdf) (section 3.01; read 2026-11-26).

**Case 1 — general category, single, cash basis, no carryovers.** Original inputs: foreign taxable USD 60,000, worldwide taxable USD 150,000, withheld foreign income tax USD 15,000 converted at withholding-date rates. The original regular-tax figure USD 28,598 is correct **if the USD 150,000 uses ordinary rates and has no other regular-tax adjustment**. Assuming the tax is fully eligible, the taxable bases are correctly allocated/adjusted and no special limitation applies: 28,598 × 60,000 / 150,000 = **USD 11,439.20 credit**, leaving **USD 3,560.80**. Test absorption in 2025 first; do not assume that year has capacity or elects credit. A remaining usable 2026 balance can run through 2036. Use Schedule B as required above.

```oa-check
name: Form 1116 general category limit and carryover, single (2026)
inputs:
  foreign_taxable: 60000
  worldwide_taxable: 150000
  foreign_tax: 15000
steps:
  - line_20: 0.10 * min(worldwide_taxable, 12400) + 0.12 * clamp(worldwide_taxable - 12400, 0, 38000) + 0.22 * clamp(worldwide_taxable - 50400, 0, 55300) + 0.24 * clamp(worldwide_taxable - 105700, 0, 96075)
  - line_19: min(foreign_taxable / worldwide_taxable, 1)
  - line_21: line_20 * line_19
  - line_24: min(foreign_tax, line_21)
  - excess: foreign_tax - line_24
expected:
  line_20: 28598.00
  line_21: 11439.20
  line_24: 11439.20
  excess: 3560.80
```

**Case 2 — joint return, election versus Form 1116.** Original inputs: only foreign fund dividends on Forms 1099-DIV, foreign tax USD 580, holding periods met, passive carryover USD 1,200 from 2025, foreign passive taxable USD 8,000, worldwide taxable USD 120,000. The USD 580 is USD 20 below the USD 600 cap. **If** all the election conditions and creditability tests are met, the election credit is USD 580 provided regular tax is at least that amount; the carryover cannot be used that year and continues aging.

The original ordinary-rate tax is USD 15,824. **If** this is the correctly computed regular tax, USD 8,000/120,000 are the correct adjusted limitation bases, and USD 1,200 is available: Form 1116 limit = **USD 1,054.93**, available taxes = USD 1,780, credit = USD 1,054.93, additional credit versus election = **USD 474.93**, balance = **USD 725.07**. Current taxes use USD 580 of capacity, so USD 474.93 of the 2025 balance is used. Without the carryover both routes give USD 580 on these assumptions. Foreign consulting fees in gross income would block the election.

**Missing fact:** fund dividends may be qualified. Their character and required foreign/worldwide adjustments are not established by the original inputs; USD 15,824 and the recommendation to file Form 1116 are therefore conditional, not established outcomes. Check this point with one of our partner accountants.

```oa-check
name: No-Form-1116 election against Form 1116 with a carryover, joint (2026)
inputs:
  foreign_tax: 580
  carryover: 1200
  foreign_passive_taxable: 8000
  worldwide_taxable: 120000
  de_minimis_cap: 600
steps:
  - regular_tax: 0.10 * min(worldwide_taxable, 24800) + 0.12 * clamp(worldwide_taxable - 24800, 0, 76000) + 0.22 * clamp(worldwide_taxable - 100800, 0, 110600)
  - headroom_under_cap: de_minimis_cap - foreign_tax
  - election_credit: min(foreign_tax, regular_tax)
  - limit: regular_tax * foreign_passive_taxable / worldwide_taxable
  - form_1116_credit: min(foreign_tax + carryover, limit)
  - extra_credit: form_1116_credit - election_credit
  - carry_forward: foreign_tax + carryover - form_1116_credit
expected:
  regular_tax: 15824.00
  headroom_under_cap: 20.00
  election_credit: 580.00
  limit: 1054.93
  form_1116_credit: 1054.93
  extra_credit: 474.93
  carry_forward: 725.07
```

**Case 3 — exclusion plus credit.** Original inputs: single filer abroad all year, foreign wages USD 180,000, no housing amounts/deductible expenses, foreign tax USD 54,000, excluded wages USD 132,900. **Assuming the exclusion is valid and that tax relates solely to those wages**, 54,000 × 132,900 / 180,000 = **USD 39,870 removed**; **USD 14,130 tax remains before the credit limit**; unexcluded wages = **USD 47,100**. Under the 2025 map line 1a has USD 47,100 and lines 3d/e include USD 180,000. Gross foreign wages prevent the election. No final credit or exclusion eligibility is established; use `us-foreign-earned-income-2555` for the exclusion comparison.

```oa-check
name: Conditional allocation of tax to excluded wages, original 2026 inputs
inputs:
  foreign_wages: 180000
  foreign_tax: 54000
  excluded_wages: 132900
steps:
  - excluded_tax: foreign_tax * excluded_wages / foreign_wages
  - remaining_tax: foreign_tax - excluded_tax
  - remaining_wages: foreign_wages - excluded_wages
expected:
  excluded_tax: 39870
  remaining_tax: 14130
  remaining_wages: 47100
```
