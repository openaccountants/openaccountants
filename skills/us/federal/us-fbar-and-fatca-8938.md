# US FBAR and FATCA Form 8938 — 2026 rules and 2025 cases

> **Correction draft — accountant approval pending.** The preserved approval metadata belongs to the earlier version. It does not approve this changed text. Current continuous-use reporting rules are stated for 2026; all original worked-case values remain historical 2025 inputs.

## Who this applies to

Use this Guide to screen US federal foreign-account and foreign-asset reporting for calendar/tax year 2026; use the separately labeled 2025 examples only with their stated historical inputs. Collect citizenship/residence, filing status, return obligation, account location, legal title, signing powers, asset type, account maxima and year-end values before deciding either filing.

FBAR and Form 8938 are separate tests. Filing Form 8938 does not satisfy an otherwise required FBAR. [IRS instructions, Purpose of Form](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i8938).

## Rates, bands and thresholds

For FBAR, apply the US-person, financial-interest/signature-authority and account/exemption tests below. For Form 8938, apply the specified-person, interest, required-return and asset/exception tests below. Each threshold uses **more than**, not equality.

| Reporting test | Last day of year | During year | Official source |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| FBAR, calendar year 2026 | Not a separate year-end test | Aggregate annual account maxima exceeds $10,000 | [FinCEN instructions, Monetary amounts](https://www.fincen.gov/system/files/shared/FBAR%20Line%20Item%20Filing%20Instructions.pdf) |
| Form 8938: unmarried, living in the US | $50,000 | $75,000 at any time | [IRS, Reporting Thresholds](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i8938) |
| Form 8938: married filing separately, living in the US | $50,000 | $75,000 at any time | [IRS, Reporting Thresholds](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i8938) |
| Form 8938: married filing jointly, living in the US | $100,000 | $150,000 at any time | [IRS, Reporting Thresholds](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i8938) |
| Form 8938: unmarried or married filing separately, qualifying abroad | $200,000 | $300,000 at any time | [IRS, Reporting Thresholds](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i8938) |
| Form 8938: married filing jointly, qualifying abroad | $400,000 | $600,000 at any time | [IRS, Reporting Thresholds](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i8938) |

**Qualifying abroad for Form 8938:** a foreign tax home plus either US citizenship and bona fide foreign residence for an uninterrupted period including an entire tax year, or US citizenship/residence and at least 330 full days in foreign countries during a consecutive 12-month period ending in the reporting tax year. London or Singapore residence alone does not establish this test. [IRS, Presence abroad](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i8938).

## The base

### FBAR

- A US person includes a US citizen, a resident under the regulation's section 7701(b) test, and a domestically formed corporation, partnership, trust or LLC. An entity's disregarded income-tax status does not remove its FBAR status. Apply the separate financial-interest test to its owner. [31 CFR 1010.350(b), (e)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-31/subtitle-B/chapter-X/part-1010/subpart-C/section-1010.350).
- A US person with a financial interest in, or signature authority over, foreign financial accounts must file when the aggregate threshold is exceeded, unless an exception applies. Calculate each reportable account's annual maximum separately; the maxima need not coincide. Include small or closed accounts once filing is required. Five otherwise reportable accounts with $2,500 each total $12,500. [FinCEN, Who Must File and Monetary amounts](https://www.fincen.gov/system/files/shared/FBAR%20Line%20Item%20Filing%20Instructions.pdf).
- Determine the maximum in the account currency, then convert at the Treasury rate for the reporting calendar year's last day, including for an account closed earlier. If no Treasury rate exists, use another verifiable rate and give its source. Periodic statements support the maximum only when they fairly reflect it. [FinCEN, Monetary amounts](https://www.fincen.gov/system/files/shared/FBAR%20Line%20Item%20Filing%20Instructions.pdf).
- Where money moved between foreign accounts, the IRS instructs that the transferred money be counted only once for the aggregate-value test. Reconcile transfers before relying on a threshold calculation. [IRM 4.26.16.2.6](https://www.irs.gov/irm/part4/irm_04-026-016).
- Foreign means the account is outside the US: a London branch of a US bank is foreign; a New York branch of a foreign bank is not. Currency and the bank's nationality do not decide location. [FinCEN, Foreign Financial Account](https://www.fincen.gov/system/files/shared/FBAR%20Line%20Item%20Filing%20Instructions.pdf).
- Bank, securities and cash-value insurance/annuity accounts are covered categories. A public mutual fund or similar pooled fund with regular net-asset-value determination and regular redemptions can itself be an account. Direct fund-house registration is not a categorical exemption. [31 CFR 1010.350(c)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-31/subtitle-B/chapter-X/part-1010/subpart-C/section-1010.350).
- Record ownership or legal title creates a financial interest even when the account is maintained for someone else's benefit. A trustee's lack of beneficial interest therefore does not by itself establish signature-only reporting. [31 CFR 1010.350(e)(1)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-31/subtitle-B/chapter-X/part-1010/subpart-C/section-1010.350).
- Signature authority means power, alone or jointly, to control disposition by direct communication with the account provider. Employee exemptions exist, and financial interest in or signing authority over 25 or more accounts has modified detail rules; this is not an automatic exemption from filing. For their application, indirect entity/trust interests or spouse-only filing: Check this point with one of our partner accountants. [31 CFR 1010.350(e)–(g)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-31/subtitle-B/chapter-X/part-1010/subpart-C/section-1010.350).
- Named pensions/provident funds, defined-benefit arrangements, gaming/prepaid products and any uncertain account exemption: Check this point with one of our partner accountants.

### Form 8938

Unless an exception applies, a specified person with an interest in specified foreign financial assets above the applicable threshold files Form 8938. If no income tax return is required for the year, Form 8938 is not required even above the threshold. [IRS, Who Must File](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i8938).

US citizens are specified individuals. Resident aliens, electing nonresident spouses, possession residents, dual residents and partial-year cases have additional rules. For those cases, or whether a corporation, partnership or domestic trust is a specified domestic entity: Check this point with one of our partner accountants. [IRS, Specified Individual](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i8938).

The general interest test asks whether income, gains, losses, deductions, credits, gross proceeds or distributions from the asset are or would be reflected on the person's income tax return; an item need not actually arise this year. The owner of a disregarded entity has an interest in its specified foreign financial assets. Signature authority alone does not establish this interest. For trust powers, entity look-through or other special ownership rules: Check this point with one of our partner accountants. [IRS, Interests in Specified Foreign Financial Assets](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i8938).

Specified foreign financial assets include accounts maintained by foreign financial institutions and, when held for investment outside a financial account, foreign corporate stock, foreign partnership interests and qualifying foreign financial instruments/contracts. Business-use and trust/estate interests need their own tests. Directly held nonaccount stock can therefore require Form 8938 without being an FBAR account. [IRS, Types of Specified Foreign Financial Assets](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i8938).

A financial account maintained by a US payer, and its underlying assets, is excluded from Form 8938. The IRS includes qualifying foreign branches/subsidiaries of US financial institutions in this rule; an account there can still be foreign for FBAR. [IRS, Certain Financial Accounts](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i8938). For directly held real estate, cash, bullion, tangible property, entity-held real estate and foreign social-security versus private-pension classification: Check this point with one of our partner accountants.

For joint-owner threshold versus reported values, special pension/trust valuation, currency conversion exceptions and incomplete valuation histories: Check this point with one of our partner accountants. Do not mix annual maxima with year-end values and describe their sum as a demonstrated year-end total.

## Deadlines and filing

The ordinary calendar-2026 FBAR is due April 15, 2027, automatically extended to October 15, 2027 without an extension request. For the original calendar-2025 cases, the corresponding dates are April 15/October 15, 2026; weekend/holiday rules and specific relief can change a deadline. File separately through FinCEN's BSA E-Filing System; a FinCEN-granted e-filing exception is possible. [FinCEN, Filing Information](https://www.fincen.gov/system/files/shared/FBAR%20Line%20Item%20Filing%20Instructions.pdf); [IRM 4.26.16.3.1](https://www.irs.gov/irm/part4/irm_04-026-016). For special signature-authority notices, disaster relief or other extensions: Check this point with one of our partner accountants.

Attach Form 8938 to the annual income/information return and follow that return's due date, including extensions. [IRS, When and How To File](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i8938). For overseas extensions, the discretionary December extension or Form 2350: Check this point with one of our partner accountants.

Form 8938 Parts I/V cover deposit and custodial account summary/detail, Parts II/VI cover other foreign assets, Part III reports attributable tax items and their return destinations, and Part IV identifies excepted assets reported on other forms. [IRS, Specific Instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i8938). For timely same-year duplicate-detail relief, the individual/entity threshold distinction, joint filings and combined foreign-grantor-trust conditions involving Forms 3520, 3520-A, 5471, 8621 or 8865: Check this point with one of our partner accountants. Filing another form does not itself clear FBAR.

Keep required FBAR account records: account name/number, institution name/address, type and maximum value. The regulation requires five years and excludes specified criminal-proceeding periods from that computation; employee signature-only records and retention starting dates need the applicable rules. [31 CFR 1010.420](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-31/section-1010.420). For Form 114a, copies of filed forms, employee exceptions and Form 8938/general tax-record retention: Check this point with one of our partner accountants.

## Penalties and interest

**AUDIT FLASH POINT** — These are potential ceilings or conditional penalties, not automatic assessments. Nonwillfulness, reasonable cause, mitigation, criminal exposure and assessment-period expiry require case-specific advice. Check this point with one of our partner accountants.

- A single non-willful FBAR reporting violation can produce only a single penalty; willful reporting penalties can be per account. For applicable post-November 2, 2015 violations, the inflation-adjusted dollar amounts in the current table are $16,536 non-willful and $165,353 willful for assessments on or after January 17, 2025. Apply the assessment-date table rather than inventing a new tax-year amount. [IRM 4.26.16.1.7, 5.4–5.5](https://www.irs.gov/irm/part4/irm_04-026-016); [31 CFR 1010.821](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-31/section-1010.821).
- For willful failure to report an account, the statutory maximum is the greater of the adjusted dollar amount or 50% of the violation-date account balance. IRS discretion/mitigation can reduce an assessment. [31 USC 5321(a)(5)](https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/119-124/t31/s5321/); [IRM 4.26.16.5.2.1](https://www.irs.gov/irm/part4/irm_04-026-016).
- Original twelve-account illustration: 12 × $200,000 = $2,400,000; 50% is $1,200,000. If the adjusted $165,353 amount applies to each violation, the theoretical aggregate ceiling is 12 × max($165,353, $100,000) = $1,984,236. This is not the actual penalty. The former 12 × $10,000 = $120,000 comparison is arithmetic using an unadjusted statutory figure, not the current non-willful reporting-penalty rule. [31 CFR 1010.821](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-31/section-1010.821); [31 USC 5321(a)(5)](https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/119-124/t31/s5321/).
- Required Form 8938 failure can attract $10,000. Continuing more than 90 days after IRS notice can attract $10,000 for each subsequent 30-day period or fraction, up to $50,000 additional ($60,000 combined). Reasonable cause and no willful neglect must be established for the filing-penalty exception. [IRS, Penalties](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i8938).
- The 40% undisclosed-foreign-asset accuracy penalty concerns attributable **tax underpayment**, not gross omitted income. [IRS, Accuracy-Related Penalty](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i8938). For defenses and criminal exposure: Check this point with one of our partner accountants.
- More than $5,000 omitted gross income attributable to specified foreign assets can allow six years after the return was filed for assessment, disregarding Form 8938 thresholds/exceptions. A missing Form 8938 is not an additional condition. Adequate disclosure of the nature and amount on the return or attached statement can exclude an omission under section 6501(e)(1)(B)(iii), except the specified basis-overstatement case. [IRC 6501(e)(1)](https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/119-124/t26/s6501/).
- Failure to furnish required section 6038D information can keep assessment open until three years after the information is furnished. Where reasonable cause and no willful neglect apply, this extension is limited to related items. [IRC 6501(c)(8)](https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/119-124/t26/s6501/).

The original calendar-2018 FBAR example used April 15/October 15, 2019 filing dates and an asserted April 2025 assessment bar. No expiry conclusion is established here. For protective dates, consents and the actual assessment deadline: Check this point with one of our partner accountants.

## Two worked examples that recompute

### Example 1 — original Indian-American case (2025)

Priya is a naturalized US citizen living in New Jersey who moved to the US in 2015. Her original inputs are:

- NRE savings at HDFC Bank, Mumbai: 2025 maximum $32,000.
- NRO account at the same bank: 2025 maximum $8,000.
- PPF at SBI: year-end $14,000.
- A 15-year-old LIC of India endowment policy: December 31, 2025 cash surrender value $11,000.
- Three Indian mutual funds directly registered with the fund house: aggregate year-end $22,000.

She filed Form 1040 each year, answered “no” on Schedule B and never filed FBAR or Form 8938. These facts do not establish nonwillfulness or catch-up eligibility.

The bank maxima alone sum to $40,000, crossing the FBAR threshold on the stipulated reportable-bank-account facts. The displayed subtotal $65,000 and all-asset sum $87,000 mix bank maxima with other year-end amounts; neither is a demonstrated year-end or complete maximum total. Obtain annual maxima, year-end histories and classification of every asset. Qualifying public funds are not excluded merely because they are directly registered. [FinCEN, Monetary amounts](https://www.fincen.gov/system/files/shared/FBAR%20Line%20Item%20Filing%20Instructions.pdf); [31 CFR 1010.350(c)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-31/subtitle-B/chapter-X/part-1010/subpart-C/section-1010.350).

Form 8938 filing status, complete valuations, other-form exceptions, pension/insurance and PFIC treatment, willfulness and the catch-up route remain unresolved. Check this point with one of our partner accountants.

Conditional arithmetic only: 5% × $65,000 = $3,250; 5% × $87,000 = $4,350, a $1,100 difference. Neither is an actual penalty without eligibility and the highest covered year-end aggregate of subject assets. The former back-tax $1,500–$3,000, PFIC $5,000–$15,000 plus interest and fee $8,000–$15,000 estimates are unsupported former outputs, retained only to identify what was discarded; they are not estimates to use. [IRS, SDOP base](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/international-taxpayers/us-taxpayers-residing-in-the-united-states).

```oa-check
name: Example 1 conditional displayed totals and SDOP base
inputs:
  nre_max: 32000
  nro_max: 8000
  ppf_year_end: 14000
  lic_year_end: 11000
  funds_year_end: 22000
steps:
  displayed_subtotal: nre_max + nro_max + ppf_year_end + lic_year_end
  displayed_all: displayed_subtotal + funds_year_end
  narrow_penalty: displayed_subtotal * 0.05
  conditional_penalty: displayed_all * 0.05
  difference: conditional_penalty - narrow_penalty
expected:
  displayed_subtotal: 65000
  displayed_all: 87000
  narrow_penalty: 3250
  conditional_penalty: 4350
  difference: 1100
```

### Example 2 — original UK pension case (2025)

James is a US citizen who has lived in London for 12 years and works for a UK financial-services firm. He files jointly with a US spouse who has no foreign accounts. The case stipulates the section 911 bona fide residence test, but does not independently establish the foreign tax home or SFOP tests.

Original supplied GBP/USD pairs (not verified currency conversions): Barclays 2025 maximum £8,000/$10,200; NatWest 2025 maximum £12,000/$15,300; Hargreaves Lansdown ISA holding £45,000 UK equity funds, year-end $57,300; defined-contribution workplace pension £180,000, year-end $229,200; SIPP holding £65,000 directly held UK stocks, year-end $82,800.

The supplied dollar values add to $394,800. Replacing only $229,200 with the assumed pension peak $250,000 gives $415,600 (about $415,000). These are mixed-date arithmetic totals, not proven year-end or annual-maximum aggregates. Even a single assumed peak does not establish the correct Form 8938 valuation; special pension rules apply. The higher married-joint thresholds are $400,000 year-end/$600,000 anytime only if the qualifying-abroad conditions and other filing conditions are met. No Form 8938 conclusion is established. [IRS, Reporting Thresholds and Valuing Interests in Foreign Pension Plans](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i8938).

The bank maxima alone are $25,500 in the supplied illustration. Actual FBAR conversion rates, each reportable maximum, account/pension classification and relief still need confirmation. For FX, ISA/PFIC, pension/treaty/section 402(b), all-account reporting and any SFOP submission: Check this point with one of our partner accountants.

### Example 3 — original Jersey trustee case (2025)

Sarah is a US citizen living in Boston. Her personal Canadian RBC account, from university in Montreal, holds $4,000. She is a co-trustee without beneficial interest of a Jersey family trust holding $2,300,000 at HSBC Jersey; its beneficiaries are her UK-resident cousins. The case stipulates signature authority over the trust account.

$4,000 + $2,300,000 = $2,304,000. On the stipulated reportable-account and authority facts, both accounts enter the FBAR test and the threshold is crossed. Legal-title facts must be obtained before calling the trust account “signature-only”: no beneficial interest does not negate record ownership/legal title. [31 CFR 1010.350(a), (e), (f)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-31/subtitle-B/chapter-X/part-1010/subpart-C/section-1010.350).

Her personal $4,000 alone is below the $50,000 unmarried-US year-end Form 8938 threshold, but marital status, other assets, tax ownership and trust powers are not established. Form 8938, income-tax and Form 3520/3520-A conclusions remain unresolved; US trustee status alone is not a filing decision. Check this point with one of our partner accountants.

## Ask the client first

Obtain every bank/investment/insurance/pension statement, legal owner, authorized signer, account location, account opening/closing dates, maximum and year-end values, transfer history, currency/rate evidence, residence history and filing status.
