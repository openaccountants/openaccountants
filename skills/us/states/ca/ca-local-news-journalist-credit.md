# California local news outlet journalist credit (R&TC 17053.76 and 23633), 2026 records and 2027 first credit year v0.1

Do not rely on it to file, pay, amend, or take a tax position without review by a qualified professional in the relevant jurisdiction.

> **Source-cited draft.** Built from the chaptered text of AB 2222 (Chapter 849, Statutes of 2026), sections 1 to 7, and the Legislature's bill status and history records for AB 2222 and AB 2319, all read from the California Legislature's official bulk data file on 2026-11-22, and from the Franchise Tax Board's final bill analysis of AB 2222, read on 2026-11-22. **Answer first.** A business that owns or operates a California local news outlet can claim a **refundable** credit for each qualifying journalist it employs, for **taxable years beginning on or after 1 January 2027 and before 1 January 2032** ([R&TC 17053.76(a); R&TC 23633(a)](https://downloads.leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/pubinfo_2025.zip)). The credit is the sum of:

- **USD 20,000** for each journalist continuously employed full time, up to five journalists;
- **USD 15,000** for each continuously employed full-time journalist beyond five;
- an additional **USD 15,000** for each full-time journalist in a new journalism position;
- an additional **USD 7,500** for each part-time journalist.

Full time means at least 30 hours a week for more than 26 weeks, with annualized pay of at least USD 35,000. Part time means 20 to under 30 hours a week for more than 26 weeks, with annualized wages or salary of at least USD 25,000. The journalist must be a California resident whose main duties are in California. Any credit above the tax is applied to other amounts owed, and the rest is refunded ([R&TC 17053.76(c); R&TC 23633(c)](https://downloads.leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/pubinfo_2025.zip)).

**Why 2026 matters now.** Three tests for a calendar 2027 claim look back to 2026:

1. A journalist is "continuously employed" in 2027 only if they were employed full time in 2026 (R&TC 17053.76(b)(2); 23633(b)(2)).
2. New journalism positions are measured against the 2026 average daily headcount ((b)(4)(B)).
3. The outlet must have been organized in California, or registered to do business here, for at least 12 months before the 2027 year starts ((b)(7)(A)), that is by 1 January 2026 for a calendar year.

**Tax year.** Tax year label 2026-27. Nothing is claimable for 2026. The first year is a taxable year beginning in 2027, and the last is one beginning before 1 January 2032. Both sections are repealed on 1 December 2032 (R&TC 17053.76(f); 23633(f)). The FTB analysis gives the bill's effective date as 1 January 2027 ([FTB analysis](https://www.ftb.ca.gov/tax-pros/law/legislation/2025-2026/AB2222-FINAL.pdf)).

**Circular 230 disclosure.** This material is general information, not written advice on a specific transaction under 31 CFR 10.37. It is not intended to be used, and cannot be used, to avoid penalties.

## Ask the client first

- What type of taxpayer owns or operates the outlet (individual or sole proprietor, partnership or LLC, C corporation, S corporation, tax-exempt organization), and when does its taxable year begin?
- What kind of outlet is it: digital (share of audience in California), broadcast (FCC license to a California community, or 501(c)(3) public broadcaster), or print (periodicals mailing privileges, office of publication or 33% California distribution, or a court-adjudicated newspaper of general circulation)?
- When was the business organized in California or registered to do business here? Does it publish its beneficial owners (or board), carry media liability insurance all year, and display an error-correction policy? Is it controlled by a political action committee, a section 527 entity or a 501(c)(4) organization?
- For each journalist, in both 2026 and 2027: start and end dates, hours a week, weeks worked, pay, California residence, and whether the main duties are news work done in California.
- What was the average daily number of qualifying journalists in each year, and did the taxpayer acquire a news business during the year? Is it part of a combined reporting group?
- Is any other credit being claimed for the same journalists' hiring or employment?

### Steps

1. **Check the year.** The taxable year must begin on or after 1 January 2027 and before 1 January 2032. A fiscal year that began in 2026 is outside the credit ([R&TC 17053.76(a); 23633(a)](https://downloads.leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/pubinfo_2025.zip)).
2. **Check the outlet.** It must be a qualifying digital news outlet, broadcast station or print publication that produces original news coverage about local communities in California ((b)(3)).
   - **Digital:** publishes news about the state or a local community at least monthly, and at least 33% of its audience is in California ((b)(9)).
   - **Broadcast:** either FCC-licensed to a community of license in California, or a 501(c)(3) nonprofit that also either received a Corporation for Public Broadcasting community service grant before the 2025-26 fiscal year or employs qualifying journalists on behalf of a university-licensed public broadcaster ((b)(8)).
   - **Print:** a local newspaper or news magazine with periodicals mailing privileges that publishes at least monthly and either keeps its office of publication in California or sends at least 33% of its print distribution to California; or a newspaper of general circulation adjudicated by a California court under Government Code section 6008 ((b)(11)).
3. **Check the taxpayer.** It must be engaged in a trade or business in California, own or operate the outlet, and meet all five tests in (b)(7):
   - organized or registered in California at least 12 months before the year;
   - its beneficial owners (or board, for a nonprofit) publicly disclosed on its website or in its publication;
   - active media liability insurance for the whole year;
   - a published error-correction and clarification policy with an accessible way to report errors and complaints;
   - not controlled, directly or indirectly, by a political action committee, a section 527 entity or a 501(c)(4) organization.

   Under the corporation tax, a tax-exempt organization that owns and operates a qualifying outlet counts as a taxpayer, and running the outlet counts as engaging in a trade or business in California ([R&TC 23633(b)(7)(F)](https://downloads.leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/pubinfo_2025.zip)).
4. **List the qualifying journalists.** Each must be a California resident under R&TC 17014 whose main job duties occur in California and consist of gathering, preparing, producing, photographing, recording, writing, editing, reporting, presenting or publishing state or local news for the local community. Examples are reporter, correspondent, photographer, videographer, editor and digital producer ((b)(10)).
5. **Sort each journalist by hours, weeks and pay for the year.**
   - **Full time:** 30 or more hours a week for more than 26 weeks, with annualized pay of at least USD 35,000 ((b)(5)).
   - **Part time:** 20 to under 30 hours a week for more than 26 weeks, with annualized wages or salary of at least USD 25,000 ((b)(6)).
   - Annualize as pay for the employment in the year, divided by the weeks of that employment, times 52 ((b)(1)).
   - A journalist who fails every test earns nothing.
6. **Test continuous employment.** A journalist is continuously employed if the taxpayer, or a qualified taxpayer in the same combined reporting group, employed them full time in the preceding taxable year and also employs them in the claim year ((b)(2)). A journalist first hired in the claim year is not continuously employed that year, so (a)(1) and (a)(2) do not apply to them.
7. **Count new journalism positions.** Work out the average number of qualifying journalists per day in the claim year and in the prior year, including any trade or business acquired during the claim year. Round each average to the nearest whole number, then subtract the prior-year figure from the claim-year figure. A negative result counts as zero ((b)(4)).
8. **Add up the four parts** ((a)(1) to (a)(4)):
   - USD 20,000 x min(continuously employed full-time journalists, 5);
   - USD 15,000 x the number above 5;
   - USD 15,000 x each full-time journalist in a new journalism position;
   - USD 7,500 x each part-time journalist.
9. **Apply the in-lieu rule.**
   - **Personal income tax:** no other credit is allowed for hiring or employing the same qualifying journalist or qualifying part-time journalist ([R&TC 17053.76(d)](https://downloads.leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/pubinfo_2025.zip)).
   - **Corporation tax:** the same rule applies to any qualifying journalist ([R&TC 23633(d)](https://downloads.leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/pubinfo_2025.zip)).
10. **Apply it against tax, then refund the excess.**
    - **Personal income tax:** the credit is allowed against "net tax" and may reduce tax below the tentative minimum tax (R&TC 17039(c)(1), as amended by AB 2222).
    - **Corporation tax:** the credit is allowed against "tax" and may reduce tax below the tentative minimum tax (R&TC 23036(d)(1), as amended by AB 2222).
    - **Ordering:** AB 2222 and AB 2319 (the post-production credit, Chapter 343) both amend R&TC 17039 and 23036, and each bill has a double-jointing section. Neither chaptered text carries an urgency or tax-levy clause, so as regular-session statutes each goes into effect on 1 January 2027 ([Cal. Const. art. IV, sec. 8(c)(1)](https://downloads.leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/pubinfo_2025.zip)). The FTB analyses state the same effective date for each bill: AB 2222 "would be effective January 1, 2027" ([FTB AB 2222 analysis](https://www.ftb.ca.gov/tax-pros/law/legislation/2025-2026/AB2222-FINAL.pdf)) and AB 2319 "would be effective January 1, 2027, and specifically operative for taxable years beginning on or after January 1, 2027" ([FTB AB 2319 analysis, bill as amended 21 May 2026](https://www.ftb.ca.gov/tax-pros/law/legislation/2025-2026/AB2319-052126.pdf)). The enacted double-jointing analysis that follows rests on the chaptered texts and the Legislature's chaptering records: AB 2222 (chaptered 30 September 2026) was enacted after AB 2319 (chaptered 19 September 2026). On those records the conditions in AB 2222 sec. 7 are met: AB 2222 secs. 1.5 and 4.5 operate and its secs. 1 and 4 do not, and AB 2319 sec. 6 leaves its own secs. 2 and 4 in place. Under secs. 1.5 and 4.5 the credit is R&TC 17039(a)(13) (personal income tax) and 23036(c)(8) (corporation tax): after the other refundable credits listed before it and before withholding credits ([AB 2222 secs. 1.5, 4.5, 7; AB 2319 sec. 6](https://downloads.leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/pubinfo_2025.zip)).
    - **Excess:** any amount above the tax liability is credited against other amounts due, and the balance is refunded, from the Tax Relief and Refund Account for personal income tax or the Corporation Tax Fund for corporation tax ((c)).
11. **Pass-through owners.**
    - **Partnerships:** the credit is computed at the partnership level and allocated to the partners under the written partnership agreement, following IRC section 704 ([R&TC 17039(e)(2), (f)](https://downloads.leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/pubinfo_2025.zip)).
    - **S corporations:** credits are computed at the S corporation level (R&TC 23036(h)). R&TC 23803 limits credits against the S corporation tax to one-third of the amount otherwise allowable. How that applies to this refundable credit is not confirmed, so refer (see Not yet confirmed).

## Worked cases

Both cases are made up for illustration. The amounts and tests come from R&TC 17053.76 and 23633 as enacted by AB 2222.

**Case 1: established digital outlet, calendar 2027, no growth.**

- The outlet had 8 journalists employed full time throughout 2026 who stay full time throughout 2027, and 3 part-time journalists (25 hours a week, all year, annualized pay above USD 25,000) in both years.
- Average daily headcount is 11 in both years, so there are no new journalism positions.
- All outlet, taxpayer and journalist tests are met.

Working:

- First five continuously employed full-time journalists: 5 x USD 20,000 = USD 100,000.
- The other three: 3 x USD 15,000 = USD 45,000.
- Part-time journalists: 3 x USD 7,500 = USD 22,500.
- Credit: USD 167,500.

```oa-check
name: Local news credit, 8 continuing full-time and 3 part-time journalists, no new positions
inputs:
  ft_continuous: 8
  part_time: 3
  new_positions_ft: 0
  first_tier_rate: 20000
  second_tier_rate: 15000
  new_position_rate: 15000
  part_time_rate: 7500
steps:
  - first_tier: min(ft_continuous, 5) * first_tier_rate
  - second_tier: max(ft_continuous - 5, 0) * second_tier_rate
  - new_position_amount: new_positions_ft * new_position_rate
  - part_time_amount: part_time * part_time_rate
  - credit: first_tier + second_tier + new_position_amount + part_time_amount
expected:
  first_tier: 100000.00
  second_tier: 45000.00
  new_position_amount: 0.00
  part_time_amount: 22500.00
  credit: 167500.00
```

**Case 2: sole proprietor's print weekly hires two reporters; one part-timer fails the pay test; refund.**

- **Taxpayer:** a California sole proprietor, calendar year, files under the personal income tax. The paper has periodicals mailing privileges and its office of publication in California.
- **Staff in both 2026 and 2027, all year:** 3 full-time journalists, and 1 part-timer at 25 hours a week for 52 weeks paid USD 23,400.
- **New hires:** 2 full-time reporters (40 hours a week), both California residents, start on Monday 1 March 2027. Each works the 306 days to 31 December 2027 (43.7 weeks) and is paid USD 31,000 in 2027.
- **Net tax** for 2027 before this credit: USD 6,200. No other amounts are due.

Working:

- **New hires' annualized pay:** USD 31,000 / (306 / 7) x 52 = USD 36,875.82. That is above USD 35,000, and 43.7 weeks is more than 26, so both are full time for 2027. They were not employed in 2026, so they are not continuously employed and earn no tier amount.
- **Part-timer's annualized pay:** USD 23,400 / 52 x 52 = USD 23,400. That is under USD 25,000, so no USD 7,500.
- **Average daily headcount, 2026:** 3 + 1 = 4.
- **Average daily headcount, 2027:** 4 + 2 x 306 / 365 = 5.68, which rounds to 6.
- **New journalism positions:** 6 - 4 = 2, filled by the 2 full-time hires.
  - The same 2 results if the part-timer is left out of both counts: 3, then 4.68 rounding to 5.
- **Credit:**
  - 3 continuously employed full-time journalists: 3 x USD 20,000 = USD 60,000.
  - 2 new journalism positions: 2 x USD 15,000 = USD 30,000.
  - Total: USD 90,000.
- **Refund:** USD 90,000 - USD 6,200 = USD 83,800, paid from the Tax Relief and Refund Account (R&TC 17053.76(c)).
- **Timing matters:** counting weeks as days / 7 (this case's convention; the statute does not say how weeks are counted), a start on 2 July 2027 still gives more than 26 weeks in 2027 (183 days, about 26.1 weeks), but a start on 3 July 2027 gives exactly 26 weeks (182 days to 31 December), which is not "more than 26 weeks". Starting on 3 July 2027 or later, the reporters would be neither full time for 2027 nor continuously employed in 2028.

```oa-check
name: Local news credit, sole proprietor, two March hires, part-timer under the pay test, refund
inputs:
  incumbents_ft: 3
  part_timer: 1
  hires_ft: 2
  days_hired: 306
  days_year: 365
  hire_pay: 31000
  pt_pay: 23400
  pt_weeks: 52
  pt_meets_tests: 0
  net_tax: 6200

  - hire_weeks: days_hired / 7
  - hire_annualized: hire_pay / hire_weeks * 52
  - pt_annualized: pt_pay / pt_weeks * 52

  - avg_current: round(incumbents_ft + part_timer + hires_ft * days_hired / days_year)
  - new_positions: max(avg_current - avg_prior, 0)
  - continuous_amount: min(incumbents_ft, 5) * 20000 + max(incumbents_ft - 5, 0) * 15000
  - new_position_amount: min(new_positions, hires_ft) * 15000

  hire_annualized: 36875.82
  pt_annualized: 23400.00
  avg_prior: 4
  avg_current: 6
  new_positions: 2

  new_position_amount: 30000.00
  part_time_amount: 0.00
  credit: 90000.00
  refund: 83800.00
```

## When to refuse or refer

- **S corporation claims:** how the R&TC 23803 one-third limit and shareholder pass-through apply to this refundable credit is not settled in the text read. Refer to a California corporate tax practitioner.
- **C corporation claims:** how a refund interacts with the USD 800 minimum franchise tax, which a credit may not reduce (R&TC 23036(d)(2)), is not settled in the text read. Refer before relying on a refund figure.
- **Unclear new journalism positions:** where headcount growth comes from part-timers, replacement hires or part-time staff moving to full time, the statute does not say how many full-time journalists are "in" a new position. Refer.
- **Disregarded entities:** an outlet held in a single-member LLC or other disregarded entity meets the R&TC 17039(g) and 23036(i) limit on credits attributable to a disregarded entity. How that limit applies to this refundable credit is not settled in the text read. Refer.
- **Combined reporting groups:** whether the five-journalist cap applies per member or across the group is not stated. Refer.
- **Disputed outlet or journalist status:** whether a role is primarily news work, whether a journalist is a resident, or whether the outlet is "controlled" by a disqualified organization. Refer.
- **The same bill's section 162(m) changes:** a separate corporate deduction topic. Refer.

## Filing and payment

- **Where it is claimed:** on the taxpayer's California return for the taxable year, for a calendar 2027 year the return filed in 2028. Use the form and manner the FTB prescribes; the text and FTB analysis read name no form or line ([R&TC 17053.76; 23633](https://downloads.leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/pubinfo_2025.zip); [FTB analysis](https://www.ftb.ca.gov/tax-pros/law/legislation/2025-2026/AB2222-FINAL.pdf)). Return due dates and the return itself: see `ca-540-individual-return` for individuals; refer for corporations.
- **Pre-approval:** R&TC 17053.76 and 23633 as enacted, and the amended credit-ordering sections reviewed (17039 and 23036), specify no application, reservation, certification or overall credit cap; the credit is computed on the return from the statutory tests. That is a statement about the sections reviewed, not about later FTB procedure: check the FTB instructions and any guidance for the first credit year before filing.
- **Exempt organizations:** claim on the exempt organization return filed under R&TC 23771 and following, regardless of unrelated business taxable income, in the form the FTB prescribes ([R&TC 23633(e)(2)](https://downloads.leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/pubinfo_2025.zip)). The FTB must issue guidance for 501(c)(3) organizations by 1 January 2028 ([R&TC 17053.76(e)(2)(B)](https://downloads.leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/pubinfo_2025.zip)).
- **Refund:** the excess over the tax liability is credited against other amounts due, and the balance is refunded ((c)).
- **Records to keep for 2026 and each claim year:**
  - per journalist: hours, weeks, pay, start and end dates, residence and job duties;
  - daily headcount;
  - audience or distribution data;
  - the insurance policy;
  - the ownership disclosure and correction policy as published;
  - the organization or registration date.

## Completion checklist

- [ ] Taxable year begins on or after 1 January 2027 and before 1 January 2032.
- [ ] Outlet type confirmed (digital, broadcast or print) and its test met.
- [ ] All five qualified-taxpayer tests met, including the 12-month organization or registration date.
- [ ] Each journalist: California resident, main news duties in California.
- [ ] Each journalist sorted full time, part time or neither, using hours, more than 26 weeks and annualized pay.
- [ ] Continuous employment checked against the prior year (2026 for the first calendar year).
- [ ] Average daily headcount for both years rounded first, then subtracted, floor zero.
- [ ] Tier amounts applied: first five at USD 20,000, the rest at USD 15,000, plus USD 15,000 per new position and USD 7,500 per part-timer.
- [ ] No other hiring credit claimed for the same journalists.
- [ ] Refund computed after other amounts due; S corporation and minimum franchise tax cases referred.
