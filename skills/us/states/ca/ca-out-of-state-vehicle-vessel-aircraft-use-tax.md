# California use tax on vehicles, vessels and aircraft bought out of state, after SB 1406 v0.1

> **General reference only.** This skill is general tax/accounting reference material for AI-assisted workflows. It has not been reviewed for any specific person's facts, documents, elections, deadlines, residency, filing status, or local procedures. Do not rely on it to file, pay, amend, or take a tax position without review by a qualified professional in the relevant jurisdiction.

> **Source-cited draft.** Built from the chaptered text of SB 1406 (Stats. 2026, Ch. 1036) and the codified Revenue and Taxation Code (R&TC) and Vehicle Code sections in the official California Legislature bulk data archive; R&TC 6201, 6202, 6247, 6248, 6292, 6406, 6485, 6485.1 and 6591 in the CDTFA Business Taxes Law Guide (Revision 2026); CDTFA Regulations 1610 and 1620; CDTFA Publication 52 (November 2025); form CDTFA-401-CUTS (rev. 12-25) and its instructions; the CDTFA Vehicles, Vessels and Aircraft industry guide; and the CDTFA sales and use tax rate table effective 1 October 2026. All were read on 2026-11-22 (California, Pacific time). No accountant has reviewed it.

**Answer first.**

- **The presumption.** A vehicle, vessel or aircraft "bought outside of this state" and "brought into California within 12 months from the date of its purchase" is presumed bought for use in California, and subject to use tax, if **any one** of four things is true ([R&TC 6248(a)](https://cdtfa.ca.gov/lawguides/vol1/sutl/6248.html)):
  1. the purchaser is a California resident under Vehicle Code 516;
  2. a vehicle was subject to California registration during the first 12 months of ownership;
  3. a vessel or aircraft was subject to California property tax during the first 12 months of ownership;
  4. a nonresident purchaser used or stored it in California more than half the time during the first 12 months of ownership.
- **What SB 1406 changed (in effect 30 September 2026):**
  - **50% test widened.** A closely held corporation, partnership, limited partnership, limited liability partnership or LLC is a California resident if 50% or more of its interests are held by California residents. Before, only corporations and LLCs were named (R&TC 6248(a)(1)(A)).
  - **Shell companies.** A "shell company" is a California resident if **any** shareholder, partner, member or beneficial owner is a California resident (6248(a)(1)(B)).
  - **Personal liability.** Officers, managers, partners, beneficial owners and members of a shell company are personally liable for the unpaid use tax, interest and penalties on the purchase (new R&TC 6829.5).
- **Rebuttal.** The purchaser can rebut the presumption with "documentary evidence that the vehicle, vessel, or aircraft was purchased for use outside of this state during the first 12 months of ownership", such as out-of-state registration (6248(b)).
- **The tax.** If tax applies: the purchase price x the sales and use tax rate for the place where the vehicle is registered, the vessel moored or the aircraft hangared, less any sales or use tax properly paid to another US state ([R&TC 6406](https://cdtfa.ca.gov/lawguides/vol1/sutl/6406.html); [CDTFA-401-CUTS](https://cdtfa.ca.gov/DownloadFile.ashx?path=/formspubs/cdtfa401cuts.pdf)). The statewide rate is 7.25%. With district taxes, the rate is, for example, 8.75% in the City of Sacramento from 1 October 2026 ([CDTFA rate table](https://cdtfa.ca.gov/taxes-and-fees/SalesTaxRates10-1-26.xlsx)).
- **Where it is paid.**
  - **Vehicles and undocumented vessels:** to DMV at registration ([Reg 1610(c)](https://cdtfa.ca.gov/lawguides/vol1/sutr/1610.html)).
  - **Aircraft and documented vessels:** to CDTFA on form CDTFA-401-CUTS.
- **Penalties:**
  - **Late payment:** 10% ([R&TC 6591](https://cdtfa.ca.gov/lawguides/vol1/sutl/6591.html)).
  - **Fraud or intent to evade:** 25% of a deficiency determination ([R&TC 6485](https://cdtfa.ca.gov/lawguides/vol1/sutl/6485.html)).
  - **Registering outside California to evade the tax:** 50% ([R&TC 6485.1](https://cdtfa.ca.gov/lawguides/vol1/sutl/6485-1.html)).

**Tax year.** Tax year 2026. SB 1406 took effect immediately as a tax levy (SEC. 5). The codified R&TC 6248 and 6829.5 in the Legislature archive each read "Effective September 30, 2026", and CDTFA's R&TC 6247 history note reads "Stats. 2026, Ch. 1036 (SB 1406), in effect on September 30, 2026". Use tax is a transaction tax, not an annual one: the rules here apply to purchases and entries into California on and after 30 September 2026. SB 1406 has no transition rule of its own (see the AUDIT FLASH POINT on timing below), so purchases before that date that turn on the new partnership or shell rules are referred.

**Circular 230 disclosure.** This material is general information, not written advice on a specific transaction under 31 CFR 10.37. It is not intended to be used, and cannot be used, to avoid penalties.

## Scope and who this is for

In scope:

- **Individuals and entities** that bought a vehicle, vessel or aircraft outside California, first used it outside California, and brought it into California within 12 months of purchase.
- **Officers, managers, partners, members and beneficial owners** of an entity that may be a shell company under R&TC 6248(a)(1)(C), for their personal exposure under R&TC 6829.5.

The job: decide whether the 12-month presumption arises, whether it can be rebutted or an exclusion applies, work out the use tax and any credit for another state's tax, say where and when to pay, and flag the shell company and personal liability risk.

Definitions used (from [Reg 1610(a)](https://cdtfa.ca.gov/lawguides/vol1/sutr/1610.html)): a "vehicle" is a device for moving people or property on a highway that must be registered under the Vehicle Code, plus off-highway motor vehicles subject to identification. A "vessel" is any boat, ship, barge, craft or floating thing designed for navigation in the water, except a seaplane, a watercraft on a fixed course, a watercraft propelled only by oars or paddles, and a sailing watercraft 8 feet or less in length. An "aircraft" is any contrivance designed for powered navigation in the air, except a rocket or a missile, and includes an airframe or fuselage without an engine.

## Ask the client first

- What was bought (vehicle, undocumented vessel, documented vessel or aircraft), from whom (a dealer or a private party), for what total price, and on what date was it delivered, and where?
- Who is the purchaser of record: an individual, or a corporation, partnership, LP, LLP or LLC? For an entity: who owns it, in what percentages, and which owners are California residents under Vehicle Code 516?
- For an entity: what does it do apart from owning this property? Does it have a place of business outside California, employees paid on Form W-2, and filed federal returns and any required other-state returns?
- When did the property first enter California, and where was it used or stored, day by day, during the first 12 months of ownership?
- Is it registered in California or elsewhere? Has the county assessed property tax on the vessel or aircraft?
- Did the purchaser pay sales or use tax to another state, and is there an invoice or closing statement showing it?
- Did it enter California only for repair, retrofit, modification, warranty work, or for use in interstate or foreign commerce?
- Has CDTFA or DMV contacted the purchaser or any owner, officer or manager about this property?

### Steps

1. **Check where it was delivered and first used.**
   - If the property was delivered to the purchaser or their representative in California, the out-of-state rules do not help. A California dealer's sale is a sales tax matter ([Publication 52, p. 8](https://cdtfa.ca.gov/formspubs/pub52.pdf)).
   - If it was bought outside California but its **first functional use** was in California, it "is regarded as having been purchased for use in this state" without any presumption ([Reg 1620(b)(5)(A)](https://cdtfa.ca.gov/lawguides/vol1/sutr/1620.html)). "Functional use" means use for the purpose the property was designed for. A personal car is functionally used when driven; a truck or bus built for carrying passengers or property is not functionally used until it is used for that purpose ([CDTFA, Vehicles](https://cdtfa.ca.gov/industry/vehicles-vessels-aircraft/vehicles.htm)).
2. **Check the 12-month window.** Count from the purchase date, which is "typically the date the property is delivered to the purchaser by the seller" ([CDTFA-401-CUTS instructions, line 2a](https://cdtfa.ca.gov/DownloadFile.ashx?path=/formspubs/cdtfa401cuts.pdf)). If the property first entered California more than 12 months after purchase, the 6248 presumption does not arise. Whether use tax still applies then turns on whether it was bought for use in California on the other facts (R&TC 6201, 6202). Refer that question.
3. **Test the four triggers (any one is enough).** Publication 52 says: "Only one of the conditions needs to be met for the presumption to apply." So a nonresident entity can still be caught by registration (2), property tax (3) or more-than-half use (4).
   - **(1) Resident purchaser.**
     - **Individual:** apply Vehicle Code 516.
     - **Closely held entity:** add up the interests held by California residents. At 50% or more, the entity is a resident (6248(a)(1)(A)). Since 30 September 2026 this covers partnerships, LPs and LLPs as well as corporations and LLCs.
     - **Shell company:** if the entity is a shell company, one California owner of any size is enough (6248(a)(1)(B)). See the AUDIT FLASH POINT below.
   - **(2) Vehicle registration:** the vehicle was subject to California registration under Vehicle Code 4000 and following during the first 12 months of ownership.
   - **(3) Property tax:** the vessel or aircraft was subject to California property tax during the first 12 months of ownership.
   - **(4) Nonresident use:** a nonresident purchaser used or stored it in California more than half the time in the first 12 months.
4. **Apply the exclusions.**
   - **Interstate or foreign commerce:** 6248 "shall not apply to any vehicle, vessel, or aircraft used in interstate or foreign commerce pursuant to regulations prescribed by the department" (6248(c)). Under Reg 1620(b)(5)(C), use tax does not apply if one-half or more of the miles, nautical miles or flight time in the 6 months after entry are commercial interstate or foreign travel.
   - **Repair, retrofit or modification (vessels and aircraft):** not acquired for use in California if brought in **exclusively** for that work. A vessel's work must be done by a repair facility holding a CDTFA permit and any required city or county business license. An aircraft's work must be done by an FAA-certified repair station or a manufacturer's maintenance facility (6248(e)).
   - **Warranty or repair (vehicles):** the presumption can be rebutted if the vehicle was in California only for warranty or repair work, for 30 days or less, with the work order and the owner's travel statement (6248(f)).
5. **Test rebuttal.** The purchaser may rebut with documentary evidence of purchase for use outside California during the first 12 months, "including, but not limited to, evidence of registration ... outside of this state" (6248(b)). CDTFA's lists of supporting documents include:
   - the purchase contract and a signed seller's statement of out-of-state delivery;
   - out-of-state registration and insurance;
   - flight logs (aircraft) or mooring and slip receipts (vessels);
   - fuel, lodging, meal, hangar, repair and maintenance receipts;
   - bank and card statements showing where the property was.
   ([CDTFA, Vehicles](https://cdtfa.ca.gov/industry/vehicles-vessels-aircraft/vehicles.htm); [Vessels](https://cdtfa.ca.gov/industry/vehicles-vessels-aircraft/vessels.htm); [Aircraft](https://cdtfa.ca.gov/industry/vehicles-vessels-aircraft/aircraft.htm); [Publication 52, p. 7](https://cdtfa.ca.gov/formspubs/pub52.pdf)).
   Publication 52 adds that evidence the purchaser had no intention, at the time of purchase, to use the property in California "alone is sufficient", and that a voluntary move to California within 12 months is one factor, not decisive (p. 6).
6. **Work out the tax.**
   - **Measure:** the total purchase price, including accessories, trade-in allowance, any loan assumed and the value of property or services given in trade (CDTFA-401-CUTS, line 1).
     - For a vehicle bought from an out-of-state dealer, the measure is the sales price.
     - For a vehicle bought privately and taxed at DMV, DMV presumes the price from vehicle license fee market value x 1.2 or x 1.8, unless the actual price is shown (Reg 1610(c)(1)).
   - **Rate:** the combined rate for the address where the vehicle or undocumented vessel is registered, the documented vessel is principally stored or moored, or the aircraft is principally hangared or tied down (CDTFA-401-CUTS, line 7).
   - **Credit:** subtract sales or use tax properly paid to another state, a US political subdivision or the District of Columbia before use in California. The credit cannot exceed the California taxes, and foreign sales taxes do not count ([R&TC 6406](https://cdtfa.ca.gov/lawguides/vol1/sutl/6406.html); CDTFA-401-CUTS, line 9). If the other state's tax was equal or higher, the credit stops at the California tax and nothing is due ([Publication 52, p. 9](https://cdtfa.ca.gov/formspubs/pub52.pdf)).
   - **Late:** add 10% of the net tax if paid after the due date, plus interest (R&TC 6591; CDTFA-401-CUTS lines 11 and 12).
7. **Flag the shell company exposure.** If the purchaser could be a shell company, name each officer, manager, partner, beneficial owner and member, and say that R&TC 6829.5 can make them personally liable (see below). Refer.

**AUDIT FLASH POINT: what makes an entity a "shell company".** R&TC 6248(a)(1)(C) defines a shell company as a closely held corporation, partnership, LP, LLP or LLC "used for the purpose of evading the payment of taxes due under this part". The four listed facts are **evidence**, not a checklist: the definition turns on the evading purpose, and the list "includes, but is not limited to":

- (i) "It lacks a specific business activity or purpose."
- (ii) "It fails to maintain a physical location outside California."
- (iii) "It fails to employ individual persons and provide those persons with Internal Revenue Service Form W-2 wage and tax statements."
- (iv) "It fails to file federal tax returns or fails to file a required state tax return in a state other than California."

A single-asset out-of-state LLC formed to buy a motorhome, boat or plane for a California owner fits several of these facts. An operating business with staff, premises and returns outside California fits none. Everything in between is a judgment call. Do not label an entity a shell company or clear it; refer.

**AUDIT FLASH POINT: two different tests, two different lists of people.**

- **The presumption (6248).** The **purchaser** must rebut it with documentary evidence. A shell company is a resident if any "shareholder, partner, member, or beneficial owner" is a California resident.
- **Personal liability (6829.5).** **CDTFA** must establish that the shell company "stored, used, or otherwise consumed the vehicle, vessel, or aircraft in this state within 12 months from the date of purchase and failed to pay the tax to the seller or to remit the tax to the department or the Department of Motor Vehicles" (6829.5(b)). Liability then falls on "any officer, manager, partner, beneficial owner, or member", "notwithstanding any provision in the Corporations Code", for "any unpaid taxes, and any interest and penalties on those taxes" on the purchase (6829.5(a)).
- **The lists differ.** 6829.5 does not use the word "shareholder", and it names officers and managers, who need not be owners. Whether "beneficial owner" reaches every shareholder is not confirmed; do not treat any owner as outside 6829.5.
- **Collection.** CDTFA collects by deficiency determination under R&TC Chapters 5 and 6. The notice must be served within 3 years after the last day of the calendar month following the quarter in which CDTFA obtains actual knowledge of the California use (6829.5(c), (d)).

**AUDIT FLASH POINT: timing of the SB 1406 changes.** R&TC 6248(d) ("shall not apply to any vehicle, vessel, or aircraft that is either purchased, or is the subject of a binding purchase contract that is entered into, on or before the operative date of this subdivision") is **not** an SB 1406 grandfather clause. It was in the text before SB 1406, and Reg 1620(b)(5)(E) ties it to the 2004 and 2008 versions of the 12-month test. SB 1406 itself contains no rule on whether its new resident tests apply to property bought, or brought into California, before 30 September 2026. Refer any case that depends on that.

## Worked cases

Both cases are made up for illustration. Rates come from the CDTFA rate table effective 1 October 2026. Interest is left out because the current modified adjusted rate was not read.

**Case 1: California resident buys a car from an out-of-state dealer.**

- **Facts:** an individual who lives and works in Sacramento (a California resident under Vehicle Code 516) buys a used car from a dealer in another state on 15 October 2026 for USD 30,000. Delivery is at the dealer's lot. The dealer collects USD 1,200 of that state's sales tax, shown on the invoice. The buyer drives the car home the next week and registers it at a City of Sacramento address.
- **Presumption:** arises under trigger (1) because the buyer is a California resident and the car entered California within 12 months. Trigger (2) also applies, since the car is subject to California registration in the first 12 months. Nothing rebuts it.
- **Measure:** USD 30,000, the sales price, because the seller is a dealer (Reg 1610(c)(1)); the DMV 1.2 factor does not apply.
- **Use tax:** USD 30,000 x 8.75% = USD 2,625.00.
- **Credit for the other state's tax:** USD 1,200.00 (R&TC 6406).
- **Net use tax:** USD 2,625.00 - USD 1,200.00 = USD 1,425.00, paid to DMV at registration with the purchase agreement showing the other state's tax (Publication 52, p. 9). If reported on CDTFA-401-CUTS instead, a vehicle's return and payment are due by the last day of the month after the month of purchase: 30 November 2026.
- **If paid late:** on the CDTFA-401-CUTS route, a 10% penalty of USD 142.50 (R&TC 6591(a); CDTFA-401-CUTS line 11), total USD 1,567.50, plus interest for each month or part of a month after the due date (not computed). On the DMV route, a late registration application carries the same 10% penalty but no interest (R&TC 6292(b)).

**Case 2: aircraft bought through an out-of-state LLC with one small California owner.**

- **Facts:** an LLC formed in another state buys a used aircraft there on 2 November 2026 for USD 800,000. One member, a resident of the City of Sacramento, holds 10%; the other 90% is held by a nonresident. The LLC has no employees, no place of business outside California other than a registered agent, and has filed no federal return. From 1 December 2026 the aircraft is hangared at an address in the City of Sacramento, and it stays in California through the end of the first 12 months of ownership (1 November 2027). The LLC's pilot flies the aircraft in the seller's state for its first week of use, so its first functional use is outside California. No tax was paid to any state.
- **50% test:** 10% is below 50%, so the LLC is not a resident under 6248(a)(1)(A).
- **Shell company:** the facts match evidence items (ii), (iii) and (iv). If the LLC was used to evade the tax, it is a shell company, and its 10% California member makes it a resident (6248(a)(1)(B)). Refer the shell question.
- **Trigger (4) anyway:** the first 12 months of ownership run from 2 November 2026 to 1 November 2027, 365 days. The aircraft is in California from 1 December 2026 to 1 November 2027, 336 days, which is more than half (182.5 days). So the presumption arises under (4) even if the LLC is not a shell company.
- **Use tax:** USD 800,000 x 8.75% = USD 70,000.00, reported on CDTFA-401-CUTS at the hangar address. With no return form from CDTFA, it is due by the last day of the twelfth month after the month of purchase: 30 November 2027 (Reg 1610(c)(2)(A)).
- **Insider exposure:** suppose the LLC does not pay, and CDTFA learns of the California use in an audit on 10 February 2028 (first quarter of 2028). If CDTFA establishes that the LLC was a shell company that used the aircraft in California within 12 months and did not pay, each officer, manager, partner, beneficial owner and member can be personally liable for the USD 70,000.00 plus interest and penalties (6829.5(a), (b)). The notice must be served by 30 April 2031: three years after 30 April 2028, the last day of the month following the quarter of knowledge (6829.5(d)).
- **Possible further penalties, not computed:** 10% for late payment (R&TC 6591), 25% if a deficiency is due to fraud or intent to evade (R&TC 6485), and 50% if the aircraft was registered outside California to evade the tax (R&TC 6485.1). How these combine was not confirmed in the sources read.

## When to refuse or refer

- **Shell company status.** Whether an entity is a shell company "used for the purpose of evading" the tax is a facts-and-intent question. Refer, and do not advise on structuring around it.
- **Personal liability.** Any client who is an officer, manager, partner, member or beneficial owner of an entity that may be a shell company. Refer to a California tax attorney or CPA.
- **Timing.** Purchases or binding contracts before 30 September 2026 where the result depends on the new partnership or shell company rules.
- **Outside the 12-month window.** Property first brought in more than 12 months after purchase, where CDTFA asserts purchase for use in California on other facts.
- **Evasion penalties.** Any case where CDTFA has asserted, or may assert, the 25% or 50% penalties, or where the client has registered the property outside California while keeping it here.
- **Out-of-scope transfers** listed in the scope table (gifts, family, entity transfers, brokers, partial exemptions, common carrier, leases).
- **Disputed residency.** When the purchaser's Vehicle Code 516 residency is unclear (split homes, students, military). Ask CDTFA for written advice; Publication 52 notes that written advice may protect the taxpayer and oral advice does not (p. 7).

## Filing and payment

- **Vehicles and undocumented vessels:**
  - **Where:** pay use tax to DMV, acting for CDTFA, when applying for registration (R&TC 6292(a); Reg 1610(c)(1), (c)(2)(B)). Paying DMV relieves the purchaser of filing a CDTFA return (R&TC 6292(c)).
  - **Late registration:** if the application to DMV is late, the 10% penalty under R&TC 6591 applies, but no interest (R&TC 6292(b)).
  - **Not paid or not applied:** if the purchaser never applies to DMV or does not pay, interest and penalties apply under R&TC Chapter 5 (R&TC 6292(d)).
  - **Exemption first:** a purchaser who needs registration quickly may pay DMV and then claim a refund through DMV from CDTFA on CDTFA-101-DMV (Reg 1610(c)(1); Publication 52).
  - **CDTFA-401-CUTS route:** due by the last day of the month after the month of purchase (CDTFA-401-CUTS instructions, line 2b).
- **Aircraft and documented vessels:**
  - **Form:** CDTFA-401-CUTS, filed with CDTFA's Consumer Use Tax Section, or online through CDTFA Online Services ("File a Return or Claim an Exemption for a Vehicle, Vessel, Aircraft, or Mobile Home").
  - **Due:** the earlier of the last day of the month after the month CDTFA mails or presents a return, or the last day of the twelfth month after the month of purchase (Reg 1610(c)(2)(A); CDTFA-401-CUTS line 2b).
  - **Permit holders:** a purchaser with a seller's permit or consumer use tax account reports the tax on its return for the period of purchase (Reg 1610(c)(2)(A)).
- **Claiming the exclusion:** on CDTFA-401-CUTS, check box 4e ("Not Purchased for Use in California") or 4c (interstate and foreign commerce), enter 0 on lines 6, 10 and 13, and attach the evidence. If the 12-month period has not ended by the due date, CDTFA recommends sending what is available and the rest after the test period ([CDTFA, Aircraft](https://cdtfa.ca.gov/industry/vehicles-vessels-aircraft/aircraft.htm)). Paying while the claim is reviewed stops interest; CDTFA refunds with interest if the claim is granted ([CDTFA, File a Return](https://cdtfa.ca.gov/industry/vehicles-vessels-aircraft/file-a-return.htm)).
- **Records:** keep the evidence of out-of-state use for at least 8 years (Publication 52, p. 7).
