# California wildfire loss mitigation payment exclusion, after SB 195 v0.1

> **General reference only.** This skill is general tax/accounting reference material for AI-assisted workflows. It has not been reviewed for any specific person's facts, documents, elections, deadlines, residency, filing status, or local procedures. Do not rely on it to file, pay, amend, or take a tax position without review by a qualified professional in the relevant jurisdiction.

> **Source-cited draft.** Built from the chaptered text of SB 195 (Stats. 2026, Ch. 261) and the codified R&TC 17138.8, 24308.10 and 17131, Government Code 8654.2 to 8654.10 and Public Resources Code 91510, all in the official California Legislature bulk data archives; the pre-SB 195 text of R&TC 17138.8 and 24308.10 as added by SB 946 (Stats. 2024, Ch. 987), from the 2023-24 archive; the Franchise Tax Board's analysis of SB 195 (as amended 28 August 2026); and the FTB 2025 instructions for Schedule CA (540), Schedule CA (540NR), Form 100, Form 565, Form 568 and form FTB 4197. All were read on 2026-11-23. No accountant has reviewed it.

**Answer first.**

- **The rule.** "Gross income shall not include any amount received by a qualified taxpayer as a California qualified wildfire loss mitigation payment", for taxable years beginning on or after 1 January 2024 and before 1 January 2031 ([R&TC 17138.8(a)](https://downloads.leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/pubinfo_2025.zip); [R&TC 24308.10(a)](https://downloads.leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/pubinfo_2025.zip)).
- **What counts (after SB 195).** "Any grant, rebate, direct assistance, or other financial assistance for wildfire mitigation, home hardening, structure hardening, vegetation management, defensible space, fuel modification activities, or community wildfire resilience" received through either:
  - (A) the **California Wildfire Mitigation Financial Assistance Program** (Government Code 8654.2 and following); or
  - (B) a **wildfire mitigation grant program under Public Resources Code 91510**, the USD 135,000,000 Proposition 4 program run by the Office of Emergency Services (R&TC 17138.8(b)(1)).
- **Who.** Only the "taxpayer that owns the structure for which" the payment was received (R&TC 17138.8(b)(2)).
- **What SB 195 changed.**
  - It extended the end date from years beginning before 1 January **2029** to before 1 January **2031**.
  - It added the Proposition 4 program.
  - It replaced "any amount which is received ... for the benefit of a residential property owner or occupant with expenses paid, or obligations incurred, for wildfire loss mitigation" with the wider list above.
  - FTB's analysis reads the changes as "operative for taxable years beginning on or after January 1, 2026" ([FTB analysis of SB 195](https://www.ftb.ca.gov/tax-pros/law/legislation/2025-2026/SB195-082826.pdf), p. 2).
- **How it is claimed.** The exclusion is a California-only adjustment. If the amount was included in federal income, subtract it ([FTB 2025 Schedule CA (540) instructions](https://www.ftb.ca.gov/forms/2025/2025-540-ca-instructions.html); [FTB 2025 Form 100 booklet](https://www.ftb.ca.gov/forms/2025/2025-100-booklet.html)):
  - **Individuals:** Schedule CA (540 or 540NR), line 8z, column B.
  - **Corporations:** Form 100, line 15.
  - **Partnerships and LLCs:** Schedule K (565 or 568), line 11b, column (c).
  - **Reporting:** also report it on form FTB 4197 with code **WMP**, amount in column (e) ([FTB 4197 instructions](https://www.ftb.ca.gov/forms/2025/2025-4197-instructions.pdf)).

**Tax year.** Tax year 2026. SB 195 took effect immediately as a bill related to the budget (SEC. 4), and the codified sections read "Effective September 18, 2026". The exclusion covers taxable years beginning on or after 1 January 2024 and before 1 January 2031. Both sections are repealed on 1 December 2031 (R&TC 17138.8(d); 24308.10(c)). FTB's analysis treats the SB 195 changes as operative for taxable years beginning on or after 1 January 2026, so the worked cases use 2026. Years beginning in 2024 and 2025 are covered by the SB 946 wording; see the AUDIT FLASH POINT on back years. The FTB forms and instructions cited here are the 2025 versions, which describe the pre-SB 195 rule (one program, years before 2029); the 2026 versions were not yet published (404 on 2026-11-23).

**Circular 230 disclosure.** This material is general information, not written advice on a specific transaction under 31 CFR 10.37. It is not intended to be used, and cannot be used, to avoid penalties.

## Scope and who this is for

In scope:

- **Individuals, estates and trusts** (Personal Income Tax Law, R&TC 17138.8) that own a structure and received a qualifying payment.
- **Corporations** (Corporation Tax Law, R&TC 24308.10) that own a structure and received a qualifying payment.
- **Partnerships and LLCs** that report the exclusion on Schedule K (565 or 568), following the FTB instructions.

The job: decide whether a payment is a California qualified wildfire loss mitigation payment received by a qualified taxpayer in a covered year, then make the California adjustment and the FTB 4197 report.

Out of scope, and where it goes:

| Topic | Where |
| --- | --- |
| Wildfire disaster settlement payments from a settlement entity (R&TC 17138.7), and the Kincade, Zogg, Thomas and Woolsey fire settlement exclusions | refer; FTB Schedule CA (540) instructions, line 8z |
| Federal "qualified wildfire relief payments" under the Federal Disaster Tax Relief Act of 2023, which California does not follow (a column C addition, not a subtraction) | refer; FTB Schedule CA (540) instructions, line 8z |
| Earthquake loss mitigation payments from the California Residential Mitigation Program or California Earthquake Authority | refer; FTB Schedule CA (540) instructions, line 8z |
| Federal income tax treatment of the payment (including IRC 139) | refer; this skill does not decide it |
| Casualty losses after a fire | refer |
| Form 100S lines | not covered here |
| Property tax, defensible space inspections and local fire rules | refer (local) |

## Ask the client first

- What exactly was received: a grant, a rebate, direct assistance (work paid for directly), or other financial assistance? From which agency or program, and in what amount and on what date? Get the award letter.
- Was it paid through the California Wildfire Mitigation Program (the Office of Emergency Services and CAL FIRE joint powers authority), or through a Proposition 4 grant program under PRC 91510, such as the Home Hardening Program? Or did it come from somewhere else (a utility, an insurer, a city, a federal grant)?
- Who owns the structure the work was done on? Is the claimant the owner of record, a tenant, a contractor, or an agency or nonprofit that passed the money on?
- Is the structure a home, a rental, or a commercial or public building?
- Was the payment included in federal income for the year, and on which line?
- What taxable year is it, and when does that year begin (calendar or fiscal)?
- Did the owner also deduct, depreciate or add to basis the cost of the work that the payment funded?

### Steps

1. **Check the year.** The taxable year must **begin** on or after 1 January 2024 and before 1 January 2031. A fiscal year beginning 1 July 2030 is covered; one beginning 1 January 2031 is not.
2. **Check the program.** The payment must be received **through** one of the two programs (R&TC 17138.8(b)(1)):
   - **(A) California Wildfire Mitigation Financial Assistance Program.** The Office of Emergency Services runs it with CAL FIRE through a joint powers agreement, to encourage structure hardening and retrofitting of "homes, businesses, and public buildings" and to support vegetation management, defensible space and fuel modification ([Gov. Code 8654.4](https://downloads.leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/pubinfo_2025.zip)). "Structure hardening" is the installation, replacement or retrofitting of exterior building materials to meet Chapter 7A of Title 24, or the low-cost retrofit list ([Gov. Code 8654.3(b)](https://downloads.leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/pubinfo_2025.zip)).
   - **(B) A wildfire mitigation grant program under PRC 91510.** This is the Proposition 4 money for "loans, rebates, direct assistance, and matching funds" for wildfire projects, including the Home Hardening Program for homes (PRC 91510(a)(4)).
   - **Not through either:** utility, insurer, city, county or federal payments that do not come through one of these programs do not qualify under this section, whatever they paid for.
3. **Check the purpose.** The assistance must be "for wildfire mitigation, home hardening, structure hardening, vegetation management, defensible space, fuel modification activities, or community wildfire resilience" (R&TC 17138.8(b)(1)).
4. **Check the owner.** Only the taxpayer that owns the structure the payment was received for qualifies (R&TC 17138.8(b)(2)). A tenant, a contractor paid for the work, or an agency or nonprofit that receives a grant to pass on is not the "qualified taxpayer". Refer if the owner and the recipient differ.
5. **Find what was included federally.** The FTB lines move only amounts "included as income for federal purposes". If nothing was included in federal income, there is nothing to subtract. This skill does not decide whether federal law includes or excludes the payment.
6. **Subtract it on the right line** (2025 FTB instructions; check the 2026 versions when published):
   - **Individuals:** Schedule CA, line 8z, column B.
   - **Corporations:** Form 100, line 15.
   - **Partnerships and LLCs:** Schedule K, line 11b, column (c).
7. **Report it.** Complete FTB 4197 Part I, column (a) "WMP" and column (e) the amount excluded, and attach it to the return.

**AUDIT FLASH POINT: back years (2024 and 2025).** R&TC 17138.8(a) has covered taxable years from 2024 since SB 946, and SB 195 rewrote the definition without a separate applicability clause. FTB's analysis reads the SB 195 changes as "operative for taxable years beginning on or after January 1, 2026" (p. 2). For years beginning in 2024 and 2025, apply the SB 946 definition: "any amount which is received through the California Wildfire Mitigation Financial Assistance Program ... for the benefit of a residential property owner or occupant with expenses paid, or obligations incurred, for wildfire loss mitigation". Refer any 2024 or 2025 claim, original or amended, that depends on the Proposition 4 program or the wider wording.

**AUDIT FLASH POINT: basis and deductions for work paid with an excluded payment.** The sections read say nothing about basis or about deducting costs funded by the payment. FTB's analysis warns: "This bill would allow an exclusion for payments received for wildfire loss mitigation expenditures that may increase the basis of the property. Providing both an exclusion and allowing the expenditure amount to be added to the basis of the property would have the effect of providing a double benefit for the same item" (p. 5). Do not add grant-funded costs to California basis, or deduct them, without review. Refer.

**AUDIT FLASH POINT: non-residential structures.** Before SB 195 the payment had to be "for the benefit of a residential property owner or occupant". The new definition drops those words, and the Gov. Code 8654.4 program covers businesses and public buildings. But R&TC 17138.8(b)(3) still defines "wildfire loss mitigation" as "an activity that reduces wildfire risks to a residential structure or its contents, or both", even though (b)(1) no longer uses that term. Whether a payment for a commercial structure qualifies is not settled in the sources read. Refer.

**PRC 91510 scope note.** The operative definition says "a wildfire mitigation grant program under Section 91510 of the Public Resources Code", without limiting it to one paragraph. The legislative findings in 17138.8(c)(1)(A)(ii) refer only to paragraph (4), the Home Hardening Program, and FTB's analysis describes the Home Hardening Program. A structure owner who receives money from another PRC 91510 category is an edge case; refer.

## Worked cases

Both cases are made up for illustration. Each **assumes** that the payment was included in federal income; whether it is federally taxable is not decided here (see Not yet confirmed). Neither case computes California tax, because rates, apportionment and minimum tax depend on facts outside this skill.

**Case 1: homeowner with a Home Hardening grant and an insurer rebate, 2026.**

- **Facts:** a calendar-year individual owns and lives in a house in a high fire hazard area. In 2026 they receive:
  - USD 12,000 under the Proposition 4 Home Hardening Program (PRC 91510(a)(4)), for ember-resistant vents and siding;
  - a USD 2,500 rebate from their home insurer for clearing defensible space.
- **Federal return (assumed):** both amounts, USD 14,500, are reported as other income on Schedule 1, line 8z, so federal AGI is USD 95,000.
- **California:**
  - The Home Hardening grant qualifies: a covered year, a PRC 91510 program, a listed purpose, and the claimant owns the house.
  - The insurer rebate came through neither program, so it stays in California income.
  - Subtract USD 12,000 on Schedule CA (540), line 8z, column B.
  - California AGI before any other adjustment: USD 95,000 - USD 12,000 = USD 83,000.
- **FTB 4197:** Part I, column (a) WMP, column (e) USD 12,000.

```oa-check
name: Homeowner 2026, Home Hardening grant excluded, insurer rebate stays taxable
inputs:
  home_hardening_grant: 12000
  insurer_rebate: 2500
  federal_agi: 95000
steps:
  - included_federally: home_hardening_grant + insurer_rebate
  - schedule_ca_8z_column_b: home_hardening_grant
  - still_in_california_income: included_federally - schedule_ca_8z_column_b
  - california_agi_before_other_adjustments: federal_agi - schedule_ca_8z_column_b
  - ftb_4197_column_e: home_hardening_grant
expected:
  included_federally: 14500
  schedule_ca_8z_column_b: 12000
  still_in_california_income: 2500
  california_agi_before_other_adjustments: 83000
  ftb_4197_column_e: 12000
```

**Case 2: corporation owning an apartment building, 2026.**

- **Facts:** a calendar-year C corporation owns a 12-unit apartment building. In 2026:
  - It receives USD 40,000 of structure-hardening assistance through the California Wildfire Mitigation Financial Assistance Program (Gov. Code Article 16.5) for new ember-resistant vents and exterior materials on the building.
  - A tenant of one unit separately receives USD 3,000 of program assistance for that tenant's contents.
- **Federal return (assumed):** the corporation reports the USD 40,000 in income; its federal taxable income is USD 500,000.
- **California:**
  - The corporation owns the structure and the program qualifies, so it deducts USD 40,000 on Form 100, line 15 (R&TC 24308.10; FTB 2025 Form 100 booklet).
  - Net income before other state adjustments: USD 500,000 - USD 40,000 = USD 460,000.
  - The tenant does not own the structure, so its USD 3,000 is not excluded under this section (R&TC 17138.8(b)(2)).
- **Basis:** the corporation does not add the USD 40,000 of grant-funded work to its California basis, or depreciate it, until that question is reviewed (AUDIT FLASH POINT above).
- **FTB 4197:** the corporation reports WMP, column (e) USD 40,000.

```oa-check

steps:

expected:
  form_100_line_15_exclusion: 40000

```

## When to refuse or refer

- **Back years.** Any 2024 or 2025 claim that relies on the Proposition 4 program or the wider SB 195 definition.
- **Basis and deductions.** Any question about adding grant-funded work to basis, depreciating it, or deducting it.
- **Commercial and public structures.** Payments for non-residential structures.
- **Owner and recipient differ.** Tenants, contractors, pass-through grantees, or a structure held in a trust or entity different from the recipient.
- **Federal treatment.** Whether the payment is excluded federally (for example under IRC 139). Refer.
- **Look-alike payments.** Wildfire settlement payments, federal wildfire relief payments and earthquake mitigation payments (out-of-scope table).
- **Unclear program.** If the award letter does not show that the money came through one of the two programs, ask the paying agency before claiming.

## Filing and payment

- **No separate filing.** The exclusion is claimed on the annual return for the year the payment was received:
  - Form 540 or 540NR with Schedule CA, line 8z, column B;
  - Form 100, line 15;
  - Form 565 or 568, Schedule K, line 11b, column (c) (2025 FTB instructions).
- **FTB 4197.** Attach FTB 4197 with code WMP and the amount in column (e). The 2025 instructions list individuals, C corporations including water's-edge filers, S corporations, exempt organizations, estates or trusts, partnerships and LLCs as filers for this item.
- **Records.** Keep the award letter, proof of payment and receipt, the owner's title or deed, and invoices for the work.
- **2026 forms.** When FTB publishes the 2026 instructions, check that the lines and the WMP code are unchanged and that they describe the two programs and the 2031 end date.

## Completion checklist

- [ ] Taxable year begins on or after 1 January 2024 and before 1 January 2031; 2024 and 2025 claims under the SB 195 wording referred.
- [ ] Award letter shows the money came through the California Wildfire Mitigation Financial Assistance Program or a PRC 91510 program.
- [ ] Purpose is one of the listed wildfire activities.
- [ ] Claimant owns the structure.
- [ ] Amount included in federal income identified; only that amount subtracted.
- [ ] Right California line used (Schedule CA 8z column B; Form 100 line 15; Schedule K 11b column (c)).
- [ ] FTB 4197 completed with code WMP.
- [ ] No basis increase or deduction for grant-funded work without review.
