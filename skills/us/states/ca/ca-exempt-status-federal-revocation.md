# California exemption by federal determination letter, and federal suspension or revocation (AB 2084) v0.1

> **General reference only.** This skill is general tax/accounting reference material for AI-assisted workflows. It has not been reviewed for any specific person's facts, documents, elections, deadlines, residency, filing status, or local procedures. Do not rely on it to file, pay, amend, or take a tax position without review by a qualified professional in the relevant jurisdiction.

> **Source-cited draft.** Built from the chaptered text of AB 2084 (Stats. 2026, Ch. 921) amending Revenue and Taxation Code (R&TC) 23701, its introduced version and the codified section history, all read in the official California Legislature bulk data archive; the Franchise Tax Board's final analysis of AB 2084; FTB's 2025 Instructions for Form FTB 3500A and the 2025 form; FTB's charities and nonprofits pages (main page, help page, annual and filing requirements, types of exemptions, revoked exempt organizations list); and the IRS automatic revocation page. All were read on 2026-11-23 (California, Pacific time). No accountant has reviewed it.

**Answer first.** A California organization that got its state exemption by sending FTB its IRS determination letter (form FTB 3500A, R&TC 23701(b)(1)) "shall notify" FTB if the IRS suspends or revokes that federal exemption. On notification FTB "shall suspend or revoke, whichever is applicable" the state exemption (R&TC 23701(b)(3)(A)). AB 2084 adds one exception, for **501(c)(3) organizations only**: FTB "may, in its discretion, maintain the exemption" if the federal suspension or revocation "is not related to" any of four reasons (R&TC 23701(b)(3)(B)):

1. "Fraud or intentional misrepresentation."
2. "Misuse or diversion of organizational funds."
3. "Failure to file required returns or reports."
4. "Other breaches of organizational reporting or governance requirements."

So: a 501(c)(4), (5), (6), (7) or (19) organization still loses the state exemption on notification. A 501(c)(3) whose federal exemption was automatically revoked for not filing for three years has, on this skill's reading of the plain words, no discretion available, because that revocation is related to "Failure to file required returns or reports"; FTB has not said so. A 501(c)(3) revoked for some other reason may keep its state exemption, but only if FTB chooses to. FTB has published no procedure for asking it to (the statute lets FTB set one; see "Not yet confirmed"). An organization FTB has revoked gets the exemption back "only upon compliance with this section" (R&TC 23701(b)(5)), by filing form FTB 3500, not FTB 3500A.

**Exempted on form FTB 3500 instead.** The mandatory rule above covers the federal-letter route only. For an organization that got its state exemption on form FTB 3500, FTB's charities page says "FTB may conduct an independent review of your California tax-exempt status. FTB will not automatically revoke your California tax-exempt status based on a federal revocation" ([FTB charities and nonprofits](https://www.ftb.ca.gov/file/business/types/charities-nonprofits/index.html)).

**When AB 2084 applies.** The act is a tax levy and "shall go into immediate effect" (AB 2084, SEC. 2); it was approved and chaptered on 30 September 2026. FTB's final analysis says it is "operative for taxable years beginning on or after January 1, 2026". The statute's own text gives no operative date. How a taxable-year rule maps onto a federal revocation event is not explained in any source read.

**Tax year.** Tax year 2026: the amended R&TC 23701 is in force from 30 September 2026 and, per FTB, operative for taxable years beginning on or after 1 January 2026.

**Circular 230 disclosure.** This material is general information, not written advice on a specific transaction under 31 CFR 10.37. It is not intended to be used, and cannot be used, to avoid penalties.

## Scope and who this is for

In scope: nonprofit organizations (corporations, trusts and unincorporated associations) that hold, or want, a California franchise and income tax exemption under R&TC 23701 based on federal recognition under IRC 501(c)(3), (c)(4), (c)(5), (c)(6), (c)(7) or (c)(19), and their advisers. The job: work out what a federal suspension or revocation does to the California exemption, what must be done, and how the exemption is restored.

Out of scope, and where it goes:

| Topic | Where |
| --- | --- |
| Tax, returns and estimated payments of an organization after its exemption is revoked | refer; not covered here (no source read sets it out) |
| Form 199, FTB 199N and Form 109 preparation, and unrelated business taxable income | refer to FTB Publication 1068; thresholds in one line below |
| Applying for federal exemption or federal reinstatement (Form 1023, 1024) | IRS; refer |
| Secretary of State suspension, Statement of Information, Attorney General registry | refer to the Secretary of State and the Attorney General |
| Homeowners' associations (23701t) and political organizations (23701r) | refer to FTB Publications 1028 and 1075 |
| Charitable contribution deductions of donors | refer |

## Ask the client first

- Which IRC 501(c) subsection did the IRS recognize the organization under, and how did it get its California exemption: form FTB 3500A (federal letter) or form FTB 3500 (full state application)?
- What exactly did the IRS do (suspension, revocation, automatic revocation), for what stated reason, and with what effective date? Ask for the IRS letter.
- Has the organization notified FTB yet, and has FTB already suspended or revoked the state exemption (check MyFTB, the entity status letter and FTB's revoked exempt organizations list)?
- Has the IRS since reinstated the exemption, and from what date?
- Is the organization active with both the Secretary of State and FTB, or listed as suspended or forfeited?
- What is the organization's taxable (accounting) year, in particular when the year in which the federal action took effect began?

### Steps

1. **Confirm the route.** An organization described in R&TC 23701a, 23701d, 23701e, 23701f, 23701g or 23701w is exempt "upon its submission to the Franchise Tax Board" of its IRS determination letter or ruling, or of a group exemption letter with proof that it is a subordinate (R&TC 23701(b)(1)). FTB's page on types of exemptions maps these to IRC 501(c)(5), (c)(3), (c)(6), (c)(4), (c)(7) and (c)(19) ([FTB types of exemptions](https://www.ftb.ca.gov/file/business/types/charities-nonprofits/types-of-exemptions.html)). That submission is form FTB 3500A. Every other organization, and any organization FTB has previously revoked, applies on form FTB 3500 under R&TC 23701(a) ([FTB 3500A instructions, A and line 1](https://www.ftb.ca.gov/forms/misc/3500a-instructions.html)). The rules in steps 3 to 6 apply to the federal-letter route.
2. **Effective date of the state exemption.** On the federal-letter route the state exemption's effective date "shall be no later than" the federal effective date (R&TC 23701(b)(2)(A)). FTB: organizations "will be tax-exempt for state purposes beginning on the federal exempt effective date on the federal determination letter" ([FTB 3500A instructions, F](https://www.ftb.ca.gov/forms/misc/3500a-instructions.html)). An organization "formed as a California corporation or qualified to do business in California" that is listed by the Secretary of State or FTB as "suspended" or "forfeited" cannot establish the exemption until it is listed as "active" (R&TC 23701(b)(2)(B)).
3. **Federal suspension or revocation: notify FTB.** "[T]he organization shall notify the Franchise Tax Board of the suspension or revocation, in the form and manner prescribed by the Franchise Tax Board" (R&TC 23701(b)(3)(A)). The 2025 FTB 3500A instructions say the same for revocation: "The organization must notify the FTB when the Internal Revenue Service (IRS) revokes their federal tax-exempt status." No source read prescribes a form, deadline or channel for the notice.
4. **Default result: FTB suspends or revokes.** "Except as provided in subparagraph (B), upon notification the Franchise Tax Board shall suspend or revoke, whichever is applicable, for state income tax purposes, the organization's exemption under paragraph (1)" (R&TC 23701(b)(3)(A)). This was already the law before AB 2084: the Legislative Counsel's digest says existing law "requires the board to suspend or revoke", and that the bill "would instead authorize the board, in its discretion, to retain" the exemption in the listed cases.
5. **The 501(c)(3) exception (AB 2084).** For an organization exempt under IRC 501(c)(3), FTB "may, in its discretion, maintain the exemption provided under paragraph (1) for state income tax purposes, provided that the suspension or revocation is not related to any of the following" four reasons (R&TC 23701(b)(3)(B)). Work through it in order:
   - Not a 501(c)(3): no discretion; FTB suspends or revokes.
   - 501(c)(3), federal action related to fraud or intentional misrepresentation, misuse or diversion of funds, failure to file required returns or reports, or "other breaches of organizational reporting or governance requirements": no discretion.
   - 501(c)(3), federal action not related to any of those: FTB may keep the exemption. It is not required to.
   FTB "may prescribe any regulations or procedures necessary to implement the evaluation process described in subparagraph (B), including, but not limited to, the process for the 501(c)(3) organization to comply" (R&TC 23701(b)(3)(C)). None had been published on FTB's pages read on 2026-11-23: FTB's main charities page (last updated 27 May 2026) still says that, on notification, "FTB will revoke your California tax-exempt status", and the 3500A instructions read are the 2025 edition.
6. **FTB's other power.** The subdivision does not stop FTB "from revoking the exemption of an organization that is not organized or operated in accordance with California law, this chapter, or Section 501(c)(3), (c)(4), (c)(5), (c)(6), (c)(7), or (c)(19)" (R&TC 23701(b)(4)). A 501(c)(3) that FTB keeps exempt under step 5 is still exposed to this.
7. **Getting the exemption back.** After FTB suspends or revokes under (b)(3) or (b)(4), "the exemption shall be reinstated only upon compliance with this section, regardless of whether the organization can establish exemption under paragraph (1)" (R&TC 23701(b)(5)). In practice, per FTB:
   - FTB has revoked: file form FTB 3500. "If the entity was previously revoked by the FTB, do not file form FTB 3500A. The entity must file form FTB 3500" ([FTB 3500A instructions, line 1](https://www.ftb.ca.gov/forms/misc/3500a-instructions.html)); "If an organization's tax-exempt status is revoked or denied, the organization will need to file form FTB 3500 to reinstate its tax-exempt status" (section C).
   - The IRS revoked and then reinstated, and FTB has not revoked: the sources conflict. Section I of the 2025 FTB 3500A instructions says to send FTB, with form FTB 3500A, the "Original IRS exemption determination letter", the "IRS revocation letter" and the "Current IRS exemption determination letter". But FTB's form-choice table, on both its main charities page and its help page (last updated 17 November 2026), sends an organization that "has had California/federal tax-exempt status revoked" to form FTB 3500, and FTB 3500A is for "No prior California exemption and has federal determination letter effective to start date". So FTB's current pages point this organization to form FTB 3500, while the 2025 instructions point it to form FTB 3500A. Confirm with FTB which form to file before filing either (916-845-4171, the number in the 3500A instructions).
   - State exemption may be retroactive: R&TC 23701(c) says the section "shall not prevent a determination from having retroactive effect". FTB may treat the form as a refund claim for tax paid in a period later found exempt, but only if the claim is timely under R&TC 19306 (FTB 3500A instructions, F).
   - Good standing: "An organization must be active and in good standing to retain tax-exempt status"; a 3500A may be treated as a request for relief from suspension or forfeiture under R&TC 23776 once filings and payments are up to date (section H).
8. **Check the public list.** FTB publishes a revoked exempt organizations list, "updated weekly and replaced when updated"; on 2026-11-23 it showed 71,738 organizations (page last updated 19 November 2026) ([FTB revoked exempt organizations list](https://www.ftb.ca.gov/file/business/types/charities-nonprofits/revoked-entity-list.html)).

**AUDIT FLASH POINT** (which federal reasons shut out the discretion). FTB's analysis lists the federal grounds for suspension or revocation, including "Automatic revocation for failure to comply with annual reporting obligations", alongside terrorist activity, campaign intervention, excessive lobbying, private benefit and excess unrelated business income. It does not say which of them fall in the four excluded reasons. This skill reads IRS automatic revocation (not filing for three consecutive years, IRC 6033(j)) as "related to" exclusion (iii), "Failure to file required returns or reports", on the plain words; FTB has not said so. Exclusion (iv), "Other breaches of organizational reporting or governance requirements", is undefined, and FTB's analysis words it differently ("Other significant breaches or organizational reporting or governance requirements"); the statute's text governs. Whether private benefit, excess lobbying or campaign intervention is a "governance" breach is open. Do not tell a client its exemption will be kept: the most the statute gives is that FTB may keep it.

## Worked cases

Both cases are made up for illustration. The arithmetic below is a yes or no gate: 1 means yes, 0 means no.

**Case 1: four organizations, four results.** Each got its California exemption on form FTB 3500A except D, each received an IRS revocation letter effective in 2026, and each notifies FTB in December 2026. Assumed: each is a calendar-year organization, so the taxable year concerned begins on 1 January 2026 and AB 2084 applies both on its immediate effect (30 September 2026) and on FTB's stated operative date.

- **A:** a 501(c)(3) food bank, automatically revoked for not filing Form 990-N for three consecutive years. Failure to file is an excluded reason (this skill's reading of exclusion (iii)). FTB must revoke.
- **B:** a 501(c)(3) arts charity, revoked because it engaged in prohibited political campaign activity. Not fraud, diversion or failure to file. The `b_excluded_reason: 0` input below is an assumption: whether it falls in "Other breaches of organizational reporting or governance requirements" is open; if not, FTB has discretion to keep the exemption.
- **C:** a 501(c)(6) chamber of commerce, revoked on the same kind of ground as B. Not a 501(c)(3): FTB must revoke.
- **D:** a 501(c)(3) exempted in California on form FTB 3500. Not on the federal-letter route, so (b)(3) does not apply. Per FTB, no automatic revocation; FTB may review it.

```oa-check
name: AB 2084 gate for four organizations after a federal revocation
inputs:
  a_federal_letter_route: 1
  a_is_501c3: 1
  a_excluded_reason: 1
  b_federal_letter_route: 1
  b_is_501c3: 1
  b_excluded_reason: 0
  c_federal_letter_route: 1
  c_is_501c3: 0
  c_excluded_reason: 0
  d_federal_letter_route: 0
  d_is_501c3: 1
  d_excluded_reason: 0

  - c_discretion_available: c_federal_letter_route * c_is_501c3 * (1 - c_excluded_reason)
  - c_must_revoke: c_federal_letter_route * (1 - c_discretion_available)
  - d_discretion_available: d_federal_letter_route * d_is_501c3 * (1 - d_excluded_reason)
  - d_must_revoke: d_federal_letter_route * (1 - d_discretion_available)

  a_must_revoke: 1
  b_discretion_available: 1
  b_must_revoke: 0
  c_discretion_available: 0
  c_must_revoke: 1
  d_discretion_available: 0
  d_must_revoke: 0
  organizations_facing_mandatory_revocation: 2
```

**Case 2: decision for organization B, both ways.** B is the 501(c)(3) in case 1 whose federal revocation is not for an excluded reason on its face. It has two routes:

- **Route 1, ask FTB to maintain the exemption.** Available only because B is a 501(c)(3) on the federal-letter route and the reason is not in (i) to (iii). The outcome is FTB's call, there is no published procedure yet, and FTB may still find the campaign activity to be a governance breach under (iv). If FTB revokes, B must use route 2 anyway: an FTB-revoked entity cannot use form FTB 3500A again.
- **Route 2, file form FTB 3500.** A full state application, judged on the state rules rather than the federal letter. It does not depend on FTB using its discretion, but it is a longer application (organizing documents, bylaws, financial statements for the current and three previous years).
- **What decides it:** whether exclusion (iv) reaches B's ground. If it does (the flag below set to 1), route 1 is closed and FTB must revoke. If it does not, route 1 is open but not assured. The same gate with the flag set shows the switch.

```oa-check
name: AB 2084 decision for a 501(c)(3) revoked for campaign activity, exclusion (iv) both ways
inputs:
  federal_letter_route: 1
  is_501c3: 1
  excluded_i_to_iii: 0
  excluded_iv_if_governance: 1
  excluded_iv_if_not_governance: 0
steps:
  - route1_open_if_not_governance: federal_letter_route * is_501c3 * (1 - max(excluded_i_to_iii, excluded_iv_if_not_governance))
  - route1_open_if_governance: federal_letter_route * is_501c3 * (1 - max(excluded_i_to_iii, excluded_iv_if_governance))
  - must_revoke_if_governance: federal_letter_route * (1 - route1_open_if_governance)
  - route2_form_3500_available_on_sources_read: 1
expected:
  route1_open_if_not_governance: 1
  route1_open_if_governance: 0
  must_revoke_if_governance: 1
  route2_form_3500_available_on_sources_read: 1
```

On these facts: B should notify FTB as the statute requires and ask FTB to maintain the exemption, while preparing form FTB 3500 in case FTB revokes. Whether to file FTB 3500 at once, before FTB rules, is a judgement for B's adviser; no source read says how FTB treats an FTB 3500 filed while a (b)(3)(B) evaluation is open.

## When to refuse or refer

- **Taxation after revocation.** What a revoked organization owes and files (franchise or income tax, minimum tax, estimated payments): refer; not covered here.
- **Federal reinstatement.** IRS procedures for reinstatement after automatic revocation: refer to the IRS.
- **Contesting FTB's decision, refunds and appeals.** Refer.
- **Fraud, diversion of funds or governance findings.** These involve the Attorney General and possibly criminal exposure; refer.
- **Grounds near exclusion (iv).** Do not predict FTB's exercise of discretion; say that it is discretionary.

## Filing and payment

- **Notice to FTB:** required on a federal suspension or revocation, "in the form and manner prescribed by the Franchise Tax Board" (R&TC 23701(b)(3)(A)). No form or deadline is prescribed in the sources read; keep proof of the date and content of the notice.
- **Form FTB 3500A** (federal-letter route): printed, signed (electronic signatures not accepted) and mailed, or uploaded through MyFTB ("Submit Exemption Application 3500/3500A"), with the IRS determination letter; after an IRS revocation and reinstatement, with the three IRS letters (FTB help page; FTB 3500A instructions, I).
- **Form FTB 3500** (full application, and the route after an FTB revocation): the same channels, with organizing documents, bylaws, income and expense statements for the current and three previous years, and a narrative of activities. No filing fee since 1 January 2021 (FTB help page).
- **Annual returns while exempt:** Form 199 or FTB 199N, and Form 109 if unrelated business taxable income is over USD 1,000 (FTB annual and filing requirements); see FTB Publication 1068.
- **Records:** the IRS letters (original, revocation or suspension, reinstatement), the notice sent to FTB, FTB's letters, and evidence of the federal reason.

## Completion checklist

- [ ] Route identified: FTB 3500A (federal letter) or FTB 3500.
- [ ] Federal subsection and federal action (suspension, revocation, automatic revocation) and its effective date taken from the IRS letter.
- [ ] FTB notified of the federal suspension or revocation; proof kept.
- [ ] Gate applied: 501(c)(3)? Federal reason related to any of (i) to (iv)? Discretion described as FTB's, not assured.
- [ ] Operative date point noted: in effect 30 September 2026; FTB says taxable years beginning on or after 1 January 2026.
- [ ] Restoration route chosen: FTB 3500 after an FTB revocation; if the IRS has reinstated and FTB has not revoked, form choice (FTB 3500A with three IRS letters, or FTB 3500) confirmed with FTB.
- [ ] Secretary of State and FTB good standing checked; FTB's revoked list checked.
