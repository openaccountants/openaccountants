# California sales and use tax: 2026 rates, permits, nexus and filing

> **General reference only.** These rules have not been applied to any particular person's documents, facts or deadlines. Obtain professional review before filing or taking a tax position. US written-advice safeguard (Treasury Circular 230 §10.37): establish the facts, state assumptions and obtain professional review before relying on this Guide.

**Answer first.** Start with the **7.25% statewide base**, then establish the district rate by address and transaction date ([CDTFA rates](https://www.cdtfa.ca.gov/taxes-and-fees/sut-rates-description.htm)). A remote retailer crosses the economic test when combined goods sales for delivery in California, including related persons, are **more than $500,000** in either the preceding or current calendar year; physical presence can require registration below that amount ([R&TC 6203](https://www.cdtfa.ca.gov/lawguides/vol1/sutl/6203.html)). Classify the transaction before applying a rate; an online sale is not automatically exempt.

## Scope and who this is for

Businesses selling goods in or into California, marketplace sellers, resale purchases and use-tax reporting. The core rules below cover 2026. The separate individual Form 540 case is explicitly for **2025**, not a substitute for 2026 instructions. Specialist topics and their original cases remain on explicit non-usable holds below.

## Ask the client first

Obtain California offices, agents, warehouses, fulfillment inventory and leased property; 2025 and 2026 goods-sales totals including related persons and marketplaces; facilitator registration evidence; permits and assigned filing frequency; transaction descriptions, invoices, media and contracts; delivery method, address and date; resale certificates; untaxed purchases; and CDTFA prepayment notices, returns and payments. Identify any matter listed under the referral holds before calculating.

## The method, step by step

### 1. Registration and marketplaces

- **Seller's permits.** Hold a permit for each California location where sales are customarily negotiated. A storage-only warehouse supporting a permitted location generally needs no extra permit; anyone maintaining stock here for sale needs at least one. A warehouse fulfilling retail sales negotiated out of state needs a permit. Exclusively interstate or foreign sales do not require a seller's permit, but use-tax registration must still be tested ([Regulation 1699(a)-(b)](https://www.cdtfa.ca.gov/lawguides/vol1/sutr/1699.html)). Temporary business locations are included; occasional-sale and trade-show exceptions are held below.
- **Physical presence.** Offices, distribution/storage locations, representatives selling, delivering, installing, assembling or taking orders, and rentals from tangible property here are included; fulfillment inventory must be checked ([R&TC 6203(c)(1)-(3)](https://www.cdtfa.ca.gov/lawguides/vol1/sutl/6203.html); [CDTFA marketplace inventory example](https://www.cdtfa.ca.gov/industry/MPFAct.htm)).
- **Economic test.** Test 2025 and 2026 separately; combine the retailer's goods sales for delivery in California with all persons related under IRC 267(b) and its regulations. More than $500,000 qualifies; exactly $500,000 alone does not. There is no transaction-count limb ([R&TC 6203(c)(4)](https://www.cdtfa.ca.gov/lawguides/vol1/sutl/6203.html)). Include exempt goods and resale sales, and facilitated sales. Services and electronic products with no tangible property are outside the goods test. Wholesale-only sellers have no retail collection duty; a retail sale requires the registration test ([CDTFA FAQ](https://www.cdtfa.ca.gov/industry/wayfair/frequently-asked-questions.htm); [general information](https://www.cdtfa.ca.gov/industry/wayfair/general-information.htm); [marketplaces](https://www.cdtfa.ca.gov/industry/MPFAct.htm)).
- **Crossing during the year.** Where there was no earlier physical or economic nexus, CDTFA's FAQ excludes the transaction that first takes the seller over the threshold; register then and collect from the next sale. The customer still owes use tax on untaxed purchases ([CDTFA FAQ, $500,200 example](https://www.cdtfa.ca.gov/industry/wayfair/frequently-asked-questions.htm)).
- **Facilitators.** Under [R&TC 6041(b)](https://www.cdtfa.ca.gov/lawguides/vol1/sutl/6041.html), a facilitator contracts for consideration and performs at least one activity in each of the two statutory groups, directly or through related persons. Do not classify every listing website as one. Facilitated sales count for its registration test ([R&TC 6042](https://www.cdtfa.ca.gov/lawguides/vol1/sutl/6042.html)). For a registered/required facilitator, the general rule from 1 October 2019 is that it collects, reports and pays on facilitated retail sales ([CDTFA guide](https://www.cdtfa.ca.gov/industry/MPFAct.htm)).
- **Marketplace sellers.** All retail sales exclusively facilitated by registered facilitators generally remove the seller's retail-registration requirement; a seller making direct California sales must test physical/economic nexus. Registered sellers report total sales including facilitated sales, then deduct those for which the facilitator is responsible as “other.” Obtain and verify the facilitator's permit/account and responsibility evidence. This does not remove a separate purchaser use-tax obligation ([CDTFA guide](https://www.cdtfa.ca.gov/industry/MPFAct.htm)).

### 3. Resale and use tax

**Resale certificates (CDTFA-230 or equivalent).** Obtain a timely certificate in proper form and good faith. Timely means before billing, within the normal billing/payment cycle, or at/before delivery. Include purchaser/representative signature, name and address, permit number or sufficient explanation for not holding one, “for resale,” a description of property (including raw materials/ingredients genuinely bought for resale), and execution date; an otherwise valid undated certificate is not invalid solely for lacking a date. Do not accept an implausible claim without the specific-property statement. Certificates remain effective until revoked in writing, but a purchase order stating taxable overrides a blanket certificate; the purchaser must establish its receipt within the seller’s billing cycle or before delivery (whichever is later), or that tax/reimbursement was paid to the seller. A “see purchase order” certificate needs a timely order specifying resale; a blank or zero tax alone is insufficient ([Regulation 1668(a)-(c)](https://www.cdtfa.ca.gov/lawguides/vol1/sutr/1668.html)). Late certificates are not retroactive: the seller must prove actual resale or continued holding for resale without disqualifying use, or that the buyer consumed the goods and reported tax on its return or paid tax under an assessment/audit (1668(e)). Drop shipments and misuse consequences are held below.

**Business use tax.** Report taxable goods bought without full California tax, including resale inventory diverted to use other than retention, demonstration or display while held for sale. That diversion is taxable on cost in the period first stored/used ([Regulation 1668(g)](https://www.cdtfa.ca.gov/lawguides/vol1/sutr/1668.html)). Permit holders report on their sales/use-tax return; use the rate where the property is used ([CDTFA Publication 110](https://www.cdtfa.ca.gov/formspubs/pub110/)).

**Qualified purchasers, 2024-2028.** Under [R&TC 6225](https://www.cdtfa.ca.gov/lawguides/vol1/sutl/6225.html), more than $10,000 in purchases subject to use tax per calendar year, not paid to a qualifying retailer, requires registration **only when all exclusions are satisfied**: not required to hold a seller's permit; not required to register under section 6226; not a use-tax direct-payment permit holder; and not otherwise registered to report use tax. Vehicles, vessels and aircraft are excluded from this section. File/pay by 15 April of the following year. The older and future gross-receipts tests are held, not applied to 2026.

**Individuals, 2025 Form 540 only.** Use line 91, not the predecessor's “Schedule G.” Persons with/required to hold a seller's permit, otherwise registered or required to register for CDTFA use tax, or over the stated untaxed-business-purchases limit cannot use this route for business purchases. They may still report eligible personal purchases. Use the worksheet for individual items of $1,000 or more and eligible business purchases; the lookup table is only for personal items below $1,000 each. Credit other-state tax only up to the California tax on that purchase. Do not transpose these instructions to a 2026 return ([FTB 2025 booklet, line 91](https://www.ftb.ca.gov/forms/2025/2025-540-booklet.html)).

## Worked cases

These cases use invented businesses. Where a rate is "assumed", it stands for the result of a CDTFA address lookup; always look up the real address and date.

1. **Remote seller crosses the line mid-year** ([CDTFA Wayfair FAQ](https://www.cdtfa.ca.gov/industry/wayfair/frequently-asked-questions.htm)). An Oregon seller with no California presence and no related-person sales to add sold goods for delivery into California of $410,000 in 2025. By 31 August 2026 it has sold $300,000 on its own website and $190,000 through a registered marketplace: $490,000 in total. On 12 September 2026 a $15,000 website order takes the 2026 total to $505,000, which exceeds $500,000. It is not liable for use tax on that order or earlier ones, but must register and collect state, local and district use tax on its direct sales from the next order. The marketplace keeps collecting on the facilitated sales. Its district obligation runs at least to 31 December 2027. Had the 2026 total stopped at exactly $500,000, the test would not be met.
2. **Counter sale, shipped sale and district tax** ([CDTFA district taxes](https://www.cdtfa.ca.gov/industry/local-and-district-retailer-taxes/district-tax.htm)). A retailer's store is at an address where the lookup shows an assumed 9.50%. A customer buys a $2,000 table at the counter: tax is 2,000 x 9.50% = $190.00. Another $2,000 table goes by common carrier to an address with an assumed 7.75%. The retailer's sales exceed $500,000, so it is engaged in business in every district: 2,000 x 7.75% = $155.00, with the 0.50% district part ($10.00) on Schedule A. Were it at or below $500,000 in both the preceding and current calendar years, with no presence in the customer's district, it would charge 2,000 x 7.25% = $145.00; its own district tax does not apply to goods shipped out for use elsewhere, and the customer owes the district use tax unless the retailer collects it as a courtesy.
3. **Software: download, disk, custom and hosted** ([Regulation 1502](https://www.cdtfa.ca.gov/lawguides/vol1/sutr/1502.html)). A business at an address with the 7.25% base rate buys a $1,200 prewritten program by download with no media: no tax. If the same program came on a USB drive, tax would be 1,200 x 7.25% = $87.00 on the whole price. A $40,000 program written to its order, other than a basic operational program: no tax. The $600 monthly hosted-software subscription, used only in a browser with nothing downloaded or shipped, is held for contract-specific classification: no tax result is authorised here. Check this point with one of our partner accountants.
4. **Grocery basket** ([Regulation 1602](https://www.cdtfa.ca.gov/lawguides/vol1/sutr/1602.html)). A supermarket selling separately priced items to go, with no other meal, admission-place or premises-consumption exception, that does not meet the 80-80 rule, at an address with the 7.25% base rate, sells a cold sandwich for $8, hot soup for $6, a soda for $2, a candy bar for $3, vitamins for $20 and a bag of ice for $4. Assume the vitamins are ordinary supplement pills with no medicine/complete-dietary-food exception. Exempt: sandwich and candy. Taxable: 6 + 2 + 20 + 4 = $32. Tax: 32 x 7.25% = $2.32.
5. **Quarterly prepayments** ([CDTFA prepayments](https://www.cdtfa.ca.gov/services/make-a-prepayment.htm)). CDTFA has notified a retailer that it must prepay. Its January state and local liability is $30,000, so it prepays at least 30,000 x 90% = $27,000 by 24 February. In the second quarter, the prepayment due 24 June covers 1 May to 15 June: either at least 90% of that period's liability ($35,000 x 90% = $31,500) or 135% of the May liability ($22,000 x 135% = $29,700). If it made no January prepayment before 30 April and filed and paid the quarterly return on time, the penalty would be 6% of 90% of the liability: 30,000 x 90% x 6% = $1,620.
6. **Late quarterly return** ([R&TC section 6591](https://www.cdtfa.ca.gov/lawguides/vol1/sutl/6591.html)). A quarterly filer with no prepayments owes $10,000 for April to June 2026, due 31 July 2026. It files and pays on 20 August 2026. The late filing and late payment penalties are each 10%, but together they are capped at 10% per return: $1,000, not $2,000. Interest runs for one month or fraction. Relief is held for professional review; do not assume it applies.
7. **Individual use tax on a 2025 return** ([FTB Form 540 booklet for 2025](https://www.ftb.ca.gov/forms/2025/2025-540-booklet.html)). A resident with California AGI of $85,000 bought a $1,500 television and $500 of small items from out-of-state sellers that charged no tax. The television is $1,000 or more, so it goes on the worksheet at the assumed home rate of 9.50%: 1,500 x 9.50% = $142.50, entered as $143 because the worksheet uses whole dollars. The small items may be estimated from the lookup table: $8 for AGI of $80,000 to $89,999. Line 91: 143 + 8 = $151.

```oa-check
  price: 2000
  counter_rate: 0.095
  delivery_rate: 0.0775
  base_rate: 0.0725
steps:
  - counter_tax: price * counter_rate
  - shipped_tax_over_500k: price * delivery_rate
  - district_part: price * (delivery_rate - base_rate)
  - shipped_tax_under_500k: price * base_rate
expected:
  counter_tax: 190.00
  shipped_tax_over_500k: 155.00
  district_part: 10.00
  shipped_tax_under_500k: 145.00
```

**Case 5 as a decision: which second quarter-2 prepayment option?** Option 1 (90% of 1 May to 15 June) is USD 31,500; option 2 (135% of May) is USD 29,700. Both meet the rule; choose option 2 here, which leaves USD 1,800 more cash until the quarterly return. Choose option 1 when May was an unusually large month.

### Return, allocation and payment

Use the [CDTFA online services portal](https://onlineservices.cdtfa.ca.gov/). Select the registered account and reporting period; use the [online filing instructions](https://www.cdtfa.ca.gov/cros/online-filing-instructions.htm) with the applicable return. Report total taxable/nontaxable sales for the whole quarter even when prepayments were made; deduct eligible resale and marketplace sales. Report untaxed purchases used by the business. Complete **Schedule A** by taxing county/city for district transactions. **Schedule C** allocates to registered California locations; **Schedule B** handles the specified county allocations, including applicable outside-state-origin shipments, itinerant sales, construction and vending. Do not call Schedule C an allocation of the whole 1.25% local component: the detailed local allocation concerns the 1% jurisdiction portion. Specialist or mixed-location allocations remain held above. Review the return, submit and retain the acknowledgement, schedules and payment reference; make payment by the deadline.

### Delinquency, assessments and records

Ordinary late payment under [R&TC 6591(a)](https://www.cdtfa.ca.gov/lawguides/vol1/sutl/6591.html) attracts 10% plus interest for each month or fraction; determination amounts under Articles 2/3 are excluded from that subsection. Late return penalty is 10% excluding prepayments; the section's combined cap is 10% excluding prepayments for one return. Specialist penalties and relief are held above; do not combine this cap with unrelated penalties.

Under [R&TC 6487](https://www.cdtfa.ca.gov/lawguides/vol1/sutl/6487.html), ordinary deficiency notices for nonannual returns use the later of three years from the end of the month following the quarter or three years from filing; annual returns use the analogous one-year period. No return ordinarily gives eight years from the end of the following month. Fraud/intent-to-evade and the cross-tax determinations in subsection (c) are exceptions, not covered by these ordinary limits. Section 6452.1 returns and notices, waivers or other special limitation cases require professional review. Check this point with one of our partner accountants.

Keep records, invoices, resale/delivery evidence and return workings at least four years unless written authority permits shorter retention ([Regulation 1698(b), (i)](https://www.cdtfa.ca.gov/lawguides/vol1/sutr/1698.html)); preserve records for any ongoing dispute rather than treating four years as permission to destroy them.

For the preserved **2025** individual case, the ordinary payment deadline was 15 April 2026 and filing extension 15 October 2026; overseas/disaster cases require their own conditions. A filing extension does not extend the ordinary payment date ([FTB 2025 booklet](https://www.ftb.ca.gov/forms/2025/2025-540-booklet.html)). A qualified-purchaser 2025 annual return was due 15 April 2026 ([R&TC 6225(b)](https://www.cdtfa.ca.gov/lawguides/vol1/sutl/6225.html)).
