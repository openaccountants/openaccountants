# Leaving California: residency, domicile and the part-year return, tax year 2026

> **Built from the law.** California individual departure rules for calendar 2026. The source table distinguishes statutes read in the browser, FTB guidance actually read, reused proof and regulation pages that could not be read. This is general information under Circular 230 §10.37; apply it to the person’s full facts and obtain professional review before filing.

**Answer first.** California taxes a resident on all income and a nonresident only on California-source income. A resident is anyone in California for other than a temporary or transitory purpose, or anyone domiciled in California who is away only temporarily (R&TC 17014(a)). Moving out ends residence when the absence stops being temporary; it ends domicile only when the person abandons any intention of returning **and** settles elsewhere to stay indefinitely. More than nine months in California in a year raises a rebuttable presumption of residence (R&TC 17016). A California domiciliary away for at least **546 consecutive days** under an employment-related contract is treated as a nonresident unless intangible income exceeds **$200,000** in any affected taxable year or the principal purpose is tax avoidance; return visits totalling 45 days or less a year are ignored (R&TC 17014(d)). Where a filing requirement exists, use Form 540NR for the move year: tax on total taxable income at California rates, times California taxable income over total taxable income.

## Scope and who this is for

For a person who lives in California and is moving to another state or country, and the adviser helping them. Out of scope: the full-year resident return (`ca-540-individual-return`), the figures (`ca-income-tax`), federal residency and multi-state allocation in general (`us-multi-state-residency-and-allocation`), a US citizen's federal position abroad (`us-citizen-moving-abroad-tax`), the federal residency tests for non-citizens (`us-tax-residency`), and the Florida side of a move there (`fl-moving-to-florida-domicile`).

## Ask the client first

1. **When did you decide not to return to live in California, and when did you settle in the new place with the plan to stay indefinitely?** Regulation 17014(c) needs both.
2. **How many days were you in California this year, and in each year after the move?** Calendar or travel records (R&TC 17016, the six-month rule, the 45-day limit).
3. **Is the absence under an employment-related contract, for how long, what return visits occur in every affected taxable year, and how much intangible income will each spouse have in each such year? Is avoiding California income tax the principal purpose?** All safe-harbour conditions matter.
4. **What happened to the California home: sold, rented, or kept for your own use?** Regulation 17014 examples and Regulation 17951-3.
5. **Any sale before the move with payments after it, a business sale with a non-compete, or pay after the move for work before it (bonus, stock options, RSUs, deferred pay, severance)?** Regulations 17952 and 17951-6; refer the compensation items.
6. **Is your spouse/RDP moving at the same time, and is there community property, a military role or a covered federal elective/appointive office?** These require separate status and income tests.

## The method, step by step

1. **Domicile before and after.** One domicile at a time. Abandon the old domicile, physically move to and reside in the new locality, and intend to remain permanently or indefinitely, demonstrated by actions. An intention to return can preserve California domicile despite a lengthy absence. [FTB Pub. 1031 L](https://www.ftb.ca.gov/forms/2025/2025-1031-publication.pdf).
2. **Residence in each part of the year.** R&TC 17014(a): resident if in California for other than a temporary or transitory purpose, or domiciled here and outside for a temporary or transitory purpose. A temporary absence does not end residence. Apply the actual purpose of departure and continuing connections; a change of domicile is not a prerequisite to nonresidence. [R&TC 17014](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=RTC&sectionNum=17014); [FTB Pub. 1031 H/L](https://www.ftb.ca.gov/forms/2025/2025-1031-publication.pdf).
3. **Test the employment-contract safe harbor** (R&TC 17014(d)) if the absence is under an employment-related contract. If it applies, the person is outside California for other than a temporary purpose for the whole period.
4. **Count days and test the presumption** (R&TC 17016). More than nine months in aggregate raises a presumption, rebuttable by evidence of a temporary purpose. Fewer days proves nothing on its own.
5. **Fix the date residence ended from the facts.** Record when departure became other than temporary and compare conduct before and after it. A new address alone does not prove the change. Review later conduct as evidence; see FTB Pub. 1031 G/H and the historical Dana case below.
6. **Source the income after that date.** Use R&TC 17951–17953 and the income table below. The all-year resident-equivalent denominator and the California numerator require their own carryover/deferred-income/suspended-loss histories under R&TC 17041(b)/(d)/(i); moving does not turn them into the same base. Do not compute an exceptional carryover result from this Guide. Check this point with one of our partner accountants. Test retirement separately under R&TC 17952.5 and 4 U.S.C. 114.
7. **Prepare Form 540NR when required.** The following line references describe the published **2025** form and the continuing method, not a claim that the final 2026 form was read: Schedule CA (540NR) columns A to D for total income as if resident all year, column E for income while resident plus California-source income while nonresident; line 36 California tax rate = tax on total taxable income (line 31) over total taxable income (line 19), rounded to four decimals; line 37 = California taxable income (line 35) times that rate; line 38 exemption credit percentage = line 35 over line 19 (not more than 1.0000).
8. **Resolve doubtful status before filing.** Preserve the residency workpaper and evidence. The precise protective filing procedure under Regulation 17014(d)(2), including a signed statement even when no California-source income is believed due, is not confirmed here: do not use this Guide to decide that no return is needed. Check this point with one of our partner accountants.

## Boundary and exception table

### Who is a resident (Regulation 17014)

| Item | Rule | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Temporary or transitory, 17014(b) | Passing through, a brief rest or vacation, completing a particular transaction or a short contract | [FTB residency manual 2200/2500](https://www.ftb.ca.gov/tax-pros/procedures/residency-and-sourcing-technical-manual-disclosure.pdf) |
| Not temporary, 17014(b) | A long or indefinite stay for health, business needing a long or indefinite period, a job that may last permanently or indefinitely, retiring with no definite intention of leaving | same |
| Closest connection, 17014(b) | Compare strength, not merely number, of connections; no one factor decides | same |
| Keeping domicile, 17014(c) | A continuing intention to return can preserve domicile; objective facts decide whether a new domicile was established | same |
| Evidence and timing | Compare the strength of the California connections with those elsewhere over the full year. A declaration, licence or voter registration alone is not conclusive | [FTB Pub. 1031, G/H/L](https://www.ftb.ca.gov/forms/2025/2025-1031-publication.pdf); [FTB residency manual, 2200/2500/2600](https://www.ftb.ca.gov/tax-pros/procedures/residency-and-sourcing-technical-manual-disclosure.pdf) |

**Closest-connection evidence** (FTB Pub. 1031 G; residency manual 2200): where the person spends time each year and for how many months; which homes they keep and use, and whether a California house is rented out or kept with a caretaker; where bank accounts are; social, club and business connections and business office space; where relatives are. Compare the strength of all ties; counting documents or obtaining a declaration does not settle status. Apply the same statutory tests to an absence abroad, with federal residency considered separately.

**Domicile and residence differ.** A domiciliary absent for other than a temporary purpose (a long posting abroad) is not a resident while away, but stays domiciled in California; while domicile stays here, any later absence that is only temporary leaves the person a resident.

### Records that decide an audit

Keep, for the move year and after: a day-by-day calendar with travel records; the reason for the move and proof it is not temporary (job offer, lease or purchase, a business set up there); what happened to the California home; where bank accounts, clubs, business office space and family are, before and after; names of people who can give affidavits on why the person is outside California.

### Move checklist

1. Decide the intention, and record when both domicile limbs became true.
2. Make the new place the actual main home; corroborate intention with conduct.
3. Decide the California home: kept and used is a connection; rented is California-source rent.
4. Move bank accounts, clubs and office space.
5. Plan visits back: after domicile moves, a visit is temporary under the six-month rule only if all four conditions hold; under the safe harbor, keep visits to 45 days or less a year.
6. Time intangible sales and separate gain from interest; a resident’s intangible sale can preserve California-source later gain. California real estate remains California-source whenever sold.
7. Keep the records above; obtain the specific protective-filing advice identified in step 8 if status is doubtful.

## Worked cases

### Case 1: the part-year return (computation)

Dana, single, left San Jose for a permanent job in Austin on **May 31, 2026**. Her stipulated taxable-income inputs are: total **$200,000**, California **$80,000**, including San Jose condo rent after the move. These are already taxable-income amounts, not gross receipts. Using [2026 Schedule X](https://www.ftb.ca.gov/forms/2026/2026-540-tax-rate-schedules.pdf), $3,310.88 + ($200,000 − $75,197) × 9.3% = $14,917.56, rounded to $14,918. Under the published 2025 Form 540NR method: $14,918 / $200,000 → 0.0746; $80,000 × 0.0746 = **$5,968 before credits**. The exemption percentage is 0.4000.

The original **$153** credit input is a **2025 amount**, not Dana’s 2026 credit. For comparison: $153 × 0.4 = $61.20, with no 2026 entitlement implied. The [FTB 2026 indexing announcement](https://www.ftb.ca.gov/about-ftb/newsroom/tax-news/2026/10.html) supplies **$158** for a single personal exemption unit; if eligible and unreduced, $158 × 0.4 = **$63.20**, or $63 in whole dollars. Dana’s AGI, dependent status and final 2026 credit-limitation worksheet are not supplied: no final credit or balance due is determined here. Check this point with one of our partner accountants.

### Case 2: does the 546-day safe harbor apply? (decision)

Sam is domiciled in **San Diego**, with a two-year **Germany** employment contract from **February 1, 2026 to January 31, 2028**. The stated **729** is the elapsed date difference (730 calendar dates including both endpoints), not a subtraction of his visit. He returns for **40 days in 2026**, expects **$150,000 interest/dividends each year**, and the alternative is **$250,000 in any affected taxable year**.

The 729-day span exceeds 546; 40 days can be disregarded under the 45-day rule; $150,000 does not exceed $200,000. This is only the numerical screen below. The original facts do **not** establish the purpose of absence, return visits in 2027/2028, actual all-intangible income in every affected taxable year or uninterrupted qualifying performance under the contract. **Do not conclude that Sam qualifies from these numbers alone.** If the absence is not principally tax avoidance and all contract, continuity, visit and annual-income conditions hold, the safe harbour supplies nonresident treatment. If $250,000 occurs in an affected taxable year, section 17014(d)(2) excludes him from it; that failure still does not automatically make him a resident under the general tests. Complete the missing factual tests before deciding. Check this point with one of our partner accountants. [R&TC 17014(d)](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=RTC&sectionNum=17014).

```oa-check
name: CA contract numerical screen only, not a residence determination
inputs:
  days_away: 729
  visit_days: 40
  intangible_income: 150000
  intangible_income_alt: 250000
steps:
  - ok_days: clamp(days_away - 545, 0, 1)
  - ok_visits: clamp(46 - visit_days, 0, 1)
  - ok_income: clamp(200001 - intangible_income, 0, 1)
  - numerical_screen_only: ok_days * ok_visits * ok_income
  - ok_income_alt: clamp(200001 - intangible_income_alt, 0, 1)
  - alternative_screen_only: ok_days * ok_visits * ok_income_alt
expected:
  numerical_screen_only: 1
  alternative_screen_only: 0
```

This block tests the original whole-dollar inputs only. It is not a general-purpose eligibility calculator: income a fraction above $200,000 also exceeds the statutory ceiling, and more than 45 return days requires a continuity analysis rather than a numeric resident ruling.

### Historical Dana case: preserve the original facts and sourcing

Dana lived and worked in San Jose for **twelve years**. In **March 2026** she sold California-startup shares on installments payable in **2027 and 2028**. On **May 31, 2026** she moved for a permanent Austin job, bought a house, moved bank accounts and resigned from the San Jose club. She kept the San Jose condo on a **twelve-month tenant lease**, sold listed shares through a Texas broker in **August**, and visited family for **three weeks in December**, staying with her parents and doing nothing but visiting.

If the permanent move and actual abandonment/settlement establish her change on May 31, resident-period income is included regardless of source; subsequent Texas employee wages for Texas work are not California-source. Condo rent remains California-source. Gain on the March intangible installment sale retains its California source in 2027/2028; separate any interest from that gain. The August personal-investment stock gain generally follows Texas residence, subject to California business situs and systematic California trading. About five months plus three weeks is below the nine-month presumption; that does not prove nonresidence. Test **all four** visitor conditions for the December visit, including the aggregate time for the taxable year, her Texas domicile and permanent abode, and visitor-only conduct. Sources: [R&TC 17014](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=RTC&sectionNum=17014) and [17016](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=RTC&sectionNum=17016), [FTB residency manual 2300/3210/3350/3450/3600](https://www.ftb.ca.gov/tax-pros/procedures/residency-and-sourcing-technical-manual-disclosure.pdf).

Alternative: departure at the **end of June plus the December three-week visit** exceeds six months, so the visitor rule does not decide status; apply the general purpose/connection tests. Continued months of condo use, the California club/office, or a short fixed Austin assignment can change the residence answer. A post-May 31 bonus for earlier work needs its own compensation sourcing/accrual analysis, covered by the referral below.

### Historical connection patterns

The earlier text’s **Nevada** pattern remains an illustration: a declaration and moved bank accounts, **three or four months** in Nevada, **six or seven months** in a kept California estate, and California clubs/business do not justify treating the declaration as decisive. Compare actual ties; do not infer nonresidence from fewer than nine months. The earlier **Minnesota spouses** pattern (family home/clubs/social life there, no California relatives, a California house used **November to mid-March**) must test each spouse separately; seasonal California time alone is not a resident finding. Reverse the locations and apply the same tests to a Californian wintering away. Likewise retain the earlier **Quebec** example’s cross-border direction: California’s statutory purpose/connection tests apply to departures abroad; a long or indefinite posting can end residence while domicile remains. A brief vacation or fixed short engagement may leave a California domiciliary resident. No foreign or federal residence result is implied. [FTB Pub. 1031 G/H/L](https://www.ftb.ca.gov/forms/2025/2025-1031-publication.pdf); [FTB residency manual 2200–2600](https://www.ftb.ca.gov/tax-pros/procedures/residency-and-sourcing-technical-manual-disclosure.pdf).

## When to refuse or refer

- Equity compensation (options/RSUs), deferred bonuses, severance and accrued income paid after the move: do not determine the item-specific sourcing or accrual outcome here. Check this point with one of our partner accountants.
- Spouses/RDPs moving at different times, one spouse staying, or community-property allocation: test each person’s residence and income separately; this Guide does not compute the split. Check this point with one of our partner accountants.
- A retained home in regular use, continuing California business or lengthy returns needs a full factual determination. For an open FTB inquiry/audit, protective filing procedure, evidential weight under CCR 17014(d)/(e), or limitation period, do not infer an answer from this Guide. Check this point with one of our partner accountants.
- Covered federal elective/appointive offices and staff under R&TC 17014(b), military/transport workers’ federal exclusions under 17951(b), and special apportionment facts are outside the ordinary departure determination here. Check this point with one of our partner accountants.
- Do not treat a domicile declaration, new licence or voter registration alone as ending residence. Do not treat safe-harbour failure as automatic residence. California’s state retirement exclusion and the federal nonresident/nondomiciliary protection are separate tests.
