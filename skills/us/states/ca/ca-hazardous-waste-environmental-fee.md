# California hazardous waste Environmental Fee, 2026 return due 1 March 2027 v0.1

> **General reference only.** This skill is general tax/accounting reference material for AI-assisted workflows. It has not been reviewed for any specific person's facts, documents, elections, deadlines, residency, filing status, or local procedures. Do not rely on it to file, pay, amend, or take a tax position without review by a qualified professional in the relevant jurisdiction.

> **Source-cited draft.** Built from CDTFA special notices L-1044 (October 2026) and L-1001, CDTFA's Environmental Fee page, Industry Topics page and hazardous substance fee rate table, CDTFA Publication 90, and Health and Safety Code 25205.6 and 25205.6.1 and Revenue and Taxation Code 43152.9 and 43155 as published in the CDTFA Business Taxes Law Guide (Revision 2026). All were read on 2026-11-23 (California, Pacific time). The Board of Environmental Safety regulation that sets the rates, and DTSC's industry code schedule, were not readable (see Sources). No accountant has reviewed it.

**Answer first.** An organization of a type on DTSC's industry list that had **100 or more employees each "employed in this state for more than 500 hours" during 2026** (HSC 25205.6(e)) owes the 2026 Environmental Fee. CDTFA's guidance, not the statute's text, says how to count: for an employee reported to the California EDD, use **all hours the organization paid in 2026, wherever the work was done**, without prorating between in-state and out-of-state hours, and include paid leave and paid travel time. An employee reported to another state's EDD "is not considered employed in California regardless of the number of hours the employee actually works in this state" ([CDTFA Industry Topics](https://cdtfa.ca.gov/taxes-and-fees/hazardous-substances-fee/industry-topics.htm)). The test is strict: exactly 500 hours does not count. It must file the return electronically and pay by **1 March 2027**. The statutory due date is the last day of February (R&TC 43152.9(a)); 28 February 2027 is a Sunday, and CDTFA accepts returns and payments on the next business day ([CDTFA L-1044](https://cdtfa.ca.gov/formspubs/L1044.pdf); [CDTFA Environmental Fee](https://www.cdtfa.ca.gov/taxes-and-fees/hazardous-substances-fee/environmental-fee.htm)).

| Qualifying employees in 2026 | 2026 fee |
| --- | --- |
| 1 to 99 | USD 0 |
| 100 to 249 | USD 1,235 |
| 250 to 499 | USD 2,651 |
| 500 to 999 | USD 15,674 |
| 1,000 or more | USD 52,997 |

These are the same rates as the 2025 return. L-1044 is titled "New Environmental Fee Rates and Filing Reminder", but CDTFA's rate table and its notice L-1001 show identical figures for 2025 ([CDTFA hazardous substance fee rates](https://www.cdtfa.ca.gov/taxes-and-fees/special-taxes-and-fees-tax-rates/hazardous-substance-taxes.htm); [CDTFA L-1001](https://www.cdtfa.ca.gov/formspubs/L1001.pdf)). CDTFA says the rates come from "the BES emergency regulations adopted on September 25, 2026"; that regulation was not read. Under HSC 25205.6.1(d), a regulation adopted under that section "shall remain in effect until repealed by the Board of Environmental Safety", and an emergency regulation is filed with, "but not be repealed by", the Office of Administrative Law.

**Tax year.** Tax year label 2026-27. The return covers calendar year 2026 (employees counted over 2026) and is due in 2027. CDTFA calls it "the 2026 return due on March 1, 2027"; HSC 25205.6(c)(1) says the fee is paid "for the fiscal year in which it is assessed".

**Circular 230 disclosure.** This material is general information, not written advice on a specific transaction under 31 CFR 10.37. It is not intended to be used, and cannot be used, to avoid penalties.

## Scope and who this is for

In scope: any "corporation, limited liability company, limited partnership, limited liability partnership, general partnership, and sole proprietorship" (HSC 25205.6(a)) that may owe the fee, including professional employer organizations (PEOs) and parent and subsidiary corporations. The job: decide whether the fee applies, count qualifying employees, pick the fee, and file and pay on time.

Out of scope, and where it goes:

| Topic | Where |
| --- | --- |
| Hazardous waste generation and handling fee, facility fee, disposal fee | refer; not covered here |
| Occupational lead poisoning prevention fee | refer; not covered here |
| Whether a particular industry code is on DTSC's schedule | refer to CDTFA or DTSC |
| Interest computation, penalty relief, refunds and appeals | refer to CDTFA |
| Hazardous waste regulation and permits (as opposed to the fee) | refer to DTSC |

## Ask the client first

- What is the legal form of each entity in the group (corporation, LLC, partnership, sole proprietorship)? Is each one registered with CDTFA for the Environmental Fee, or did any receive a CDTFA reminder?
- What is each entity's industry (SIC or NAICS code)? Is it a private household, nonprofit residential care facility, insurer taxed on gross premiums, bank taxed on net income, nonprofit credit union or federal agency?
- Which employees were reported to the California EDD in 2026, and which to another state's EDD?
- For each employee reported to the California EDD, how many hours did the organization pay in 2026 in total, wherever worked, including vacation, sick leave, holidays and paid travel time?
- Were any employees terminated in 2026, and were severance, sick or vacation payouts made on termination?
- Does the entity use a PEO, or is it a PEO? Under whose EDD number are the employees reported?
- Did the business close or sell in 2026?

### Steps

1. **Is the organization covered?** It must be one of the listed legal forms (HSC 25205.6(a)) and of a type on DTSC's schedule of SIC or NAICS codes (HSC 25205.6(b)). CDTFA: "At this time, all industry groups and SIC codes or NAICS codes that are not specifically exempt are included in the list" ([Publication 90](https://www.cdtfa.ca.gov/formspubs/pub90.pdf), June 2023).
2. **Exemptions.** The statute exempts a nonprofit corporation primarily engaged in residential social and personal care, "SIC Code 8361 ... or as described in Codes 623220, 623312, and 623990" of NAICS (HSC 25205.6(g)). CDTFA also lists as exempt: "Private households (SIC code 88 and NAICS code 814)", "Insurance companies that pay tax on gross premiums in lieu of all other California taxes and licenses", "Banks that pay a tax on net income in lieu of all other California taxes and licenses", "U.S. government agencies and instrumentalities" and nonprofit credit unions (Publication 90). A registered organization that believes it is exempt "must still file your environmental fee return but also include a statement explaining why it qualifies for an exemption" (Publication 90).
3. **Count qualifying employees for 2026.** "[T]he number of persons employed in this state for more than 500 hours during the calendar year preceding the calendar year in which the fee is due" (HSC 25205.6(e)). Hours "includes vacation, sick leave, and holidays for which an employee is paid during their employment"; for a terminated employee, "vacation, sick leave, holidays or other severance pay is not included" ([CDTFA Environmental Fee](https://www.cdtfa.ca.gov/taxes-and-fees/hazardous-substances-fee/environmental-fee.htm)). The statute does not say how to measure "employed in this state"; CDTFA's guidance does ([CDTFA Industry Topics](https://cdtfa.ca.gov/taxes-and-fees/hazardous-substances-fee/industry-topics.htm)):
   - **Employees reported to the California EDD:** "the hours worked in California should be calculated based on the total number of hours paid by the organization, regardless of the number of hours the employee works in California or out-of-state. The number of hours should not be prorated between in-state and out-of-state".
   - **Paid travel time:** "An employee's paid travel time should be included in the calculation of the 500-hour threshold", for example paid time on an airplane.
   - **Employees reported to another state's EDD:** "not considered employed in California regardless of the number of hours the employee actually works in this state".
   - **Threshold:** strictly more than 500 hours; an employee paid for exactly 500 hours does not count.
4. **Parents and subsidiaries register and file separately.** "All parent and subsidiary corporations must separately register and file returns" (Publication 90). This skill reads that as a separate employee count for each entity; CDTFA does not say so in those words, and aggregation is listed as not yet confirmed. PEOs "are responsible for paying the hazardous waste environmental fee for any employees reported under their EDD number" (Publication 90). This skill reads that as meaning employees reported under a PEO's EDD number are counted by the PEO; CDTFA does not say so in those words.
5. **Pick the fee** from the band for the count. Under 100: USD 0.
6. **File and pay.** File the return "electronically through our online services system" and pay by 1 March 2027 (CDTFA Environmental Fee page; L-1044; R&TC 43152.9(b)). "Returns must be received by the due date to be considered timely, even if no amount is owed" (CDTFA Environmental Fee page). Anyone who received a CDTFA reminder must file, even with fewer than 100 qualifying employees (Publication 90).
7. **Late.** CDTFA: "The penalty for filing a late return and/or late fee payment is ten percent of the fee amount due for the period" (CDTFA Environmental Fee page). Under the statute, a late payment carries "a penalty of 10 percent ... plus interest at the modified adjusted rate per month"; a late return also carries 10%, but the penalties "shall be limited to a maximum of 10 percent" per return (R&TC 43155(a) to (c)).
8. **Closing or selling the business.** Notify CDTFA and file the final return online "by the last day of the month following the quarter in which you close or sell your business" (Publication 90).

**AUDIT FLASH POINT** (counting employees). The count turns on which state's EDD each employee was reported to and, for those reported to the California EDD, on total hours paid in 2026 wherever worked (no in-state proration), with paid leave and paid travel time included, termination payouts excluded, and exactly 500 hours not enough. This method is CDTFA guidance, not statutory text. Part-year, seasonal, travelling, remote, multi-state and PEO-reported staff are the usual errors. Keep the hour-by-person workpaper and the EDD reporting records.

## Worked cases

Both cases are made up for illustration.

**Case 1: counting employees and picking the fee.**

- **Facts:** a manufacturing corporation, not exempt, filing for 2026. All of the groups below were reported to the California EDD in 2026, except the last.
  - 240 employees on staff all year, each paid for well over 500 hours (some of them partly worked in Oregon).
  - 30 part-time field representatives, each paid for 300 hours worked in California, 140 hours worked in Arizona, 40 hours of paid travel time and 40 hours of vacation during employment: 520 hours.
  - 10 part-time employees, each paid for exactly 500 hours.
  - 25 seasonal workers, each paid for 480 hours.
  - 12 employees terminated mid-year, each paid for 450 hours worked, plus vacation paid out on leaving.
  - 8 employees who worked 1,800 hours each at the corporation's California plant but were reported to Nevada's EDD.
- **Count:**
  - The 240 count on total hours paid.
  - The 30 field representatives count: 300 + 140 + 40 + 40 = 520 hours, more than 500. Counting only their 300 California hours would wrongly exclude them; CDTFA says not to prorate.
  - The 10 at exactly 500 hours do not count.
  - The 25 seasonal workers (480 hours) do not count.
  - The 12 terminated employees do not count: 450 hours, and the payout on leaving is excluded.
  - The 8 reported to Nevada's EDD are not employed in California under CDTFA's guidance.
  - Qualifying employees: 240 + 30 = 270.
- **Fee:** 270 falls in the 250 to 499 band: **USD 2,651**, due 1 March 2027.

**Case 2: a late return and payment.**

- **Facts:** a corporation with 1,200 qualifying employees in 2026 files and pays the 2026 return on 20 March 2027.
- **Fee:** USD 52,997.
- **Penalty:** 10% for late payment and 10% for a late return, limited to 10% per return: USD 5,299.70. Interest at the modified adjusted rate also runs from the due date; it is not computed here.

## When to refuse or refer

- **Industry code questions.** Whether a code is on DTSC's schedule, or whether an exemption applies: refer to CDTFA or DTSC.
- **Group structures.** Counting across parents, subsidiaries, disregarded entities and PEOs beyond Publication 90's statements: refer.
- **Interest, penalty relief and refunds.** Refer to CDTFA.
- **Other hazardous waste fees and the occupational lead fee.** Not covered here.

## Filing and payment

- **Registration:** register online with CDTFA for an Environmental Fee account ("Register a New Business Activity").
- **Return:** the Environmental Fee return, filed online through CDTFA Online Services; electronic filing is required (R&TC 43152.9(b)).
- **Due:** on or before 1 March 2027 for the 2026 return. CDTFA: "If the due date falls on a Saturday, Sunday or state holiday, returns received by the next business day will be considered timely."
- **Payment:** online, or by CDTFA's other payment methods; payments "must be received or postmarked by the due date".
- **Extensions and relief:** requested online through the CDTFA account.
- **Records:** total paid hours by person (including paid leave and travel time) and the state EDD each employee was reported to for 2026, the industry code relied on, and any exemption statement.

## Completion checklist

- [ ] Legal form and industry code checked; exemptions considered.
- [ ] Employees reported to the California EDD in 2026 counted at more than 500 total paid hours (wherever worked, no proration; paid leave and paid travel in; termination payouts out); employees reported to another state's EDD left out.
- [ ] Each parent and subsidiary registered and filed separately (per-entity count is this skill's reading); PEO-reported staff identified.
- [ ] Fee taken from the 2026 band table.
- [ ] Return filed online and fee paid by 1 March 2027, even at USD 0 if registered or reminded.
- [ ] Late penalty (10% maximum) and interest considered if filed or paid late.
