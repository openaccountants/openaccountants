# California Occupational Lead Poisoning Prevention Fee, 2026 and 2027 rates v0.1

> **General reference only.** This skill is general tax/accounting reference material for AI-assisted workflows. It has not been reviewed for any specific person's facts, documents, elections, deadlines, residency, filing status, or local procedures. > **Source-cited draft.** Built from Health and Safety Code sections 105190, 105191 and 105195, Revenue and Taxation Code sections 43056, 43101 and 43152.13, and 17 CCR 38001, all as published in the CDTFA Business Taxes Law Guide (Revision 2026); CDTFA special notices L-1041 (November 2026), L-997 (November 2025) and L-773; the CDTFA Occupational Lead Poisoning Prevention Fee Guide (Overview, Getting Started, Industry Topics, Resources); CDTFA Publication 94 (rev. 1-23); and the CDPH Occupational Lead Poisoning Prevention Program fee page, read on 2026-11-21. No accountant has reviewed it.

**Answer first.** An employer pays this fee once a year if its business is in an industry the California Department of Public Health (CDPH) has identified as having a potential for occupational lead poisoning, it had lead or lead-containing materials in its operations in the previous calendar year, and it had **10 or more employees who each worked 160 hours or more in California** in that year, whatever their jobs. The fee is a flat amount set by the employee-count band and by the business's SIC category: **Category A** (an SIC code with fewer than 20 reported cases of elevated blood lead levels in the prior three years) or **Category B** (20 or more). The 2027 rates, effective 1 January 2027 to 31 December 2027, run from **USD 468** (Category A, 10 to 99 employees) to **USD 5,365** (Category B, 500 or more). The return and payment are due **by the last day of February for the previous calendar year**, filed even when nothing is owed or a waiver is being sought. Late filing or payment costs 10% of the fee plus interest.

**Tax year.** The 2026 rates (effective 1 January to 31 December 2026) and the 2027 rates (effective 1 January to 31 December 2027) are both set out. CDTFA's notices give each year's rates by effective period; the CDTFA pages read do not say in words which annual return each year's rates apply to, and the online return calculates the fee itself from the period, SIC code, category and employee count ([CDTFA L-773](https://cdtfa.ca.gov/formspubs/l773.pdf)). ## Scope and who this is for

In scope: employers in the industries listed in Health and Safety Code section 105195 (62 four-digit SIC industries, from 1622 bridges, tunnels and elevated highways to 7999 amusement and recreation, plus any the CDPH adds by regulation), registering for, computing, filing and paying the annual fee, and asking the CDPH for a waiver ([H&SC 105195](https://cdtfa.ca.gov/lawguides/vol4/olppf/olppf-ch2-all.html); [CDTFA Getting Started](https://cdtfa.ca.gov/taxes-and-fees/occupational-lead-poisoning-prevention-fee/getting-started.htm)). CDTFA administers the fee with the CDPH; the money funds the CDPH Occupational Lead Poisoning Prevention Program ([CDTFA fee guide](https://www.cdtfa.ca.gov/taxes-and-fees/occupational-lead-poisoning-prevention-fee/)).

Out of scope, and where it goes:

## Ask the client first

- What does the business do, and what four-digit SIC code did CDTFA assign at registration? Has the business been told whether it is Category A or B?
- Was any lead or lead-containing material present in the business's operations during the previous calendar year? Is it only a very small amount?
- How many people worked for the business **in California** for **160 hours or more** in the previous calendar year (counting part-time staff, salaried staff and corporate officers)?
- Is the business registered for an Occupational Lead Poisoning Prevention account with CDTFA? Which years have been filed?
- Did the business close, sell or change ownership during the year?
- Has a CDPH fee waiver been granted before, and for which year?

### Steps

1. **Is the business in a listed industry?** The fee applies to employers in four-digit SIC codes (1987 edition) that the CDPH has identified as having a potential for occupational lead poisoning ([H&SC 105190(a)](https://cdtfa.ca.gov/lawguides/vol4/olppf/olppf-105190.html); [H&SC 105195(a)](https://cdtfa.ca.gov/lawguides/vol4/olppf/olppf-ch2-all.html)).
2. **Was lead present last year?** Registration and payment apply where lead or lead-containing materials were present in the business's operations during the previous calendar year ([CDTFA Getting Started](https://cdtfa.ca.gov/taxes-and-fees/occupational-lead-poisoning-prevention-fee/getting-started.htm); [CDTFA Pub. 94](https://cdtfa.ca.gov/formspubs/pub94.pdf)).
3. **Count the employees.** Count every individual who worked 160 hours or more in California from 1 January to 31 December of the year before the year the fee is due, whatever their job ([17 CCR 38001(c), (i)](https://cdtfa.ca.gov/lawguides/vol4/olppfr/olppfr-reg38001.html); [CDTFA Pub. 94](https://cdtfa.ca.gov/formspubs/pub94.pdf)). Fewer than 10: no fee.
4. **Register.** Every employer subject to the fee registers with CDTFA ([R&TC 43101](https://cdtfa.ca.gov/lawguides/vol4/olppf/olppf-rtc-all.html)), online through CDTFA Online Services by choosing "Register a New Business Activity" ([CDTFA Getting Started](https://cdtfa.ca.gov/taxes-and-fees/occupational-lead-poisoning-prevention-fee/getting-started.htm)).
5. **Find the category.** CDTFA determines whether the employer is in Category A or B from the CDPH's case data for its SIC code ([H&SC 105190(b)](https://cdtfa.ca.gov/lawguides/vol4/olppf/olppf-105190.html)). A business that thinks its SIC code is wrong calls 1-800-400-7115 or writes to CDTFA's Registration and Licensing Section (MIC: 88), PO Box 942879, Sacramento, CA 94279-7072, describing its main and other activities ([CDTFA Industry Topics](https://cdtfa.ca.gov/taxes-and-fees/occupational-lead-poisoning-prevention-fee/industry-topics.htm)). SIC questions go to CDTFA, not the CDPH ([CDPH fee page](https://www.cdph.ca.gov/Programs/CCDPHP/DEODC/OHB/OLPPP/Pages/LeadFees.aspx)).
6. **Read the fee off the table.** One flat amount for the band (10 to 99, 100 to 499, 500 or more) in the employer's category. The fee is not per employee. The rates are adjusted each year for the cost of living ([H&SC 105190(d)](https://cdtfa.ca.gov/lawguides/vol4/olppf/olppf-105190.html)).
7. **Consider a waiver.** The CDPH, not CDTFA, may exempt an employer that shows lead is not present at its workplace, and may grant a temporary waiver for a year in which no lead, or only a minimal amount, was present. A waiver covers only that year and must be renewed each year. Request it in the Fee Waiver section of the online return (or Section II, box 2 of a paper CDTFA-501-LA); the CDPH then sends instructions and decides ([CDTFA Industry Topics](https://cdtfa.ca.gov/taxes-and-fees/occupational-lead-poisoning-prevention-fee/industry-topics.htm); [H&SC 105190(e)](https://cdtfa.ca.gov/lawguides/vol4/olppf/olppf-105190.html)). Employers within the CDPH's de minimis definition are exempt ([H&SC 105191(b)](https://cdtfa.ca.gov/lawguides/vol4/olppf/olppf-ch2-all.html)).
8. **File and pay** by the last day of February: see Filing and payment.

## Worked cases

Both cases are made up for illustration; the rates come from CDTFA L-1041 and L-997.

**Case 1: three employers on the 2027 rates, and the same employers on the 2026 rates.** A Category A auto-body shop with 120 qualifying employees, a Category B battery recycler with 600, and a Category B plumbing contractor with 45.

- 2027 rates: USD 939 + USD 5,365 + USD 672 = USD 6,976.
- 2026 rates: USD 908 + USD 5,190 + USD 650 = USD 6,748.
- The 2027 rates are USD 228 higher in total for these three, from the annual cost-of-living adjustment.

```oa-check
name: Lead fee by band and category, 2027 against 2026 rates
inputs:
  a_100_499_2027: 939
  b_500_plus_2027: 5365
  b_10_99_2027: 672
  a_100_499_2026: 908
  b_500_plus_2026: 5190
  b_10_99_2026: 650
steps:
  - total_2027: a_100_499_2027 + b_500_plus_2027 + b_10_99_2027
  - total_2026: a_100_499_2026 + b_500_plus_2026 + b_10_99_2026
  - increase: total_2027 - total_2026
expected:
  total_2027: 6976.00
  total_2026: 6748.00
  increase: 228.00
```

**Case 2: counting employees, then paying late.** A Category B plumbing contractor had 12 workers in California in the previous year: 9 full-time, 1 part-timer who worked 400 hours, and 2 summer helpers who worked 120 hours each. It counts 10 employees (the helpers are under 160 hours), so it is in the 10 to 99 band. On the 2027 rates the fee is USD 672. The return is due on the last day of February; it files and pays two weeks late.

- Employees counted: 9 + 1 = 10, so a fee is owed.
- Late penalty: 10% x USD 672 = USD 67.20, plus interest (rate not stated on the pages read).
- Fee plus penalty: USD 739.20.
- If the part-timer had worked 150 hours, the count would be 9 and no fee would be owed.

```oa-check
name: Employee count and late penalty, Category B 10 to 99 band
inputs:
  full_time: 9
  part_time_over_160_hours: 1
  fee_b_10_99_2027: 672
  penalty_rate: 0.10
steps:
  - employees: full_time + part_time_over_160_hours
  - penalty: fee_b_10_99_2027 * penalty_rate
  - fee_plus_penalty: fee_b_10_99_2027 + penalty
expected:
  employees: 10
  penalty: 67.20
  fee_plus_penalty: 739.20
```

## When to refuse or refer

- Whether the business's activity falls under a listed SIC code, or whether the assigned code is right: refer to CDTFA's Registration and Licensing Section.
- Whether lead was "present", or within the de minimis definition: the CDPH decides; refer the waiver question to the CDPH (LeadFeeWaiver@cdph.ca.gov, 1-866-627-1587).
- Past years never filed, or a CDTFA billing: refer to a California practitioner.
- Anything about the Childhood Lead Poisoning Prevention Fee: a different program; refer.

## Filing and payment

- **Return:** CDTFA-501-LA, Occupational Lead Poisoning Prevention Fee Return for Category A or B, filed online through CDTFA Online Services (CDTFA's preferred method) or on paper. Online filers enter the number of employees over 160 hours and the system calculates the fee from the SIC code, category and count; paper filers enter the amount from CDTFA's rate tables ([CDTFA L-773](https://cdtfa.ca.gov/formspubs/l773.pdf)).
- **Due:** the fee is due and payable on the last day of the second month after the end of the calendar year ([R&TC 43152.13](https://cdtfa.ca.gov/lawguides/vol4/olppf/olppf-rtc-all.html)), that is the last day of February for the previous calendar year, received or postmarked by then. A weekend or state holiday moves it to the next business day ([CDTFA Getting Started](https://cdtfa.ca.gov/taxes-and-fees/occupational-lead-poisoning-prevention-fee/getting-started.htm)). For calendar 2026, 28 February 2027 is a Sunday, so the return is timely on Monday 1 March 2027; for calendar 2027 the date is Tuesday 29 February 2028.
- **Late:** 10% of the fee due for the period, plus interest at the rate under R&TC 43155 ([CDTFA Pub. 94](https://cdtfa.ca.gov/formspubs/pub94.pdf)).
- **Help:** CDTFA 1-800-400-7115 (TTY 711), select Special Taxes and Fees, Monday to Friday 7:30 a.m. to 5:00 p.m. Pacific ([CDTFA L-1041](https://cdtfa.ca.gov/formspubs/l1041.pdf)).

## Completion checklist

- [ ] Listed industry and SIC category (A or B) confirmed with CDTFA.
- [ ] Lead presence in the previous calendar year established, or a waiver request planned.
- [ ] Employees with 160 or more California hours in the previous calendar year counted, officers included.
- [ ] Fee read from the band for the category, using the rates for the right period.
- [ ] Return filed by the last day of February (next business day if a weekend or holiday), even with no fee or a waiver request.
- [ ] Waiver renewed if still needed; CDTFA and CDPH told of any business change.
- [ ] Records kept at least 4 years.
