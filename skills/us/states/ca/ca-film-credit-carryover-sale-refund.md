# California film credits after SB 186: carryover, sale and the program 4.0 refund v0.1

> **General reference only.** This skill is general tax/accounting reference material for AI-assisted workflows. It has not been reviewed for any specific person's facts, documents, elections, deadlines, residency, filing status, or local procedures. Do not rely on it to file, pay, amend, or take a tax position without review by a qualified professional in the relevant jurisdiction.

> **Source-cited draft.** Built from the Franchise Tax Board's analysis of SB 186 as amended 28 August 2026 (readable PDF); the chaptered text of SB 186 (Stats. 2026, Ch. 344) and the codified section history in the official California Legislature bulk data archive; the chaptered text of SB 122 (Stats. 2026, Ch. 23) for the earlier wording of R&TC 17039.4; and FTB's 2025 instructions for form FTB 3541 and for Form 100S. All were read on 2026-11-23 (California, Pacific time). No accountant has reviewed it.

**Answer first.** SB 186 changed three things for credits the California Film Commission (CFC) has already allocated and certified:

- **Carryover of credits 2.0 and 3.0 (R&TC 17053.95, 17053.98, 23695, 23698): 15 years.** Excess credit carries to "the following taxable year, and succeeding 14 taxable years". In the 10th to 15th taxable years after the year the credit was allowed, the credit can be used only in a year when the taxpayer or one of the related entities the statute lists is an "active participant", meaning it holds a program 4.0 allocation not yet certified or was issued a program 4.0 credit certificate for that year. The rule "shall apply to credit carryovers beginning on January 1, 2025, including a credit carryover for which the carryover period had expired". Program 4.0 keeps its own carryover of the following year and eight succeeding years.
- **Sold credits leave the business credit limit.** "For taxable years beginning on or after January 1, 2027, any credit, or portion thereof, sold" under the independent film sale paragraphs of the 2.0, 3.0 and 4.0 sections "is not included in the limitation" of USD 5,000,000 (R&TC 17039.4(g), 23036.4(g)). The same exclusion applies under the permanent limit from 2030, the greater of 70% of tax or USD 5,000,000 (R&TC 17039.6(g), 23036.6(g)).
- **Program 4.0 refund election: 95% in two years.** "Total refundable amount" is now 95% (was 90%) of the credit amount over the tax in the first year. "Annual refundable amount" is 50% (was 20%) of that amount, and the "refundable period" is the certificate year "and the succeeding taxable year" (was the succeeding four years) (R&TC 17053.98.1(k), 23698.1(k)). FTB says the change applies to taxable years beginning on or after 1 January 2026 ([FTB analysis of SB 186](https://www.ftb.ca.gov/tax-pros/law/legislation/2025-2026/SB186-082826.pdf), p. 3); the amended sections themselves state no operative date for it.

Already in the sections before SB 186 (compared with the text SB 122 enacted), unchanged by it: from taxable years beginning in 2027, a program 4.0 refund election takes both the first-year credit and the annual refundable amount outside the USD 5,000,000 limit, and any refundable 4.0 credit held back by the limit is allowed in the first taxable year beginning on or after 1 January 2027 (R&TC 17039.4(e), (k), 23036.4(f), (j)).

**Tax year.** Tax year label 2026-27. SB 186 took effect immediately as a bill related to the budget (SEC. 12); the codified sections read "Effective September 19, 2026". The rules have three start points: carryovers "beginning on January 1, 2025"; the program 4.0 refund changes, taxable years beginning on or after 1 January 2026 (FTB); and the sold-credit exclusion, taxable years beginning on or after 1 January 2027 (and 2030 for the permanent limit). Both 2026 and 2027 figures are shown.

**Circular 230 disclosure.** This material is general information, not written advice on a specific transaction under 31 CFR 10.37. It is not intended to be used, and cannot be used, to avoid penalties.

## Scope and who this is for

In scope:

- **Holders of a certified credit:** the qualified taxpayer, an individual, partner or shareholder receiving a pass-through share (Personal Income Tax Law: R&TC 17053.95, 17053.98, 17053.98.1), and C corporations, including affiliated corporations that received an assigned credit (Corporation Tax Law: R&TC 23695, 23698, 23698.1).
- **Buyers** of a credit attributable to an independent film, who are "treated as a qualified taxpayer".

The job: how much of a certified credit can be used, carried over, sold or refunded in a year, and how it sits against the business credit limit.

## Ask the client first

- Which credit (2.0, 3.0 or 4.0), which credit certificate, and in which taxable year was the credit allowed or the certificate issued?
- Is the client the qualified taxpayer, an affiliated corporation that received an assignment, a partner or shareholder with a pass-through share, or a buyer?
- Is the credit attributable to an independent film (the only credits that can be sold)?
- For carryovers of 2.0 or 3.0: how much is left, from which year, and does the client or a related entity hold a program 4.0 allocation or certificate in the years it wants to use it?
- For program 4.0: was the refund election made, or will it be made, on the original, timely filed return for the certificate year? In which taxable year was the certificate issued?
- Does the client have other business credits that could push it to the USD 5,000,000 limit?
- Is the taxpayer in a combined report (the limit applies to all members together)?

### Steps

1. **Identify the credit and the holder.** Credit 2.0, 3.0 or 4.0; certificate year; whether the holder is the qualified taxpayer, an assignee, a pass-through owner or a buyer. A buyer cannot elect the program 4.0 refund (R&TC 17053.98.1(k)(7)), and "A credit shall not be sold ... to more than one taxpayer, nor may the credit be resold".
2. **Credits 2.0 and 3.0, carryover years.** Count the taxable years after the year the credit was allowed. Years 1 to 9: use against tax as normal. Years 10 to 15: use only in a year when one of these is an active participant: for personal income tax, "the qualified taxpayer, an entity directly or indirectly owned by the qualified taxpayer, or an entity directly or indirectly owned by an entity that directly or indirectly owns the qualified taxpayer" (R&TC 17053.95(c)(2)(B)(i), 17053.98(c)(2)(B)(i)); for corporation tax, "the qualified taxpayer, an affiliated corporation of the qualified taxpayer, an entity directly or indirectly owned by the qualified taxpayer or an affiliated corporation of the qualified taxpayer, or an entity directly or indirectly owned by an entity that directly or indirectly owns the qualified taxpayer" (R&TC 23695(c)(11)(B)(i), 23698(c)(11)(B)(i)). The last limb reaches sister entities under common ownership. An active participant is one that "has an outstanding credit allocation under Section 17053.98.1 or 23698.1 for which a credit certificate has not yet been issued, or has been issued a credit certificate under Section 17053.98.1 or 23698.1 for the taxable year". A year that fails the test does not end the carryover and "shall not extend that carryover period". After the 15th year the credit expires, unless the period was lengthened for years the business credit limit disallowed it (R&TC 17039.4(i), 23036.4(i)); how that extension combines with the 15-year rule is not confirmed.
3. **Revived carryovers.** A 2.0 or 3.0 carryover that ran out under the old nine-year rule is brought back by the words "including a credit carryover for which the carryover period had expired" (R&TC 17053.95(c)(2)(D) and the parallel paragraphs). FTB: "including the ones for which the original credit carryover period expired" (FTB analysis, p. 2).
4. **Selling an independent film credit.** Only a credit "attributable to an independent film" may be sold, to an unrelated party, reported to FTB before the sale (R&TC 17053.95(c)(1), 17053.98(c)(1), 17053.98.1(c)(1); 23695(c)(3), 23698(c)(3), 23698.1(c)(2)). FTB's form is FTB 3551. FTB's 2025 FTB 3541 instructions say the seller reports gain "in the amount of the sale price", and a buyer that paid less than the credit amount reports income for "the difference between the credit amount claimed on its return and the purchase price".
5. **The limit, for buyers and sellers.** In a taxable year beginning in 2024, 2025 or 2026, a purchased credit counts toward the USD 5,000,000 limit like any other business credit. From taxable years beginning in 2027 it does not (R&TC 17039.4(g), 23036.4(g)). Credits the taxpayer did not sell remain inside the limit.
6. **Program 4.0 refund election.** One-time, irrevocable, on "an original, timely filed return ... for the taxable year that the credit certificate is issued" (R&TC 17053.98.1(k)(6)). Then:
   - First year: the credit is allowed against tax; if the credit exceeds tax, the annual refundable amount is refunded.
   - Total refundable amount = 95% x (credit amount - tax in the first year). Annual refundable amount = 50% of that.
   - Second year: the annual refundable amount is allowed against tax and "the excess, if any, shall be refunded".
   - "[I]f an election is made pursuant to this subdivision, no amount of credit shall be allowed after the refundable period" (R&TC 17053.98.1(k)(4)(C), 23698.1(k)(4)(C)). The 5% not refunded is lost.
   - Refunds are first "credited against other amounts due, if any".
7. **Check the limit for the 4.0 election.** First year beginning in 2026 (see the flash point below): the credit and annual refundable amount count toward the USD 5,000,000 limit, and any refundable amount over the limit is allowed in the first taxable year beginning on or after 1 January 2027 (R&TC 17039.4(k), 23036.4(j)). From 2027: both are outside the limit (R&TC 17039.4(e), 23036.4(f)). If a taxpayer also makes the FTB 3870 election for the same 4.0 credit, the two elections together cannot exceed the credit amount (R&TC 17039.4(l), 23036.4(k)).
8. **Report.** Form FTB 3541 for each certificate (credit codes 237, 239, 248 on the 2025 forms); form FTB 3551 for a sale; Part IV of FTB 3541 for the 4.0 refund election. The 2025 forms predate SB 186: their Part IV still describes the 90% and five-year rule.

**AUDIT FLASH POINT** (program 4.0 elections already running). The amended R&TC 17053.98.1(k) and 23698.1(k) contain no operative-date clause or transition rule. FTB says the changes apply to "taxable years beginning on or after January 1, 2026" (FTB analysis, p. 3). That date appears only in FTB's analysis, in a sentence that also places the extended carryover provisions in "Sections 17039.4, 23036.4, 17039.6 and 23036.6" (the cap sections, not the credit sections where the 15-year rule sits). So the 95% and two-year treatment for certificates issued in taxable years beginning in 2026 rests on FTB's sentence alone. Nothing read says whether an election made for a certificate issued in a 2025 taxable year (refundable period 2025 to 2029 under the 2025 FTB 3541 instructions) moves to the new rule or stays on 90% and five years. For a first year beginning in 2026, first-year credit held back by the USD 5,000,000 limit is "allowed in the first taxable year beginning on or after January 1, 2027" for refundable amounts (R&TC 17039.4(k), 23036.4(j)), but (k)(4)(C) allows no credit after the refundable period; how the two fit is unsettled. Do not recompute a running election, or a 2026 first year at the limit, without confirmation from FTB.

**AUDIT FLASH POINT** (active participant and revived carryovers). The active participant test turns on related-entity ownership ("directly or indirectly owned") and on a program 4.0 allocation or certificate "for the taxable year", which the statute does not further define. Using a revived 2.0 or 3.0 carryover on a return, or on an amended return for a year beginning in 2025, depends on that test and on how FTB applies "credit carryovers beginning on January 1, 2025". Document the ownership chain and the CFC allocation, and refer.

## Worked cases

All three cases are made up for illustration and assume calendar-year C corporations with a valid CFC credit certificate.

**Case 1: program 4.0 refund election, certificate issued in 2027.**

- **Facts:** credit amount USD 10,000,000 on a certificate issued for taxable year 2027. Tax for 2027 before this credit, after other credits, USD 2,000,000; for 2028, USD 500,000. The refund election is made on the original, timely 2027 return.
- **2027:** credit allowed against tax USD 2,000,000. Excess USD 8,000,000. Total refundable amount 95% x 8,000,000 = USD 7,600,000. Annual refundable amount 50% = USD 3,800,000, refunded for 2027.
- **2028:** the USD 3,800,000 annual refundable amount is allowed against the USD 500,000 tax and the USD 3,300,000 excess is refunded.
- **Total:** USD 2,000,000 + 3,800,000 + 3,800,000 = USD 9,600,000 of the USD 10,000,000 credit; USD 400,000 is lost. Both years are outside the USD 5,000,000 limit (R&TC 23036.4(f)).
- **Under the old rule** the same credit would have given 2,000,000 + 5 x 1,440,000 = USD 9,200,000 over 2027 to 2031.

```oa-check
name: Program 4.0 refund election after SB 186, 95 percent over two years
inputs:
  credit_amount: 10000000
  tax_year1: 2000000
  tax_year2: 500000
  new_total_pct: 0.95
  new_annual_pct: 0.50
  old_total_pct: 0.90
  old_annual_pct: 0.20
steps:
  - credit_used_year1: min(credit_amount, tax_year1)
  - excess_year1: credit_amount - credit_used_year1
  - total_refundable: excess_year1 * new_total_pct
  - annual_refundable: round(total_refundable * new_annual_pct, 2)
  - refund_year1: annual_refundable
  - credit_used_year2: min(annual_refundable, tax_year2)
  - refund_year2: annual_refundable - credit_used_year2
  - total_value: credit_used_year1 + refund_year1 + credit_used_year2 + refund_year2
  - amount_lost: credit_amount - total_value
  - old_annual_refundable: excess_year1 * old_total_pct * old_annual_pct
  - old_total_value: credit_used_year1 + 5 * old_annual_refundable
expected:
  credit_used_year1: 2000000
  excess_year1: 8000000
  total_refundable: 7600000
  annual_refundable: 3800000
  refund_year1: 3800000
  credit_used_year2: 500000
  refund_year2: 3300000
  total_value: 9600000
  amount_lost: 400000
  old_annual_refundable: 1440000
  old_total_value: 9200000
```

**Case 2: a buyer of an independent film credit in 2027.**

- **Facts:** an unrelated corporation buys a USD 3,000,000 program 3.0 credit attributable to an independent film for USD 2,700,000 and claims it for taxable year 2027. Its 2027 tax before credits is USD 9,000,000, and it has USD 7,000,000 of California research credit available. It is not in a combined report.
- **Limit:** the research credit is limited to USD 5,000,000. The purchased credit is outside the limit from 2027 (R&TC 23036.4(g)), so all USD 3,000,000 is allowed. Total credits USD 8,000,000; tax after credits USD 1,000,000; USD 2,000,000 of research credit carries over.
- **Income:** the buyer reports USD 3,000,000 - 2,700,000 = USD 300,000 (FTB 3541 instructions, 2025).
- **Same facts in 2026:** all business credits together stop at USD 5,000,000.

```oa-check
name: Purchased independent film credit outside the 5 million limit from 2027
inputs:
  tax_before_credits: 9000000
  research_credit: 7000000
  purchased_credit: 3000000
  purchase_price: 2700000
  limit: 5000000
steps:
  - research_allowed: min(research_credit, limit)
  - purchased_allowed: min(purchased_credit, tax_before_credits - research_allowed)
  - tax_after_credits: tax_before_credits - research_allowed - purchased_allowed
  - research_carryover: research_credit - research_allowed
  - buyer_income: purchased_credit - purchase_price
  - credits_allowed_2026_rule: min(research_credit + purchased_credit, limit)
expected:
  research_allowed: 5000000
  purchased_allowed: 3000000
  tax_after_credits: 1000000
  research_carryover: 2000000
  buyer_income: 300000
  credits_allowed_2026_rule: 5000000
```

**Case 3: a 2.0 credit carried into its 10th to 15th years.**

- **Facts:** a corporation was allowed a 2.0 credit (R&TC 23695) in taxable year 2017. USD 1,200,000 remains unused at the start of 2027. The corporation itself holds a program 4.0 allocation not yet certified, or a 4.0 certificate, for 2027 and for 2029, but neither for 2028, and no related entity holds one for 2028. The credit was never held back by the business credit limit. Tax available for the credit: USD 900,000 in 2027 and USD 1,000,000 in 2029.
- **Window:** the 10th to 15th taxable years after 2017 are 2027 to 2032 (under the old nine-year rule the carryover would have ended with 2026).
- **Use:** 2027 USD 900,000 (USD 300,000 left); 2028 nothing, not an active participant; 2029 USD 300,000; nothing left. The last year it could have been used was 2032, as no limit extension applies here.

## When to refuse or refer

- **No CFC credit certificate, or eligibility questions.** Refer to the California Film Commission.
- **A program 4.0 election already running from a 2025 certificate.** Refer until FTB says which schedule applies.
- **Active participant through related entities, and revived carryovers.** Refer with the ownership chain and CFC records.
- **Combined reports, assignments among affiliated corporations, and the FTB 3870 election.** Refer.
- **Pricing a credit sale or the buyer's tax treatment beyond the FTB 3541 statements.** Refer.
- **S corporations, the sales and use tax election, credit 1.0, the soundstage credit, the AB 2319 post-production credit.** Refer.

## Filing and payment

- **Credit claims:** form FTB 3541 for each certificate, with the credit code for the program (2025 forms: 237, 239, 248). FTB's instructions ask taxpayers attaching FTB 3541 to write "CFC Credit" at the top of the return.
- **Sales:** form FTB 3551, Sale of Credit Attributable to an Independent Film, with the report to FTB made before the sale.
- **Program 4.0 refund election:** Part IV of form FTB 3541 on the original, timely filed return for the taxable year the certificate is issued. FTB had not published forms reflecting SB 186 in the sources read.
- **Records:** CFC certificates, allocation letters, ownership charts for the active participant test, sale reports and purchase agreements, and a year-by-year carryover schedule.
