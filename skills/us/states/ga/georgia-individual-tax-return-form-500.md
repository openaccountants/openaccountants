# Georgia Individual Tax Return (Form 500): 2025

Use the **2025** Form 500 and IT-511 instructions. Georgia's 2025 rate is **5.19%**; the ordinary calendar-year filing and payment deadline was **15 April 2026**. This Guide covers full-year residents and the Schedule 3 route for part-year residents and nonresidents. It does not compute the held outcomes listed under **When to refuse or refer**.

## Scope

Full-year Georgia residents are taxed on income from all sources except exempt income; temporary absence does not itself end Georgia legal residence. Apply the filing triggers above. For a withholding or estimated-payment refund below those triggers, prepare the Form 500 overpayment calculation; do not assume every payment is refundable or promise a refund without the return and any applicable claim-limit/setoff review. [IT-511, pp.13,18–19](https://dor.georgia.gov/document/document/2025-it-511-individual-income-tax-booklet/download).

Part-year residents required to file federally must file in Georgia. Nonresidents required to file federally with Georgia work or source income generally file **Form 500 with Schedule 3**. A narrow exception covers a legal resident of another state whose only Georgia financial activity is employee services and whose Georgia service wages do not exceed the **lesser of 5% of service income everywhere or $5,000**. It is not a general exclusion for every nonresident's first wages. Prior-service deferred compensation, stock options and federally protected retirement income need the separate review below. [IT-511, p.13](https://dor.georgia.gov/document/document/2025-it-511-individual-income-tax-booklet/download).

## Ask the client first

- Completed federal return for **2025**, its schedules and any amendments; prior Georgia return and DOR notices.
- Domicile, move dates, actual work locations/days and income sources; both spouses' residency and filing status.
- W-2s, 1099s and applicable Georgia withholding statements; payment confirmations, estimated installments and prior-year overpayment applied.
- Each spouse's age, retirement income ownership/type and any disability evidence; distinguish taxable benefits from Social Security and Railroad Retirement.
- Municipal bond issuers and expenses, Path2College contributions/beneficiaries and withdrawals, HSA records, military pay/retirement and federal/state adjustment records.
- Dependents, Schedule A and spouse's itemizing choice; low-income-credit facts, pass-through statements, purchased/film credits and certificates.
- Extension confirmation and payment history; filing acceptance, payment authorization and any deadline-relief notice.

## The method, step by step

1. **Use the matching year.** Complete the federal 2025 return and reconcile its inputs before the state computation. Open the software's **2025 Form 500**, with its state updates applied. Form 500EZ is discontinued for tax years beginning in 2025. Do not import later-year deductions or rates into this return. [IT-511, pp.9,16](https://dor.georgia.gov/document/document/2025-it-511-individual-income-tax-booklet/download).
2. **Set filing and residency status.** Follow the 2025 instructions: line 4 code **1** full-year; **2** part-year, including one spouse a Georgia resident and the other part-year/nonresident; **3** nonresident for both spouses. Use the federal filing status subject to the instructions' mixed-residency spouse exception. Do not follow the conflicting undated FAQ's mixed-spouse code in place of this year's form. Stop disputed domicile, source and separate/joint election cases for review. [IT-511, pp.15–16](https://dor.georgia.gov/document/document/2025-it-511-individual-income-tax-booklet/download).
3. **Reconcile the starting income.** Line **8** is federal **adjusted gross income**, not federal taxable income. For full-year residents, Schedule **1** net adjustments go to line **9**; line **10** is lines 8 plus 9. Part-year/nonresident filers skip lines **9–14** and follow Schedule **3**. [IT-511, p.16](https://dor.georgia.gov/document/document/2025-it-511-individual-income-tax-booklet/download).
4. **Apply the supported Georgia adjustments.** Add federally excluded interest/dividends on other states' municipal obligations, with the related-expense adjustment in the instructions. Subtract Social Security and Railroad Retirement benefits only to the extent included in federal AGI. Georgia-exempt municipal interest already absent from federal AGI is not subtracted again; federally taxable Georgia bonds need the specific statutory exemption. Path2College contributions are deductible up to the actual amount contributed, capped at **$4,000 per beneficiary**, or **$8,000 married filing jointly**; other states' plans are not this deduction. Follow §48-7-27(a)(11.1)'s federal IRA contribution-deadline rule for after-year contributions. Actual nonqualified withdrawals/rollovers, HSA and other conformity adjustments are held below. [§48-7-27(a)(11.1), (b)(1),(5)](https://www.legis.ga.gov/api/document/docs/default-source/joint-features-document-library/t48-ch7-8-(v37)-pdf.pdf); [IT-511, pp.20–23](https://dor.georgia.gov/document/document/2025-it-511-individual-income-tax-booklet/download).
5. **Compute retirement exclusions separately for each spouse.** Use the ceilings above against eligible taxable retirement income and no more than **$5,000 earned income** within the ceiling. Allocate jointly owned income **50/50**. Do not include Social Security, Railroad Retirement, exempt interest, lottery/gambling or illegal-source income in this calculation. Qualifying rental/royalty/partnership income subject to FICA/self-employment tax and materially participated S-corporation business income belong in the earned-income bucket. Part-year/nonresident earned and unearned exclusions use **separate Georgia-source-to-total-income ratios for their respective buckets**, not one overall ratio or an automatic months fraction. Follow the worksheet rather than transferring one spouse's unused limit. For military retirees **under 62**, the separate exclusion is up to **$17,500**, plus up to another **$17,500** only when Georgia earned income **exceeds $17,500**; each spouse must qualify. [§48-7-27(a)(5),(5.1)](https://www.legis.ga.gov/api/document/docs/default-source/joint-features-document-library/t48-ch7-8-(v37)-pdf.pdf); [IT-511, pp.21–22,24](https://dor.georgia.gov/document/document/2025-it-511-individual-income-tax-booklet/download).
6. **Deduct and allocate.** Full-year standard deduction is line **11**; itemized deductions are lines **12a–c** (federal Schedule A less Georgia adjustments, including disallowed other-state income taxes and exempt-income investment interest). Line **13** subtracts the deduction; line **14** multiplies qualifying dependents on line 7c by **$4,000**. There is no taxpayer/spouse dependent exemption. Georgia law separately adds back IRC §164 deductions above **$10,000**, or **$5,000 married filing separately**: do not blindly carry a higher federal SALT allowance into Georgia. Actual overlapping SALT/addback/conformity computations remain held below. [§48-7-27(b)(3)](https://www.legis.ga.gov/api/document/docs/default-source/joint-features-document-library/t48-ch7-8-(v37)-pdf.pdf); [IT-511, pp.16–17](https://dor.georgia.gov/document/document/2025-it-511-individual-income-tax-booklet/download).
7. **For Schedule 3, allocate income before deductions.** Column A is income as if resident all year, B is non-Georgia, C is Georgia; allocate federal and Georgia adjustments to reach line **8**. Ordinary income ratio line **9** is C8/A8, limited to **0%–100%**: C8 nonpositive gives 0%; A8 nonpositive gives 100%; if both are nonpositive, use 100%. Add the applicable deduction (line **10**) and dependent exemptions (line **11**) on line **12**; multiply by line 9 on line **13**; subtract from C8 on line **14**, carrying to Form 500 line **15a**. Part-year residents also have a **days-in-Georgia/days-in-year** time-ratio option for standard deduction/exemptions, and an actual Georgia itemized-deductions option for deductions paid while resident. DOR limits those alternative examples to software filings; actual-itemized line 13 differs from the ordinary formula. The example below uses only the ordinary positive-income ratio. Alternative-option and difficult allocation outcomes are held for review. [IT-511, pp.26–28](https://dor.georgia.gov/document/document/2025-it-511-individual-income-tax-booklet/download); [DOR alternative methods](https://dor.georgia.gov/individual-part-year-residents-time-ratio-and-georgia-itemized-deductions).
8. **Tax and credits.** Line **15a** is income before Georgia NOL; line **15b** requires a separately supported Georgia NOL computation, not an automatic federal carryover; line **15c** is the difference. Multiply by **5.19%**, rounded to the nearest dollar, for line **16**. Low-income credit line **17** requires federal AGI **below $20,000**, no eligibility as another taxpayer's dependent, and the other conditions below; use the actual worksheet, not the dependent exemption count. Other-state credit is line **18**; other qualified credits flow through lines **19–21**, including IND-CR/Schedule 2 as appropriate. Total nonrefundable credits on line **22** cannot exceed line 16; line **23** is the nonnegative difference. A pass-through entity tax deduction is not automatically an individual tax credit. Actual special credit claims are held below. [IT-511, pp.17–18,25,34–36](https://dor.georgia.gov/document/document/2025-it-511-individual-income-tax-booklet/download).
9. **Reconcile payments and the result.** Only Georgia W-2/1099 withholding goes on line **24**; applicable G2 withholding goes on line **25**. Line **26** includes estimated payments, prior-return credits and electronic/IT-560 payments. Supported refundable credits are line **27**; add payments on line **28**. Line **29** is tax on line 23 minus payments if positive; line **30** is the reverse overpayment. The final line **45** amount due includes applicable donations, estimated penalty, late penalties and interest; line **46** refund subtracts next-year applied overpayment and applicable amounts on lines **31–44**. Review the year-on-year change and payment records before treating an overpayment as cash available. [IT-511, pp.18–19](https://dor.georgia.gov/document/document/2025-it-511-individual-income-tax-booklet/download).
10. **File, retain acceptance and pay.** Review the return and obtain the client's filing/payment authorization. E-file through the supported provider and retain actual acceptance, not just a transmission record. Paper filers follow the 2025 Form 500 instructions and address. For e-filed returns, DOR says to retain W-2/W-2G/1099-R and supporting documents rather than mailing a duplicate paper package; transmit required data through software. Paper withholding claims need the specified statements. Pay separately through an allowed DOR payment route or the **525-TV** voucher when paying by check, and retain the confirmation. A payment through GTC does not file Form 500. [DOR e-file FAQ](https://dor.georgia.gov/individual-electronic-filing-faq); [IT-511, pp.3–4,18–19](https://dor.georgia.gov/document/document/2025-it-511-individual-income-tax-booklet/download).

## Worked cases

These are hypothetical ordinary **2025** cases, with no special adjustments, NOL, credits, penalty, interest, donations or next-year overpayment election. They are not additional client facts.

### Full-year resident: single, one qualifying dependent

Federal AGI **$60,000**, Georgia standard deduction **$12,000**, dependent exemption **$4,000** and Georgia withholding **$2,800** give taxable income **$44,000**, tax **$2,284** after rounding, and overpayment **$516**. Rules: [IT-511, pp.16–19](https://dor.georgia.gov/document/document/2025-it-511-individual-income-tax-booklet/download).

```oa-check
name: Georgia 2025 full-year single ordinary Form 500
inputs:
  agi: 60000
  standard: 12000
  dependents: 1
  exemption: 4000
  withholding: 2800
steps:
  taxable: max(0, agi - standard - dependents * exemption)
  tax: round(taxable * 0.0519)
  overpayment: max(0, withholding - tax)
expected:
  taxable: 44000
  tax: 2284
  overpayment: 516
```

### Part-year joint return: ordinary income-ratio method

Assume the joint filing/residency status is established, Schedule 3 A8 **$100,000**, C8 **$40,000**, two qualifying dependents, standard deduction **$24,000** and Georgia withholding **$1,500**. The selected **40%** income-ratio method allows **$12,800** combined deduction/exemptions, leaving **$27,200** taxable, tax **$1,412** and overpayment **$88**. This does not decide whether an alternative method is preferable. Rules: [IT-511, pp.17–19,26–28](https://dor.georgia.gov/document/document/2025-it-511-individual-income-tax-booklet/download).

```oa-check
name: Georgia 2025 part-year joint ordinary income ratio
inputs:
  all_income: 100000
  georgia_income: 40000
  standard: 24000
  dependents: 2
  exemption: 4000
  withholding: 1500
steps:
  ratio: georgia_income / all_income
  allocated_deductions: (standard + dependents * exemption) * ratio
  taxable: max(0, georgia_income - allocated_deductions)
  tax: round(taxable * 0.0519)
  overpayment: max(0, withholding - tax)
expected:
  ratio: 0.4
  allocated_deductions: 12800
  taxable: 27200
  tax: 1412
  overpayment: 88
```

## The traps

- Keep the **2025** tax year. HB463's later changes apply to taxable years beginning on or after **1 January 2026**; they do not change these 2025 figures. [HB463 §5-1](https://gov.georgia.gov/document/2026-signed-legislation/hb-463/download).
- Reconcile remote-worker/mover withholding to actual source and residency facts. A W-2 state code does not establish Georgia source or entitlement to an other-state credit. Physical work-day sourcing has exceptions; stop disputed allocations. [Rule 560-7-8-.01](https://rules.sos.ga.gov/gac/560-7-8).
- Retirement exclusions are per person and taxable-income category. Do not subtract benefits absent from federal AGI twice or use the overall Schedule 3 ratio for both retirement buckets.
- Keep federal/state itemizing, Georgia SALT addbacks and other-state tax credits separate. Credits cannot repair an unproved deduction or sourcing assumption.
- A purchased film/transferable credit requires its own eligibility, transfer, certificate and claim-year work. A generic K-1 or purchase receipt is not a usable eligibility conclusion here.
- A valid extension protects the filing date, not the original payment date. When filing and payment penalties overlap, apply the concurrent-penalty reduction above.

## When to refuse or refer

The following outcomes are **not usable from this Guide**. Keep the client's original amounts, year and records; do not enter a guessed deduction, allocation, credit or deadline. **Check this point with one of our partner accountants.**

- Disputed domicile, mixed-spouse filing elections, remote-work sourcing exceptions, prior-service deferred compensation/stock options, federal protection of nonresident retirement income, negative/complex allocation, and choice/application of Schedule 3 software-only alternative methods.
- Actual HSA/federal-conformity adjustments, overlapping 2025 federal SALT/Georgia addbacks, nonqualified Path2College withdrawal/rollover recapture, unusual retirement losses/disability evidence, and military pay/residency/relief outcomes beyond the stated retirement ceilings. HSA contributions and high-deductible insurance premiums are different items; this Guide gives no automatic HSA subtraction.
- Actual low-income-credit amount: the worksheet has its own person-count/bands. Eligibility also requires part-year claimants to be Georgia residents at year-end; separate filers cannot exceed the credit a joint return would allow; inmates are excluded; claim by the end of the twelfth month after year-end (**31 December 2026** for calendar 2025); credit is limited to tax liability. These eligibility rules do not supply the held worksheet result. [IT-511, pp.17–18,35](https://dor.georgia.gov/document/document/2025-it-511-individual-income-tax-booklet/download).
- Actual other-state, IND-CR, itemizer, pass-through/PTE, purchased film or other transferable/refundable credit eligibility, amounts, carryovers and attachments; entity-level tax may instead require an income adjustment. [IT-511, pp.18,22,25,34–36](https://dor.georgia.gov/document/document/2025-it-511-individual-income-tax-booklet/download).
- Georgia NOL amount; special estimated-payment methods/safe harbors or **500-UET** penalty; actual late-tax payoff, reasonable-cause relief, special disaster/combat extensions, fiscal exceptions, amended/old refund claims and setoffs. The ordinary dates and penalty framework above do not resolve these outcomes.
