## Who this is for

C-corporations incorporated in Georgia, or foreign (out-of-state) corporations registered or doing business in Georgia.
Preparers handling a first-year Georgia filing or picking up an existing client.
Partnerships and LLCs taxed as pass-throughs use their applicable return. Do not categorically exclude a federal S corporation: required nonresident-shareholder Form 600S-CA consents affect Georgia S recognition. The failure described in that filing paragraph negates Georgia S recognition and requires Form 600 and regular corporate tax. Apply the actual 2025 recognition conditions in the [IT611 filing paragraph](https://dor.georgia.gov/document/document/2025-it611-corporate-income-tax-instruction-booklet/download), read 2026-11-22, with the missing exact quotation recovered from the same filing paragraph on 2026-11-25 (printed page 8; this is not a new whole-booklet review).

## Before you start, ask the client

Federal return (Form 1120) — is it final? Georgia typically starts from federal taxable income (confirm the starting line before filing).
Is the company doing business in other states? Apportionment method and factor(s) — confirm before filing.
Georgia-only activity, property, payroll, and receipts figures for apportionment.
Any prior-year Georgia returns, carryforwards (NOL, credits), and estimated payments made this year.
Registration status with the state tax authority and the Secretary of State (concept — confirm requirements).
Balance sheet as of year-end, if a net worth tax component applies (concept — confirm before filing).
Any Georgia-specific credits claimed or planned (jobs, investment, etc. — confirm availability and forms).

## The method, step by step

Open the finalized federal Form 1120 and confirm it is the version being filed — Georgia builds from it.
Check the client's registration and account standing on the state tax authority's portal (portal name — citation to confirm).
Start from federal taxable income and apply Georgia additions and subtractions (specific adjustments — confirm before filing).
Compute apportionment if the corporation operates in multiple states (method and factors — confirm before filing).
Apply the corporate income tax rate to Georgia taxable income (rate — confirm before filing).
Compute the net worth tax component from the year-end balance sheet, if applicable (existence, base, and schedule — confirm before filing).
Subtract estimated payments, prior-year overpayments applied, and any credits (credit forms and ordering — confirm before filing).
Check whether e-filing is mandatory or optional for this client (confirm before filing).
Review the return against the prior year for consistency; reconcile any large swings.
File by the due date, pay any balance due through the approved payment channel, and calendar next year's estimated payment dates (all dates — confirm before filing).

## The traps

Assuming the federal and Georgia treatment of every item matches — state addback/subtraction rules differ (confirm the current list).
Missing the net worth tax component, which may be due even in a loss year (concept — confirm before filing).
Wrong apportionment method or stale factor weighting from an old year's software rollover.
Extension of time to file may not extend time to pay (confirm before filing).
Estimated payment requirements and underpayment penalties for larger corporations (thresholds — confirm before filing).
First-year and short-year returns: due dates and net worth computation may work differently (confirm before filing).
Filing under the wrong entity registration or FEIN mismatch with the state account.
