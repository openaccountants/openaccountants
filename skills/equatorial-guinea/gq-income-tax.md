# Equatorial Guinea Personal Income Tax v0.2

> **General reference only.** This skill is general tax/accounting reference material for AI-assisted workflows. It has not been reviewed for any specific person's facts, documents, elections, deadlines, residency, filing status, or local procedures. Do not rely on it to file, pay, amend, or take a tax position without review by a qualified professional in the relevant jurisdiction.

## Who this applies to

Individuals with income taxed in Equatorial Guinea in tax year 2026, the calendar year. There is no single personal income tax: Ley 1/2024 splits it into the taxes below, and the annual return (DGRA) brings them together (art 29).

A person is tax resident if any one applies (art 126.2): a permanent home as owner, usufructuary or tenant; the centre of vital interests in the country; at least 183 days present, continuous or not, in any 12-month period; or a State employee living abroad. A resident is taxed on salary even when the work is done abroad or the payer is abroad (art 126.1.a). A non-resident is taxed where the work is done in the country or the payer is established there (art 126.1.b).

## The base

- Salary: net pay plus benefits (art 128). A director's benefits are valued at real value or as a share of gross pay: housing 15%, domestic service 5%, water and electricity 5%, car 10%, food 15% up to 300,000 XAF a month (art 128.2).
- Deduct from salary the employee's INSESO and Work Protection Fund contributions, and pension contributions for resident expatriates up to 50% of the employee's social security contribution (art 129).
- Exempt from salary tax: art 124, including expense reimbursements meeting art 52, redundancy pay under a dismissal plan, accident compensation, State family and social allowances, and death or widow benefits.
- Exceptional pay (retirement indemnity, pay on termination, annual profit-share bonuses) follows art 131: average the last 12 months of pay and work out a reference tax on it.
- IBN: profit as for companies (art 84), with straight-line depreciation only, no bad debt provisions and losses carried 3 years (art 84.2). Owner's pay, personal costs and rent paid to oneself are not deductible (art 85). Mixed-use costs that cannot be split are 50% deductible, and representation costs 50% (art 85.3, 85.4).
- DGRA deductions (art 29.2): home loan interest on a main home in the country, court-ordered maintenance, pension and social payments, mutual fund payments, professional association fees, school fees for up to 10 children under 25.
- Income at or below the annual minimum wage is exempt, with amounts paid on account set off or refunded (art 29.6).
- Tax already paid on each income type is credited in the DGRA (art 29.4).

| IRCM withholding | 15th of the following month (art 107.2) | same |
| DGRA and payment | 30 April of the following year; refunds or set-off October to December (art 29.7) | same |

Steps for any self-assessed tax: download the form from the https://minhacienda-gob.com/formularios-de-declaraciones-fiscales page, complete it, pay at one of the Treasury's banks shown on the form (BANGE, BGFI, CCEIBANK, SGBGE, ECOBANK), then lodge the form and bank proof at the Ministry. The Ministry and the Treasury then verify. This route is the Minister's own description at https://www.guineaecuatorialpress.com/noticias/presentacion_oficial_de_la_nueva_ley_general_tributaria_de_guinea_ecuatorial. The tax administration designs the DGRA form (art 29.8); it is not on the forms page.

## Penalties and interest

Late filing or payment carries a surcharge of 5% if regularised within 15 days, 10% beyond one month without a moratorium request, 15% after a formal demand (art 410). Interest runs at 2.5% a month or part of a month (art 412). Under-declaration: 10%, rising to 15%, and 25% for fraud (art 411.A). Assessment rights lapse after 4 years (art 338).

## Cross-border points

Non-residents pay 15% on capital income and salary. Foreign capital income of a resident is declared, with foreign tax offset where a double tax treaty exists (arts 104.10, 109). A non-resident also pays IRCM on gains from shares in a company registered here if held for the previous 183 days, or holding at least 25% (art 103.2). See [Cross-border](gq-cross-border.md).

## Two worked examples that recompute

**Resident employee, net taxable income 6,000,000 XAF.** Tax at the top of the 10% band is 360,000. The 1,000,000 above 5,000,000 at 15% adds 150,000. Tax is 510,000.

```oa-check
name: Resident salary 6,000,000
inputs:
  net: 6000000
steps:
  - band_15: (net - 5000000) * 0.15
  - tax: 360000 + band_15
expected:
  tax: 510000
```

**Decision: IBN or REAM for a trader in a regional capital, turnover 20,000,000 and profit 8,000,000.** IBN is 25% of profit, 2,000,000, against a minimum of 300,000. REAM at 5% of turnover is 1,000,000 and final (art 95). REAM saves 1,000,000, but the customer cannot be charged VAT and input VAT is lost (art 90.3). Opting for IBN is irrevocable (art 91.2). Check the Patente category before comparing.

```oa-check
name: IBN against REAM
inputs:
  turnover: 20000000
  profit: 8000000
steps:
  - ibn: max(profit * 0.25, turnover * 0.015)
  - ream: turnover * 0.05
  - saving: ibn - ream
expected:
  ibn: 2000000
  ream: 1000000
  saving: 1000000
```

## Ask the client first

1. Residence: home, centre of interests, days in the country?
2. Employee, self-employed or both?
3. Annual gross income, and where is the business located?
4. Other income: rent, dividends, interest, foreign income?
5. Which year did the activity start?
