## Required coverage and earnings intake

For each fictional illustration, expressly establish all of the following; none is a default for actual work:

1. Selected source text and period, a complete calendar quarter wholly after March 2017, one ordinary worker and one employer, and the legal applicability of the illustrated branch. A registration, employment label, nationality or age alone cannot establish this.
2. Complete contribution coverage under the applicable employee, employer and worker definitions and coverage provisions, including any agreement or Compact qualification. Directors, contractors, taxi drivers, self-employment, off-island work, benefit recipients, concurrent status and special exemptions remain held.
3. Complete positive pre-ceiling earnings G for the quarter, in USD with an independently established whole-cent amount. G is earnings after the legally established earnings exclusions but before this ceiling; it is not necessarily gross cash salary, take-home pay or income-tax wages.
4. Each paid earnings component, its quarter, inclusion/exclusion and any non-cash value. L1 section 103(r) includes compensation in cash or another form and has qualified exclusions. Normal sick-leave wages remain included. Housing, travel/per diem, medical, trust/annuity, scholarship, religious, domestic and casual-service exclusions require their stated conditions. Do not copy an income-tax or Health Fund exclusion into this base.
5. The selected US$10,000 quarterly maximum for current contributors applies, no relevant Board increase applies and no separate floor, relief, special rate or adjustment changes the illustrated ordinary branch. This stipulated fixture premise is not a finding that no such instrument or floor exists.
6. All payments/components for the same worker and quarter are accounted for. Multiple employers, split or cross-quarter allocations, first-quarter 2017, correction/refund and special-status branches are outside this calculation. The Fund's fiscal year ending 30 September is not a calendar-quarter reset rule.

L1's nearest-cent provision concerns earnings. It does not by itself establish a contribution rounding rule or an earnings midpoint rule. G must therefore already be independently established; this guide does not round the earnings input to create eligibility for an example.

## Selected figures and exact-cent illustration

All figures in this table refer to the selected 2017 amended text, subject to the premises above.

| Item | Selected rule | Primary source |
|---|---|---|
| Covered-earnings unit | Worker earnings during the quarter, subject to the applicable maximum | L1 section 102(1), replacing section 103(k), physical pages 5–6 |
| Selected quarterly maximum | US$10,000 for current contributors after 6 March 2017; Board increases remain possible | L1 section 103(k), physical pages 5–6; L2 section 147 |
| Worker share W | 8% of covered earnings | L1 notice physical page 1 and Act physical page 8; L2 section 129 |
| Employer share E | Separate 8% of the worker's covered earnings, from employer funds | L1 notice physical page 2 and Act physical page 8; L2 section 130 |
| Contribution-product precision | Not established by this intake | L1 section 103(r) establishes earnings precision only; actual rounding remains held |

For positive canonical whole-cent USD fixtures, let g be G in cents. Use exact integer arithmetic; no floating-point or intermediate rounding is necessary:

~~~text
b = min(g, 1000000)       selected covered-earnings cents
if (8 * b) mod 100 != 0:
    Held                 contribution rounding is not established
w = (8 * b) / 100         exact worker-share cents
e = (8 * b) / 100         exact employer-share cents
t = w + e                conditional sum of the two shares
~~~

Divide cents by 100 for dollar display only. The test is on the capped base b, not necessarily on pre-ceiling g: a fractional-cent product below the ceiling remains held, whereas an independently qualified G above the selected ceiling can give the exact capped product. Divisibility of b by 25 is a mathematical fixture boundary, not a statutory restriction on permissible earnings. A non-integral product is Held, not legally invalid or rounded by an invented rule. Even an integral-cent product does not prove the statutory rounding unit, aggregation order or remittance precision. The social contributions base's unknown-rounding Needs Input gate therefore remains applicable to every actual result. Zero, negative, reversal, correction, foreign-currency and finer-than-cent inputs remain held rather than coerced.

Calculate W and E separately even though these selected rates match. T is their conditional sum, not a combined programme rate, a net wage or proof of an amount actually withheld, remitted or deductible for income tax. Under L2 sections 129(2)–(3), the employer collects the worker contribution by deduction, remits it with its own contribution and is liable for payment of the worker contribution; section 130 requires its own share from its own funds. Actual deduction authority, timing, recovery of a missed deduction and employer funding/cost need complete applicable law and evidence. An unmade deduction or missing payment does not create a contribution credit.

N5 uses 100025 cents as G and B. Multiplying by 8 gives 800200, then dividing by 100 gives exactly 8002 cents for each share, or 80.02. N7 is below the selected ceiling and gives 799.98 per share. N8 is exactly at the ceiling. N9 exceeds it by 0.01, so B remains 10000.00 and each share remains 800.00; the precision gate applies after that ceiling.

These aggregation examples also require independently established scope, coverage, law and complete allocation for each unit:

- **A1, one quarter with several payments:** One worker's 5000.00, 4000.00 and 3000.00 included earnings all belong to the same complete quarter. G is 12000.00, B is 10000.00, W is 800.00, E is 800.00 and T is 1600.00. Resetting the cap for each payment would instead give W = 960.00 and E = 960.00, which does not implement the stipulated quarterly base. No per-payslip deduction or allocation is calculated here.
- **A2, separate complete quarters:** One worker has independently qualified G = 12000.00 in each of 2 separate complete quarters. Each has B = 10000.00, W = 800.00, E = 800.00 and T = 1600.00. Their sums are B = 20000.00, W = 1600.00, E = 1600.00 and T = 3200.00. Do not impose one pooled quarterly ceiling on both quarters or infer an annual ceiling from this illustration.
- **A3, separate workers:** Two independently qualified ordinary workers each have G = 6000.00 in the same complete quarter with the same employer. Each has B = 6000.00, W = 480.00, E = 480.00 and T = 960.00. The sums are B = 12000.00, W = 960.00, E = 960.00 and T = 1920.00. The worker-based ceiling is not one ceiling for the employer's entire payroll. This does not decide one worker's multiple-employer treatment.

## Dated procedure and distinct programme holds

The following L2 provisions are selected statutory locators, not a qualified historical or current operating procedure:

- Section 131 requires both the report and contribution payment no later than the tenth day after each quarter. It is not a monthly deadline. The actual calendar, holiday treatment, form, channel and any extension remain held; no actual due date or penalty is calculated.
- Section 133 provides for assigned employer and worker account numbers/cards. Current registration requirements, approved identifiers and return/payment references still need evidence. Keep actual identifiers outside agent examples.
- Section 132 concerns excess worker contributions actually withheld and paid, over the 4 quarters ending 31 December, including multiple employers. It specifies refund within 90 days after that date and no refund below US$5. Employer refunds are restricted to correcting an error. This does not authorise payroll-time cap sharing, a new payroll credit or employer netting; no refund is calculated here.
- L3 has separate Health Fund rules. Its section 202(h) permits Board changes to the base; sections 216 and 217 are subject to Cabinet action under section 221(5). Section 216(2) requires payment before quarter end, whilst section 218 requires both payment and reporting by the tenth day after quarter end. That payment-timing conflict remains unresolved. Do not import section 132's 90-day wording into Health Fund refunds.
- Retirement Fund earnings, Health Fund benefit eligibility, income-tax wages and deductions are separate questions. No tax deduction, benefit entitlement, accounting expense or employer funding result is established by W, E or T.
