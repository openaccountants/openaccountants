# Mongolia Corporate Income Tax

## Who this applies to

For ordinary corporate income, residence, specified gross-basis income and corporate withholding in tax year 2026. A Mongolia-incorporated entity is resident. A foreign entity can also be resident through management in Mongolia: CIT Law art. 5.4 requires at least 3 (in words on the page) of the following 5 criteria (the count of enumerated criteria is derived): more than 50% of shareholders, including persons exercising shareholder rights/duties through direct or indirect representation, are resident in Mongolia; more than 50% of shareholder meetings in the preceding consecutive 4 years (in words on the page) were held in Mongolia; accounting and financial documents are kept in Mongolia; at least 25% of board members or persons exercising their rights/duties through representation are resident in Mongolia; or at least 60% of total sales are earned in or sourced from Mongolia. Residence is not decided by incorporation alone. Resident companies are taxed on worldwide income; a foreign permanent establishment (PE) on connected Mongolian income; other non-residents on Mongolian-source income (arts. 5, 7). [CIT Law](https://legalinfo.mn/mn/detail?lawId=14407).

**Quick reference:** establish the taxpayer, income category, taxable base, related-party aggregation, eligible small-business regime and treaty position before quoting a rate. The consolidated law displays future amendments. The scale below is for 2026; the replacement scale starts in 2027, not in 2026.

The prohibited sectors include mineral/radioactive-mineral exploration, extraction, use, transport and sale; alcohol and tobacco production/import and the specified tobacco activity; and the listed petroleum, fuel and oil activities. Read the precise activity list, not just the sector name (art. 22.1.1-22.1.3). Aggregate taxable income of related taxpayers transacting with the taxpayer during the year when testing the direct small-business rate and reduction (art. 22.3). Do not stack other art. 22.5 concessions with those regimes (art. 22.4).

## The base

1. Decide residence and whether a foreign recipient's income is attributable to a registered PE. Do not apply gross non-resident withholding to every foreign business automatically.
2. Separate ordinary income under arts. 18.2-18.5 from income with a separate statutory base under art. 18.6 and securities gains under art. 18.13.
3. Reconcile ordinary taxable receipts to allowed costs. Art. 13.1 requires the period, direct income connection, actual expenditure, accounting evidence, required unique-number receipts/customs proof, and payment or an obligation reported by the taxpayer. Restricted costs and interest limitations need their own review. A bank debit alone does not establish a deduction.
4. Apply eligible losses, exemptions and credits only after checking their statutory conditions. Test the small-business limits using taxable income, not turnover. The elected simplified regime instead uses gross income and switches off the specified exemptions, reductions, foreign credits and carried losses (art. 29); VAT registration or prohibited activity terminates it under the statutory rules.
5. Keep separate tax payable, withholding, advances and any annual refund claim. The annual reduction is not permission to omit scheduled payments.

[CIT arts. 5, 7, 13, 18-19, 21-24, 29](https://legalinfo.mn/mn/detail?lawId=14407).

## Penalties and interest

General Tax Law art. 73 provides late-payment interest with the applicable year's rate set separately. Do not invent a daily percentage or a filing fine from this overview. Obtain the assessment, underlying due date, actual payment date and current rate decision before computing arrears. [General Tax Law art. 73](https://legalinfo.mn/mn/detail?lawId=14403).

## Cross-border points

Treaty entitlement, beneficial recipient, payment character, source and PE connection come before a treaty rate. The ordinary domestic non-resident rate has the specific bank-instrument and software-payment exceptions above. A stabilisation certificate may preserve its statutory covered rates (art. 20.3). This skill does not determine a particular treaty rate, transfer-pricing adjustment or mining licence tax. Refer those facts for a dedicated analysis. [CIT arts. 2, 5, 18, 20, 24](https://legalinfo.mn/mn/detail?lawId=14407).

## Two worked examples that recompute

### Ordinary company above the upper threshold

Assume MNT 6,500,000,000 of established ordinary taxable income in 2026, no reduction, exemption or credit. The excess is MNT 500,000,000. Tax is MNT 600,000,000 + MNT 125,000,000 = **MNT 725,000,000**, derived from the pre-2027 scale above.

```oa-check
name: Ordinary corporate profit above threshold
inputs:
  taxable: 6500000000
  threshold: 6000000000
  fixed_tax: 600000000
  excess_rate: 25
steps:
  - excess: taxable - threshold
  - tax: fixed_tax + excess * excess_rate / 100
expected:
  excess: 500000000
  tax: 725000000
```

### Taxable-income small-business test

Assume an eligible ordinary business with receipts of MNT 500,000,000 and fully allowable costs of MNT 300,000,000; no other adjustments, losses or related-taxpayer income. Taxable income is MNT 200,000,000. Although receipts exceed the limit, taxable income qualifies: MNT 200,000,000 × 1% = **MNT 2,000,000**. All amounts are illustrative inputs or derived; the sector and aggregation tests remain mandatory.

```oa-check
name: Eligible small-business taxable income differs from revenue
inputs:
  receipts: 500000000
  allowable_costs: 300000000
  rate: 1
steps:
  - taxable: receipts - allowable_costs
  - tax: taxable * rate / 100
expected:
  taxable: 200000000
  tax: 2000000
```

## Ask the client first

Obtain incorporation and management facts, accounting period, previous-year taxable income, current receipts and deductible-cost evidence, related-party transactions, licences/activities, elected regime and VAT status, separately taxed income, foreign recipients and treaty documents, withholding records, advances and the authority's filing schedule. Ask for stabilisation certificates and existing tax notices where relevant.
