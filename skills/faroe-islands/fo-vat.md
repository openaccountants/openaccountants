# Faroe Islands VAT registration and returns v0.2

> **Source-cited draft.** Professional review is pending. Figures are limited to the periods and assumptions below. This Guide prepares working papers; it is not authority to file or pay.

## Scope

Prepare ordinary Faroese MVG registration and quarterly VAT working papers in 2026. Exempt sectors, detailed place-of-supply rules, real property and import valuation require additional classification.

Sources were accessed on 22 October 2026. A dated rate table controls its stated period; an undated guidance page does not prove that all later amendments have been incorporated.

## Registration and rates

TAKS states a standard **25% MVG** rate. [General VAT](https://www.taks.fo/en/business/vat/general-about-vat).

Under **section 4**, the ordinary small-business exemption applies to annual sales **below DKK 50,000**. Exactly DKK 50,000 is outside that exemption. Section 28 instead uses **more than DKK 50,000** for specified foreign services. Classify the supply and charging provision before choosing a threshold. The English summaries are not interchangeable. [VAT Act, ss4 and 28, consolidation amended 15 May 2026](https://logir.fo/Logtingslog/136-fra-08-09-1992-um-meirvirdisgjald-sum-seinast-broytt-vid-logtingslog-nr-28-fra-24).

Section 5 requires registration eight days before activity starts, subject to section 23b(2). Foreign businesses without a local establishment register through a resident person or local business; section 5 expressly preserves the Hoyvík treaty exception. Do not impose the representative requirement without checking that exception. [VAT Act, s5](https://logir.fo/Logtingslog/136-fra-08-09-1992-um-meirvirdisgjald-sum-seinast-broytt-vid-logtingslog-nr-28-fra-24).

## Computation

1. Classify sales by territory, taxable/exempt/export treatment and tax point.
2. Reconcile the taxable domestic sales ledger to output VAT.
3. Verify each input amount's recoverability and invoice/import evidence; private or exempt-use purchases cannot simply be included as though fully deductible.
4. Reconcile the VAT control account and submit the actual return, including nil periods where required.
5. Keep foreign representative and customs obligations separate from an income-tax PE analysis.

**Ordinary fully taxable example:** net sales DKK 100,000 × 25% = **25,000 output VAT**. Reviewed deductible input VAT DKK 10,000 gives **15,000 payable**. A VAT-inclusive price of DKK 125 contains **25 VAT**, calculated as 125 × 25/125, not 125 × 25%.

## Filing calendar and records

**Section 20 expressly sets 24 August for Q2**; the one-month-and-ten-day rule covers Q1, Q3 and Q4. Section 19 requires purchase/sales accounting records for five years after the accounting year ends, including businesses exempt under section 4. [VAT Act, ss19–20](https://logir.fo/Logtingslog/136-fra-08-09-1992-um-meirvirdisgjald-sum-seinast-broytt-vid-logtingslog-nr-28-fra-24).

The ordinary dates are **10 May, 24 August, 10 November and 10 February of the following year**, moving weekend/holiday dates to the next working day. Nil returns remain required. TAKS specifies a late-return increment of 1%, minimum DKK 300 and maximum DKK 1,000, and unpaid-tax interest of 0.7% per month. [Faroese quarterly guidance](https://www.taks.fo/fo/vinna/mvg/at-lata-inn-mvg-uppgerd).

**Penalty arithmetic only:** 1% of DKK 15,000 is 150, so the stated minimum gives 300; 1% of 200,000 is 2,000, capped at 1,000. Interest timing and any other charge need separate verification.

## Release gaps

Complete the exempt-sector rules, input restrictions, invoice requirements and import/export evidence. Foreign-service classifications and the Hoyvík exception need a separate case-specific review. Do not represent a simple output-minus-input example as coverage of all VAT transactions.
