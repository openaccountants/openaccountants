# Venezuela: cross-border income tax (2026) v0.1

> **General reference only.** This skill is general tax/accounting reference material for AI-assisted workflows. It has not been reviewed for any specific person's facts, documents, elections, deadlines, residency, filing status, or local procedures. Do not rely on it to file, pay, amend, or take a tax position without review by a qualified professional in the relevant jurisdiction.

**Answer layer.** First determine residence/domicile and any permanent establishment (PE) or fixed base. Then classify the income's source and legal category. Venezuelan residents/domiciliaries generally face worldwide income tax; nonresidents without a PE/fixed base face Venezuelan-source income tax; a foreign taxpayer with a PE/fixed base is taxed on domestic and foreign income attributable to it. Treaty relief needs an in-force treaty and evidence of residence. Credit foreign income tax actually paid only within the statutory Venezuelan cap. [LISLR arts. 1-4, 7, 51](https://contraloriadebarquisimeto.gob.ve/images/baselegal/LOAFSP.pdf#page=4); [COT art. 30](https://www.cmchacao.gob.ve/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/Decreto-con-Rango-Valor-y-Fuerza-de-Ley-que-Reforma-la-Ley-que-Establece-el-Impuesto-al-Valor-Agregado.pdf#page=5).

**Computation limit.** This Guide computes the specified nonresident-individual and proportional-tax cases and the foreign-credit cap using an independently established Venezuelan pre-credit liability. An ordinary company's Tarifa 2 computation and a resident individual's annual tax need their own current, source-checked workpaper. The corporate Guide is not yet an established reviewed handoff here; source/base classification alone is not a complete ordinary-company liability calculation.

The primary texts are the consolidated **Ley de Impuesto sobre la Renta** (LISLR), Gaceta Oficial 6.210 Extraordinario, 30 December 2015, and the **Código Orgánico Tributario** (COT), Gaceta Oficial 6.507 Extraordinario, 29 January 2020. Both official gazette facsimiles were opened/read on 11 November 2026 from Venezuelan government hosts. The filenames refer to other enactments in those multi-law gazettes; the cited articles belong to the LISLR and COT themselves. This is a new source-based Guide, tier 2, with no accountant approval. Administrative items not confirmed from accessible primary material are listed at the end.

## Who this applies to

Use this Guide for an incoming or outgoing individual, a Venezuelan resident with foreign income/assets, a Venezuelan payer dealing with a foreign beneficiary, or a foreign enterprise with Venezuelan income or operations. Treat the income recipient, payer, company and PE as distinct legal/tax subjects. Identify the year, source, recipient type and treaty position before selecting a base or rate. [LISLR arts. 1, 6-9](https://contraloriadebarquisimeto.gob.ve/images/baselegal/LOAFSP.pdf#page=4).

### Residence is not a stand-alone day-count test

COT art. 30 treats these as domiciled for tax purposes:

- Individuals present in Venezuela continuously or discontinuously for **more than 183 days** in the calendar year **or the immediately preceding year**.
- Individuals who establish their residence/home in Venezuela, unless they spend more than 183 days, continuously or discontinuously, in another country in the calendar year and prove that they acquired fiscal residence there.
- Venezuelans performing official representation/functions abroad for the Republic, states, municipalities or their functionally decentralized entities and paid by those bodies.
- Legal persons incorporated in Venezuela or legally domiciled there.

The article equates tax residence with that domicile definition; foreign residence under its home/residence exception is evidenced by a competent foreign authority's certificate. Venezuelan nationals are presumed resident unless proved otherwise. [COT art. 30, all paragraphs](https://www.cmchacao.gob.ve/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/Decreto-con-Rango-Valor-y-Fuerza-de-Ley-que-Reforma-la-Ley-que-Establece-el-Impuesto-al-Valor-Agregado.pdf#page=5).

LISLR art. 51 defines a nonresident individual as someone whose stay does not exceed 183 days in a calendar year **and who is not domiciled under the COT**. Its sole paragraph also treats a continuous stay over 183 days in the preceding year as resident. These texts are reconciled by applying both: the LISLR itself sends the domicile question to the COT, whose current text also counts discontinuous prior-year presence and the other domicile grounds. Exactly 183 days does not activate the “more than” limb, but it does not defeat a home, prior-year, official-duty or citizenship-presumption issue. Do not substitute a rolling twelve-month test for the statutory calendar-year tests. [LISLR art. 51](https://contraloriadebarquisimeto.gob.ve/images/baselegal/LOAFSP.pdf#page=13); [COT art. 30](https://www.cmchacao.gob.ve/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/Decreto-con-Rango-Valor-y-Fuerza-de-Ley-que-Reforma-la-Ley-que-Establece-el-Impuesto-al-Valor-Agregado.pdf#page=5).

For a departure, record presence in both years, continued housing and actual foreign fiscal residence; a flight, foreign bank account or foreign company is not by itself proof that the Venezuelan statutory tests ceased to apply. A dual-resident case requires the actual applicable treaty's residence article before a treaty conclusion is made. No country-specific tie-breaker is assumed here. [LISLR arts. 3, 51](https://contraloriadebarquisimeto.gob.ve/images/baselegal/LOAFSP.pdf#page=5); [COT art. 30](https://www.cmchacao.gob.ve/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/Decreto-con-Rango-Valor-y-Fuerza-de-Ley-que-Reforma-la-Ley-que-Establece-el-Impuesto-al-Valor-Agregado.pdf#page=5).

### Permanent establishment and fixed base

The domestic definition covers a fixed place/centre of activity and the examples in LISLR art. 7, third paragraph: management seat, branch, office, factory, workshop, installation, warehouse, shop, and other listed premises; an agent/representative authorized to contract for the taxpayer; and the listed extraction, agricultural and professional activities/places of work. A construction, installation or assembly work triggers the stated duration limb when it lasts **more than six months**. That is not a universal six-month exemption for all other PE categories. The independent-agent exclusion does not apply where that agent can conclude contracts in the principal's name. Permanent buying/service-acquisition centres and leased real estate are expressly included, as are fixed bases through which a foreign-resident individual performs independent personal services. [LISLR art. 7, third paragraph](https://contraloriadebarquisimeto.gob.ve/images/baselegal/LOAFSP.pdf#page=6).

A treaty may change the result for an entitled resident. Establish the domestic analysis first, then read that treaty; do not import a foreign country's construction or service-PE threshold. A PE's taxable income includes foreign-source income attributable to it, not automatically the foreign enterprise's entire worldwide income. [LISLR arts. 1, 3, 7](https://contraloriadebarquisimeto.gob.ve/images/baselegal/LOAFSP.pdf#page=4).

## The base

### The method, step by step

1. **Classify the taxpayer.** Establish residence/domicile for the relevant calendar years, entity incorporation/domicile and any PE/fixed base. Keep a written domestic conclusion and the evidence underlying it; if a treaty is claimed, keep the separate treaty conclusion. Use the residence and PE conditions above. [LISLR arts. 1, 3, 7, 51](https://contraloriadebarquisimeto.gob.ve/images/baselegal/LOAFSP.pdf#page=4); [COT art. 30](https://www.cmchacao.gob.ve/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/Decreto-con-Rango-Valor-y-Fuerza-de-Ley-que-Reforma-la-Ley-que-Establece-el-Impuesto-al-Valor-Agregado.pdf#page=5).
2. **Build a source and category schedule.** For each receipt, identify the work/property/right, place of performance and use, issuer/debtor, applicable PE and contract. Venezuelan-source income includes the causes in art. 6: domestic economic activity, locally exploited IP/royalties, PE/base income, work/capital used in Venezuela, domestic property and the listed domestic securities/assets and other categories. Technical assistance/technological services used in Venezuela are expressly addressed. A foreign invoice or collection account does not itself resolve these tests. Official state functions abroad are also treated as domestic activity under the final paragraph. [LISLR art. 6](https://contraloriadebarquisimeto.gob.ve/images/baselegal/LOAFSP.pdf#page=5).
3. **Compute each applicable net base.** Ordinary net income is gross less permitted costs/deductions, with the timing required by arts. 4-5 and 32. Keep foreign and territorial schedules separate. Common costs/deductions are distributed proportionately to the respective income. Foreign-source expenses must be normal and necessary for the worldwide-taxed operation and supported by foreign documents meeting local-law requirements, identifying provider/vendor and domicile, nature, date and amount, with Spanish translation. [LISLR arts. 4-5, 23, seventh paragraph; 27, sixteenth paragraph; 32](https://contraloriadebarquisimeto.gob.ve/images/baselegal/LOAFSP.pdf#page=5).
4. **Apply specific cross-border bases and exclusions.** Use the professional, technical, technological and royalty classifications below where applicable; do not subtract ordinary actual costs again from a statutory presumed net base without legal authority. A PE may deduct demonstrated transaction-purpose expenses, including demonstrated management/general-administration expenses incurred domestically or abroad, but art. 27's seventeenth paragraph disallows the specified head-office/group royalties, fees, technical-assistance and commission payments except actual expense reimbursements; interest is referred to the TP chapter. [LISLR arts. 27, seventeenth paragraph; 39-48](https://contraloriadebarquisimeto.gob.ve/images/baselegal/LOAFSP.pdf#page=10).
5. **Apply the recipient's tax regime.** The 34% rate here is the general nonresident-individual rule. A foreign company is governed by arts. 9 and 52 or its applicable special regime; do not assign the individual's flat rate merely because the company is foreign. The specific 4.95% lending rule applies only to a qualifying foreign, non-domiciled financial institution, as defined in art. 52's second paragraph; an ordinary group lender does not qualify merely because it charges interest. Handle foreign dividends and the branch deemed dividend separately as described below. [LISLR arts. 9, 50, 52, 66, 69](https://contraloriadebarquisimeto.gob.ve/images/baselegal/LOAFSP.pdf#page=6).
6. **Test treaty entitlement before taking relief.** Identify the actual in-force treaty, covered person/taxes, residence evidence and relevant distributive and relief provisions. Only then change a domestic result. Do not infer a zero rate from residence alone, nor conflate a treaty business-profits rule with a royalty, employment or independent-services article. [LISLR art. 3](https://contraloriadebarquisimeto.gob.ve/images/baselegal/LOAFSP.pdf#page=5).
7. **Compute the foreign-tax credit.** Match foreign income tax actually paid to income included in the Venezuelan base, translate the foreign tax at its actual payment-date exchange rate under art. 2, compute the correct cap and apply the smaller amount. Keep separately taxed proportional items separate. Record foreign assessments, receipts, income/source mapping and the conversion date/rate. [LISLR art. 2](https://contraloriadebarquisimeto.gob.ve/images/baselegal/LOAFSP.pdf#page=4).
8. **Check controlled foreign investments and close compliance workpapers.** Apply arts. 98-108 if the investments are established to be in a low-tax jurisdiction and the statutory control/timing criteria are met. Reconcile credits, foreign-loss balances, distributions, notices and return schedules. Establish the applicable current withholding instructions and calendar before remitting or filing; the unconfirmed administrative items below are not guessed. [LISLR arts. 55, 77-85, 97-108](https://contraloriadebarquisimeto.gob.ve/images/baselegal/LOAFSP.pdf#page=14).

LISLR art. 5 generally treats income as available when the producing transaction occurs, with the stated multi-year credit/discount exception. It uses accrual for the specified credit, entity and rental income, while employment remuneration and windfalls are available when paid. Article 32 links deduction timing to the applicable income-availability treatment and its specified exceptions. Determine the actual category before assigning a foreign receipt or expense to 2026; a bank-transfer date alone does not decide every case. [LISLR arts. 5, 32](https://contraloriadebarquisimeto.gob.ve/images/baselegal/LOAFSP.pdf#page=5).

### Professional, technical and royalty payments

Non-commercial professional income under art. 39 uses a 90% net base, **without prejudice to art. 41**. Technical assistance is not every overseas professional invoice: art. 42 describes technical instructions/instruments for making a work or product for sale or providing a specified service for sale, including the listed engineering, R&D/project, advisory/consultancy and technical-management content. Art. 43's technological-services category concerns the stated patentable elements/rights. Art. 44 excludes teaching, unrelated services, investments in fixed/non-sale assets and reimbursements for goods acquired abroad. Read the actual deliverable, not merely an invoice label such as “consulting.” [LISLR arts. 39, 41-44](https://contraloriadebarquisimeto.gob.ve/images/baselegal/LOAFSP.pdf#page=12).

If a contract genuinely combines technical assistance and technological services without separating the remuneration, art. 45 supplies the 25/75 allocation; apply the 30% and 50% net-base fractions to their respective allocations. If an unallocated remuneration combines foreign-supplied and Venezuelan-performed services, art. 46 supplies the 60/40 location allocation, with permitted actual costs/deductions on the Venezuelan portion. Do not apply either allocation to a contract whose amounts and services are actually identified, or mix the two sets of percentages as if they were tax rates. [LISLR arts. 45-46](https://contraloriadebarquisimeto.gob.ve/images/baselegal/LOAFSP.pdf#page=13).

The Venezuelan payer's deduction is a separate question: art. 27, fourteenth paragraph, disallows cost/deduction treatment for technical assistance or technological services paid to foreign enterprises where the services are, or could be, provided in Venezuela when incurred; the taxpayer must provide documentation of its efforts to obtain the services locally. Do not infer deductibility merely from the foreign recipient's taxable 30% or 50% net base. [LISLR art. 27, fourteenth paragraph](https://contraloriadebarquisimeto.gob.ve/images/baselegal/LOAFSP.pdf#page=11).

Trade/factory/service names and similar identifying signs are addressed in art. 47: ordinary permitted costs/deductions are available except where paid as a royalty to a non-domiciled beneficiary. Art. 48's 90% royalty base is also expressly subject to art. 41. Determine whether the payment is for a patentable technological service, a royalty/similar participation or another right before applying a base. [LISLR arts. 43, 47-48](https://contraloriadebarquisimeto.gob.ve/images/baselegal/LOAFSP.pdf#page=12).

### Foreign-tax credit and foreign losses

For ordinary global-income taxation, define global net enrichment, the included foreign net enrichment and the Venezuelan tax on global income before this credit. The cap is that tax multiplied by the foreign/global net-enrichment proportion. For an item taxed proportionally, cap the credit at the Venezuelan tax that would apply to that same income. Art. 2 defines creditable income tax to include taxes on total income or its elements, including capital gains, wages and specified gains; disputed classification is for SENIAT to determine. An unpaid foreign assessment is not evidence of tax paid. [LISLR art. 2](https://contraloriadebarquisimeto.gob.ve/images/baselegal/LOAFSP.pdf#page=4).

Article 4 forbids applying a foreign-source loss against territorial enrichment/loss. Article 55 allows foreign losses only against foreign-source enrichment within the next three tax periods and subject to the 25% limitation; keep the provenance/year and each period's utilization calculation. Do not treat a foreign loss as a credit, or reduce domestic-source profit with it because the taxpayer is worldwide-taxed. No excess-credit carryforward or refund entitlement is invented from these provisions. [LISLR arts. 4, 55](https://contraloriadebarquisimeto.gob.ve/images/baselegal/LOAFSP.pdf#page=5).

### Foreign dividends and branch deemed dividends

Foreign dividends described in art. 66's first paragraph are excluded from the net-income calculation described there and taxed separately at 34%, with foreign tax paid for that concept potentially credited under art. 2. Other foreign-source income already included under art. 1 is treated as part of taxed fiscal net income under the second paragraph. Keep the dividend classification, payer incorporation/domicile and foreign tax proof. [LISLR art. 66](https://contraloriadebarquisimeto.gob.ve/images/baselegal/LOAFSP.pdf#page=15).

Article 69 applies to its specified foreign-incorporated company/community domiciled in Venezuela or foreign company/community with a Venezuelan PE. The entity pays, for its members, 34% on the specified non-exempt/non-exonerated net-income excess, deemed received by them at fiscal-year close. The exception requires full domestic reinvestment to SENIAT's satisfaction, maintenance for at least five years and an annual external-auditor certificate with the return. A cash remittance by itself is not the measure of this deemed-dividend charge; conversely, no remittance is not proof of exemption. Do not assume all branch profit is additionally taxed at 34%. [LISLR art. 69](https://contraloriadebarquisimeto.gob.ve/images/baselegal/LOAFSP.pdf#page=15).

For an ordinary Venezuelan-company dividend paid abroad, prepare the following dividend account before applying a rate:

1. **Establish accounting and fiscal profits.** Art. 64's net profit is the amount approved by the shareholders' assembly based on the art. 88 financial statements; even regulated banks/financial/insurance entities use this definition. Taxed fiscal net profit is the profit subject to LISLR tariffs/proportional rates other than the dividend tax. If no assembly approved the balance/income statement, SENIAT applies the statutory attribution rules. Art. 65 covers the non-exempt/non-exonerated previously untaxed excess paid/account-credited in money or kind, including the specified own-company shares issued on a capital increase.
2. **Compute the potential excess.** Under art. 66, subtract taxed fiscal net profit and income from dividends received from other companies from net profit. Keep its separately taxed foreign dividends outside the net profit defined there, and count other foreign-source income already included under art. 1 in taxed fiscal net profit as its second paragraph provides.
3. **Allocate the distribution in order.** Art. 67 first imputes payment/account credit to the immediately preceding year's taxed fiscal net profit (not taxed again); second to the preceding year's received dividends already taxed as such or originating in taxed fiscal profit of the distributing company; third to the preceding year's net profit exceeding fiscal net profit (taxable under this chapter). Do not tax the first two pools merely because cash is sent abroad.
4. **Work backward through profit years.** When the preceding year's profits are exhausted or absent, art. 68 moves to the closest earlier profit year and repeats the same attribution order. Continue until the distributed profits belong to a year governed by the law that this law amended, for which art. 68 states non-taxability; establish the actual historical profit-year evidence before using that boundary.
5. **Screen shareholder advances.** Art. 70 treats company credits, deposits and advances to members as paid dividends up to the profits/reserves in the approved balance supporting distributions, using art. 67's order. Its exception requires **both** company receipt of interest at a rate not below the BCV-set active bank rate minus three percentage points **and** the member's cash repayment of the credit/deposit/advance before the company's fiscal close. The specified uniform savings-plan loans under art. 14(8) are excluded. A loan label or interest accrual alone does not establish the exception.
6. **Apply and reconcile the ordinary charge.** Art. 71's ordinary rate is **34% on the taxable dividend determined above**, with full withholding at payment or account credit. For own-company stock dividends, its first paragraph instead requires a **1% advance on the total declared dividend**, credited against the proportional tax payable on declaration; the company must require proof of advance payment before registering share ownership in its shareholder book. Art. 72 requires proration where the payer's enrichment faced different tariffs, using net fiscal profit taxed under each. Establish actual treaty entitlement before changing a domestic result. Art. 71's sector exceptions require their own current specialist analysis and are outside this ordinary computation.

Keep approved accounts, fiscal-profit reconciliation, received-dividend records, profit-year pools, shareholder advance/repayment/interest evidence, distribution resolutions and withholding/advance proof together. [LISLR arts. 64-73](https://contraloriadebarquisimeto.gob.ve/images/baselegal/LOAFSP.pdf#page=15).

### Investments in low-tax jurisdictions

Article 192 assigns designation of low-tax jurisdictions to the tax administration through a providencia. The current official list is unconfirmed, so this is a **conditional method**, not a finding that any named country qualifies. Arts. 98-108 cover the listed direct, indirect and interposed investments where the taxpayer can decide distribution timing or controls their management; influence/control is rebuttably presumed. Public-entity investments specified in art. 98 are excluded. Location tests include accounts, postal address, effective management/PE, incorporation, physical presence or legal transactions under that jurisdiction's law. Accounts owned by or benefiting the listed spouse/cohabitant, ascendants/descendants or representative may count under art. 101, and transfers to the relevant accounts are rebuttably presumed the taxpayer's under art. 102. [LISLR arts. 98, 100-102](https://contraloriadebarquisimeto.gob.ve/images/baselegal/LOAFSP.pdf#page=19); [art. 192](https://contraloriadebarquisimeto.gob.ve/images/baselegal/LOAFSP.pdf#page=29).

The business-activity exception requires **more than 50%** qualifying fixed assets in that jurisdiction; it ceases if the specified passive receipts exceed **20%** of investment income. The two strict inequalities differ from “at least.” If included, income is recognized as it accrues in the taxpayer's direct/indirect participation proportion, even without distribution, unless previously taxed. Costs/deductions require accessible accounting and the information return; retain the annual taxed-income/distribution account to avoid simply taxing a previously included receipt again. The information return accompanies the definitive income-tax return and includes deposits/withdrawals and supporting statements. Share disposal and liquidation/capital-reduction income follow the general foreign-income computation under art. 107. Foreign tax credit remains subject to art. 2 through art. 108. [LISLR arts. 99, 103-108](https://contraloriadebarquisimeto.gob.ve/images/baselegal/LOAFSP.pdf#page=19).

## Deadlines and filing

This Guide prepares cross-border working papers; it does not supply an executable 2026/2027 portal process. LISLR art. 77 sets annual-return obligations for the specified companies/entities whatever their profit/loss amount; art. 79 requires nonresident individuals to declare their Venezuelan enrichment/loss whatever the amount, under the Regulation's terms. Do not infer that withholding always removes the annual-return duty. Resident-individual thresholds and the actual annual income-tax calculation belong to the individual-income-tax workpaper, not an assumed nonresident threshold. [LISLR arts. 77-79](https://contraloriadebarquisimeto.gob.ve/images/baselegal/LOAFSP.pdf#page=16).

Identify whether a withholding duty exists and the applicable administrative rate. LISLR art. 196 continues Decreto 1.808 pending the providencias envisaged by art. 84; that transition does not establish that an old administrative table is the current rule for a particular payment. [LISLR art. 196](https://contraloriadebarquisimeto.gob.ve/images/baselegal/LOAFSP.pdf#page=29). Where required, art. 84 says withhold at **payment or credit to account, whichever comes first**; account credit means the amount credited in the payer's books/records. Articles 83 and 85 distinguish advance payments and annual settlement. COT art. 27 makes a withholding agent solely liable to the Fisco for amounts withheld; if the agent does not withhold, it is jointly liable with the taxpayer. Its deduction/evidence conditions must also be considered. A gross-to-net tax-liability example is not authority for a particular current withholding code or remittance day. [LISLR arts. 83-85](https://contraloriadebarquisimeto.gob.ve/images/baselegal/LOAFSP.pdf#page=17); [COT art. 27](https://www.cmchacao.gob.ve/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/Decreto-con-Rango-Valor-y-Fuerza-de-Ley-que-Reforma-la-Ley-que-Establece-el-Impuesto-al-Valor-Agregado.pdf#page=4).

For a residence/domicile or company seat/principal-establishment change, LISLR art. 97 specifies notice within 20 days. COT art. 35 separately requires fiscal-domicile and its other listed changes within a maximum one month. Calendar both applicable obligations; do not silently replace the income-tax-specific requirement with the general month. Under COT art. 10, day periods are working days unless the law makes them continuous, year/month periods run continuously to the equivalent day and a non-working expiry moves to the next working day. Confirm the actual office calendar and current notice procedure before quoting a calendar due date. [LISLR art. 97](https://contraloriadebarquisimeto.gob.ve/images/baselegal/LOAFSP.pdf#page=18); [COT arts. 10, 35](https://www.cmchacao.gob.ve/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/Decreto-con-Rango-Valor-y-Fuerza-de-Ley-que-Reforma-la-Ley-que-Establece-el-Impuesto-al-Valor-Agregado.pdf#page=3).

COT art. 33 sets the place for dealings with a foreign-domiciled taxpayer: its Venezuelan representative, otherwise its Venezuelan activity/PE/fixed-base location, otherwise the taxable-event location. Art. 34 permits a mandatory electronic fiscal domicile taking priority. These are legal contact rules, not proof of the current registration or portal procedure. Keep the RIF/representation/notice details, foreign residence certificate, treaty text, contracts, source/PE analysis, foreign tax receipts and conversion evidence, and final acknowledgement once the current submission route is established. [COT arts. 33-34](https://www.cmchacao.gob.ve/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/Decreto-con-Rango-Valor-y-Fuerza-de-Ley-que-Reforma-la-Ley-que-Establece-el-Impuesto-al-Valor-Agregado.pdf#page=5); [LISLR arts. 3, 88, 96](https://contraloriadebarquisimeto.gob.ve/images/baselegal/LOAFSP.pdf#page=5).

No separate exit-tax exemption or no-filing conclusion is established here. Changing residence does not remove the need to determine the relevant year's residence/source, final/short-period return requirements, notices, retained Venezuelan assets or any entity/PE obligations. Current departure-clearance and short-period mechanics remain unconfirmed rather than supplied from memory.

## Penalties and interest

### Returns, communications and ordinary tax

COT art. 103 distinguishes the formal duties already covered above:

- No return or a delay **over one year**: a fine of **150 times** the official exchange rate of the highest-valued currency published by BCV, plus **ten continuous days' closure** where premises exist.
- An incomplete return or delay **up to and including one year**: **100 times** that rate.
- The specified missing/incomplete/late communications, excess/late substitute return and unauthorized form/medium/location offences in items 2, 4, 5 and 6: **50 times** that rate.
- No low-tax-jurisdiction investment information return: **2,000 times** that rate plus **ten continuous days' closure** where premises exist; a late such return: **1,000 times**, without that closure sanction for mere lateness.

The article applies its closure sanction to all the taxpayer's establishments/branches. Its ten-day declaration closure is distinct from art. 100's registration offences. For designated special taxpayers, COT art. 108's final paragraph increases the pecuniary sanctions for formal offences in that chapter **by 200%**, producing three times the ordinary fine; this is not a general multiplier for the material payment/underassessment offences below. [COT arts. 103, 108](https://www.cmchacao.gob.ve/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/Decreto-con-Rango-Valor-y-Fuerza-de-Ley-que-Reforma-la-Ley-que-Establece-el-Impuesto-al-Valor-Agregado.pdf#page=13).

Under COT art. 110, ordinary payment after the due date, without an extension and without the specified verification/audit/determination, incurs **0.28% of the unpaid amount per day**, capped at **100%**, within one year of the original due date. Payment beyond one year attracts an **additional 50%** of the amount owed; beyond two years the statute adds **150%**. Payment during audit/determination is routed, as applicable, to art. 112; an obtained extension excludes art. 110's sanctions. These rules do not replace art. 115's separate withholding-remittance sanctions. [COT art. 110](https://www.cmchacao.gob.ve/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/Decreto-con-Rango-Valor-y-Fuerza-de-Ley-que-Reforma-la-Ley-que-Establece-el-Impuesto-al-Valor-Agregado.pdf#page=14).

COT art. 112 generally imposes **100%-300% of omitted tax** for an illegitimate reduction of tax revenue, including improper exemption/benefit claims, without prejudice to art. 119's criminal sanction. Where the law requires property valuation and administrative valuation increases it by no more than one quarter, its first paragraph excludes that valuation sanction; special laws may exclude other estimation differences. The **30%** treatment in its second paragraph applies only to the art. 196 accepted-and-paid assessment procedure: art. 195 gives fifteen working days after notice to file/rectify and pay; art. 196 preserves interest and other applicable fines, and partial acceptance/payment limits the 30% treatment to that part while the contested portion proceeds separately. It is not an unconditional early-payment discount or a substitute for art. 115. [COT arts. 112, 195-196](https://www.cmchacao.gob.ve/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/Decreto-con-Rango-Valor-y-Fuerza-de-Ley-que-Reforma-la-Ley-que-Establece-el-Impuesto-al-Valor-Agregado.pdf#page=15).

### Withholding and interest

Failure to withhold, under-withholding, late remittance and non-remittance are different offences under COT art. 115. In the ordinary non-public-agent case, non-withholding attracts **500% of the unwithheld tax**; under-withholding attracts **100% of the shortfall**. Late remittance is **5% of withheld amounts per day**, for at most **100 days** under paragraph (3); later remittance or remittance during the stated verification/audit circumstances invokes paragraph (4) and art. 121 as the statute provides. Non-remittance attracts **1,000%**, without prejudice to art. 121's criminal consequences. The Republic, governorships and municipalities have express alternative rules, so do not apply the ordinary non-public-agent paragraph (3)/(4) fine to them. The article preserves these sanctions even where the agent accepts the assessment under art. 196. A live withholding failure requires tax counsel, not only a spreadsheet correction. [COT art. 115, complete article](https://www.cmchacao.gob.ve/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/Decreto-con-Rango-Valor-y-Fuerza-de-Ley-que-Reforma-la-Ley-que-Establece-el-Impuesto-al-Valor-Agregado.pdf#page=15).

Use the applicable statutory offence and current monetary conversion rules before producing a fine. COT arts. 91-92 distinguish fixed exchange-rate multiples from percentage-fine conversion at the offence/payment dates; do not assume the highest-value currency is always the US dollar. Interest under art. 66 is **1.2 times** the applicable BCV-calculated active lending rate for each period from the tax payment deadline until discharge, with the specified publication fallback. Actual period rates and exchange rates have not been confirmed for a monetary fine/interest total here. [COT arts. 66, 91-92](https://www.cmchacao.gob.ve/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/Decreto-con-Rango-Valor-y-Fuerza-de-Ley-que-Reforma-la-Ley-que-Establece-el-Impuesto-al-Valor-Agregado.pdf#page=9).

## Cross-border points

**Treaties.** LISLR art. 3 requires a treaty that has entered into force, proof of residence and compliance with the actual treaty. Foreign authorities' residence certificates require official translation and legalization under that article. The domestic withholding/base table is not a treaty-rate table. No current treaty partner count, rate, beneficial-owner conclusion, tie-breaker or mutual-agreement time limit is asserted without that treaty. COT art. 1's sole paragraph says treaty mutual-agreement procedures are optional and may be requested independently of domestic administrative/judicial remedies; it does not supply an unverified country-specific request deadline. [LISLR art. 3](https://contraloriadebarquisimeto.gob.ve/images/baselegal/LOAFSP.pdf#page=5); [COT art. 1](https://www.cmchacao.gob.ve/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/Decreto-con-Rango-Valor-y-Fuerza-de-Ley-que-Reforma-la-Ley-que-Establece-el-Impuesto-al-Valor-Agregado.pdf#page=2).

**Related parties.** A cross-border related transaction separately needs an arm's length analysis under LISLR arts. 109-117, including an interposed-person rule, related-interest restrictions and the rebuttable low-tax-jurisdiction presumption. Taxable source, deductibility, pricing and withholding are separate tests. Article 112's corresponding foreign adjustment requires a treaty-permitted foreign adjustment and Venezuelan acceptance; a foreign assessment alone does not authorize an automatic Venezuelan deduction. The pack's transfer-pricing work is tracked separately; this Guide does not reproduce its computation. [LISLR arts. 109-117](https://contraloriadebarquisimeto.gob.ve/images/baselegal/LOAFSP.pdf#page=20).

**When to refuse or refer.** Stop a final tax or filing conclusion where residence, source, entity type, PE attribution, treaty entitlement, contract classification, foreign tax paid, current withholding rule or required administrative procedure is missing. Refer live disputes, uncertain PE/treaty application, opaque low-tax structures or unsupported reinvestment claims. Record the supported domestic calculation and precise missing evidence; do not replace a missing source with a rate from a secondary guide or a stale pack file.

## Two worked examples that recompute

All monetary inputs below are hypothetical VES equivalents already placed on one consistent measurement basis. Foreign tax has been converted at the assumed evidenced payment-date rate; no real 2026 exchange rate or resident graduated-tax calculation is implied.

### Example 1: foreign-tax credit capped by the Venezuelan share

A Venezuelan resident has ordinary global net enrichment of VES 300,000, including VES 100,000 of foreign net enrichment. The separately reviewed domestic computation gives Venezuelan tax before this credit of VES 90,000; that tax amount is an **input**, not a claimed 30% statutory rate. Qualifying foreign income tax actually paid, translated under art. 2, is VES 45,000. There are no separately taxed dividends or other proportional items in this example. [LISLR arts. 1-2, 4](https://contraloriadebarquisimeto.gob.ve/images/baselegal/LOAFSP.pdf#page=4).

The Venezuelan cap is 90,000 × 100,000 / 300,000 = VES 30,000. Credit the smaller of VES 45,000 paid and VES 30,000 cap. Venezuelan tax after this credit is VES 60,000; VES 15,000 of foreign tax does not reduce this example's Venezuelan liability. A separate refund/carryforward claim is not established by this calculation.

```oa-check
name: Ordinary foreign tax credit capped at attributable Venezuelan tax
inputs:
  global_net_income: 300000
  foreign_net_income: 100000
  venezuelan_tax_before_credit: 90000
  qualifying_foreign_tax_paid_ves: 45000
steps:
  foreign_tax_credit_cap: venezuelan_tax_before_credit * foreign_net_income / global_net_income
  allowed_credit: min(qualifying_foreign_tax_paid_ves, foreign_tax_credit_cap)
  venezuelan_tax_after_credit: venezuelan_tax_before_credit - allowed_credit
  foreign_tax_above_cap: qualifying_foreign_tax_paid_ves - allowed_credit
expected:
  foreign_tax_credit_cap: 30000
  allowed_credit: 30000
  venezuelan_tax_after_credit: 60000
  foreign_tax_above_cap: 15000
```

### Example 2: decide the payment category from the actual deliverable

A nonresident individual without COT domicile or Venezuelan PE/fixed base receives VES 100,000 of Venezuelan-source non-commercial professional fees. There is no established treaty reduction. If the deliverable is ordinary professional work outside the technical-services definitions, art. 39 gives a 90% net base, VES 90,000; art. 50 gives tax of 34%, VES 30,600. [LISLR arts. 39, 50](https://contraloriadebarquisimeto.gob.ve/images/baselegal/LOAFSP.pdf#page=12).

Now change the facts: the foreign-supplied deliverable is evidenced technical instructions/engineering for the Venezuelan customer's production of a product for sale, meeting arts. 41-42; it is not teaching, a fixed-asset investment/reimbursement or another art. 44 exclusion, not a patentable-right technological service and not a combined/unallocated contract. Article 41's technical-assistance base is 30% of the same VES 100,000, or VES 30,000. For this nonresident individual, the 34% tax is VES 10,200. The difference is VES 20,400. The contract's substance decides the category; the taxpayer cannot simply elect the lower figure or relabel ordinary work. [LISLR arts. 39, 41-44, 50](https://contraloriadebarquisimeto.gob.ve/images/baselegal/LOAFSP.pdf#page=12).

Both results are **domestic income-tax liability illustrations**, not a confirmed current withholding/remittance instruction. A corporate recipient, a PE, actual treaty relief, technological service or mixed contract changes the analysis.

```oa-check
name: Nonresident professional fee versus qualifying technical assistance
inputs:
  gross_fee: 100000
  professional_net_fraction: 0.90
  technical_assistance_net_fraction: 0.30
  nonresident_individual_tax_rate: 0.34
steps:
  professional_net_base: gross_fee * professional_net_fraction
  professional_tax: professional_net_base * nonresident_individual_tax_rate
  technical_net_base: gross_fee * technical_assistance_net_fraction
  technical_tax: technical_net_base * nonresident_individual_tax_rate
  difference: professional_tax - technical_tax
expected:
  professional_net_base: 90000
  professional_tax: 30600
  technical_net_base: 30000
  technical_tax: 10200
  difference: 20400
```

## Ask the client first

1. What are the taxpayer's legal type, citizenship/incorporation, actual domicile/homes and physical-presence dates in this calendar year and the preceding year; what foreign fiscal-residence proof exists?
2. What work, assets, rights and transactions generated each receipt; where were they performed, used or situated, and what contracts/deliverables support the source and category?
3. What Venezuelan premises, personnel, agents with contracting authority, projects and duration, PE/fixed base or branch are involved, and which income/costs are attributable to them?
4. Which actual in-force treaty, residence certificate, translation/legalization and entitlement documents support any requested relief?
5. What foreign income tax was actually paid, on which included income, on what date/currency/rate; what foreign-loss history, distributions or controlled foreign investments exist?
6. What are the payer/recipient RIF and special-taxpayer status, withholding/payment events, fiscal-year close, change/notice dates and current primary administrative instructions or outstanding assessments?
