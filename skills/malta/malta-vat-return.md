# Malta VAT returns: Article 10, Article 11 and Article 12

## Scope

This skill covers preparing and checking Maltese VAT filings for a business established in Malta: the Article 10 periodic return, the Article 11 small-enterprise declaration and the Article 12 obligations. It covers registration type, rates, input VAT, cross-border reverse charge, recapitulative statements, the One Stop Shop (OSS), penalties, interest and records.

Figures are for tax year 2026. The law is the VAT Act, Chapter 406 of the Laws of Malta, in the consolidated text cited below (the PDF lists amendments through Act III of 2026 and Legal Notices 75 and 86 of 2026), plus its subsidiary legislation. VAT in Malta is charged per transaction and per tax period, so "tax year 2026" means periods falling in calendar 2026. A short section near the end covers 2025 periods still being filed.

The authority is the Malta Tax and Customs Administration (MTCA); the Act calls the office-holder the Commissioner for Tax and Customs. Returns, registrations and statements go through MTCA's online services. This skill does not reproduce the box numbers of the return form. Take them from the live form on the MTCA site, and check MTCA's published filing calendar against the statutory dates below.

## Ask the client first

- Which article are you registered under: 10, 11, 11A or 12, or more than one? Send the registration certificate. It states the article, the effective date and the registration number. An MT prefix means Article 10 or 12. An Article 11 number has no MT prefix and is not a VAT identification number.
- What are the start and end dates of your tax period? Do not assume calendar quarters. The Commissioner sets or varies the period; use the actual period on the account, including any monthly or other special allocation (art. 17).
- What was your turnover under the Sixth Schedule definition below, excluding VAT and capital-asset disposals, in calendar 2025, and so far in 2026? Include the specified exempt supplies; do not use only sales on which VAT was charged. If you are not an individual (for example a company or a partnership), does any person, including an individual, own or control more than 10% of you, directly or indirectly? Their turnover counts in proportion ([Act, Sixth Schedule Part One item 4](https://legislation.mt/getpdf/6ac352947b5e681a8405fbf4)).
- What do you sell, and to whom? Split sales into: Malta sales by rate, exempt sales, exports outside the EU, EU business customers (goods or services), and EU consumers.
- Do you buy goods or services from suppliers outside Malta, and do their invoices show foreign VAT, Maltese VAT or no VAT?
- Do you make any exempt-without-credit supplies (residential letting, insurance, financial services, medical care, education)?
- Did you buy any car, boat or aircraft, or pay for fuel, repairs, entertainment, alcohol or tobacco in the period?
- Did you acquire goods for long-term use as fixed assets, including equipment, tools or office furniture costing €1,160 or more, or immovable property? Record both the deduction period and first use; do not derive the adjustment period from first use alone. ([S.L. 406.12](https://legislation.mt/getpdf/60225988bc8272018c0f72fb))
- Is there an excess credit from the previous period, and has any of it become refundable, been refunded or been set off? A credit already refundable cannot be deducted again as a carried credit (art. 21(1)).
- Have you filed every earlier VAT return and income tax return? A refund is withheld while a return is missing.
- Who will file: you, with your own e-ID, or an authorised tax practitioner or trusted person acting for you? If someone acts for you, that access must be set up in MTCA's online services before the due date: form MTCA02 for a registered tax practitioner, form MTCA03 for a trusted person who is not a registered practitioner ([MTCA VAT return cycle](https://mtca.gov.mt/personal-tax/self-employed/vat-return-cycle)).

## The method, step by step

1. Confirm the registration type from the certificate before anything else ([Act, arts 10-13](https://legislation.mt/getpdf/6ac352947b5e681a8405fbf4)). Article 10 files periodic returns and recovers input VAT. Article 11 charges no VAT, recovers none and files a declaration. Article 12 only pays VAT on its EU acquisitions and reverse-charge services. If you prepare the wrong filing, every later step is wrong.
2. Fix the period from the certificate or the MTCA account (art. 17). Sort every invoice by the date VAT became chargeable, not by bank date.
3. Split sales into ([Act, Eighth and Fifth Schedules](https://legislation.mt/getpdf/6ac352947b5e681a8405fbf4)): taxable at 18%, 12%, 7% or 5%; exempt with credit (exports, intra-EU goods to a valid VAT number, food, medicines, international passenger transport); exempt without credit; and outside the scope (for example general-rule B2B services supplied to a customer established abroad, after checking place-of-supply exceptions). Use the rate table below.
4. Classify each foreign purchase before calculating VAT. Determine goods acquisition, import or service; its place of supply; whether it is taxable; the supplier's establishment and Article 10 status; and the customer-liability condition in art. 20. For a taxable general-rule B2B service supplied in Malta by a supplier not established here and not registered under Article 10, art. 20(2)(b) makes the Malta recipient liable. A supplier with a Malta fixed establishment is treated as not established here for this rule when that establishment does not intervene in the taxable Malta supply (art. 20(2) proviso). An Article 10 recipient declares output tax and claims only the deduction legally allowed. A hotel abroad or an exempt service does not become Malta reverse charge merely because its invoice has no VAT. Foreign VAT is not Maltese input tax; if charged incorrectly it does not remove the separate Maltese liability. Obtain a corrected invoice. For unresolved foreign-VAT valuation: Check this point with one of our partner accountants ([Act, arts 2, 19-22 and Third Schedule](https://legislation.mt/getpdf/6ac352947b5e681a8405fbf4)).
5. Remove blocked input VAT (Tenth Schedule item 3), then apportion anything partly private (item 4). If you also make exempt-without-credit supplies, stop and refer (partial exemption).
6. Flag goods acquired for long-term use as fixed assets ([S.L. 406.12, regs 2-3](https://legislation.mt/getpdf/60225988bc8272018c0f72fb)); small equipment, working tools and office furniture below €1,160 are excluded. Record the deduction period and first use. The ordinary reference period starts with the deduction period; delayed first use has a specific rule, so refer that case before fixing the 5-year or 20-year period.
7. Net output against input tax, deduct only excess credit brought forward that has not already been deducted, refunded or become refundable (art. 21(1)), and read the balance: payable, or an excess credit to carry forward or claim.
8. File and pay by the due date. First confirm who files and that access works: the person with their own e-ID, or an authorised tax practitioner (form MTCA02) or trusted person (form MTCA03) already set up in MTCA's online services ([MTCA VAT return cycle](https://mtca.gov.mt/personal-tax/self-employed/vat-return-cycle); allow time before the deadline). The return is filed electronically through the web portal the Commissioner designates, or on a prescribed form, and it counts as furnished only if it is full and complete in all material aspects (art. 27(3)-(4)). For an ordinary Article 10 return, the seven-day relief in the filing table works in two parts: filing electronically within seven days after the due date is enough to avoid the late-filing penalty (art. 42(1)(d)), but interest is avoided only if the tax due is also paid with that return (art. 21(4A)). Pay online or at MaltaPost and retain proof of submission/payment ([MTCA VAT return cycle](https://mtca.gov.mt/personal-tax/self-employed/vat-return-cycle)). For authenticated form fields and submission screens: Check this point with one of our partner accountants. Check separately whether a recapitulative statement or OSS return is required.
9. Keep the VAT account and working papers with the records listed below, for at least six years. For capital goods, the six years start only when the 5-year or 20-year adjustment period ends.

Occasional supplies of new means of transport have a separate exception; refer those cases (art. 10(1)-(2)). Charges VAT, recovers input VAT, files periodic returns. Check this point with one of our partner accountants. |
| Article 12 | Goods: a taxable person not registered under Article 10, or a non-taxable legal person, whose relevant intra-Community acquisitions exceed €10,000 in the current calendar year. Services received: a taxable person established in Malta outside Article 10 liable under art. 20(2). Services supplied: a taxable person established in Malta outside Article 10 supplying in another Member State where the recipient alone pays under Article 196 ([Act, art. 12(1), (3), (3a)](https://legislation.mt/getpdf/6ac352947b5e681a8405fbf4)) | Apply by the qualifying acquisition, service received or service supplied. The whole threshold-crossing acquisition is included. The threshold does not decide special-goods liability; refer those cases. An existing Article 12 registration does not end automatically each January; cancellation has the distinct conditions in art. 12(6), (7), (10): Check this point with one of our partner accountants. Pays the VAT; no ordinary input VAT recovery. Article 11 does not remove these separate Article 12 duties. |

Turnover for the small-enterprise thresholds is not limited to VAT-charged sales: it includes the specified exempt-with-credit supplies and property, financial and insurance transactions unless ancillary under the Sixth Schedule definition. VAT and disposals of tangible or intangible capital assets are excluded. For any applicant or registrant that is not an individual (a company, a partnership or another body), add the proportionate turnover of any person, including an individual, who owns or controls it, directly or indirectly, by more than 10% ([Act, Sixth Schedule Part One item 4](https://legislation.mt/getpdf/6ac352947b5e681a8405fbf4)).

Cancellation of Article 10, including a switch to Article 11, can create a taxable self-supply of remaining business goods where input VAT was wholly or partly deducted. The annual capital-goods adjustment does not replace this check ([Act, Second Schedule item 14 and Seventh Schedule item 6](https://legislation.mt/getpdf/6ac352947b5e681a8405fbf4)). For the actual exception, valuation and any Commissioner exemption where the taxable value is below €250: Check this point with one of our partner accountants. No exemption is assumed here. This is not the Article 11-to-10 direction in Case 5.

## Input VAT: recovery and blocked items ([Act, arts 22-24 and Tenth Schedule](https://legislation.mt/getpdf/6ac352947b5e681a8405fbf4))

An Article 10 person deducts qualifying input tax under art. 22(4) and the Tenth Schedule, including purchases used for taxable or exempt-with-credit supplies. For the separate outside-Malta and financial/insurance deduction categories in art. 22(4): Check this point with one of our partner accountants.

Evidence: a tax invoice in your name (or an import document naming you as importer), held and producible, with the tax properly accounted for in the required records (Tenth Schedule item 2(2)(d)). For reverse-charge purchases and intra-EU acquisitions, the VAT must also be reported as due in your tax return (Tenth Schedule item 2(2)(b)). Output tax and the input tax credit both belong to the tax period in which the tax becomes chargeable (art. 22(1) and (3)(a)), so both go on the return for that period. Where the tax invoice indicates "Cash Accounting", deduction arises when you pay the supplier (Tenth Schedule item 13); this is not a rule for every purchase paid in cash.

| Blocked (item 3(1)) | Exception (item 3(2) and (1)(c)) |
| --- | --- |
| Tobacco and tobacco products; alcoholic beverages; works of art, collectors' items and antiques | Bought for resale in the normal course of business |
| Motor vehicles, vessels and aircraft, including hire and leasing, and their repair, maintenance and fuel | Resale; carriage of goods or passengers for a consideration; hire with a driver or self-drive hire (self-drive fuel stays blocked); driving instruction; vehicles designed for goods with seats next to the driver, or with seats for nine or more; listed vessel and aircraft charter and commercial uses |
| Receptions, entertainment and hospitality | Where you provide them for consideration in the normal course of your business |
| Transport or entertainment for employees or officers | Transport on vehicles with a seating capacity of not less than seven |

Partly private use: only the business proportion is input tax.

Partial exemption: for mixed deductible/non-deductible inputs, the annual ratio, attribution method, rounding and cancellation adjustments: Check this point with one of our partner accountants. The de minimis test is stated under referrals below.

Capital goods ([S.L. 406.12, regs 2-3](https://legislation.mt/getpdf/60225988bc8272018c0f72fb)) must be acquired for long-term use as fixed assets, not resale inventory. Small equipment, working tools and office furniture below €1,160 are outside the scheme. For non-property capital goods, the 5-year adjustment period starts on the first day of the month of the tax period during which the right to deduct arises; for immovable property, the 20-year period starts on the first day of the first month of that period. If the goods or construction works are not used in that period, it starts on the first day of the month of the tax period during which they are first used (reg. 3). Annual adjustments use one-fifth or one-twentieth; disposal, cessation or wholly private/non-deductible use can require one adjustment for the remaining period (regs 4-5). For the actual adjustment and the €1,160 price/open-market-value boundary where VAT treatment matters: Check this point with one of our partner accountants. Keep a list of the capital goods subject to adjustment, showing the input tax deducted and each adjustment. For these goods, the six-year retention period in art. 48(4) starts from the end of the adjustment period (regs 6 and 7).

Credit notes and price reductions ([Act, Tenth Schedule item 11](https://legislation.mt/getpdf/6ac352947b5e681a8405fbf4)): if the taxable value of a supply or intra-EU acquisition is reduced after it takes place, the output tax on the reduction is deducted in the period in which the cause of the reduction occurs. You do not amend the original period. The condition is that the output tax on the original value was properly accounted for.

Foreign currency ([Act, Seventh Schedule item 8](https://legislation.mt/getpdf/6ac352947b5e681a8405fbf4)): convert to euro at the latest selling rate applied by commercial banks in Malta at the time the tax becomes chargeable. The latest European Central Bank rate at that time is also accepted. Imports follow the customs valuation rules. This transaction-conversion rule does not govern OSS return reporting: that uses the ECB rate on the last day of the tax period, or the next publication day if none is published that day (Fourteenth Schedule Part Seven section 3 item 8). OSS preparation remains referred.

Excess credit ([Act, art. 24](https://legislation.mt/getpdf/6ac352947b5e681a8405fbf4)): if deductions exceed output tax, the excess is carried forward or refunded. A refund is due not later than 5 months after the later of the return's due date or its filing date. The Commissioner can extend this by up to twelve months for verification by written notice; that extension alone does not suspend refund interest (art. 24(4)). The refund is withheld while a VAT or income tax return is missing. Refund interest excludes the periods of missing returns or failure to produce information requested for verification (art. 24(2)-(3)).

## Cross-border: reverse charge, recapitulative statements and OSS

### Reverse charge ([Act, art. 20 and Third Schedule](https://legislation.mt/getpdf/6ac352947b5e681a8405fbf4))

- Services bought by a business: taxed where the customer is established (Third Schedule Part Two item 2(a)). When the supplier is not established in Malta and not registered under Article 10, the Maltese customer pays the VAT under the following art. 20(2) conditions. A Malta fixed establishment that does not intervene in the taxable supply does not prevent this treatment (art. 20(2) proviso). For B2B services taxed in Malta under the general rule, this means any taxable person, including an Article 11 person, and any VAT-identified non-taxable legal person (art. 20(2)(b)). For other goods and services it means an Article 10 or Article 12 person, or a VAT-identified non-taxable legal person (art. 20(2)(c)).
- Article 10 customer: declare the VAT as output tax and deduct it as input tax on the return for the period in which the tax becomes chargeable (art. 22(1) and (3)(a); Tenth Schedule item 2(2)(b)), subject to the blocks above. For a fully taxable business the net is nil.
- Article 12 customer (including an Article 11 person that registered under Article 12): pays the VAT by the 15th of the second month after the invoice date or the month of receipt, whichever is earlier (art. 21(3)), with a notice of payment (art. 30A). No deduction.
- Goods bought from another Member State by an Article 10 person are an intra-EU acquisition, taxed at the rate for the same goods in Malta. Declare and deduct as above.
- Foreign VAT is not Maltese input tax. If charged incorrectly on a service whose place of supply is Malta and whose customer is liable under art. 20(2), request correction and still account for Maltese VAT. Legitimate foreign VAT, such as VAT on accommodation abroad, follows the foreign recovery rules; do not treat an incorrectly charged amount as automatically eligible for a foreign refund.

### Sales to other EU countries

- Under the general B2B place-of-supply rule, services to a business established in another Member State are taxed there. Establish the place of supply, including any exception, and the customer's sole VAT liability before using the no-Malta-VAT/Article 10 or 12 registration conclusion; not every foreign business service follows this rule ([Act, Third Schedule Part Two item 2 and art. 12(3a)](https://legislation.mt/getpdf/6ac352947b5e681a8405fbf4)).
- Goods to a business in another Member State: exempt with credit only if the customer's valid and active VAT number of another Member State is on the invoice, the goods leave Malta, and you file a correct recapitulative statement, subject to the Commissioner accepting a duly justified shortcoming in that statement condition (Fifth Schedule Part One item 3(1)). Supplies by Article 11 and 11B persons and supplies subject to the profit-margin scheme in Part Two of the Fourteenth Schedule are excluded.

### Recapitulative statements ([S.L. 406.14](https://legislation.mt/getpdf/678a0da50dba9f0950780cb6))

Article 10 persons report the qualifying intra-EU goods, triangulation onward supplies and recipient-liable services listed in art. 30(2). Services exempt from VAT in the Member State where taxable are excluded. Call-off-stock reporting requires additional information under S.L. 406.14: Check this point with one of our partner accountants.

| Filer | Period | Due |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Default | Each calendar month | 15th of the following month |
| Goods: quarterly total, excluding VAT, not over €50,000 in the quarter or in any of the previous four quarters | May opt for each calendar quarter | 15th of the month after the quarter |
| Goods: quarterly total passes €50,000 during the quarter | Monthly, from the end of the month in which it is passed; a statement is due for each month already elapsed in the quarter | 15th of the month after the last month covered |
| Services only | May opt for each calendar quarter | 15th of the month after the quarter |
| Both goods and services | Follow the goods rules | As for goods |

### OSS for sales to EU consumers ([Act, Third Schedule and Fourteenth Schedule Part Seven](https://legislation.mt/getpdf/6ac352947b5e681a8405fbf4))

- Intra-EU distance sales of goods, together with telecommunications, broadcasting and electronically supplied services to consumers in other Member States, stay taxed in Malta while their total, excluding VAT, does not exceed €10,000 in the current calendar year and did not in the previous one. This applies only to a supplier established in one Member State, and for the goods limb only where the goods are dispatched or transported from that State to another Member State (Third Schedule Part One item 4(5), Part Two item 10). The dispatch-origin condition follows the [Commission OSS threshold guidance, section 9](https://vat-one-stop-shop.ec.europa.eu/one-stop-shop_en). A foreign-stock shipment is not covered merely because the supplier has a single establishment. Above that total, from the moment it is passed, they are taxed in the customer's State. A below-threshold destination-taxation option requires written notice in a form acceptable to the Commissioner, effective no earlier than thirty days after furnishing it; it lasts at least two calendar years (Third Schedule Part One item 4(5)(c), Part Two item 10(4)).
- For transactions eligible for the Union OSS scheme, declare the covered foreign VAT on one quarterly return filed through MTCA, due by the end of the month after the quarter, even if nothing was sold. OSS records must be kept for ten years from 31 December of the year of the transaction. For scheme eligibility and preparing the actual OSS return: Check this point with one of our partner accountants.

## Worked cases

Hypothetical figures, for tax year 2026 periods.

**Case 1: deadline for a March quarter.** An Article 10 person's tax period is January to March 2026. The period ends in March; the second month after March is May, so the return and payment are due by 15 May 2026 ([art. 27(1) and art. 21(1)](https://legislation.mt/getpdf/6ac352947b5e681a8405fbf4)). If the return is filed electronically by 22 May 2026, no late-filing penalty is due (art. 42(1)(d)), and no interest is due if the payment is made with it (art. 21(4A)).

**Case 2: a simple Article 10 return.** Malta sales of €10,000 at 18% give output tax of €1,800 ([Act, art. 19](https://legislation.mt/getpdf/6ac352947b5e681a8405fbf4)). Purchases for the business of €2,000 at 18% give input tax of €360. There is also a car lease invoice in the same period. If no listed vehicle-block exception applies, its VAT is left out and payable is €1,800 minus €360 = €1,440. The case facts do not establish whether an exception applies. Before using the actual payable total: Check this point with one of our partner accountants. (Tenth Schedule item 3(2)).

**Case 3: an Article 11 consultant buys software from an Irish company.** The consultant is registered under Article 11. On 10 March 2026 an Irish supplier invoices €1,000 for a software subscription, with no VAT. The consultant receives a service on which it must pay the VAT under art. 20(2), so it must also register under Article 12 (art. 11(10), art. 12(3)) ([Act](https://legislation.mt/getpdf/6ac352947b5e681a8405fbf4)). VAT due: €1,000 at 18% = €180. Payment is due by 15 May 2026 only if the earlier of the invoice month and the service-receipt month is March (art. 21(3)). The receipt month is not supplied, and an earlier receipt would bring the deadline forward. For the actual payment deadline: Check this point with one of our partner accountants. It cannot deduct the €180, and it still charges no VAT on its own sales.

**Case 4: a late return.** An Article 10 return for January to March 2026 shows net tax of €5,000. It was due on 15 May 2026 but filed and paid on 20 July 2026: three months or part months late, so the seven-day relief does not apply ([art. 38(1) and art. 21(4)](https://legislation.mt/getpdf/6ac352947b5e681a8405fbf4)). If the €5,000 is the Article 38(1) base (output less deductions before any carried credit), the higher of the two limbs applies for every month or part: max(1% × €5,000, €20) × 3 = €150, below the €250 cap. If the stated net figure includes carried credit, the penalty base must first be established: Check this point with one of our partner accountants. Interest ([S.L. 406.19](https://legislation.mt/getpdf/632835ddda6f862638d03ac1)): 0.6% of €5,000 = €30 a month, three months = €90.

**Case 5: leaving Article 11.** A shop registered under Article 11 had 2025 turnover of €31,000, so it qualified at the start of 2026 ([Act, Sixth Schedule](https://legislation.mt/getpdf/6ac352947b5e681a8405fbf4)). Its 2026 turnover passes €35,000 on 10 September 2026. It stops qualifying on that date and must apply to cancel by 25 September 2026 (fifteen days, art. 11(5)(b)). If it applies, the cancellation takes effect on 1 October 2026 (art. 11(7)). Because it makes taxable supplies, it is registered under Article 10 from that same date (art. 10(6)(c), (7a)), and charges VAT on sales from then. If the Commissioner cancels under art. 11(8) instead, both dates are 10 September 2026. Missing the cancellation deadline costs the higher of 10% of the first period's net tax and €100 for each month or part month, capped at €500; the clock ends at the earlier of application or Commissioner cancellation (art. 40).

## When to refuse or refer

- Partial exemption: the business makes both deductible supplies and exempt-without-credit supplies (for example lettings, insurance, finance, medical or education) and there is mixed deductible/non-deductible input. The de minimis exception requires the non-deductible amount to be strictly less than €20 multiplied by the months or parts of months in the period; equality does not qualify. Refer the calculation rather than treating equality as automatically deductible. The annual ratio needs a year-end calculation ([Act, Tenth Schedule items 6 and 9](https://legislation.mt/getpdf/6ac352947b5e681a8405fbf4)).
- Capital goods adjustments: a change of use, sale or cessation within the 5-year or 20-year period.
- Margin schemes for second-hand goods, works of art, collectors' items and antiques, and travel agents.
- Several persons registered as a single taxable person (VAT group).
- A supplier not established in Malta, with or without a representative.
- Preparing an OSS or import-scheme return, or Article 11A and 11B cases.
- Immovable property transfers or developments, investment gold, new means of transport, excise goods, call-off stock.
- An assessment, provisional assessment, investigation or appeal to the Administrative Review Tribunal. The thirty-day assessment-appeal period is not a universal reference deadline: the Ninth Schedule provides a two-year route for a non-assessment administrative-penalty reference. Hold the actual route/deadline until the decision is classified ([Act, arts 43-44 and Ninth Schedule](https://legislation.mt/getpdf/6ac352947b5e681a8405fbf4)).
- VAT refund claims under the Eighth and Thirteenth Directives (refunds of VAT to businesses established in another EU country or outside the EU), and VAT refunds to travellers from outside the EU.
- Anything the client cannot evidence with invoices.
- A request about Maltese income tax: this skill covers VAT only.

No administrative penalty is due where there is a reasonable excuse. Lack of funds, or relying on someone else, is not a reasonable excuse. The Commissioner may remit part of a penalty or, for a genuine mistake, all of it (art. 42).

**Calendar adjustment:** under [art. 73(5)](https://legislation.mt/getpdf/6ac352947b5e681a8405fbf4), a statutory filing or payment date falling on a Saturday, Sunday, national day or public holiday moves to the first following working day other than Saturday. Apply this domestic rule before calculating lateness. OSS return deadlines do not move for weekends or public holidays; OSS payment is tied to submission and must be made no later than that return deadline ([Commission: Declare and pay in OSS](https://vat-one-stop-shop.ec.europa.eu/one-stop-shop/declare-and-pay-oss_en); Fourteenth Schedule Part Seven).

### Interest ([S.L. 406.19](https://legislation.mt/getpdf/632835ddda6f862638d03ac1))

Unpaid VAT carries interest of 0.6% for each month or part month from the due date (Act, art. 21(4)). Balances that became payable before 1 September 2022 stay at 0.33%. For interest suspension during an appeal or cessation on fully appropriated payment under art. 21(4)-(4B): Check this point with one of our partner accountants. The same rate applies to late refunds, excluding periods of missing returns or failure to provide requested verification information; an extension for verification alone does not suspend it (art. 24(2)-(4)).

### Records ([Act, art. 48 and Eleventh Schedule](https://legislation.mt/getpdf/6ac352947b5e681a8405fbf4))

- Keep full records of every transaction for at least six years from the end of the year they relate to. For a late return or a correction, the six years run from the date of filing or of the correction request. For capital goods, they run from the end of the 5-year or 20-year adjustment period, and the list of capital goods is part of the records ([S.L. 406.12, regs 6-7](https://legislation.mt/getpdf/60225988bc8272018c0f72fb)).
- An Article 10 person keeps: proper accounts; a VAT account for each period that ties to every figure on the return; an annual VAT account; copies of invoices issued; all invoices received; customs documents; fiscal receipts issued; credit and debit notes; registers for goods sent to other Member States, goods received for work, and call-off stock.
- Store invoices in the form they were sent or received, paper or electronic. If stored electronically, MTCA must be able to access and download them online.
- An Article 11 or 11A person keeps: proper accounts, fiscal receipts issued, fiscal receipts and tax invoices received, credit and debit notes, and other documents given to customers.
- A person not registered under Article 10 keeps records of its intra-EU acquisitions and reverse-charge purchases.
- If records requested by written notice are not produced within thirty days without reasonable excuse, they cannot be relied on later in an assessment or appeal.

## Completion checklist

- Registration article, number and tax period confirmed from the certificate.
- Every sale placed in one of ([Act, Eighth Schedule](https://legislation.mt/getpdf/6ac352947b5e681a8405fbf4)): 18%, 12%, 7%, 5%, exempt with credit, exempt without credit, outside the scope.
- Rates checked against the Eighth Schedule item (CN code for goods).
- Ordinary intra-EU goods exemption: valid active other-State VAT number, invoice, movement and recap conditions checked, including the profit-margin exclusion and any accepted duly justified recap shortcoming. For EU B2B services, establish customer status and the applicable place/liability rule separately. Apply the conditional evidence rules and service-specific permissions in [Implementing Regulation 282/2011, arts 17(1)–18(2)](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/ALL/?uri=CELEX%3A02011R0282-20250414), including its permitted non-taxable treatment; do not use the goods-exemption checklist as a universal service rule. Hold actual evidence and customer classification until those conditions are established ([Act, Fifth Schedule Part One item 3 and Third Schedule Part Two item 2](https://legislation.mt/getpdf/6ac352947b5e681a8405fbf4)).
- Reverse-charge purchases in both output and input tax (Article 10), or paid under Article 12.
- Blocked input VAT removed; private use apportioned; partial exemption referred if present.
- Long-term fixed-asset capital goods identified, with the €1,160 named-equipment exclusion applied; deduction period and first-use date listed, delayed-first-use cases referred ([S.L. 406.12](https://legislation.mt/getpdf/60225988bc8272018c0f72fb)).
- Excess credit brought forward reconciled to the last return; amounts already deducted or already refundable excluded, with refunds and set-offs checked (art. 21(1)).
- Errors from earlier periods: 5% rule applied, or the art. 28(1) form prepared subject to art. 28(3)-(4) assessment restrictions ([Act, Eleventh Schedule item 4](https://legislation.mt/getpdf/6ac352947b5e681a8405fbf4)).
- Authenticated form fields and submission screens referred to a partner accountant.
- Ordinary Article 10 return/payment: second-month fifteenth and the separate seven-day filing/payment relief conditions checked. Use the distinct Article 11, 11A, Article 12 goods/service, recap and OSS deadlines above for those filings; do not apply the Article 10 date universally.
- Recapitulative statement and OSS return filed where required.
- Credit notes and price reductions deducted in the period they occur; ordinary foreign-currency transactions converted at the chargeability-date bank selling or ECB rate; OSS reporting uses the period-end ECB rule and remains referred.
- VAT account and supporting documents kept for at least six years (capital goods: six years from the end of the adjustment period, plus the capital goods list).


## Two worked examples that recompute

For these hypothetical transactions, registration, place of supply, chargeable period and rate eligibility have been established under the [VAT Act, arts 19-23 and Tenth Schedule](https://legislation.mt/getpdf/6ac352947b5e681a8405fbf4). Case A has only deductible domestic purchases. Case B has a taxable general-rule foreign service supplied in Malta; the recipient is Article 11/12 and has no input credit. They do not validate portal boxes or specialist classifications.

```oa-check
name: Article 10 domestic taxable return with fully deductible purchases
inputs:
  sales: 10000
  purchases: 2000
  vat_rate: 0.18
steps:
  - output_tax: sales * vat_rate
  - input_credit: purchases * vat_rate
  - payable: output_tax - input_credit
expected:
  output_tax: 1800
  input_credit: 360
  payable: 1440
```

```oa-check
name: Article 11 and 12 recipient pays service reverse charge without credit
inputs:
  service_fee: 1000
  vat_rate: 0.18
steps:
  - output_tax: service_fee * vat_rate
  - input_credit: 0
  - payable: output_tax - input_credit
expected:
  output_tax: 180
  input_credit: 0
  payable: 180
```
