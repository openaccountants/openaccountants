# Malta residence programmes for wealthy families: non-dom residence, the Global Residence Programme, permanent residence and the nomad permit

## Scope and who this is for
Figures are for tax year 2026. Malta taxes by year of assessment: income of calendar 2026 is assessed in year of assessment 2027. This Guide is for a principal, a family office or an adviser comparing the ways a wealthy, internationally mobile family can live in Malta. It sets the four routes side by side and states what each one taxes, what it costs and what keeps it alive. It does not repeat the full non-dom rules: residence, domicile, evidencing remittances and filing are in `mt-non-dom`, and the wider move is in `moving-to-malta-tax-residence`. Ordinary rate tables are in `malta-income-tax`. Related Guides publishing alongside this one: `family-office-residency-regimes-compared`, `mt-family-office-setup` and `mt-inheritance-and-succession`. Statutes are cited from the consolidated texts on legislation.mt. All worked figures in the example section are hypothetical.

## Ask the client first
- What nationality does each family member hold, including any second passport from an EU, EEA or Swiss state or Malta? This decides which programme is open, or whether one is open at all.
- Will the principal be domiciled in Malta, and is the spouse ordinarily resident and domiciled in Malta? Either answer can remove the remittance basis.
- Does anyone hold, or plan to apply for, long-term resident status or a permanent residence card or certificate? Holding that status ends the remittance basis from the year it is granted (art. 4(1)). Under the programme Rules even applying for it ends programme status and brings worldwide taxation (rules 2, 5(4)(a) and 6(1)(c)).
- How much foreign income, as opposed to capital and gains, will be brought to Malta each year, and how much Malta-source income is there?
- How many days a year will each person spend in each other country?
- Who will live in the Malta home: which children, at what ages, working or not, and any parents? Will the property ever be let?

## The method, step by step
1. Fix each family member's nationality and immigration position first. The Global Residence Programme is for third-country nationals (rule 4(a), [S.L. 123.148](https://legislation.mt/eli/sl/123.148/eng/pdf)); the Residence Programme is for EU, EEA or Swiss nationals who are not Maltese (rule 4(a), [S.L. 123.160](https://legislation.mt/eli/sl/123.160/eng/pdf)); the permanent residence programme is for third-country nationals (regulation 15(1)(a), [S.L. 217.26](https://legislation.mt/eli/sl/217.26/eng/pdf)).
2. Decide whether the principal will be ordinarily resident and not domiciled in Malta. That opens the remittance basis in art. 4(1) of the [Income Tax Act](https://legislation.mt/eli/cap/123/eng/pdf). Test the carve-outs in the same proviso (long-term resident, permanent residence card or certificate, spouse ordinarily resident and domiciled in Malta). The full test is in `mt-non-dom`.
3. Map the family's income by source: foreign income, foreign capital gains, Malta-source income. Estimate how much foreign income will be received in Malta each year. Compare the ordinary rates plus the non-dom minimum tax ([Cap. 123 art. 56(27)](https://legislation.mt/eli/cap/123/eng/pdf)) with the 15% programme rate and its minimum tax ([S.L. 123.148 rule 5](https://legislation.mt/eli/sl/123.148/eng/pdf)).
4. If a programme fits, check the property, insurance, resources, language and fit-and-proper conditions in rule 4 and the cessation events in rule 6 of [S.L. 123.148](https://legislation.mt/eli/sl/123.148/eng/pdf) or [S.L. 123.160](https://legislation.mt/eli/sl/123.160/eng/pdf). The application goes through an authorised registered mandatary (rule 10).
5. If the family wants a permanent immigration status, test the permanent residence programme separately: costs and the capital test in [S.L. 217.26](https://legislation.mt/eli/sl/217.26/eng/pdf), due diligence in regulations 13 and 14. It changes nothing on the tax side by itself.
6. For a family member who will work remotely for foreign employers or clients on a nomad permit, apply [S.L. 123.210](https://legislation.mt/eli/sl/123.210/eng/pdf) to the main applicant only.
7. Diarise the yearly duties: the programme minimum tax and the rule 4 return by 30 April, the four-week cessation notice, and the mandatary's 31 December long-term resident check ([S.L. 123.148 rules 5 and 6](https://legislation.mt/eli/sl/123.148/eng/pdf)).

### 1. Non-domiciled ordinary residence and the remittance basis
| Item | Value | Note (verbatim) | Source |

The rule. Under art. 4(1) proviso (i) of the [Income Tax Act](https://legislation.mt/eli/cap/123/eng/pdf), a person who is not ordinarily resident in Malta or not domiciled in Malta pays tax on income arising outside Malta only on "the amount received in Malta". Under proviso (ii), no tax is payable on capital gains arising outside Malta to such a person, whether or not the gain is brought to Malta. Malta-source income is taxed in the ordinary way at the art. 56(1) rates (for 2026 income the rates are those marked applicable from year of assessment 2027; tables in `malta-income-tax`). The consolidated text prints no start date for provisos (i) and (ii).

Who is out. The same proviso says items (i) and (ii) do not apply to a long-term resident, or to the holder of a permanent residence certificate or card, for income of the year that status or right is granted and later years. This carve-out was added by Act VII of 2018, applicable from year of assessment 2019. They also do not apply to "an individual whose spouse is ordinarily resident and domiciled in Malta" ([Cap. 123 art. 4(1)](https://legislation.mt/eli/cap/123/eng/pdf)).

The minimum tax. Art. 56(27) of [Cap. 123](https://legislation.mt/eli/cap/123/eng/pdf) (added by Act VII of 2018, applicable from year of assessment 2019, amended by Act VII of 2019) applies to an individual who is ordinarily resident but not domiciled in Malta, to whom provisos (i) and (ii) of art. 4(1) apply, and who "derives income ... amounting to not less than thirty five thousand euro (€35,000) ... arising outside Malta and referred to in proviso (i) to sub-article (1) of article 4 of the Act, but which is not received or not fully received in Malta". The threshold in the table above is therefore tested on the total foreign income derived, including any part brought to Malta. The words "not fully received" show that income partly brought to Malta is still caught; only a person who brings all of it to Malta is outside the minimum. `mt-non-dom` applies the same test. For a married couple taxed jointly the income of both spouses counts. Such a person has a tax liability of at least the minimum tax in the table. If the tax on income actually charged is lower, the person is deemed to have received enough extra foreign income to reach it. It does not apply to someone taxed under a scheme that already sets a minimum tax, such as the programmes in section 2. If the person proves to the Commissioner that the tax without provisos (i) and (ii) of art. 4(1) would have been less than the minimum tax, the liability is capped at that lower amount. If the foreign income derived is below the threshold, there is no minimum tax. The tax compared with the minimum is the tax "before taking into account any relief granted in terms of articles 76 to 89 of the Act", and tax paid under the Act by withholding or otherwise on all income counts towards it; income from transfers of immovable property under article 5A is left out (art. 56(27)).

The instruments. The Global Residence Programme Rules are S.L. 123.148 (L.N. 167 of 2013, heading date 1 July 2013, amended by L.N. 267 of 2014 and L.N. 69 of 2020). The Residence Programme Rules are S.L. 123.160 (L.N. 270 of 2014, amended by L.N. 69 of 2020; the consolidated heading also prints 1 July 2013). Both rest on art. 56(23) of [Cap. 123](https://legislation.mt/eli/cap/123/eng/pdf), which covers an individual granted special tax status "after 1 January 2011" and requires a minimum tax. The two sets of rules carry the same figures. They differ on nationality and on the residence status that excludes the person: a long-term resident for the [Global Residence Programme](https://legislation.mt/eli/sl/123.148/eng/pdf), a "permanent resident of Malta" under the EU free movement order for the [Residence Programme](https://legislation.mt/eli/sl/123.160/eng/pdf).

What is taxed at 15%. Rule 5(1) of [S.L. 123.148](https://legislation.mt/eli/sl/123.148/eng/pdf) applies the rate in the table to income arising outside Malta in the year before the year of assessment which is received in Malta. In the year status is granted it covers foreign income received in Malta during the whole of that year. It covers the beneficiary, the beneficiary's spouse, and children within paragraphs (b) and (d) of "dependant" (minor children, and adult children unable to maintain themselves because of serious illness or disability). Double tax relief under art. 74(a) and (b) of the Act may be claimed.

The minimum tax. The minimum in the table is per year of assessment, for the foreign income of all those persons together. It is payable in full in the year status is granted and in the year it ends, with no reduction for a part year. It is due by 30 April of the year immediately preceding the relevant year of assessment, with a return proving the rule 4 conditions still hold (no return in the year of grant). In the year of grant, if status will not be granted before 30 April, it is paid before status is granted. Tax paid under rule 5(1) is not refundable (rule 5(3), [S.L. 123.148](https://legislation.mt/eli/sl/123.148/eng/pdf)).

What is NOT covered at 15%.
- Income of the beneficiary, spouse and (b) or (d) children that is chargeable but not at the 15% rate, such as Malta-source income, is charged "as separate income" at the rule 5(2) rate in the table. That is a flat rate, not the ordinary art. 56(1) bands ([S.L. 123.148 rule 5(2)](https://legislation.mt/eli/sl/123.148/eng/pdf)).
- The Rules do not say how rule 5(2) applies to Malta income that the Act already taxes by a final withholding tax or a special rate, such as investment income or the rental option. This Guide does not settle that point; refer it.
- Rule 5 gives no rate for the other dependants: children under 25 who are not economically active (paragraph (c)) and dependent brothers, sisters, parents and grandparents (paragraph (e)). They may live in the property, but their own income is outside rules 5(1) and 5(2) and follows the Income Tax Act ([S.L. 123.148 rule 2](https://legislation.mt/eli/sl/123.148/eng/pdf)).
- A beneficiary or dependant who is a long-term resident is taxable on worldwide income at the art. 56 rates (rule 5(4)(a), [S.L. 123.148](https://legislation.mt/eli/sl/123.148/eng/pdf)); for the Residence Programme the same applies to a "permanent resident of Malta" ([S.L. 123.160 rule 5(4)(a)](https://legislation.mt/eli/sl/123.160/eng/pdf)). In these Rules "long-term resident" includes a person who applies for long-term resident status, and in the Residence Programme Rules "permanent resident of Malta" includes a person who applies for the right of permanent residence (rule 2 of each). An application alone therefore triggers rule 5(4)(a) and ends status under rule 6(1)(c).
- Foreign capital gains: rule 5 charges "income". The exemption for foreign capital gains is in art. 4(1) proviso (ii) of [Cap. 123](https://legislation.mt/eli/cap/123/eng/pdf) and depends on the person being not ordinarily resident or not domiciled in Malta, not on the programme.

Who counts as a dependant (rule 2). The spouse or a person in a stable and durable relationship; minor children (adopted or in care and custody included); children under 25 who are not economically active; adult children unable to maintain themselves through serious illness or disability; and dependent brothers, sisters and relatives in the ascending line. In every case the person must not benefit under the listed older schemes and must reside with the beneficiary in the qualifying property ([S.L. 123.148](https://legislation.mt/eli/sl/123.148/eng/pdf)).

Conditions to qualify (rule 4). The person must prove ALL of these: the nationality test; no benefit under the listed older schemes (Residents Scheme, high net worth rules, Malta Retirement Programme, qualifying employment in innovation and creativity, highly qualified persons); a qualifying property holding occupied as primary residence; stable and regular resources for the family without Maltese social assistance; a valid travel document; sickness insurance for all risks across the EU for the family; the ability to communicate adequately in one of the official languages of Malta; and being fit and proper ([S.L. 123.148 rule 4](https://legislation.mt/eli/sl/123.148/eng/pdf)). Only the beneficiary, dependants and household staff may live in the property, and the Commissioner must be notified that the dependants or staff will reside there.

Property. A qualifying owned property is bought for at least the purchase price in the table; a qualifying rented property is leased for at least the yearly rent in the table. The lower figures apply in Gozo and in the south of Malta, which is the list of localities in the Schedule to the Rules (Birżebbuġia to Żurrieq). A property bought before the Rules were published for less can qualify on an independent architect's valuation at the date of application ([S.L. 123.148 rule 2](https://legislation.mt/eli/sl/123.148/eng/pdf)). The reduced application fee is only for an OWNED property in the south of Malta, not Gozo and not a rented property (rule 3(1)).

How status ends (rule 6). Status ends if the person becomes a Maltese, EEA or Swiss national (Residence Programme: a Maltese or third country national); stops holding a qualifying property holding, including by letting or subletting it; becomes a long-term resident (Residence Programme: a permanent resident of Malta); loses the sickness insurance; if the stay is not in the public interest; or if the person stays in any other jurisdiction for more than one hundred and eighty-three days in a calendar year. The person must notify within four weeks of becoming aware, or the person responsible is charged the penalty in the table. The Ministers may condone a failure caused by unforeseen circumstances that was notified and that the person tried to remedy ([S.L. 123.148 rule 6](https://legislation.mt/eli/sl/123.148/eng/pdf)). The day test in rule 6(1)(f) is about time in any other jurisdiction; the Rules print no minimum number of days in Malta. Status also ends from the relevant year of assessment if the person notifies the Commissioner of the intention not to keep it (rule 6(3)), and from the beginning of the relevant year of assessment if the person is in breach of the Act or the Income Tax Management Act (rule 6(4)).

Mandatary duties. Every application, return and notice is filed by one authorised registered mandatary (rule 10). Each year the mandatary must check whether any beneficiary or dependant was a long-term resident at 31 December and notify the Commissioner by the following 30 April, or pay the mandatary penalty in the table (rule 5(4)(b), [S.L. 123.148](https://legislation.mt/eli/sl/123.148/eng/pdf)).

### 3. Malta Permanent Residence Programme
What it is. The Malta Permanent Residence Programme Regulations, S.L. 217.26 (L.N. 121 of 2021, heading date 29 March 2021, amended by L.N. 57 and L.N. 310 of 2024 and L.N. 146 of 2025), are made under art. 7A of the Immigration Act. A certificate lets the beneficiary and the dependants on it "reside, settle or stay indefinitely in Malta" while they meet the conditions (regulation 10, [S.L. 217.26](https://legislation.mt/eli/sl/217.26/eng/pdf)). The regulations contain no income tax rule. A certificate holder's tax depends on residence, ordinary residence and domicile under [Cap. 123](https://legislation.mt/eli/cap/123/eng/pdf), as for anyone else; section 1 applies.

Who. A third-country national (not Maltese, EEA or Swiss) aged 18 or over, applying through a licensed agent (regulations 4 and 9). Dependants (regulation 3): the spouse or equivalent partner; a child under 18; a child aged 18 to under 29 who is unmarried and principally dependent on the main applicant; a parent or grandparent of the main applicant or spouse who is not in full-time employment and is principally dependent; and an adult child certified as having a disability. The per-dependant fee is not charged for the spouse, minor children or adult children with a disability (First Schedule, [S.L. 217.26](https://legislation.mt/eli/sl/217.26/eng/pdf)).

Costs and timing. On application, part of the administration fee is paid within one month, or the Agency may refuse the application at once. Within two months of the Letter of Approval in Principle, the balance and any dependant fees are paid. Within eight months of that letter, the applicant pays the contribution, presents title to the qualifying property, makes the donation and proves health insurance (regulation 9, [S.L. 217.26](https://legislation.mt/eli/sl/217.26/eng/pdf)). The amounts of the fee instalments, contribution, donation and residence card fee are in `mt-permanent-residence-programme`.

Property and capital. The property thresholds (in `mt-permanent-residence-programme`) apply in Malta and Gozo alike; there is no reduced figure for the south. The capital test is (a) OR (b), not both: test (a) or test (b), each a total asset figure with a minimum financial part (regulation 9(2); figures in `mt-permanent-residence-programme`). The beneficiary must hold the qualifying property for at least five years from the appointed day and then still hold a residential property in Malta or Gozo, owned or leased (regulation 15(1)(c)). The capital must be held for five years from the appointed day (regulation 15(1)(g), [S.L. 217.26](https://legislation.mt/eli/sl/217.26/eng/pdf)).

Due diligence. The Agency runs the due diligence, uses one or more internationally recognised third-party providers for every main applicant and dependant, and needs the Approvals Board's authorisation before issuing a certificate (regulation 14). The eligibility rules in regulation 13 exclude, among others, anyone listed by INTERPOL or EUROPOL, named in sanctions the Agency follows, or previously refused a certificate or Maltese citizenship. Conduct certificates are needed for the main applicant and each dependant older than 14 (regulation 12, [S.L. 217.26](https://legislation.mt/eli/sl/217.26/eng/pdf)). The Agency monitors compliance every year for the first five years (regulation 10(3)).

Combining with a tax programme. The exclusion list in regulation 15(1)(b) of [S.L. 217.26](https://legislation.mt/eli/sl/217.26/eng/pdf) names the Residence Programme Rules but not the Global Residence Programme Rules. Rule 4(b) of [S.L. 123.148](https://legislation.mt/eli/sl/123.148/eng/pdf) does not name the permanent residence programme. Neither text bars holding both. Confirm with Residency Malta and the Commissioner before relying on it.

### 4. Nomad residence permit: the tax rule
| Item | Value | Note (verbatim) | Source |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Rate on income from authorised work (rule 3(1)) | 10% | "income tax at the rate of ten percent (10%) on his chargeable income derived from authorised work" | https://legislation.mt/eli/sl/123.210/eng/pdf |

The Nomad Residence Permits (Income Tax) Rules, S.L. 123.210 (L.N. 277 of 2023, heading date 7 December 2023), tax the "eligible main applicant" holding a nomad residence permit at the rate in the table on chargeable income from "authorised work". That means remote work for an employer that is not resident in Malta and has no fixed place of business there, or self-employed work for clients that meet the same test. Double tax relief under Part X of the Act applies. That income counts as the first part of the person's chargeable income. No tax is due on it before the end of twelve months from the permit date or 1 January 2024, whichever is later, unless the person files a declaration with Residency Malta that the residence is not merely casual (rule 3(3), [S.L. 123.210](https://legislation.mt/eli/sl/123.210/eng/pdf)). Income other than authorised work follows the Act (rule 3(4)). Income of a family member on the permit is not governed by these Rules (rule 3(5)).

The permit's income threshold is set on the Residency Malta nomad pages, which refused automated access (error code 403). The Residency Malta home page and the Immigration Regulations (S.L. 217.04) do not print it. This Guide therefore gives no threshold.

The Residency Malta home page, read 14 November 2026, names the permanent residence programme, the Nomad Residence Permit and the Malta Startup Residence Programme; it does not name a "Family Office Residency" programme, so none is covered here; see `family-office-residency-regimes-compared`.

## Worked cases
### Worked hypothetical: a family remitting part of its foreign income
All amounts in this section are hypothetical. Facts: a married couple, both non-EU nationals, with two minor children and a daughter aged 20 at university who is not economically active. They live in an owned home in Malta. In 2026 the couple's foreign investment income (dividends and interest paid without foreign tax) is EUR 400,000. They bring EUR 80,000 of it to Malta and keep EUR 320,000 abroad. The husband also earns Malta-source fees of EUR 10,000. They also sell foreign shares at a gain of EUR 200,000 and bring the proceeds to Malta. Which of these amounts is taxed turns on the remittance rules in [Cap. 123 art. 4(1)](https://legislation.mt/eli/cap/123/eng/pdf).

Route A, Global Residence Programme. Remitted foreign income EUR 80,000 times the 15% rate is EUR 12,000. That is below the minimum tax, so the tax on foreign income is EUR 15,000 ([S.L. 123.148 rule 5(1)](https://legislation.mt/eli/sl/123.148/eng/pdf)). The Malta-source fees of EUR 10,000 at EUR 0.35 per euro give EUR 3,500 (rule 5(2)). Total: EUR 18,500, before any double tax relief. If the couple instead remit EUR 150,000, the 15% charge is EUR 22,500, above the minimum ([S.L. 123.148](https://legislation.mt/eli/sl/123.148/eng/pdf)).

Route B, ordinary residence as non-doms, no programme. Chargeable income is the remitted EUR 80,000 plus the Malta fees of EUR 10,000, so EUR 90,000. Taking the married joint table and no qualifying-child table: EUR 90,000 times 35% minus EUR 10,550 is EUR 20,950 ([Cap. 123 art. 56(1)](https://legislation.mt/eli/cap/123/eng/pdf)). The minimum-tax trigger is met (EUR 400,000 of foreign income derived, not all of it received in Malta), but the tax already exceeds the minimum tax of EUR 5,000, so there is no top-up (art. 56(27)). The two-children tables would lower this figure only if their conditions are met (see `malta-income-tax`).

The gain. Under both routes, if the couple are not domiciled in Malta, the EUR 200,000 gain on foreign shares is not taxed even though it is brought to Malta ([Cap. 123 art. 4(1) proviso (ii)](https://legislation.mt/eli/cap/123/eng/pdf)). This is why the source of every remittance (income, gain or capital) has to be evidenced; see `mt-non-dom`.

The daughter. Under Route A she can live in the property as a paragraph (c) dependant, but rule 5 gives her income no rate. If she has EUR 20,000 of her own foreign income, the 15% rate does not apply to it; her position follows the Income Tax Act ([S.L. 123.148 rules 2 and 5](https://legislation.mt/eli/sl/123.148/eng/pdf)).

**The decision, worked both ways.** On these facts Route A (GRP) costs EUR 18,500 and Route B (ordinary non-dom, married joint table) EUR 20,950, so the programme saves EUR 2,450 a year before its own costs (fee, qualifying property, mandatary). With Malta fees of EUR 10,000 and chargeable income above EUR 60,000, Route B costs 35% x (R + 10,000) minus 10,550 for remitted income R, and Route A stays at EUR 18,500 until 15% of R passes EUR 15,000 (R above EUR 100,000). The two cost the same at R = (18,500 + 10,550) / 0.35 minus 10,000 = EUR 73,000. Below that the ordinary route is cheaper: at EUR 40,000 remitted, chargeable income is EUR 50,000 in the 25% band, Route B is EUR 7,950 against EUR 18,500. Above EUR 73,000 the GRP wins. Programme costs and the non-tax conditions move the crossover up.

```oa-check
name: GRP or ordinary non-dom, decision both ways
inputs:
  remitted_high: 80000
  remitted_low: 40000
  malta_fees: 10000
steps:
  - grp_high: max(pct(remitted_high,15),15000) + pct(malta_fees,35)
  - ord_high: pct(remitted_high + malta_fees,35) - 10550
  - saving_high: ord_high - grp_high
  - grp_low: max(pct(remitted_low,15),15000) + pct(malta_fees,35)
  - ord_low: pct(remitted_low + malta_fees,25) - 4550
  - extra_cost_of_grp_low: grp_low - ord_low
  - crossover: (18500 + 10550) / 0.35 - 10000
expected:
  grp_high: 18500
  ord_high: 20950
  saving_high: 2450
  grp_low: 18500
  ord_low: 7950
  extra_cost_of_grp_low: 10550
  crossover: 73000
```

## When to refuse or refer
- Immigration status, the nomad permit and the permanent residence application: refer to a licensed agent under S.L. 217.26 and to Residency Malta. This Guide does not advise on immigration eligibility.
- Due diligence, sanctions and source-of-wealth questions: refer. The Agency's process and the Approvals Board decide them ([S.L. 217.26 regulations 13 and 14](https://legislation.mt/eli/sl/217.26/eng/pdf)).
- Double tax treaties and credit for foreign tax against the 15% or the minimum tax: refer to a tax adviser who has the treaty in hand.
- Domicile and ordinary residence disputes: use `mt-non-dom` and refer contested cases.
- The departure country's rules (exit taxes, continuing residence, inheritance tax): refer to an adviser in that country.
- Family office structures and the Family Office Residency programme: refer to `mt-family-office-setup` and `family-office-residency-regimes-compared`. Succession: `mt-inheritance-and-succession`.
- Refuse to state a nomad permit income threshold or a family-office residency figure from this Guide: no allowed page prints them.
