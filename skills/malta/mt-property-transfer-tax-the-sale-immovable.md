# Malta property transfer tax and duty on the sale of immovable property, 2026

## Scope

A sale of immovable property situated in Malta carries two separate charges. The **seller** pays the final tax under article 5A of the Income Tax Act, Cap. 123, normally on the **transfer value**, with distinct inherited/older-donation gain rules and exemptions. A loss does not remove a transfer-value charge. The **buyer** pays duty under the Duty on Documents and Transfers Act, Cap. 364. The official MTCA property pages distinguish these seller and buyer charges. The **notary** normally remits both charges after the deed; the buyer pays provisional duty at the promise of sale (*konvenju*).

Figures are for deeds in **2026**. The article 5A rates apply to transfers made on or after 1 January 2015 and are set out here from the current consolidated law read on 26 November 2026.

Covered: individuals and companies, residents and non-residents, selling Malta property bought, inherited or received by donation. This file absorbed `how-to-submit-capital-gain-taxes-in-malta` on 14 November 2026 (the accountant's order of work around the deed), and the article 5A detail previously repeated in [mt-capital-gains](mt-capital-gains.md), which now links here.

Not covered (refer or use the named Guide): the transfer causa mortis and its declaration (only the later sale is covered); share transfers ([selling-a-company-in-malta](selling-a-company-in-malta.md)); article 5 gains on foreign property, shares and other assets ([mt-capital-gains](mt-capital-gains.md)); the assignment of a right under a promise of sale, which "is not regulated by the property transfers tax regime" (MTCA FAQ Q17); projects, emphyteusis and usufruct splits, partitions, special designated areas and AIP permits.

Built from the law: not yet approved by an accountant. Working paper for the accountant supporting the client around the deed; the notary is the collector.

## Ask the client first

1. **When and how was the property acquired?** Bought, inherited (date of death: before 25 November 1992, or after 24 November 1992) or donated (more than five years before this sale?). Get the deed; never accept an oral acquisition value.
2. **Own home?** Owned and occupied as own residence for at least three consecutive years right before the sale, sold within twelve months of moving out, declared as main residence? If held under three years: did the purchase deed declare it the sole ordinary residence, and does the seller own any other residential property?
3. **Works and projects.** In the last five years, did the seller or a related person carry out works needing a development permission, or did a related person own it as part of a project?
4. **Promise of sale.** Is there a konvenju, and was any promise of sale on this property notified to the Commissioner before 17 November 2014 (including one later cancelled or expired)?
5. **Special property.** Urban conservation area or scheduled, restored under a permit; leased to a tenant on a Housing Authority scheme benefit for three years or more, and is the buyer that tenant?
6. **Residence of the seller and the price.** Malta resident? Consideration, market value, and brokerage paid by the seller with invoices. For the buyer: sole ordinary residence, first purchase of any residence?

## The method, step by step

Do these in order. A wrong order changes the rate, not only the arithmetic.

1. **Acquisition date and means first** (purchase, inheritance, donation). This fact set, not the price, picks the rate. For a donation five years or less before the transfer, use the date the donor acquired it (art. 5A(5)(b), third proviso). A company that took the property under an intra-group exemption uses the date the group last acquired it outside that exemption (art. 5A(5)(e) and (f) provisos).
2. **Exemptions** (art. 5A(4)), then **exclusions** (art. 5A(3)). If excluded, the transfer leaves article 5A: test the provisional-tax exceptions in ITMA article 43(2); otherwise 7% provisional tax on consideration within fifteen days under article 43(1)(b), with the applicable income or gain reported under article 4 or 5 ([mt-capital-gains](mt-capital-gains.md)).
3. **Transfer value**: the higher of market value and the consideration (art. 5A(6)(a)), less brokerage paid by the transferor where [S.L. 123.92 rule 3](https://legislation.mt/eli/sl/123.92/eng/pdf) is met: a signed receipt identifies payment date, broker name, identification number, address and amount; the notary records the fee and broker particulars in the deed and submits a certified receipt with the notice. Fees covering several transfers are apportioned by relative transfer value.
4. **Pick the rate** from the table below. The table is a selection map, not a universal priority rule. Test all applicable overrides, exclusions and special reliefs. Start with inheritance and donation rows (5A(5)(b), (c)); the pre-2004 10% (5A(5)(f)), which overrides (a), (d) and (e) but not (b); the restored-property 5% (5A(5)(h)), which overrides (a), (e) and (f); the 2%, 5% and Housing Authority rows must also be tested. For overlapping special rates or a project/legacy case: Check this point with one of our partner accountants.
5. **Inherited after 24 November 1992, or donated more than five years before**: the default is 12% of the excess of transfer value over acquisition value. The transferor may elect, by a declaration recorded in the deed, that this "paragraph (b) shall not apply", and then pays the transfer-value rate. Model both in writing; for a request to change an election after publication: Check this point with one of our partner accountants. (MTCA FAQ Q13 says the seller "can elect either"; the statute makes 12% of the gain the default.)
6. **Buyer's duty**: EUR 5 per EUR 100 or part of the higher of consideration and value, with the reliefs below. Provisional duty is 20% of the duty determinable on the promise date's available information (Cap. 364 article 3(6)); it equals 1% of value only when the unreduced 5% duty applies.
7. **Hand the notary** a computation and the evidence before the deed date, and reconcile it to the notary's own figure.

### Exemptions: no article 5A tax (art. 5A(4))

- **Own residence (5A(4)(c)).** A dwelling house, not part of a project, owned **and** occupied as own residence "for a period of at least three consecutive years immediately preceding the date of transfer", disposed of within twelve months of vacating, and declared as main residence by election. "Own residence" includes garden or grounds and a garage attached or of not more than seventy square metres within five hundred metres, on the same deed (art. 5(5)(c)). Absences abroad for work, study, illness or care count if the premises are not used otherwise; a spouse's or direct ascendant's ownership-and-occupation period counts as the heir's **ownership**, not automatically occupation; an undivided share qualifies proportionately; a part used exclusively for business within two years is excluded proportionately. Housing Authority occupation under its promise of sale can count as ownership; changed residence/business use permits an adjustment. Where spouses occupied it for three years and one leaves due to separation/divorce, vacation is when the other also leaves the sole ordinary residence. For mixed-use or separated-family cases: Check this point with one of our partner accountants.
- **Family donations (5A(4)(a)):** to a spouse, descendant or ascendant in the direct line or their spouse, or with no descendants to a brother or sister or their descendant, or to an approved philanthropic institution.
- **Separation and divorce (5A(4)(d), (e)); intra-group transfers and business incorporation (5A(4)(f), (g))**, with an article 5A(12A) six-year degrouping rule (8%, or 10% for the specified pre-2004 acquisition), subject to its dissolution, ownership and other exceptions; **trusts, exempt persons and 95% shareholder liquidation transfers (5A(4)(h) to (j))**: Check this point with one of our partner accountants.

### Exclusions: outside article 5A (art. 5A(3)), provisional tax needs a separate test

- Non-resident taxed on the gain abroad, with a certificate from the home tax authority, not owned or controlled by or acting for Malta residents (5A(3)(h)).
- One co-owner of a declared sole residence transferring to the other, on election (5A(3)(d)); Government acquisitions meeting the pre-1 November 2005 possession/order, Land Commissioner letter and deed-election conditions (5A(3)(e)); business property used at least three years and replaced within one year solely for a similar business use, with deed election and the two-year retention/use rule (5A(3)(g)); qualifying listed-bond projects, with first-transfer election and prospectus/use-of-proceeds conditions (5A(3)(j)). For these exclusions: Check this point with one of our partner accountants.
- Judicial sale by auction or court winding up (5A(3)(f)), except a judicial auction of property inherited on or after 25 November 1992, which stays in 5A at 7%.
- The ordinary twelve-year election for non-project transfers is closed: its post-2015 conditions included a pre-17 November 2014 promise notice and transfer before 1 January 2016. Project and special-designated-area legacy provisions have further rules and must not be described as universally closed. Check this point with one of our partner accountants.

### Buyer relief and inherited acquisition-value conditions

First-buyer relief is for an onerous transfer qualifying under article 32(4)(a). The post-28 October 2025 rule tests previous inter vivos acquisitions wholly or partly used or intended as residential, including land, airspace and existing/pending residential-development permission. A previous undivided acquisition aggregating less than 25% of real value may be disregarded only under all conditions of the new proviso, including no prior article 32(4)(a) or first-buyer concession. The benefit applies proportionately to the share acquired. The notarial declaration and legal evidence must accompany the statutory notice. For any prior share or unclear history: Check this point with one of our partner accountants.

For inherited acquisition value, [S.L. 123.92 rule 4](https://legislation.mt/eli/sl/123.92/eng/pdf) uses the declared value for inheritance on or after 25 November 2003 only if the deed is within six months and notice by the later of fifteen working days after the deed or the six-month period. Otherwise market value at acquisition applies; a final duty assessment, court decision or written settlement overrides. Historic pre-25 November 2003 deed/notice conditions need a separate test. Check this point with one of our partner accountants.

### Defaults and traps

- Occupancy for the own-residence exemption uncertain: assume no exemption, compute the tax, and get the evidence before the deed.
- Acquisition value on an old deed missing or ambiguous: do not estimate; get the deed from the Public Registry.
- "No profit, no tax" is wrong for an article 5A transfer-value charge. Applying 8% by default misses the 10%, 5% and 2% rows.
- The notary collects; a wrong figure can hold up the deed, and do not assume a published election can be changed; Check this point with one of our partner accountants.
- The undervalue comparison is declared value against 85% of market value, not market against 115% of declared.

## Worked cases

All sellers are invented; rates are the 2026 law. Assume the stated values are properly established, no other exemption/special route applies, and the relevant deed, notice and eligibility conditions are met.

### Case 1: inherited house, keep 12% of the gain or elect the transfer-value rate (a decision)

A seller inherited a house from a parent who died in 2010; the value in the timely causa mortis declaration is EUR 150,000. Assume the notice meets S.L. 123.92 rule 4 and no final valuation overrides it. Sold in 2026 for EUR 300,000 (market value not higher), no brokerage, acquired after 2003, held over five years. Default: 12% of EUR 150,000 = EUR 18,000. Electing out: 8% of EUR 300,000 = EUR 24,000. Keep the default; it saves EUR 6,000. If the declared value had been EUR 60,000, the default would be EUR 28,800 and electing 8% would save EUR 4,800. With these assumptions, including no other relief or rate, the appreciation since the prescribed acquisition value decides it: the default wins while the gain is under two thirds of the transfer value (8 / 12).

```oa-check
name: Inherited 2010, sale EUR 300,000, 12 percent of gain versus 8 percent of transfer value, two declared values
inputs:
  transfer_value: 300000
  acq_high: 150000
  acq_low: 60000
steps:
  - default_high: pct(transfer_value - acq_high, 12)
  - default_low: pct(transfer_value - acq_low, 12)
  - elected: pct(transfer_value, 8)
  - default_saves_high: elected - default_high
  - election_saves_low: default_low - elected
expected:
  default_high: 18000.00
  default_low: 28800.00
  elected: 24000.00
  default_saves_high: 6000.00
  election_saves_low: 4800.00
```

### Case 2: investment flat with brokerage, and the buyer's duty

A seller bought a flat in 2010 and sells it in 2026 for EUR 300,000, paying a verified agency fee of EUR 9,000. Base EUR 291,000; 8% = EUR 23,280, withheld by the notary. Had it been sold within five years with no permit works, 5% = EUR 14,550. The buyer qualifies for article 32(4)(a), including its AIP and previous-relief conditions, but is not a first-time buyer, and pays 3.5% on EUR 200,000 (EUR 7,000) and 5% on EUR 100,000 (EUR 5,000): EUR 12,000, of which EUR 2,400 (20% of the EUR 12,000 duty, assuming these relief facts were already available) is paid at the promise of sale, leaving EUR 9,600 on the deed.

### Further cases (prose)

- **Pre-2004 purchase.** Bought 1998, sold 2026 for EUR 400,000: 10% = EUR 40,000.
- **Restored property under 5A(5)(d), 2026 deed.** EUR 300,000 at 10% = EUR 30,000. The 7% figure (EUR 21,000) applied only to qualifying 2015 deeds.
- **Undervalue test.** Declared EUR 100,000 against market EUR 116,000: 85% of market is EUR 98,600, so no order on that ground. Against market EUR 120,000, 85% is EUR 102,000 and the declaration falls EUR 2,000 short of that threshold. Any qualifying order uses the proper market-value tax, not tax merely on the EUR 2,000 threshold gap.
- **MTCA's duty example.** Market price EUR 200,000, contract EUR 180,000: final duty 5% of EUR 200,000 = EUR 10,000; provisional 1% = EUR 2,000.

## When to refuse or refer

- Refuse to give a seller's rate until the acquisition date and means, project status, permit works in five years, own-residence facts and the 2014 promise-of-sale question are known.
- Refer: projects and developers; emphyteusis, usufruct and bare-ownership splits; partitions; non-residents opting out under 5A(3)(h); special designated areas and AIP permits; promises of sale notified before 17 November 2014; intra-group, trust and liquidation exemptions.
- Refer the buyer-side reliefs not computed here: the transfer causa mortis and its 3.5% band on an inherited dwelling (EUR 35,000 to EUR 400,000, L.N. 306 of 2025, from 28 October 2025); first-time buyer claims with an unclear ownership history, or before 28 October 2025; the reliefs in S.L. 123.203 and S.L. 364.19 (vacant, old, UCA and traditional-style property), extended by L.N. 284 of 2025 to transfers up to 31 December 2026, whose conditions are not set out here; the second-time buyer refund (S.L. 364.17). Check this point with one of our partner accountants.
- A valuation dispute beyond the 15% tolerance goes through objection and appeal.

## Filing and payment

1. **Engage the notary**, usually chosen by the buyer, for searches, deeds and provisional duty (MTCA Buying Property; Selling Property).
2. **Promise of sale**: presented to the Property Tax Directorate within 21 days of signing, with the provisional duty computed under article 3(6). For the current promise-notice form and its submission requirements (previously labelled DDT3 here): Check this point with one of our partner accountants. A receipt confirms the notary submitted the promise and the duty was paid.
3. **Before the deed**: fix the seller's rate, exemption or exclusion and any deed election; deliver the computation and evidence to the notary.
4. **After the deed**: within fifteen working days the notary submits, through the Notaries Online Portal, the DDT1 with the transfer details, a copy of the contract, site plans, the Public Registry note, the buyer's duty, the seller's tax and, for residential property, Schedule 8 (physical attributes) (MTCA Promise of Sale; Selling Property). The seller's 5A tax is due within fifteen working days.
5. **After**: receipts normally issue within 3 weeks; an internal board decides whether an architect values the property. Apply the separate duty and income-tax assessment provisions; article 49 and article 52 govern duty liability, and article 5A(12) governs the seller's tax and additional tax. Do not treat the duty authority's description of a typical collection as a limit on statutory joint liability.
6. **Reporting**: 5A tax is final; the transferor does not declare that income in a return (ITMA art. 43(4)(a)). A transfer outside 5A follows the applicable article 4 or 5 return and provisional-tax rules. In particular, article 5A(3)(h) prevents refund and disapplies ITMA article 43(4)(b) for its post-2014 non-resident transfers; do not promise the ordinary credit/refund treatment there. Every owner keeps an account of proceeds and expenditure on 5A transfers (art. 5A(10)(c)); a company resident in Malta allocates the profits to its final tax account (art. 5A(10)(d)).
