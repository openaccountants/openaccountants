# Setting up a family office in Malta: building blocks, tax touch-points and the order of steps

## Scope and who this is for
Figures are for tax year 2026. Malta taxes by year of assessment, so income of calendar 2026 is taxed in year of assessment 2027. This Guide is the starting point for a principal, or an adviser, who is thinking about running a family's wealth from Malta. It names the usual Maltese building blocks, says in one table how each is taxed and which Guide covers it in depth, sets out the family office's own running costs, gives the order of the setup steps and the documents each step needs, and says what a Malta structure does not do. It does not repeat the deep Guides; it links to them. Current primary-source evidence was checked on 26 November 2026, reusing the completed foundation, non-dom, corporate-tax and holding-company proof for unchanged applicable conditions. The Acts print most rates and fractions in words, and this Guide keeps them in words.

The deep Guides in this set are `mt-holding-company-participation-exemption`, `mt-foundations-and-trusts`, `mt-company-residence-and-substance`, `mt-residence-programmes-for-wealthy-families` and `mt-inheritance-and-succession`. For a family member moving to Malta, see `moving-to-malta-tax-residence` and `mt-non-dom`. For the comparison with other countries, see `family-holding-company-location-compared` and `family-office-residency-regimes-compared`.

## Ask the client first
- Where is each family member resident and domiciled now, and where will they be in 2026 and after? Will anyone move to Malta? This informs recipient status, dividend exemptions/deemed distributions and article 56(27); residency alone does not decide every dividend charge.
- What are the assets, where are they, and does any company hold immovable property in Malta? This decides the participation exemption, duty on transfers and the property company rules.
- For each strategic holding: how much of the equity does the family hold, where is the company resident, and what foreign tax does it pay? Can the subsidiary deduct the dividend it pays? This decides whether the holding is a participating holding and passes the anti-abuse conditions.
- Will the investment portfolio earn mainly interest, dividends or gains, and with what foreign tax? This informs statutory income character, actual refund entitlement and the final-tax base/floor; a headline fraction does not establish the preferred regime.
- Does the family want a foundation or a trust on top, and will any beneficiary be ordinarily resident and domiciled in Malta? A beneficiary who is ordinarily resident and domiciled and is not wholly exempt under article 12 fails that limb of the look-through condition; use the complete test in `mt-foundations-and-trusts`.
- What will the management company do, and for whom? This decides VAT, transfer pricing and whether a licence is needed.

## The method, step by step
This is the setup order. Each step names the law and the documents it needs.

1. **Agree the family's facts.** Where each family member is resident and domiciled now and will be after the move, what the assets are and where they sit, and which home-country rules apply to each member. Nothing below can be decided without these answers (see "Ask the client first").
2. **Take home-country advice first.** Moving assets into a Malta company can be a disposal, and owning a Malta company can bring home-country controlled foreign company rules. See `cfc-and-substance-rules-compared` and `exit-taxes-when-a-family-moves`. Malta law does not answer those questions.
3. **Choose the top vehicle, if any.** A foundation is constituted by public deed inter vivos or will under Civil Code Second Schedule art. 29. For an ordinary non-public-benefit foundation, the minimum endowment is EUR 1,164.69 in money/property. Prepare the full article 29(4) statute, not merely a name/address form: name including “foundation”, Malta address, purposes, assets, administrators or appointor, and the applicable beneficiary statement and nonresident-administrator local representative. The beneficiary statement may be separately signed by the founder before a notary. A noncash endowment needs the administrators' value statement. Registration, signing and administrative powers/authorisation are in `mt-foundations-and-trusts`; this is an evidence list, not a complete constitutive instrument. For a trust, establish its instrument, actual trustees, residence and authorisation. Do not assume every trust must have a resident trustee, or that any private/family trustee qualifies for company treatment.
4. **Make the top-vehicle elections within their actual windows.** Cap. 123 art. 27D(1) company treatment requires a **written investment-income-only instrument** and a Malta-resident trustee authorised under Cap. 331 art. 43(3) or within the specified art. 43(6) category. The irrevocable election form is due within 30 days after trust establishment or resident-trustee appointment, whichever is later; timing is not postponed until first income. A foundation within S.L. 123.114 reg. 2 may elect trust taxation by irrevocable written notice under reg. 4. **Not yet confirmed:** its effective timing and a particular private/family trustee's eligibility. Check this point with one of our partner accountants.
5. **Form the companies.** Holding company, investment company and management company, each registered in Malta. Documents and the registry process are in `malta-formation`. A company incorporated in Malta on or after the date in the rates table is resident in Malta ([Cap. 123 art. 2(1)](https://legislation.mt/eli/cap/123/eng/pdf)). Board composition and where decisions are taken are in `mt-company-residence-and-substance`.
6. **Move the assets in.** Each transfer is a transaction in the home country and, for some assets, in Malta. Duty under the [Duty on Documents and Transfers Act, Cap. 364](https://legislation.mt/eli/cap/364/eng/pdf) can apply to transfers of immovable property in Malta and of marketable securities (article 42 charges €2 per €100 **or part**, on higher consideration/real value in the stated cases, including specified foreign-security transfers). Where seventy-five percent or more of a company's assets, leaving out current assets other than immovable property, are immovable property or rights over it, article 42(2)(a) increases the duty "by three euro for every one hundred euro or part thereof". Indirect property holdings need the additional aggregate gate and liability rules in article 42(2)(b)/(c); the duty test differs from the Income Tax Act property-company definition. Actual valuation, exemptions and clocks are in `selling-a-company-in-malta`, `mt-foundations-and-trusts` and `mt-inheritance-and-succession`; do not execute an asset transfer before those conditions and home-country treatment are resolved.
7. **Test each holding of the holding company** against the non-property/equity-rights and participating-holding definitions, dividend anti-abuse, EU-deduction/non-cooperative-list conditions and direct/indirect Malta-interest gain tests ([Cap. 123 art. 2(1) and 12(1)(u)](https://legislation.mt/eli/cap/123/eng/pdf)). Documents: share registers, the subsidiaries' residence and foreign tax evidence. See `mt-holding-company-participation-exemption`.
8. **Decide the investment company's tax route** before the applicable election deadline: the article 56(6) rate with shareholder refunds under [Cap. 372 art. 48](https://legislation.mt/eli/cap/372/eng/pdf), or the final tax under [S.L. 123.217](https://legislation.mt/eli/sl/123.217/eng/pdf). The final tax is elected by a notice in the form and by the date the Commissioner sets (reg. 3(2)), and locks in for the period in the rates table. Apply regulation 3's income exclusions, including the stated other-company non-final-account dividends and income already taxed finally, before making any 15% comparison. MTCA's 27 March 2026 notice requires the election through the return questions by the applicable online filing deadline. Registration alone does not establish a refund: account, income character, actual tax payment, certificate, filing compliance, no-double-refund and individual exemption/deemed-distribution rules also apply. Use `mt-corporate-tax` and `mt-holding-company-participation-exemption`.
9. **Set up the management company's payroll and VAT position.** Employer registration, FSS and Class 1 in `malta-payroll` and `malta-ssc`; VAT registration and the treatment of the charges in `malta-vat-return` ([Cap. 406](https://legislation.mt/eli/cap/406/eng/pdf)). Write the service agreement and fee basis before the first invoice.
10. **Check licensing** for the management company and any trustee or administrator role (refer; see "The family office's own costs").
11. **Family members who move.** Residence route and the article 56(27) minimum tax: `mt-residence-programmes-for-wealthy-families` and `mt-non-dom` ([Cap. 123 art. 4(1) and 56(27)](https://legislation.mt/eli/cap/123/eng/pdf)).
12. **Every year.** Company returns/accounts and applicable audit duties; refund claims within four years from eligibility, subject to all Cap. 372 art. 48 conditions; trust returns and beneficiary certificates plus the vested-but-unpaid allocation/payment conditions described below; tax deducted on untaxed dividends paid to "recipients" (art. 62); duty and succession planning reviewed (`mt-inheritance-and-succession`).

### What a single-family office is, and the Maltese building blocks
For this Guide, “single-family office” means the people and entities that hold, manage and administer one family's wealth, and serve no outside clients. These are possible building blocks to assess against the family's facts, rather than a required four-vehicle structure.

1. **A family holding company.** A company registered in Malta that holds the family's strategic shareholdings (operating companies, large stakes). Its main tax question is the participation exemption.
2. **A family investment company.** A company registered in Malta that holds the liquid portfolio (bonds, funds, small listed stakes). Its income is mostly interest and portfolio dividends, so its main tax questions are the company rate, the shareholder refund and the elective final tax.
3. **A foundation or a trust.** A vehicle at the top that holds the companies for the family over generations. A Malta foundation is constituted by public deed or by will under the Second Schedule to the Civil Code; the assets of a trust are held by its trustee.
4. **A management company.** A company registered in Malta that employs the family office team (investment staff, accountants, administration) and charges the family entities for its services.

### Trust allocation is conditional

Under the default route, properly allocated amounts are those vested, entitled or distributed by the income-year end within art. 27D(4)(a). Exclusion from trust income requires the Cap. 372 art. 24A return and beneficiary certificates. Vested/entitled amounts still unpaid by 31 March after the income year additionally require tax paid on beneficiaries' behalf at 35% by 30 June, available to the beneficiaries as credit/refund. Retained unallocated income is normally taxed in the trust, not automatically in beneficiaries' hands. Special clergy and commercial-trust routes need separate treatment.

For art. 27D(3)(b) look-through, **all** income must be foreign-source or art. 12(1)(c) income and **every** beneficiary must be not ordinarily resident, not domiciled or wholly art. 12 exempt. Foreign income received in Malta by the trustee is then deemed received in Malta by beneficiaries, with notification duties. Article 27D(3)(c) has a separate all-nonresident/certificate route. **Not yet confirmed:** actual beneficiary allocations, receipt/remittance, special routes, returns/certificates and tax due. Check this point with one of our partner accountants. Use `mt-foundations-and-trusts` before choosing or operating a trust.

### The family office's own costs: the management company
**Corporate tax.** A management company incorporated in Malta is resident in Malta (the date in the rates table) and pays tax on its chargeable income at the article 56(6) rate. In working out its income, it deducts outgoings and expenses only to the extent they were wholly and exclusively incurred in the production of the income ([Cap. 123 art. 14(1)](https://legislation.mt/eli/cap/123/eng/pdf)). The same test applies when a family company deducts the fee it pays the management company. How the fee is priced between related family entities is in `malta-transfer-pricing`. The annual accounts and audit are in `malta-financial-statements`. Forming the company is in `malta-formation`.

**VAT.** The [VAT Act, Cap. 406](https://legislation.mt/eli/cap/406/eng/pdf), article 19(1), charges tax on a taxable supply at "eighteen per cent of the taxable value of the supply", other than the supplies in sub-article (2). Article 7 sends the question of where a supply takes place to the Third Schedule, and article 9(1) exempts the supplies in Parts One and Two of the Fifth Schedule. So whether the management company's charge to a family company bears Malta VAT, and whether the family company can recover it, turns on where the customer is established, what the service is and whether an exemption applies. This Guide does not settle that. **Not yet confirmed:** registration, place of supply, exemption and input-tax recovery for these services and family entities. Check this point with one of our partner accountants. Use `malta-vat-return`; an exempt/passive/nonbusiness holding activity does not establish recoverable VAT.

**Staff.** Establish who employs each person, where work is performed and any cross-border/social-security exception before deciding FSS withholding and Class 1 contributions. **Not yet confirmed:** the actual employer registration, withholding/contribution scope, rates, certificates and deadlines for these employees. Check this point with one of our partner accountants. See `malta-payroll` and `malta-ssc`. Staff who move to Malta for the job may also look at the individual routes in `moving-to-malta-tax-residence`.

**Licensing.** Managing investments, acting as trustee or administering a foundation for others can need a licence from the Malta Financial Services Authority. The Income Tax Act itself refers to trustees authorised under article 43 of the Trusts and Trustees Act, Cap. 331 ([Cap. 123 art. 27D(1)](https://legislation.mt/eli/cap/123/eng/pdf)). Whether a management company that serves one family needs one depends on what it does and for whom. This Guide does not state those rules. **Not yet confirmed:** the actual MFSA authorisation, exemption or registration for the proposed investment/trustee/foundation work. Check this point with one of our partner accountants. Do so before commencing that activity.

### Family Office Residency and the other residence routes
**Not yet confirmed:** a “Family Office Residency” programme's existence, immigration eligibility, conditions, fees or tax consequences for this family. Check this point with one of our partner accountants. A programme's absence from a homepage would not prove its absence from law. No immigration approval, employment tax concession or residence effect is established by the family-office label. See `family-office-residency-regimes-compared`.

The named routes (non-dom ordinary residence, the Global Residence Programme, the Residence Programme, the Malta Permanent Residence Programme and the nomad permit) are covered in `mt-residence-programmes-for-wealthy-families`, with the individual position in `mt-non-dom` and `moving-to-malta-tax-residence`.

One rule catches family members who move to Malta and keep their wealth abroad. Article 56(27) of the [Income Tax Act](https://legislation.mt/eli/cap/123/eng/pdf) applies to an individual who is ordinarily resident but not domiciled in Malta, to whom provisos (i) and (ii) of article 4(1) apply, who is not taxed under another minimum-tax scheme, and who "derives income (including, in the case of a married couple whose income is chargeable to tax in terms of article 49 of the Act, the income derived by both spouses) amounting to not less than thirty five thousand euro (€35,000) ... arising outside Malta ... but which is not received or not fully received in Malta". That person has a yearly tax liability of at least the minimum tax in the rates table. The EUR 35,000 is tested on total foreign income derived. The rule then applies where that income is "not received or not fully received in Malta". The words "not fully received" mean that income partly brought to Malta is still caught. The tax compared with the minimum is the tax before relief under articles 76 to 89, on income other than article 5A property transfers. Tax paid under the Act counts towards the minimum, except article 5A tax. If the individual proves to the Commissioner that tax on worldwide income would be lower, the liability is capped accordingly. The underlying article 4(1) remittance-basis exclusions must first be checked: specified long-term/permanent residence statuses and a spouse ordinarily resident **and** domiciled in Malta can disapply it. MTCA remittance guidance section 6.2 permits eligible double-tax relief **after** the pre-relief minimum comparison, only for actually remitted income taxed abroad. **Not yet confirmed:** exact family status, separate/joint computation, eligible relief and the worldwide cap proof. Check this point with one of our partner accountants. Do not infer the €5,000 rule merely from a residence permit.

### What Malta does not do for the family
- **Whole-asset tax profile.** **Not yet confirmed:** absence of a yearly wealth tax or a separate inheritance tax for every asset, owner and relevant jurisdiction. Check this point with one of our partner accountants. The cited statutes do not prove an exhaustive absence. Malta duty can apply on death/property/securities under Cap. 364, with its own scope, exemptions and notification requirements; use `mt-inheritance-and-succession`.
- **Foreign tax needs its own analysis.** Malta law cannot determine the family's foreign-country outcome. Obtain that country's analysis of transfers, residence, CFC/attribution, distributions and reporting before moving assets. See `cfc-and-substance-rules-compared`. This Guide states no foreign rule.
- **Participation exemption has a defined scope.** The participation exemption is for income and gains "derived by a company registered in Malta from a participating holding or from the transfer of such holding" ([Cap. 123 art. 12(1)(u)](https://legislation.mt/eli/cap/123/eng/pdf)). A gain on a holding in a Malta-resident company is exempt only under the beneficial owner test in `mt-holding-company-participation-exemption`. Sales of Malta property follow `mt-capital-gains`.
- **Malta has its own controlled foreign company rule** for a Malta company's low-taxed foreign subsidiaries and permanent establishments (regulation 7 of [S.L. 123.187](https://legislation.mt/eli/sl/123.187/eng/pdf)), covered in `mt-company-residence-and-substance`.

## Worked cases

### Worked hypothetical: a family moving its investments into a Malta structure
All original amounts and both executable blocks are preserved and recomputed. They are separate conditional illustrations, not a full-structure tax estimate. Assume no general article 51 or relevant specific anti-abuse denial, valid ordinary company registration, no applicable regulated-fund/sector regime, no relevant non-cooperative-list restriction, and all applicable source/account/filing conditions. All amounts in this section are hypothetical. The family members live outside Malta and are assumed **not resident in Malta** for this illustration, rather than residence being inferred from their address, none of them is ordinarily resident or domiciled in Malta, none acts on behalf of such a person, and none is an EU or EEA individual taxed under the provisos to article 56(1)(c) (the four kinds of "recipient" in [Cap. 123 art. 61(a)](https://legislation.mt/eli/cap/123/eng/pdf)). The family has EUR 50,000,000 of investments and puts them into Malta companies that the family members own directly. A foundation on top is left out to keep each step provable; see `mt-foundations-and-trusts` for how one changes the answer.

**Who holds what.**
- Holding company: EUR 30,000,000, made up of three stakes of EUR 10,000,000, each at least five percent of the equity shares (and of the votes and profits) of a company resident in the European Union, each **not a property company** and with the stated genuine equity rights. Each is a participating holding under route (a) ([Cap. 123 art. 2(1)](https://legislation.mt/eli/cap/123/eng/pdf)).
- Investment company: EUR 20,000,000 of foreign bonds that pay interest with no foreign tax, assumed not derived directly or indirectly from a trade or business ([Cap. 123 art. 2(1)](https://legislation.mt/eli/cap/123/eng/pdf), "passive interest or royalties").
- Management company: employs the team. Its costs are EUR 900,000 and it charges the family companies EUR 990,000, excluding any VAT ([Cap. 123 art. 14(1)](https://legislation.mt/eli/cap/123/eng/pdf)).

**Year 2026, touch-points.**

0. Moving the stakes in. For duty, shares are "marketable securities" ("a holding of share capital in any company"). Article 42(1)(a)(iii) of the [Duty on Documents and Transfers Act](https://legislation.mt/eli/cap/364/eng/pdf) charges duty "on every document whereby any other foreign marketable security is transferred inter vivos to, or by any person resident in Malta". Where the shares are not in a property company or a company with more than half its business interests in Malta, "no duty shall be chargeable where such transfer is effected through a local bank or through a person holding an investment services licence under the Investment Services Act". This example does not compute the duty; settle it before the transfer. The bonds are assumed genuine debt, not share capital or an equity-linked instrument; that label alone does not settle every transfer charge. **Not yet confirmed:** duty/exemptions on every actual asset transfer. Check this point with one of our partner accountants.
1. Holding company. It receives EUR 1,200,000 of dividends from the three stakes. The subsidiaries are resident in the European Union, so condition (i)(1) of the anti-abuse proviso is met. Because the subsidiaries are in the EU, the exemption "shall only apply to the extent that such profits are not deductible by the relevant subsidiary in that other EU Member State"; this example assumes no subsidiary deducts the dividend. If the company leaves the dividends out of its return, they are exempt and its Malta tax on them is nil ([Cap. 123 art. 12(1)(u)](https://legislation.mt/eli/cap/123/eng/pdf)). **Conditional onward distribution only:** the original example assumes an untaxed-account distribution to persons outside every article 61 recipient category, for which articles 62/66 give no Malta charge. The correct participation-exempt-profit allocation is not established here; it cannot be inferred solely from no company tax, because specified exempt profits can belong to the final account under S.L. 123.101. **Not yet confirmed:** the account and actual onward family charge, including any intermediary. Check this point with one of our partner accountants. Foreign taxation is separately unresolved.
2. Investment company, ordinary route. Interest of EUR 400,000 is assumed statutory passive interest: no direct/indirect trade/business source and no foreign tax, with no double-tax relief. Tax at thirty-five cents on every euro is EUR 140,000 ([Cap. 123 art. 56(6)](https://legislation.mt/eli/cap/123/eng/pdf)). The profits go to the foreign income account. Assuming actual company tax paid, proper dividend/certificate, registration, all tax/FSS/VAT compliance gates and no other refund bar, the entitled shareholders claim five-sevenths of eligible ACIT, EUR 100,000 in total ([Cap. 372 art. 48(4A)(a) proviso (i)](https://legislation.mt/eli/cap/372/eng/pdf)). Net Malta tax: EUR 40,000. No refund is due until a dividend is paid and claimed, within the time limit in article 48(5).
3. Investment company, final tax route instead, **conditional arithmetic only**. Preserve the original EUR 400,000: 15% is EUR 60,000. If that is the eligible elected base and the correctly established floor is ordinary EUR 140,000 less claimable EUR 100,000 = EUR 40,000, the maximum is EUR 60,000, EUR 20,000 above the assumed refund route. Final tax gives no credit/refund and the relevant profits go to the final account. The minimum elected period is five YAs; return to the normal system also binds at least five YAs. **Not yet confirmed:** the actual base/floor, all shareholders' claimed/claimable entitlement and article 43(6), and any retained-profit comparison. Check this point with one of our partner accountants. No actual election decision is established.
4. Management company. Profit EUR 990,000 less EUR 900,000 is EUR 90,000, taxed at thirty-five cents on every euro: EUR 31,500 ([Cap. 123 art. 56(6)](https://legislation.mt/eli/cap/123/eng/pdf)), assuming every cost is deductible under article 14(1). Payroll/social-security eligibility and obligations must be resolved through `malta-payroll` and `malta-ssc`, not inferred from this company-profit calculation. VAT on the EUR 990,000 charge is not computed here; see "The family office's own costs".
5. The investment company's computation above ignores the management fee. Whether, and how much of, its share of the EUR 990,000 it may deduct under [Cap. 123 art. 14(1)](https://legislation.mt/eli/cap/123/eng/pdf) changes its tax, and is not computed. **Not yet confirmed:** allocation, deductibility and transfer-pricing support for those fees. Check this point with one of our partner accountants.

## When to refuse or refer
- Licensing of the management company, trustee or foundation administrator: Check this point with one of our partner accountants. No authorisation or exemption is established here.
- Immigration and any Family Office Residency or other residence permit: refer to the Residency Malta Agency or an immigration adviser. Refuse to state a family office residency condition, fee or tax effect from this Guide; they are not proved here.
- Banking and account opening: refer.
- Home-country tax of every family member, including exit taxes, controlled foreign company rules and the tax of trusts and foundations abroad: refer to an adviser in that country (`cfc-and-substance-rules-compared`, `exit-taxes-when-a-family-moves`, `succession-and-forced-heirship-compared`).
- A non-dom family member whose foreign income derived is at least the article 56(27) threshold but who keeps less than that abroad is still caught: the rule covers income "not received or not fully received in Malta" ([Cap. 123 art. 56(27)(ii)](https://legislation.mt/eli/cap/123/eng/pdf)).
- Refer before telling a Malta-resident individual shareholder to rely on a refund under article 48(4) or (4A) of Cap. 372: article 12(1)(c)(iii) of the Income Tax Act and the bar in the last proviso to article 48(1) may reach it, and the text does not settle this (`mt-holding-company-participation-exemption`).
- VAT on the management company's charges where a family company makes exempt or no supplies: refer (`malta-vat-return`).
- Actual trust/company tax-avoidance or non-genuine arrangement concerns: the Commissioner may disregard a trust used "with the sole or main purpose of reducing the amount of tax payable by a person" ([Cap. 123 art. 27D(10)](https://legislation.mt/eli/cap/123/eng/pdf)). Check this point with one of our partner accountants. General article 51 and specific exemption conditions must also be considered.
- Malta property, Malta-resident subsidiaries and gains on them: see `mt-capital-gains` and `mt-holding-company-participation-exemption`, and refer where a Malta-resident owner sells a Malta-resident subsidiary.
