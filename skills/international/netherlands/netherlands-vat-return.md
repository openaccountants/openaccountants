# Netherlands: VAT (btw) and the VAT return (2026)

> **Source-cited draft: not reviewed by an accountant.** This skill is general tax reference material for AI-assisted work. It has not been reviewed for any business's facts. Do not rely on it to file or pay without review by a qualified professional in the Netherlands (belastingadviseur).

**Which text of the law.** Wet op de omzetbelasting 1968 (Wet OB 1968) and Uitvoeringsbesluit omzetbelasting 1968, both as in force on 1 January 2026 on wetten.overheid.nl. Algemene wet inzake rijksbelastingen (AWR) as in force on 1 January 2026. Practical figures come from the Belastingdienst's own pages and its notes to the 2026 return, all read on 24 October 2026.

## Who this applies to

- **In scope:** an entrepreneur (ondernemer) established in the Netherlands who makes taxable supplies there: a sole trader (eenmanszaak), a partnership (vof, maatschap) or a company (bv, nv). The tax is charged on the entrepreneur who makes the supply (Wet OB 1968 art. 12(1)).
- **The return:** a registered business files the return the Belastingdienst puts ready for each period, even when there is nothing to declare (a nil return). The yearly letter (aangiftebrief) lists every period, deadline and payment reference.
- **KOR participants** do not charge VAT, do not deduct input VAT and do not file normal returns (art. 25a(4)).
- **Not covered here:** businesses established outside the Netherlands (other deadlines; see Cross-border points), a fiscal unity (fiscale eenheid), the margin and travel agency schemes, OSS and import scheme returns, the sale and letting of real estate, and Bonaire, Sint Eustatius and Saba. Registration is in nl-vat-registration.

**The 2026 rate change.** Only accommodation moved. Post b11 of Table I (logies) is repealed from 1 January 2026, so hotels, B&Bs, holiday homes, static caravans and seasonal letting of furnished rooms charge 21%. A payment made in 2025 for a stay in 2026 is also at 21%, and so is a single-purpose voucher for such a stay. Facilities sold separately keep their own rate: breakfast sold on its own is food at 9%; separately charged wifi is 21%. The admission and sport posts (b3, b14) and short-stay camping (b10) are still in Table I on 1 January 2026, so culture, sport and campsites stay at 9%.

## The taxable base

- **What is taxed.** Supplies of goods and services by an entrepreneur in the Netherlands, intra-EU acquisitions, and imports. The base is the price agreed, excluding VAT.
- **Boxes of the return** ([Toelichting 2026](https://download.belastingdienst.nl/belastingdienst/docs/toelichting_bij_btw_aangifte_ob0731t62fd.pdf)):
  - 1a sales at 21%; 1b sales at 9%; 1c sports canteen flat rate; 1d private use (last return of the year only); 1e domestic 0% sales and sales where the VAT is shifted to the customer.
  - 2a domestic reverse charge received.
  - 3a exports; 3b intra-EU supplies of goods and B2B services; 3c installation and distance sales in other EU states.
  - 4a goods and services from outside the EU with VAT shifted to you (including imports under an article 23 licence); 4b goods and services from EU suppliers.
  - 5a total VAT due (calculated); 5b input VAT (voorbelasting).
- **Exempt sales** (Wet OB 1968 art. 11, for example medical care, finance, insurance, residential letting) are not entered and carry no input VAT deduction.
- **Input VAT** (art. 15) is deducted in the period of a valid invoice, for purchases used for taxed supplies. Not deductible: private purchases, costs of exempt sales, food and drink in restaurants and cafes, BUA items above EUR 227 per recipient, and VAT charged in error. Shared costs of taxed and exempt sales are split by turnover or actual use and trued up in the last return of the year.
- **Year-end adjustments** go in the last return of the year: private use in 1d (the car at actual use, or 2.7% or 1.5% of list price), BUA corrections and the final taxed/exempt ratio.
- **Rounding.** Every amount on the return is in whole euros. You may round in your favour. Negative amounts take a minus sign.
- **Bad debts.** VAT on a debt that will not be paid is reclaimed once that is certain, and at the latest 1 year after the agreed payment date ([Toelichting 2026](https://download.belastingdienst.nl/belastingdienst/docs/toelichting_bij_btw_aangifte_ob0731t62fd.pdf)).
- **KOR turnover test.** Count 21%, 9% and 0% sales (including exports and intra-EU supplies), domestic reverse-charge sales, and exempt real estate, financial and insurance sales. Do not count private use, sales of the business's own investment goods, supplies taxed abroad, or intra-EU acquisitions (art. 25(1) and (2); [KOR conditions](https://www.belastingdienst.nl/wps/wcm/connect/nl/btw/content/kor-voorwaarden)).

## Deadlines and filing

- **Period.** Most businesses file quarterly. Monthly filing is on request by letter, or imposed after late returns or payments. An annual return needs a request and all of: less than EUR 1,883 VAT a year, no article 23 licence, a natural person or a partnership of natural persons only, and less than EUR 10,000 of each intra-EU flow. The Belastingdienst answers within 6 weeks; a change starts with the next period ([Aangiftetijdvak](https://www.belastingdienst.nl/wps/wcm/connect/nl/btw/content/wijziging-aangiftetijdvak-btw)).
- **Deadline.** Return and payment must be received by the last day of the month after the period (AWR art. 10(2) and art. 19(1); [deadlines](https://www.belastingdienst.nl/wps/wcm/connect/nl/btw/content/uiterste-aangifte-en-betaaldatums)):

| Period 2026 | Return and payment received by |
|---|---|
| Q1 | 30 April 2026 |
| Q2 | 31 July 2026 |
| Q3 | 31 October 2026 |
| Q4 | 31 January 2027 |
| A month (for example August) | Last day of the next month (30 September 2026) |
| Year 2026 | 31 March 2027 |

- **Filing.** Online, through Mijn Belastingdienst Zakelijk or software. Software can send the return from the 24th of the last month of the period; earlier is rejected.
- **Payment.** To IBAN NL04 RABO 0200 1122 44, name "Belastingdienst" (BIC RABONL2U), with the payment reference. The Belastingdienst moved from ING to Rabobank on 1 May 2026 ([btw betalen](https://www.belastingdienst.nl/wps/wcm/connect/bldcontentnl/belastingdienst/zakelijk/btw/btw_aangifte_doen_en_betalen/btw_betalen)). Payment counts when it reaches that account.
- **ICP listing (opgaaf ICP).** Lists intra-EU B2B supplies of goods, B2B services taxed in the customer's state, and call-off stock. Due by the last day of the month after the period (art. 37a(1)). Goods may be listed quarterly if not more than EUR 50,000 per quarter, this quarter and each of the last 4; services may always be quarterly; annual needs a permit. If goods pass EUR 50,000 in a quarter, monthly listing starts from that quarter. A KOR participant does not file the listing (art. 37a(1)). Reconcile it to box 3b.
- **Reverse charge (verleggingsregeling).** The supplier invoices without VAT, writes "btw verlegd" and puts the turnover in 1e. The customer declares the VAT in 2a and deducts it in 5b if the purchase serves taxed sales. Domestic cases: subcontracting and hiring out staff in construction and shipbuilding (Uitvoeringsbesluit art. 24b), plus real estate supplied with the option to tax, gold of 325/1000 or more, forced sales, emission rights, telecom between telecom providers, gas and electricity certificates, and the electronics above EUR 10,000 per supply (art. 24ba).
- **KOR joining and leaving.** Joining takes effect from the next period that starts at least 4 weeks after the notice (art. 25a(5)). Going over EUR 20,000 in a year ends the exemption at once: the supply that crosses the limit is taxed in full, and the business cannot use the KOR for the rest of that year and the next (art. 25a(9)). A voluntary exit takes effect on the first day of the next calendar quarter starting at least 4 weeks after the notice, with no re-entry for the rest of that year and the next (art. 25a(7)).
- **Corrections (suppletie).** An error of EUR 1,000 or less goes in the next return, in the box where it belongs. An error above EUR 1,000 needs the Suppletie form in Mijn Belastingdienst Zakelijk, as soon as possible and at the latest within 8 weeks of finding it. Corrections are possible up to 5 years after the year, so 2026 can be corrected until the end of 2031. For underpaid VAT, wait for the additional assessment (naheffingsaanslag), usually within 8 weeks, then pay ([Btw-aangifte corrigeren](https://www.belastingdienst.nl/wps/wcm/connect/bldcontentnl/belastingdienst/zakelijk/btw/btw_aangifte_doen_en_betalen/aangifte_corrigeren)). The duty to report errors rests on AWR art. 10a.

## Penalties and interest

- **Late or missing return (aangifteverzuim):** EUR 82 if the return arrives after 7 calendar days' grace. In exceptional cases, such as repeated late filing, up to EUR 165, the statutory maximum (AWR art. 67b(1)).
- **Late or short payment (betaalverzuim):** 3% of the late or unpaid amount, minimum EUR 50, maximum EUR 6,709 (AWR art. 67c(1)). Payment within the 7 days' grace brings only a notice if the previous return was paid on time and in full; otherwise the 3% penalty applies. Repeated late payment can raise it to 10%. Too little VAT declared, or too much refunded, can bring 10%, never more than EUR 6,709 a year ([bijzondere situaties](https://www.belastingdienst.nl/wps/wcm/connect/bldcontentnl/belastingdienst/zakelijk/btw/btw_aangifte_doen_en_betalen/boetes/uitzonderlijke_of_bijzondere_situaties)).
- **Late and short together:** the filing and payment penalties are separate and both apply.
- **Vergrijpboete:** up to 100% of the tax for gross negligence or intent, including not sending a required suppletie (AWR art. 10a(3)).
- **Tax interest (belastingrente):** charged on late payment and on additional assessments for VAT (AWR art. 30h). It runs from 1 January after the tax year until payment, or until 14 days after the date of the additional assessment. The rate is 5% for days from 1 January 2026, and was 6.5% for days in 2025. Interest uses 30-day months and a 360-day year and is rounded down to whole euros. No interest if the error is corrected voluntarily, or the late amount paid, within 3 months after the year ([belastingrente bij btw](https://www.belastingdienst.nl/wps/wcm/connect/bldcontentnl/standaard_functies/prive/contact/rechten_en_plichten_bij_de_belastingdienst/belastingrente/belastingrente_betalen_bij_loonbelasting_btw_en_overdrachtsbelasting)).

## Cross-border points

- **Foreign suppliers.** If the supplier is not established in the Netherlands and the customer is a Dutch entrepreneur or legal entity, the customer owes the VAT (art. 12(2) and (3)). Purchases go in 4a (non-EU) or 4b (EU) and are deducted in 5b. Services connected with Dutch real estate from an EU supplier go in 2a, not 4b.
- **Intra-EU supplies of goods (3b, 0%).** Need proof the goods left the Netherlands, a customer with a valid VAT number from another EU state, and a correct, timely ICP listing. Report goods by invoice date.
- **B2B services to EU customers (3b).** The customer pays VAT in its own state; report in the period the service was performed and on the ICP listing. Real-estate services, passenger transport, event admission, restaurant services and short-term vehicle hire are not reported this way.
- **Exports (3a)** of goods outside the EU are at 0%.
- **Imports.** With an article 23 licence, import VAT is declared in 4a and deducted in 5b instead of being paid at the border.
- **Distance sales and digital services to EU consumers** above EUR 10,000 a year are taxed in the customer's state; use box 3c or the OSS return (not covered here).
- **EU SME scheme.** A Dutch-established business can use the small business exemption in other EU states if its EU turnover (including the Netherlands) is not more than EUR 100,000 this year and last year, it stays under each state's own limit, and it does not use the import scheme. It gets a number with the suffix "EX" and files a quarterly turnover report with the Belastingdienst (art. 25c). A business established in another EU state can use the Dutch KOR on the same EUR 100,000 condition (art. 25a(2)).
- **Non-established businesses** file under other deadlines and may need a fiscal representative. Refer.
- **Foreign VAT** paid in another EU state is not deducted on the Dutch return; it is reclaimed through the EU refund procedure.
- **United Kingdom** is a third country. Northern Ireland counts as EU for goods only.

## Worked examples

### Example 1: Bicycle shop, Q3 2026 quarterly return

A Dutch bicycle shop sells bicycles for EUR 18,000 excluding VAT in Q3 2026 (21%, box 1a) and does repairs for EUR 2,000 (bicycle repairs are Table I post b4, 9%, box 1b). It buys parts for EUR 1,000 from a German supplier who shifts the VAT; the shop declares 21% in 4b and deducts the same amount in 5b. Its Dutch purchase invoices carry EUR 1,260 of VAT.

Output VAT is EUR 3,780 in 1a, EUR 180 in 1b and EUR 210 in 4b, so 5a is EUR 4,170. Input VAT in 5b is EUR 1,260 plus EUR 210, so EUR 1,470. The shop pays EUR 2,700. Return and payment must be received by 31 October 2026.

```oa-check
name: Netherlands Q3 2026 return, bicycle shop with an EU purchase
inputs:
  sales_21: 18000
  repairs_9: 2000
  eu_parts: 1000
  domestic_input_vat: 1260
steps:
  - box_1a_vat: sales_21 * 0.21
  - box_1b_vat: repairs_9 * 0.09
  - box_4b_vat: eu_parts * 0.21
  - box_5a: box_1a_vat + box_1b_vat + box_4b_vat
  - box_5b: domestic_input_vat + box_4b_vat
  - payable: box_5a - box_5b
expected:
  box_1a_vat: 3780
  box_1b_vat: 180
  box_5a: 4170
  box_5b: 1470
  payable: 2700
```

### Example 2: B&B, Q1 2026, the accommodation rate change, a small correction and a late payment

A B&B files quarterly. In Q1 2026 it sells room nights for EUR 12,000 excluding VAT. From 1 January 2026 these are at 21% (box 1a). It sells breakfast separately for EUR 1,500, which stays at 9% as food (box 1b). Its input VAT is EUR 600.

In December 2025 guests prepaid EUR 4,000 excluding VAT for stays in February 2026. The B&B charged and declared 9% on that in its Q4 2025 return. Because the stay is in 2026, the rate is 21% even though the money came in 2025. The shortfall is 12% of EUR 4,000, so EUR 480. That is EUR 1,000 or less, so no suppletie is needed: the B&B adds it to its next return (Q1 2026) in the box where it belongs.

The Q1 return shows EUR 2,520 plus EUR 135 plus EUR 480, less EUR 600 input VAT: EUR 2,535 to pay by 30 April 2026. The return arrives on time, but the money reaches the Belastingdienst on 11 May 2026, after the 7 days' grace. The penalty is 3% of EUR 2,535, which is EUR 76.05. That is above the EUR 50 minimum and below the EUR 6,709 maximum.

```oa-check
name: Netherlands Q1 2026 B&B with the logies change, a correction and a late-payment penalty
inputs:
  rooms: 12000
  breakfast: 1500
  prepaid_2025: 4000
  input_vat: 600
  suppletie_threshold: 1000
steps:
  - box_1a_vat: rooms * 0.21
  - box_1b_vat: breakfast * 0.09
  - shortfall: prepaid_2025 * (0.21 - 0.09)
  - over_threshold: max(0, shortfall - suppletie_threshold)
  - payable: box_1a_vat + box_1b_vat + shortfall - input_vat
  - penalty: min(6709, max(50, payable * 0.03))
expected:
  box_1a_vat: 2520
  box_1b_vat: 135
  shortfall: 480
  over_threshold: 0
  payable: 2535
  penalty: 76.05
```

If the shortfall had been more than EUR 1,000, the B&B would have filed a suppletie for 2025 within 8 weeks of finding it. Filed before 1 April 2026, it carries no belastingrente.

## Ask the client first

1. Is the business established in the Netherlands, and what is its legal form? Non-established businesses follow other rules.
2. Which period do you file (month, quarter, year)? Send the aangiftebrief with the deadlines and payment references.
3. Are you in the KOR, or what was your turnover in 2025 and so far in 2026? It decides whether you file at all.
4. Do you sell accommodation, culture, sport or food? The 2026 rate change hits accommodation only.
5. Do you buy from or sell to businesses abroad, and do you hold your EU customers' VAT numbers? It decides 2a, 3b, 4a, 4b and the ICP listing.
6. Have you found any errors in earlier returns, and how large are they? Above EUR 1,000 a suppletie is required.
