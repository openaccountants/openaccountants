# Netherlands: the 30% ruling (expat scheme) for incoming employees, 2026

## Who this applies to

The expat scheme lets an employer pay part of the wage of an incoming employee as a tax-free allowance for extraterritorial costs (the extra costs of a temporary stay outside the home country), without proof of the actual costs. The law calls this the bewijsregel. The Belastingdienst calls it the expatregeling; until 2024 it was the 30%-regeling. Legal basis: Wet op de loonbelasting 1964 (Wet LB) art. 31a(2)(e) and (8), and Uitvoeringsbesluit loonbelasting 1965 (UB LB) art. 10e to 10ej.

An incoming employee (ingekomen werknemer, UB LB art. 10e(2)(b)) is someone who:

- is an employee, recruited from another country or sent to the Dutch employer from another country. The self-employed are out. So is someone who came to the Netherlands for another reason, such as a refugee (Handboek Loonheffingen 2026, §19.4.9);
- has specific expertise that is scarce or not available on the Dutch labour market, shown through the salary norm (UB LB art. 10eb);
- lived, for more than two thirds of the 24 months before starting work in the Netherlands (so more than 16 months; exactly 16 fails), more than 150 km from the Dutch border. People from Belgium and Luxembourg never meet this. Northern France, large parts of Germany and a small part of the UK fail it too (Handboek §19.4.1).

Two exceptions to the 150 km rule:

- **PhD graduates.** If work starts within one year of the doctorate, the PhD period spent in the Netherlands or within 150 km of it is ignored (UB LB art. 10e(3)).
- **Returning employees.** The 150 km rule does not apply if the person met it before an earlier Dutch employment that started no more than five years before the new one (UB LB art. 10e(4)). The earlier period still shortens the new term.

Residents of Aruba, Curaçao, Sint Maarten and the BES islands can also qualify (Handboek §19.4.1). The employer must hold a decision (beschikking) from the Belastingdienst. Employees sent abroad from the Netherlands (uitgezonden werknemers) have their own version and are not covered here.

Who keeps the old salary norm: an employee whose decision was valid in 2024 and whose ruling was applied by the last pay period of 2024 keeps the 2024 norm, indexed each year, for the whole term. Rulings first applied from 1 January 2025 move to the new norm from 2027 (Handboek §19.4.1 and §19.4.3).

The 2024 phasing (30% for 20 months, then 20%, then 10%) does not apply from 2025. Belastingplan 2025 (Wet van 18 December 2024, Stb. 2024, 434) set a flat maximum in Wet LB art. 31a(8): 30% for 2025 and 2026, 27% from 1 January 2027. The government left UB LB art. 10ea(1)(a) and (3) unamended by mistake, so the consolidated text for 2026 still shows 30/20/10. The draft Fiscaal verzamelbesluit 2026 (art. II, part B) deletes the phasing with retroactive effect to 1 January 2025 and (part C) changes 30% to 27% from 2027. The Belastingdienst applies 30% for the full term in 2025 and 2026 (Handboek §19.4.3). For 2026, apply 30% for every incoming employee.

## The taxable base

**The allowance.** Up to 30% (2026) of the wage including the allowance, which equals 30/70 of the wage excluding it. The base is wage from current employment taxed in Dutch payroll, so far as the employee has no right to double tax relief on it, plus the allowance itself (UB LB art. 10ea(1)). The employer is not obliged to pay the maximum. Three limits apply:

1. **Salary norm.** The taxable wage excluding the allowance must be more than the norm, tested on a full-year basis (Handboek §19.4.1). If a full 30% would push it to or below the norm, cut the allowance so the wage stays above it. In the Handboek example, a wage including the allowance of EUR 50,000 gives a maximum allowance of EUR 1,986 (§19.4.3).
2. **WNT cap.** The allowance is calculated on at most the WNT norm: EUR 78,600 for a full year in 2026 (Wet LB art. 31a(8)). For a part year the cap is pro rated (a month counts as 65/3 days, a year as 260). The rule delaying the cap for rulings applied in the last pay period of 2022 ended on 1 January 2026, so from 2026 the cap applies to everyone (Handboek §19.4.3).
3. **School fees.** Fees for international schools can be paid tax-free on top of the allowance (UB LB art. 10e(8), 10ea(1)(b)).

Anything above the maximum is taxable wage. It can be designated as final-levy wage under the werkkostenregeling where that is customary (Handboek §19.4).

**Ruling or actual costs, each year.** For the first pay period of each calendar year in which extraterritorial costs are reimbursed, the employer chooses either the ruling or reimbursing proven actual costs. The choice holds for the whole year (Wet LB art. 31a(17)). After a timely application the employer may choose per pay period in the first 4 months, then for the rest of that year (art. 31a(18)). If the employer uses the ruling and also pays actual extraterritorial costs, the extra payment is taxable wage, except school fees (Handboek §19.4).

**What counts as extraterritorial cost** (relevant when actual costs are reimbursed): cost-of-living differences, an orientation trip, permits and visas, medical checks, double housing, first housing above 18% of wage, storage, home leave, language courses, the extra cost of having the tax return prepared, A1 certificate applications and international school fees (Handboek §19.4.5). For periods of work or stay in the Netherlands, extra living expenses and private calls home are excluded by law (Wet LB art. 31a(2)(e)). Never extraterritorial: expat or foreign-service allowances, bonuses, capital losses, buying and selling a home, tax equalisation.

**Salary-norm failure.** If the employee no longer has the expertise (wage at or below the norm), the term ends from that moment (UB LB art. 10ee). The Handboek treats a failed annual test as ending the ruling from 1 January of that year, and the ruling does not come back if the wage later rises (§19.4.1). For parental, pregnancy, birth, foster and adoption leave, test the wage the employee would have had without the leave (§19.4.1).

**Employee insurance premiums.** The allowance is a targeted exemption, so no employee insurance premiums are due on it (Handboek §19.4.8). The employee's annual statement already shows the wage after the allowance; that is the figure for the income tax return.

## Deadlines and filing

- **Application.** Employer and employee apply together on the form "Verzoek Loonheffingen expatregeling" for the year (Handboek §19.4.1). Filed within 4 months of the start of work, the decision applies from the first working day. Filed later, it applies from the first day of the month after the month of the request, and the term is reduced by the gap (UB LB art. 10ei(2), 10eg). The Belastingdienst says it decides within 8 weeks.
- **Term.** At most 5 years from the first working day, ending on the last day of the pay period after the one in which employment ends (UB LB art. 10ec(1)). It is reduced by earlier Dutch work or stay that ended within the 25 years before, rounded up to whole months (art. 10ef, 10eh). Not counted: up to 20 working days a year, up to 6 weeks a year for holidays or family, and once a single stay of up to 3 consecutive months (art. 10ef(3)-(4)).
- **Change of employer.** The ruling continues for the remaining term if the new employment starts within 3 months and the new employer and employee request it jointly (UB LB art. 10ed). Within a connected group of withholding agents no new request is needed (art. 10ej).
- **Payroll return and payment.** The allowance goes in the normal payroll tax return (aangifte loonheffingen). The return is due within the term the inspector sets, at least one month after the end of the period (AWR art. 10(2)). Payment is due within one month of the end of the period (AWR art. 19(1)).
- **Corrections.** After a salary-norm failure, correct the payroll returns for that year (Handboek §19.4.1).
- **Income tax return.** The employee reports the wage from the annual statement. Partial non-resident status, where still allowed, is chosen in the return (see Cross-border points).

## Penalties and interest

- **Late, missing, incorrect or incomplete payroll return:** an administrative penalty of up to EUR 1,675 (AWR art. 67b(2)).
- **Late or short payment:** up to EUR 6,709 (AWR art. 67c(1)).
- **Intentional or grossly negligent short payment** (for example, an allowance paid knowing the employee fails the norm): up to 100% of the tax not paid (AWR art. 67f).
- **Interest:** belastingrente on a payroll additional assessment set after the end of the year (AWR art. 30h), at 5% from 1 January 2026 (Belastingdienst rate table).
- **Decision withdrawn.** The inspector can withdraw or revise a wrong decision, normally for future pay periods only, and for past periods in cases of bad faith or a visible error by the inspector (UB LB art. 10ei(4)-(5)).

## Cross-border points

- **Partial non-resident status ends.** Until 2024 a resident with the ruling could choose to be treated as a non-resident for box 2 and box 3. This was abolished from 1 January 2025 (Handboek §19.4.7). Transitional rule: if the ruling was applied over the last pay period of 2023 and the employee has not become a new incoming employee after a break after 31 December 2023, the choice stays open up to and including the 2026 return. A move to a new employer within 3 months with a joint request is not a break. From the 2027 tax year nobody has it. Box 1, including the salary, was never affected. The employer's simplified arrangement for settling income tax through payroll can be used until 2027 only for employees still covered.
- **Fiscal partners.** A partner can choose partial non-resident status only if they meet the conditions themselves.
- **Double tax relief.** Wage on which the employee has a right to relief from double taxation is left out of the base (UB LB art. 10ea(1)(a)). Employees who live abroad, work in several countries or need an A1 certificate: see nl-cross-border and nl-double-tax-relief.
- **After the ruling.** When the decision ends, the full wage is taxed. Actual extraterritorial costs can be reimbursed tax-free for at most 5 years, so they cannot replace the ruling afterwards (Handboek §19.4).
- **2025 returns being filed now.** The 2025 norm was EUR 46,660 (EUR 35,468 under 30 with a master's) and the maximum was 30% for everyone. Use the wage on the 2025 annual statement. Partial non-resident status on the 2025 return is open only under the 2023 transitional rule.

## Worked examples

**Example 1: full year 2026, standard case (simple).** An employee aged 35 earns a wage including the allowance of EUR 70,000 for all of 2026. Maximum allowance: 30% x 70,000 = EUR 21,000. Taxable wage: EUR 49,000. That is EUR 987 above the EUR 48,013 norm, so the ruling holds. The full-year cap (EUR 78,600) does not bite. This follows the Handboek §19.4.3 example.

```oa-check
name: Full year 2026, wage including allowance EUR 70,000
inputs:
  wage_incl: 70000
  norm: 48013
  cap_full_year: 78600
steps:
  - allowance: min(wage_incl * 0.30, cap_full_year)
  - taxable: wage_incl - allowance
  - margin_over_norm: taxable - norm
expected:
  allowance: 21000.00
  taxable: 49000.00
  margin_over_norm: 987.00
```

**Example 2: high earner starting mid-year, part-year cap (edge case).** Work starts on 17 June 2026, with 10 working days left in June. Wage including the allowance from 17 June to 31 December: EUR 150,000. Step 1: 30% x 150,000 = EUR 45,000. Step 2: the period is 10 days plus 6 months of 65/3 days = 140 days; the WNT norm for the period is 262,000 x 140 / 260 = EUR 141,076.92. Step 3: 30% of that, rounded up to whole euros as in the Handboek, is EUR 42,324. The allowance is capped at EUR 42,324 and EUR 107,676 is taxable, far above the norm on a full-year basis. This follows the Handboek §19.4.3 example.

```oa-check
name: Part-year 2026 start on 17 June, WNT cap pro rated
inputs:
  wage_incl: 150000
  wnt_norm: 262000
  days_june: 10
  full_months: 6
steps:
  - uncapped: wage_incl * 0.30
  - days: days_june + full_months * 65 / 3
  - scaled_norm: wnt_norm * days / 260
  - cap: ceil(scaled_norm * 0.30)
  - allowance: min(uncapped, cap)
  - taxable: wage_incl - allowance
expected:
  days: 140.00
  scaled_norm: 141076.92
  cap: 42324.00
  allowance: 42324.00
  taxable: 107676.00
```

## Ask the client first

- **When the ruling was first applied in payroll:** by the last pay period of 2022, of 2023, during 2024, or from 2025. This fixes the 2027 percentage (30% or 27%), the salary norm, and partial non-resident status.
- **Residence in the 24 months before the first Dutch working day,** month by month, and any PhD or earlier Dutch employment in the last 5 years.
- **Earlier work or stays in the Netherlands in the last 25 years,** including study and earlier rulings. These shorten the term.
- **Expected taxable wage per year without the allowance, age, and highest degree,** or whether the person is a researcher at a designated institution or a doctor in specialist training.
- **Dates:** first working day, date of the application, any change of employer and the gap between jobs.
- **What the employer pays:** the agreed allowance, any actual-cost reimbursements, housing and school fees.

Refer to a Dutch tax adviser: objections against a decision, scarcity-test cases (sectors where nearly everyone earns above the norm), unclear earlier stays, intra-group secondments on a foreign payroll, agreements that might keep a ruling after a salary-norm failure (knowledge group position KG:204:2023:21), and planning for large foreign portfolios now that partial non-resident status ends.
