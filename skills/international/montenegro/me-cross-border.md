# Montenegro corporate cross-border preparation v0.1

## Who this applies to

Prepare a separate residence/PE, income, withholding and credit analysis for each Montenegro resident company, nonresident company or Montenegro PE. Record payer/recipient legal type, incorporation and actual management, business locations, contracts, services/property, payment date, gross/net terms and foreign tax evidence. Do not apply corporate rules to an individual simply because a foreign bank account is used. [Articles 2–5, 29–29c, 33–34b][law]

This 2026 draft uses the Ministry consolidation through 88/24. The enacted July 2026 amendment applies from January 2027; it also defers specified provisions to EU accession. It is not a basis for immediate 2026 EU-directive relief or a new 2026 interest/exit calculation. Full amendment implementation, current forms, individual mobility/treaties and independent country/professional review remain open. [104/26 Articles 15–19][amend]; [existing law Article 45a][law]

## Rates, liability and relief gates

| Item | Domestic rule / gate | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Corporate residence | Incorporated in Montenegro or actual management/control there | Article 3 |
| Resident / PE scope | Resident worldwide profit; nonresident Montenegro profit; PE profit attributable to that PE | Article 4 |
| Listed corporate payments | Ordinary withholding 15% of gross at payment; classify the actual Article 29 population | Article 29(1)–(4) |
| Specified territory | 30% gross rule with Article 29(5)–(7) conditions/exception; use the Ministry list and actual residence/management evidence | Article 29; official territory list |
| Treaty relief | Effective applicable treaty, recipient treaty residence proved by competent-authority evidence and beneficial ownership; payer bears the underpayment difference if required conditions are absent | Article 29a |
| Foreign-profit credit | Qualifying foreign tax paid, capped by Montenegro tax computed on the foreign profit | Article 33 |
| Foreign subsidiary dividend credit | Separate gross-up, cap, five-year unused-credit provision and minimum ownership/holding/evidence conditions | Articles 34a–34b |

Source: [corporate law][law]; [official list attachment][territories]. The ordinary CIT computation is progressive under Article 28. Neither 9% nor 15% is a universal rate for every overseas receipt.

## Residence and permanent establishment

An incorporation certificate does not settle actual-management residence. Keep board/management decision evidence, offices, staff, functions, assets and contracts. If two domestic laws claim residence, obtain the actual treaty and resolve its applicable residence provision; do not pick whichever address produces less tax. [Articles 3–4, 34][law]

Article 4's domestic PE definition includes fixed places such as management office, branch, office, factory/workshop and extraction locations. Its construction/assembly rule requires duration over six months; its storage/delivery/purchasing/information/preparatory or auxiliary exclusions have their own factual conditions. Build a site/project timeline and activity register before applying an exception. A six-month building-site rule is not a universal safe period for every business presence or every treaty. [Article 4(4)–(5)][law]

For an established PE, reconcile local accounts to attributable revenue, supported expenses/assets and tax adjustments. Keep head-office dealings and allocations in a separate evidence schedule and apply the [transfer-pricing documentation method](me-transfer-pricing.md), including Article 38c(4). Do not treat an internal head-office invoice as automatic deduction, or equate company-register entry with complete PE-tax analysis. [Articles 4, 7–20, 38c][law]

## Outbound payment decision

### Classify income and payer

Article 29(1) covers dividends/profit shares to resident and nonresident legal persons; specified interest, IP, capital gains, movable/immovable rent, consulting, market research and audit fees to nonresident legal persons; and liquidation distributions to legal persons. Paragraphs (2)–(3) add specified purchases from individuals and performance/programme payments. Those populations are not interchangeable. [Article 29][law]

Article 29b supplies definitions. Consulting includes specified legal/tax/accounting/audit and other advice but excludes the listed teaching, engineering/architectural deliverables, intermediary/representation and database-use categories. Royalties include specified rights/software/equipment use. Read contracts and deliverables: a “software”, “consulting” or “reimbursement” invoice label does not establish the result. Determine the right payment class and any treaty article before applying a rate. [Article 29b][law]

Reconcile the gross fee and who bears the tax. If an agreement guarantees a net sum and the payer bears withholding, calculate the gross amount and withholding consistently. If the contract states a gross fee, subtract withholding from that gross. The examples assume no VAT, foreign-exchange or additional charges; withholding does not determine those taxes. [Article 29(4)][law]

A Montenegro PE making covered payments has its own withholding duty under Article 29(8). Paragraph (9) provides an exception where the remuneration is attributed as income to a nonresident legal person's PE. Obtain attribution and return evidence; a bank account or an assertion that “the supplier has a branch” is insufficient. [Article 29(8)–(9)][law]

### Territory and treaty evidence

Screen the actual recipient against the Ministry's list and Article 29(5)–(7), including incorporation, registered/management/actual-management office and the other-treaty-residence exception. The retrieved Ministry list names Jersey, Guernsey and other territories; do not substitute a generic OECD/EU list or assume an entity's ownership alone determines its status. [Article 29(5)–(10)][law]; [official list][territories]

For treaty relief, retain the current treaty text/protocol, entry/application dates, income classification, residence certificate or appropriate competent-authority-certified document, beneficial-owner facts, PE connection and any relevant anti-abuse/MLI analysis. Article 34 gives a double-tax agreement priority, but a treaty's signature or appearance on a list does not prove a rate for a payment. The Ministry's [treaty index][treaties] is a discovery route; this draft contains no certified country-rate matrix or treaty-rate example. [Articles 29a, 34][law]

If the payer applies relief without the statutory conditions and underpays, Article 29a(3) makes it liable for the difference. A later refund/correction route requires current procedural/form evidence; do not promise automatic recovery or use missing documents as proof of treaty entitlement. [Article 29a][law]

## Nonresident gains and rents where the payer does not withhold

Article 29c separately addresses qualifying nonresident corporate capital gains from another nonresident legal person or a resident/nonresident individual, and specified rent received from a payer without withholding duty. It requires the nonresident to file through a tax representative within 30 days of earning the income, with the competent local authority issuing the decision. Classify the precise payer/asset/source before routing the return; do not use a general year-end corporate return as a substitute. [Article 29c][law]

Articles 21–24 govern gain versus sale proceeds, market valuation and adjusted acquisition cost, including special property/share rules. Maintain acquisition/valuation and sale-cost evidence. Property VAT, property transfer/annual tax and individual gains are separate methods and remain outside this corporate example set. [Articles 21–24][law]

## Incoming foreign profit and dividend credits

For a resident's foreign business profit, create a country/source/period schedule reconciling gross profit, Montenegro adjustments, the foreign tax actually paid, the local cap and credit claimed. Article 33 caps the credit by the amount obtained applying this law to foreign profit; a foreign-tax percentage alone is not a local credit. Preserve tax assessments/payment and the applicable treaty analysis. Multi-source progressive-cap mechanics and general-credit carryforward treatment require further implementation work; the example keeps all profit within the first band and claims no carryforward. [Articles 28, 33–34][law]

A foreign subsidiary dividend uses the separate Articles 34a–34b route: gross up the dividend by the paid withholding specified in Article 34a, apply the statutory cap and maintain any eligible unused-credit register up to five years. Article 34b requires at least 10% ownership for an uninterrupted year preceding return submission and appropriate evidence of holding and foreign tax. Do not carry the dividend-credit holding/carryforward conditions into a general foreign-profit credit without authority, or count the same foreign tax twice. A complete dividend-credit computation and form mapping remain open. [Articles 34a–34b][law]

| Route | Sourced timetable | Evidence / open implementation |

Sources: [Articles 29, 29c, 39–40][law]. Exact current form fields, portal workflow, calendar/holiday adjustments and treaty refund/correction documents have not been validated. Do not describe a prepared workpaper as a submitted return.

## Penalties and procedure

Article 42a specifies EUR 1,000–20,000 legal-person fines for the listed computation, withholding/reporting and return/document failures, with EUR 500–2,000 for the responsible person. The reproduction contains a withholding-report cross-reference inconsistency; use Article 29(11) for the actual reporting duty and check the current operative offence before quoting liability. Article 42 sends interest, appeals and collection to tax-procedure law. Exact current correction/interest/appeal methods remain open; no exposure estimate is cleared here. [Articles 29, 42–42a][law]

## Worked examples

Hypothetical EUR calculations. Each assumes the required legal/entity/income classification is established. Arithmetic is not a treaty decision, proof of paid foreign tax or filing acceptance.

### 1. Ordinary gross consulting fee

A resident company pays a qualifying nonresident corporate consulting fee of 20,000 gross. The ordinary 15% domestic rate applies; no treaty or listed-territory exception is claimed. [Articles 29, 29b][law]

```oa-check
name: Montenegro ordinary gross corporate withholding
inputs:
  gross_fee: 20000
  applicable_rate: 0.15
steps:
  - withholding: gross_fee * applicable_rate
  - supplier_cash: gross_fee - withholding
expected:
  withholding: 3000
  supplier_cash: 17000
```

### 2. Net guarantee and payer-funded withholding

The same qualifying ordinary service instead guarantees supplier cash of 17,000; the payer bears the 15% tax. Gross up the net guarantee. Deductibility of the payer's cost needs separate evidence. [Article 29(4)][law]

```oa-check
name: Montenegro corporate fee net guarantee gross up
inputs:
  guaranteed_net: 17000
  applicable_rate: 0.15
steps:
  - gross_taxable_fee: guaranteed_net / (1 - applicable_rate)
  - withholding: gross_taxable_fee * applicable_rate
  - supplier_cash: gross_taxable_fee - withholding
expected:
  gross_taxable_fee: 20000
  withholding: 3000
  supplier_cash: 17000
```

### 3. Specified-territory branch

Assume a corporate recipient incorporated and managed in Jersey receives a covered consulting fee of 10,000 gross and the Article 29(5)–(7) analysis establishes the 30% rate, with no other treaty-residence exception. This is a conditional statutory example, not a finding about an actual recipient. [Article 29][law]; [Ministry list naming Jersey][territories]

```oa-check
name: Montenegro conditional specified territory withholding
inputs:
  gross_fee: 10000
  established_special_rate: 0.30
steps:
  - withholding: gross_fee * established_special_rate
  - recipient_cash: gross_fee - withholding
expected:
  withholding: 3000
  recipient_cash: 7000
```

### 4. Foreign business-profit credit in the first CIT band

A resident's total adjusted taxable profit is 90,000, comprising domestic 50,000 and foreign 40,000. Qualifying foreign profit tax of 6,000 is evidenced as paid. There are no losses, reliefs, other credits or treaty changes. Both total and foreign profit remain in the 9% band, avoiding an unsupported multi-band allocation convention. Excess foreign tax is not assumed refundable or carried forward. [Articles 28, 33–34][law]

```oa-check
name: Montenegro first band foreign business tax credit cap
inputs:
  domestic_profit: 50000
  foreign_profit: 40000
  first_band_rate: 0.09
  qualifying_foreign_tax_paid: 6000
steps:
  - total_profit: domestic_profit + foreign_profit
  - montenegro_tax_before_credit: total_profit * first_band_rate
  - foreign_profit_cap: foreign_profit * first_band_rate
  - credit_used: min(qualifying_foreign_tax_paid, foreign_profit_cap)
  - montenegro_tax_after_credit: montenegro_tax_before_credit - credit_used
  - foreign_tax_above_cap: qualifying_foreign_tax_paid - credit_used
expected:
  total_profit: 90000
  montenegro_tax_before_credit: 8100
  foreign_profit_cap: 3600
  credit_used: 3600
  montenegro_tax_after_credit: 4500
  foreign_tax_above_cap: 2400
```
