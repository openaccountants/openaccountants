# Montenegro transfer-pricing preparation v0.2

## Who this applies to

Use for a Montenegro taxpayer with controlled transactions, including domestic related parties and the documented dealings between a nonresident head office and its Montenegro PE. Identify the taxpayer, period, counterparties, ownership/control/family links, transaction categories and tax-authority large-taxpayer status before choosing the pricing or documentation route. A small accounting-company classification does not establish non-large-taxpayer status. [Articles 38–38c][law]

This 2026 preparation draft uses the Ministry's consolidation through 88/24 and the official 121/22 instruction. The enacted 104/26 amendment applies from 1 January 2027, with additional EU-accession deferrals: do not apply its new anti-profit-shifting chapter to an ordinary 2026 calculation. Full current implementation, specialist facts and independent country/professional review remain open. [Consolidation][law]; [104/26 Articles 15, 17, 19][amend]

## Relationship, thresholds and dates

| Test | Sourced rule | Workpaper consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ownership/control | At least 25% direct/indirect shares/interests, or actual ability to control decisions; Article 38 also specifies voting/profit/family and common-control links | Keep an ownership and control map, including rights outside share percentages. Do not infer independence from a holding below 25% alone. |
| Specified territory | A nonresident legal person within Article 29(5)–(6) is also related under Article 38(6) | Inspect the Ministry's current list and the entity's incorporation/office/management evidence. |
| Ordinary large-taxpayer documentation | Deliver with the Article 40 return under Article 38c(1) | Establish official large-taxpayer classification; attach the required documentation. |
| Other taxpayers | Possess documentation at return time; provide it within 45 days of receiving an authority request | Record readiness, request receipt and delivery evidence. |
| Simplified documentation | Non-large taxpayers can hold a shortened document where annual transactions with the related person do not exceed EUR 75,000 | Aggregate by counterparty/year, not invoice; this is a documentation rule, not an arm's-length tax exemption. |
| Transitional deadline | Article 44b specifies 30 June following the year, “until 2027”, for the respective delivery/possession populations | Resolve the transition boundary for 2026-income documentation filed in 2027; do not assume a general June 2027 extension. |

Sources: [Articles 29, 38, 38c, 40, 44b][law]; [instruction Article 10][instruction]; [Ministry territory list route][territories]. The ordinary Article 40 return/payment deadline is three months after the period ends and electronic filing is required. The documentation transition does not extend the tax return/payment deadline. The exact 2027 transition interpretation and current portal attachments have not been independently settled.

## The base and method

### Large-taxpayer status workpaper

The Ministry's April 2026 reproduction of the 31/17 criteria rule requires at least two of: fiscal capacity above EUR 600,000, turnover above EUR 10 million, or more than 50 employees. Fiscal capacity is the total public-revenue tax obligations over the two preceding business years; turnover likewise uses the stated two-year total, not EUR 10 million in each individual year. Record the exact period and source data, rather than a current-year accounting-size label. [Criteria Articles 2–3][large]

Banks/insurers have large status regardless of those tests. A related entity whose total or predominant turnover is with a large taxpayer can also be designated. The authority issues written notification of designation/loss of status. Retain that current notification and any clarification, rather than treating a self-calculated threshold sheet or old public list as the actual classification decision. This is the gate for Article 38c's delivery-versus-possession distinction; it is separate from CbCR group size. [Criteria Articles 4–6][large]; [Article 38c][law]

### Transaction and evidence register

For each counterparty, record the relationship provision, contract, goods/services/finance/IP, period, booked income/expense, receivables/payables, actual performance and legal/economic terms. Reconcile totals to the ledger, invoices and annual return. Keep head-office/PE dealings in the scope assessment rather than omitting them because there is no external invoice. [Articles 38, 38c(4)][law]; [instruction Articles 4, 10][instruction]

Perform a functional analysis covering actual functions, assets and risks. Explain any aggregated or separated transaction approach and why the comparables reflect the same economic conditions. Keep selection/rejection, source and comparability-adjustment evidence. Prefer internal comparables where reliable; when external data is needed, the instruction gives priority to Montenegro data, then permits other-country data with market comparability considered. A foreign group's policy or unsupported “standard markup” is not a local benchmark. [Instruction Articles 4–10][instruction]

### Select the method

| Method | Application and evidence |
| --- | --- |
| Comparable uncontrolled price (CUP) | Compare prices for comparable property, rights or services; support differences in contracts, market, substance and risk. Use only reliably quantified or immaterial differences. |
| Cost plus | Identify comparable direct and relevant indirect costs, then a supported gross markup on cost. Reconcile accounting-policy/cost-base differences. |
| Resale price | Start with unrelated-customer resale revenue and subtract a supported gross margin on sales. Extensive distributor value addition reduces reliability. |
| Transactional net margin (TNMM) | Segment the transaction's operating results and select sales, costs or assets as the supported denominator. Explain adjustments and the comparable population. |
| Profit split / other / combination | Profit split requires the integrated/unique-contribution analysis in Article 9. Other methods are available where the listed methods cannot establish an arm's-length price; a combination requires reasons. |

Sources: [Article 38b][law]; [instruction Articles 3–9][instruction]. Cost-plus gross markup divides gross profit by cost; resale gross margin divides gross profit by sales. They are not interchangeable percentages.

TNMM uses the instruction's three-to-five-year weighted net comparable data and interquartile range. Record available years, numerator/denominator consistency and quartile method. The calculation below verifies a weighted result only; it does not build a market range or establish that a selected point is legally required. Do not substitute an arithmetic mean of company/year percentages for a supported weighted measure. [Instruction Article 8][instruction]

### Finance and other tax interactions

Article 38b(2) provides the Ministry-prescribed financing-rate route. The actual 145/25 Gazette text, Articles 1–3, prescribes **4.97% for 2026**, applying from 1 January 2026 to the specified related-party loans/credits and other financial instruments. Record the instrument, year and chosen pricing route. Article 38b(3) permits an alternative supported general-method analysis; the hypothetical 6% example below is that separate route, not the prescribed rate. [Article 38b][law]; [145/25 operative text][rate]

Reconcile actual principal movements and earning/accrual period to interest; the new prescribed-rate examples assume a constant full-year principal. Do not invent an actual/360 or actual/365 convention, apply a 2026 rate to another year, or treat prescribed interest as automatic clearance of expense deductibility, withholding or a contractual price change. Compare booked and arm's-length income/expense separately under Article 38a, without unsupported downward netting. [Articles 38a–38b][law]; [145/25][rate]

For a general-method loan study, preserve principal movements, dates, currency, maturity, credit risk, security, options and reliable price evidence. Keep the arm's-length expense adjustment separate from withholding, deductibility, foreign exchange and any other limitation. IP, guarantees, cash pools, restructurings and unique assets require their own method/evidence development; this ordinary draft does not clear them.

### Adjustment and return reconciliation

Article 38a(4) requires positive differences for understated controlled income or overstated controlled expense to be included in the tax base. Compute by transaction or supported category/counterparty and reconcile the total adjustment to the return. Do not net an unsupported downward claim against a required upward adjustment. Separate a tax-base adjustment from a commercial credit note and any counterparty/corresponding-relief procedure. [Article 38a][law]; [instruction Article 10(1)(5)][instruction]

The actual CIT effect depends on all other adjustments and the progressive Article 28 computation. A 15% multiplication is not a universal Montenegro TP tax charge. This Guide's controls establish adjustment arithmetic only. [Articles 28, 38a, 40][law]

## Documentation, filing and penalties

The full instruction Article 10 file contains group analysis, industry analysis, functions/assets/risks and transaction description, selected-method justification, adjustment conclusion and a data/comparable appendix. Its limited industry-analysis exception requires both a non-main activity and less than 10% of total revenue; it is not an exemption from the rest of the file. A shortened file identifies transaction description/value and related counterparty, subject to the statutory eligibility gate. [Instruction Article 10][instruction]

### International-group reporting and notification

The actual instruction through 43/25, including its attached form, was read. Keep this obligation separate from local pricing documentation and the EUR 75,000 shortened-file threshold. Its international-group definition uses **at least EUR 750 million of consolidated revenue in the immediately preceding fiscal year**, ownership/control/consolidation and the stated international/PE conditions. Included constituents cover specified immaterial entities and separately reporting PEs as well as consolidated companies. Foreign-parent currency fluctuation uses point 9a's referenced authority/OECD guidance, not an invented conversion threshold. [Reporting points 2, 9–9a][cbcr]

Classify the Montenegro entity as ultimate parent, surrogate parent or other constituent; record identity/residence, group fiscal year and the actual reporting entity. Points 2b and 4 contain separate local-reporting gates: the former suspends the specified other-constituent report while Montenegro has not met the OECD appropriate-use/confidentiality minimum standards; it does not cover ultimate/surrogate parents. Current satisfaction of that condition has **not been verified here**. Do not declare all subsidiaries exempt indefinitely, or all subsidiaries required to file, without the current authority/OECD position. Points 4 and 16–18 add foreign-parent obligation/exchange/systemic-failure and surrogate conditions. A generic exchange agreement is not proof of the relevant activated reporting relationship. [Reporting points 2a–5, 10–18][cbcr]

Points 19–20 require status/reporting-entity identity and tax-residence notifications no later than the CIT-return filing date for the preceding fiscal year. Under point 21, no new notice is required while the reporting entity's identity and tax residence remain unchanged. Preserve the original notice and evidence of no change; do not confuse this notification with the full report. Parent-data refusal has separate notice provisions, including point 7's 12-month period. Those references are **not a verified general CbCR return deadline**; the current enabling statute, status guidance, notification format and accepted electronic report/deadline remain to be reconciled. [Reporting points 6–8, 14, 19–21][cbcr]

### Published CbCR form and data controls

The attached form contains Table 1 jurisdiction totals, Table 2 constituent identities/incorporation jurisdictions/activities and Table 3 additional information. Table 1 fields 1.5(a–c) separate unrelated/related/total revenue; 1.6 pre-tax profit; 1.7 current accrued income tax; 1.8 paid income tax; 1.9 capital; 1.10 accumulated earnings; 1.11 employees; 1.12 tangible assets. This is the published form mapping, not a tested IRMS/XML schema. [Reporting form Tables 1–3][cbcr]

Aggregate by the proper residence/PE jurisdiction and retain source-to-row reconciliations. Report revenue under the form definition, excluding qualifying intragroup dividends; do not automatically eliminate all intercompany revenue as in consolidated accounts. Pre-tax profit includes extraordinary items. Current accrued tax excludes deferred/contingent liabilities; paid tax includes actual payments and qualifying withholding paid by other entities, and need not equal current tax expense. Tangible assets exclude cash/equivalents, intangibles and financial assets. The instructions place PE assets in its jurisdiction, while capital/accumulated earnings have their distinct head-office rules; prevent double counting. [Reporting points 22–29; form instructions 1–18][cbcr]

Use consistent data sources/periods, disclose sources and reasons for changes, reconcile differing entity fiscal years under point 27, and document currency treatment. Table 2/3 complete the report's constituent/activity and explanation context. The calculation below illustrates one jurisdiction's data definitions after applicability and facts are established; its revenue is not the prior-year consolidated group threshold. [Reporting points 27–29; form instructions 19–22][cbcr]

Prepare the documentation and return reconciliation, identify the correct statutory/transitional deadline, obtain authorised sign-off, submit where required and save acceptance/delivery evidence. For request cases, record the date of receipt and the 45-day deadline rather than the date someone opened the email. No actual portal schema or delivery has been inspected here. [Articles 38c, 40, 44b][law]

Article 42a lists EUR 1,000–20,000 legal-person fines for specified failures, including documentation, computation and filing; the responsible-person range is EUR 500–2,000. Classify the actual offence before quoting exposure. Interest, correction, assessment and appeals require the Tax Administration Law under Article 42; those current procedures remain open. [Articles 42–42a][law]

## Worked examples

Hypothetical EUR cases, with separately established related-party and method eligibility. Benchmarks, comparability and taxpayer facts are assumptions, not verified market findings.

### 1. CUP and understated sales

A reliable comparable, after documented adjustments, supports 52 per unit for 10,000 units sold to a related person. Booked price is 49. Include the positive income difference; do not treat it as a tax payment by itself. [Article 38a][law]; [instruction Article 5][instruction]

```oa-check
name: Montenegro CUP upward income adjustment
inputs:
  units: 10000
  booked_unit_price: 49
  supported_unit_price: 52
steps:
  - booked_revenue: units * booked_unit_price
  - arm_length_revenue: units * supported_unit_price
  - upward_base_adjustment: max(0, arm_length_revenue - booked_revenue)
expected:
  booked_revenue: 490000
  arm_length_revenue: 520000
  upward_base_adjustment: 30000
```

### 2. Cost-plus service pricing

Verified comparable costs of 200,000 support a hypothetical 8% gross markup on cost. The provider booked revenue 210,000. This assumes costs/functions are comparable and services actually occurred; it is not a statutory service safe harbour. [Instruction Article 6][instruction]; [Article 38a][law]

```oa-check
name: Montenegro cost plus denominator and income adjustment
inputs:
  comparable_cost_base: 200000
  supported_markup_on_cost: 0.08
  booked_service_revenue: 210000
steps:
  - supported_gross_profit: comparable_cost_base * supported_markup_on_cost
  - arm_length_revenue: comparable_cost_base + supported_gross_profit
  - upward_base_adjustment: max(0, arm_length_revenue - booked_service_revenue)
expected:
  supported_gross_profit: 16000
  arm_length_revenue: 216000
  upward_base_adjustment: 6000
```

### 3. Resale margin and excessive purchase expense

An ordinary distributor resells goods to independent customers for 500,000. A supported hypothetical gross margin is 20% of sales. Related-party goods cost is booked at 430,000 and treated as expense in this example. Derive the allowable purchase amount from the sales denominator, not a 20% markup on cost. [Instruction Article 7][instruction]; [Article 38a][law]

```oa-check
name: Montenegro resale margin expense adjustment
inputs:
  unrelated_resale_revenue: 500000
  supported_margin_on_sales: 0.20
  booked_related_goods_expense: 430000
steps:
  - supported_gross_profit: unrelated_resale_revenue * supported_margin_on_sales
  - arm_length_goods_expense: unrelated_resale_revenue - supported_gross_profit
  - upward_base_adjustment: max(0, booked_related_goods_expense - arm_length_goods_expense)
expected:
  supported_gross_profit: 100000
  arm_length_goods_expense: 400000
  upward_base_adjustment: 30000
```

### 4. Weighted comparable TNMM input

Three comparable years have operating profit 30/100/90 and sales 1,000/2,000/3,000, on consistent accounting bases. Calculate the weighted operating return. This single comparable input is not an interquartile range or a selected adjustment point. [Instruction Article 8][instruction]

```oa-check
name: Montenegro TNMM weighted sales denominator
inputs:
  profit_year_1: 30
  profit_year_2: 100
  profit_year_3: 90
  sales_year_1: 1000
  sales_year_2: 2000
  sales_year_3: 3000
steps:
  - combined_profit: profit_year_1 + profit_year_2 + profit_year_3
  - combined_sales: sales_year_1 + sales_year_2 + sales_year_3
  - weighted_margin_percent: combined_profit / combined_sales * 100
expected:
  combined_profit: 220
  combined_sales: 6000
  weighted_margin_percent: 3.6666666667
```

### 5. General-method loan expense

A 300,000 loan is outstanding for the full year. Booked annual interest is 8%; a separately supported hypothetical general-method study establishes 6%. This deliberately does not use or certify the Ministry's 2026 prescribed rate. Other deduction restrictions and withholding are excluded from this illustration. [Article 38b(3), 38a][law]

```oa-check
name: Montenegro hypothetical loan pricing adjustment
inputs:
  principal: 300000
  booked_rate: 0.08
  supported_general_method_rate: 0.06
steps:
  - booked_interest: principal * booked_rate
  - arm_length_interest: principal * supported_general_method_rate
  - upward_base_adjustment: max(0, booked_interest - arm_length_interest)
expected:
  booked_interest: 24000
  arm_length_interest: 18000
  upward_base_adjustment: 6000
```

### 6. Prescribed 2026 rate: borrower expense adjustment

A qualifying related-party loan is 300,000 for the entire 2026 year, booked at 8%. The taxpayer uses the prescribed 4.97% route, with no general-method election. Booked interest 24,000 exceeds priced interest 14,910 by 9,090. No other restriction/withholding or automatic contractual refund is computed. [Articles 38a–38b][law]; [rate Articles 1–3][rate]

```oa-check
name: Montenegro 2026 prescribed borrower interest
inputs:
  full_year_principal: 300000
  booked_rate: 0.08
  prescribed_2026_rate: 0.0497
steps:
  - booked_expense: full_year_principal * booked_rate
  - priced_expense: full_year_principal * prescribed_2026_rate
  - positive_base_adjustment: max(booked_expense - priced_expense, 0)
expected:
  booked_expense: 24000
  priced_expense: 14910
  positive_base_adjustment: 9090
```

### 7. Prescribed 2026 rate: lender income adjustment

A separate qualifying full-year loan of 300,000 earns booked interest of 2%. Using the prescribed route gives interest 14,910 versus booked 6,000: positive income adjustment 8,910. This is not netted against the unrelated borrower example or treated as automatic foreign corresponding relief. [Articles 38a–38b][law]; [rate][rate]

```oa-check
name: Montenegro 2026 prescribed lender interest
inputs:
  full_year_principal: 300000
  booked_rate: 0.02
  prescribed_2026_rate: 0.0497
steps:
  - booked_income: full_year_principal * booked_rate
  - priced_income: full_year_principal * prescribed_2026_rate
  - positive_base_adjustment: max(priced_income - booked_income, 0)
expected:
  booked_income: 6000
  priced_income: 14910
  positive_base_adjustment: 8910
```

### 8. Published CbCR jurisdiction data reconciliation

Established in-scope reporter and jurisdiction, EUR source records: unrelated revenue 100 million, related receipts 45 million including 5 million qualifying excluded dividends. Tax expense 2.1 million includes deferred tax 0.3 million and no contingent tax. Actual current-year tax payments include current-period principal 1 million, prior-period tax 0.4 million and eligible withholding remitted for the entities 0.2 million, with no double counting. Asset total 30 million includes cash 5 million, intangibles 3 million and financial assets 2 million. No PE/currency complication in this illustration. [Form Table 1/instructions 1–17][cbcr]

```oa-check
name: Montenegro CbCR jurisdiction revenue tax and tangible assets
inputs:
  unrelated_revenue: 100000000
  related_receipts: 45000000
  excluded_dividends: 5000000
  tax_expense_including_deferred: 2100000
  deferred_tax: 300000
  current_tax_paid: 1000000
  prior_period_tax_paid: 400000
  qualifying_withholding_paid: 200000
  total_assets: 30000000
  cash: 5000000
  intangibles: 3000000
  financial_assets: 2000000
steps:
  - related_revenue: related_receipts - excluded_dividends
  - total_reported_revenue: unrelated_revenue + related_revenue
  - current_accrued_tax: tax_expense_including_deferred - deferred_tax
  - total_paid_tax: current_tax_paid + prior_period_tax_paid + qualifying_withholding_paid
  - tangible_assets: total_assets - cash - intangibles - financial_assets
expected:
  related_revenue: 40000000
  total_reported_revenue: 140000000
  current_accrued_tax: 1800000
  total_paid_tax: 1600000
  tangible_assets: 20000000
```
