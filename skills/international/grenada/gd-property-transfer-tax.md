# Grenada: ordinary property-transfer tax

## Scope and source limits

This Guide covers ordinary land transfers under the Property Transfer Tax Act, Chapter 257C, using Parliament's published consolidation and the [IRD property-transfer summary](https://ird.gd/taxes/property-transfer-tax). The consolidation lists amendments through 2010; it is not represented as a complete 2026 consolidation. The 2015 and 2016 amendments were retrieved separately and read alongside it. The 2015 Act prints commencement as 14 August 2015. The 2016 amendment commenced on **1 May 2016**, established by [SRO 27 of 2016, section 2](https://grenadaparliament.gd/wp-content/uploads/2021/08/S.R.O.-27-of-2016-Property-Transfer-Tax-Amendment-Act-Commencement-Order.pdf). Full amendment reconciliation, current forms and independent review remain outstanding.

The ordinary rates and thresholds below agree with the current official indexed IRD summary. This Guide does not certify eligibility for investment or tourism concessions, treaty or regional-nationality treatment, or complete closing costs.

## Classify the transaction and each party

Section 2 includes transfers by sale, exchange, gift or other lifetime disposition. Property includes land interests, leases of **ten years or more**, and company shares, including debentures. A transaction need not be an outright sale of freehold land to enter the Act. This Guide's calculations concern ordinary land transfers, not shares or long-lease valuation. [Act, section 2](https://grenadaparliament.gd/wp-content/uploads/2021/08/Cap257C-PROPERTY-TRANSFER-TAX-ACT.pdf#page=2).

“Citizen” has a statutory extended definition. It includes specified dependants, qualifying partnerships and companies controlled by citizens. Section 2(2) recognises control where citizens hold more than half the shares or sufficient voting power to determine the company's affairs. For a company without share capital, section 2(3) uses effective management in Grenada and more than half citizen directors. Incorporation in Grenada alone does not establish the classification. Obtain ownership, voting and management evidence for the actual parties. [Act, section 2](https://grenadaparliament.gd/wp-content/uploads/2021/08/Cap257C-PROPERTY-TRANSFER-TAX-ACT.pdf#page=2).

## Ordinary rates and taxable value

Section 5(1) provides the following separate liabilities for land:

| Party | Ordinary rate |
|---|---:|
| Citizen vendor | 5% |
| Noncitizen vendor | 15% |
| Noncitizen purchaser | 10% |

The vendor's and purchaser's taxes are separate. Section 5(1) does not impose the ordinary purchaser charge on a citizen purchaser. This does not exempt that person from other acquisition charges. [Act, section 5](https://grenadaparliament.gd/wp-content/uploads/2021/08/Cap257C-PROPERTY-TRANSFER-TAX-ACT.pdf#page=4).

For nongift land transfers, section 5(3) taxes only the value **exceeding EC$20,000**. Use the established value and calculate `max(value − 20,000, 0)` before applying the relevant ordinary rate. The threshold is not a deduction from the resulting tax bill. Section 5(5), inserted in 2015, applies the threshold to the entire property regardless of the number of transferors. Transactions between the same or connected persons involving contiguous property or property owned by the same person or persons are treated together for this purpose. Do not give each co-owner or connected split transfer another allowance. [Act 23 of 2015, section 2](https://grenadaparliament.gd/wp-content/uploads/2021/08/Act-No.-23-of-2015-Property-Transfer-Tax-Amendment.pdf#page=4). [Act, section 5(3)](https://grenadaparliament.gd/wp-content/uploads/2021/08/Cap257C-PROPERTY-TRANSFER-TAX-ACT.pdf#page=4).

The Act expresses the rates by reference to market value. Under section 8, the Comptroller obtains a valuation where there is no money consideration or the consideration does not represent fair market value. A nominal related-party sale price does not establish the taxable value. Keep price, valuation and any determination distinct; do not compute tax merely on profit over the seller's original cost. [Act, sections 5 and 8](https://grenadaparliament.gd/wp-content/uploads/2021/08/Cap257C-PROPERTY-TRANSFER-TAX-ACT.pdf#page=4).

**Gift boundary:** section 5(2) applies the **EC$150,000** gift threshold and expressly references the vendor-rate paragraphs (a), (b) and (c). Section 6(1)(e) exempts gifts not exceeding that value. This Guide does not assume that the larger gift threshold automatically determines every noncitizen recipient's liability above that amount; obtain the transaction-specific treatment. [Act, sections 5(2) and 6](https://grenadaparliament.gd/wp-content/uploads/2021/08/Cap257C-PROPERTY-TRANSFER-TAX-ACT.pdf#page=4).

## Mechanical examples

Assume an ordinary nongift land transfer, value EC$500,000 established for tax purposes, no exemption and no special development/investment relief. Taxable value is EC$480,000.

- Citizen seller: 480,000 × 5% = **EC$24,000**.
- Noncitizen seller: 480,000 × 15% = **EC$72,000**.
- Noncitizen buyer: 480,000 × 10% = **EC$48,000**, in addition to the applicable seller liability.

Thus a citizen seller/noncitizen buyer transaction has EC$72,000 combined transfer tax, while a noncitizen seller/noncitizen buyer transaction has EC$120,000. These totals do not include stamp duty, legal fees or other acquisition/holding obligations. At a value of exactly EC$20,000 the ordinary nongift land base under section 5(3) is zero. [Act, section 5](https://grenadaparliament.gd/wp-content/uploads/2021/08/Cap257C-PROPERTY-TRANSFER-TAX-ACT.pdf#page=4).

**Co-owner threshold check:** if two citizen co-owners transfer one ordinary property valued at EC$500,000, the total vendor tax remains (500,000 − 20,000) × 5% = **EC$24,000**. Giving each half a separate EC$20,000 threshold would wrongly produce EC$23,000. Allocation between owners requires their actual interests and assessment; the aggregate allowance is not doubled. [Act 23 of 2015, section 2](https://grenadaparliament.gd/wp-content/uploads/2021/08/Act-No.-23-of-2015-Property-Transfer-Tax-Amendment.pdf#page=4).

## Exemptions, special relief and completion evidence

Section 6 includes specified mortgage-related instruments, transfers by operation of law to a liquidator or bankruptcy trustee, specified trust transfers, testamentary/intestacy transfers, Crown/publicly funded statutory-board transfers and transfers by the named charitable/nonprofit bodies. Match the exact provision and conditions. In particular, the mortgage exemption has a declaration condition in section 6(2); a later sale by a mortgagee is not automatically an exempt mortgage instrument. [Act, sections 5(1a) and 6](https://grenadaparliament.gd/wp-content/uploads/2021/08/Cap257C-PROPERTY-TRANSFER-TAX-ACT.pdf#page=4).

**Tourism amendment:** section 3 of Act 23 of 2015 repealed the former sections 5A–5B and substituted a tourism-complex rule. Its first transfer must be by the developer: the printed rates are **1% for the vendor** and **5% for a noncitizen purchaser**; subsequent transfers use **2.5% for the vendor** and **2.5% for a noncitizen purchaser**. The complex must be geographically contiguous, constructed or substantially reconstructed after the enactment date, operated by agreement as an integrated facility principally for tourists/short-term visitors, with separate ownership of some or all units. An operating agreement giving any owner occupation exceeding **four weeks per year** disqualifies the complex under subsection (3). These are cumulative statutory eligibility checks, not a marketing label. [Act 23 of 2015, section 3](https://grenadaparliament.gd/wp-content/uploads/2021/08/Act-No.-23-of-2015-Property-Transfer-Tax-Amendment.pdf#page=4).

The same amendment preserves the former sections 5A–5B for approved tourism developments with Cabinet approval **before the amendment's commencement**. Obtain the approval date, relevant instruments and transfer history to identify which regime applies. Do not impose the old Cabinet-order test on every new tourism-complex transaction, or use the new 1% first-vendor rate for a saved old-regime development. No special-regime calculation is certified here until the property's actual status and the remaining amendment checks are resolved. [Act 23 of 2015, section 3(2)](https://grenadaparliament.gd/wp-content/uploads/2021/08/Act-No.-23-of-2015-Property-Transfer-Tax-Amendment.pdf#page=5).

Section 4 of the 2015 amendment also repeals the old section 7(5) discretionary remission/refund provision. Do not rely on that paragraph merely because it remains printed in the older consolidation. [Act 23 of 2015, section 4](https://grenadaparliament.gd/wp-content/uploads/2021/08/Act-No.-23-of-2015-Property-Transfer-Tax-Amendment.pdf#page=5).

## Qualifying-investment waiver and recovery

Act 9 of 2016 substitutes section 5(6)–(7) and adds subsections (8)–(9). Its effective date is **1 May 2016**, not the assent or Gazette publication date. [Commencement Order, section 2](https://grenadaparliament.gd/wp-content/uploads/2021/08/S.R.O.-27-of-2016-Property-Transfer-Tax-Amendment-Act-Commencement-Order.pdf).

The waiver concerns a **noncitizen purchaser** acquiring land for a qualifying investment under the Investment Act 2014 or its regulations. The following is the statutory waiver schedule, conditional on establishing qualifying-investment status, priority sector, qualifying costs and location. The percentages reduce the **tax payable**, not the property's value or the vendor's separate liability. [Act 9 of 2016, section 2, substituted section 5(6)](https://grenadaparliament.gd/wp-content/uploads/2021/08/Act-No.-9-of-2016-Property-Transfer-Tax-Amendment-Act.pdf#page=4).

| Qualifying costs in a priority sector | Location | Purchaser tax waived |
|---|---|---:|
| Up to EC$30 million, inclusive | Any parish | 50% |
| Over EC$30 million, up to EC$80 million inclusive | Parish of St. George | 75% |
| Over EC$30 million | Outside the parish of St. George | 100% |
| Over EC$80 million | Any parish | 100% |

At exactly EC$30 million, the printed schedule gives 50%; at exactly EC$80 million in St. George, it gives 75%. A project outside St. George needs costs **exceeding**, not merely equal to, EC$30 million for the 100% band. These are threshold checks, not a finding that a particular project qualifies. [Act 9 of 2016, section 2](https://grenadaparliament.gd/wp-content/uploads/2021/08/Act-No.-9-of-2016-Property-Transfer-Tax-Amendment-Act.pdf#page=4).

The purchaser must repay the waived tax within **two months** after any of these recovery triggers: failure to commence within **two years of the waiver grant**; failure to meet the relevant qualifying-cost threshold by completion or the investment-agreement deadline, whichever occurs first; or transfer to another party of the property where the qualifying investment has not been completed. Interest and penalties under the Tax Administration Act 2016 apply after the repayment period. “Commence” means commencement of **infrastructural work**. [Act 9 of 2016, substituted section 5(7) and added section 5(9)](https://grenadaparliament.gd/wp-content/uploads/2021/08/Act-No.-9-of-2016-Property-Transfer-Tax-Amendment-Act.pdf#page=5).

The Comptroller, consulting the investment-promotion agency, can extend the commencement or qualifying-cost time limits by no more than **twelve months**. This is a granted extension, not an automatic grace period; subsection (8) does not extend the unfinished-project transfer trigger. Preserve the waiver date, agreement, location evidence, qualifying-cost records, infrastructure commencement evidence and any written extension. [Act 9 of 2016, section 5(8)](https://grenadaparliament.gd/wp-content/uploads/2021/08/Act-No.-9-of-2016-Property-Transfer-Tax-Amendment-Act.pdf#page=5).

### Establish qualifying investment and qualifying costs

The [2016 priority-sector regulations](https://grenadaparliament.gd/wp-content/uploads/2021/08/S.R.O.-32-of-2016-Investment-Priority-Sectors-Regulations.pdf) also commenced on 1 May 2016. Regulation 3 names tourism accommodation; agriculture, agribusiness and agricultural research; restauranteering; tourism; manufacturing; education; health and wellness; medical services; sports; business processing outsourcing; creative arts; energy; and information technology/telecommunication services. Apply the detailed activity definitions in regulation 2, including sector-specific certification requirements. The exclusion of IT and telecommunications in regulation 3(2) expressly concerns Income Tax Act section 36A; do not silently apply that exclusion to property-transfer tax.

Regulation 4 covers new priority-sector facilities, new buildings within them, expansion and qualifying reconstruction. Reconstruction costs must **exceed 40%** of the reconstructed building's or structure's written-down value. Medical equipment in a new medical facility is separately covered; installation in an existing medical facility requires equipment cost **exceeding 75%** of existing equipment's written-down value. An acquisition of land alone does not establish one of these project conditions. [Regulation 4(1)](https://grenadaparliament.gd/wp-content/uploads/2021/08/S.R.O.-32-of-2016-Investment-Priority-Sectors-Regulations.pdf#page=7).

The minimum cumulative qualifying costs in regulation 4(2) are strict “exceeds” tests:

| Sector group | Required costs |
|---|---:|
| Agriculture/agribusiness/agricultural research, manufacturing, creative arts | Over EC$1 million |
| Restauranteering, tourism, education, health/wellness, sports, outsourcing, energy, IT/telecommunications | Over EC$3 million |

Regulation 4(3) imposes the EC$3 million threshold on a facility qualifying through both groups. Tourism accommodation and medical services are not listed in those two minimum-cost groups; their own definitions and project conditions still require checking. These eligibility thresholds are distinct from the EC$30 million/EC$80 million waiver bands. [Regulation 4(2)–(3)](https://grenadaparliament.gd/wp-content/uploads/2021/08/S.R.O.-32-of-2016-Investment-Priority-Sectors-Regulations.pdf#page=8).

**Land price is excluded from qualifying costs.** Regulation 5 includes specified construction materials/equipment, construction labour and management, and operating capital goods, subject to its exclusions. Exclusions include land, financing costs, legal/professional fees, preparatory planning/design costs, office equipment/furnishings and vehicles/aircraft/boats. Do not treat the total purchase price or project budget as the qualifying-cost amount. [Regulation 5](https://grenadaparliament.gd/wp-content/uploads/2021/08/S.R.O.-32-of-2016-Investment-Priority-Sectors-Regulations.pdf#page=9).

Regulation 5(1)(d)–(e) separately includes percentages of specified engineering/study costs for the medical-equipment investments in regulation 4(1)(e), while regulation 5(2) excludes preparatory/professional costs. Their interaction requires reconciliation before counting these costs; no blanket professional-fee allowance is certified here. The mixed-sector formula in regulation 4(4) expressly concerns an **investment allowance**; this Guide does not transpose it into a property-transfer waiver calculation.

### Investment-law framework and evidence

The Investment Act 42 of 2014 commenced on **29 April 2016**, under [SRO 31 of 2016](https://grenadaparliament.gd/wp-content/uploads/2021/08/S.R.O.-31-of-2016-Proclamation.pdf). Its title year is not its commencement year. Act 7 of 2016 prints **15 April 2016** as its commencement date and replaces section 15 with express regulation-making powers for priority sectors, large-scale projects and projects with specified projected capital investment. The priority-sector regulations and the property-transfer waiver amendment then commenced on 1 May 2016. Keep these distinct dates when examining an older transaction. [Act 7 of 2016, sections 1–2](https://grenadaparliament.gd/wp-content/uploads/2021/08/Act-No.-7-of-2016-Investment-Amendment-1.pdf).

Section 6(2) of the Investment Act requires every investment entity to register with the investment agency. Section 8 entitles an entity to incentives for which it qualifies under the applicable incentive law; registration alone is not a waiver decision. Section 14 requires compliance with applicable Grenadian law, including labour, health and safety, and environmental law. Preserve agency registration and the separate permissions relevant to the project. [Investment Act, sections 6, 8 and 14](https://grenadaparliament.gd/wp-content/uploads/2021/08/Act-No.-42-of-2014-Investment-Act.pdf).

**Remaining eligibility gap:** subsequent amendments and the actual approval procedure remain to be reconciled. Obtain sector certification, project scope, written-down values, an itemised cost schedule and the waiver decision before applying relief. This Guide does not certify a currently available concession for a particular purchaser.

## Recording the transfer

Sections 7 and 18 require evidence of payment or no-tax treatment for recording the transfer. Obtain the Comptroller's stamp/receipt and the required evidence before treating the conveyance as ready for recording. Do not invent a universal payment deadline from a deadline that concerns reassessment or appeal. Stamp duty and landholding permissions are separate questions. [Act, sections 7 and 18](https://grenadaparliament.gd/wp-content/uploads/2021/08/Cap257C-PROPERTY-TRANSFER-TAX-ACT.pdf#page=6).
