# Grenada: withholding tax on payments

## Scope and source status

This Guide reads Parliament's published Income Tax Act, Chapter 149, with Act 8 of 2016. The [IRD's G-TAX FAQ](https://www.ird.gd/gtax/index.html) corroborates a 15% rate for income paid by residents to nonresidents. The Tax Administration Act commenced on 1 May 2016 under [SRO 34](https://grenadaparliament.gd/wp-content/uploads/2021/08/S.R.O.-34-of-2016-Tax-Administration-Act-Commencement-Order-2016.pdf). The older income-tax consolidation is not certified as complete for 2026. Later amendments, administrative practice, forms, treaty relief and the timing conflicts below remain outstanding. Do not use this draft alone to set a remittance deadline.

## Classify the payment before applying a rate

Section 50(1) covers payments to nonresidents including nonexempt interest, discounts, commissions, fees, management charges, rent, lease premiums, licence charges and royalties. It applies whether or not the payer carries on a Grenadian business and whether or not the payer can deduct the expense. Payment to an overseas address creates a rebuttable presumption of nonresidence under section 50(4); obtain residence evidence instead of assuming nationality or bank location settles residence. [Act, section 50](https://grenadaparliament.gd/wp-content/uploads/2021/08/Cap149-INCOME-TAX-ACT.pdf).

The Third Schedule applies to specified payments, including independent personal services performed in Grenada. Its ordinary rate is **15% of the gross payment**, with final liability treatment under paragraph 3 subject to the Act's exceptions. Identify the precise provision before extending final-tax treatment to another category. Section 51 separately addresses specified deductible employment income paid by a Grenadian business to a nonresident; section 51A addresses partnership income accruing to a nonresident partner. Ordinary employee PAYE is a separate section 54 regime. [Act, sections 50–54 and Third Schedule](https://grenadaparliament.gd/wp-content/uploads/2021/08/Cap149-INCOME-TAX-ACT.pdf).

**Resident-payment boundary:** section 52 expressly provides 15% withholding on fees, royalties, discounts and management charges paid by a person carrying on business, with a credit against the recipient's tax. It contains an exception for specified regular banking, industrial and commercial activities, and a hardship-waiver certificate procedure. Do not state that all resident payments are exempt, or that all resident fees require withholding, without checking the activity exception, any prescription and later amendments. A waiver application is not an issued certificate; revocation also matters. [Act, section 52(1)–(9)](https://grenadaparliament.gd/wp-content/uploads/2021/08/Cap149-INCOME-TAX-ACT.pdf).

**Interest boundary:** section 50 excludes interest exempt under section 25(1)(aa), and section 53 prints a separate zero-rate rule for specified nonresident deposit interest. This draft does not establish the complete current exemption chain. Do not apply the ordinary 15% rate indiscriminately to every bank interest payment. [Act, sections 50(1) and 53](https://grenadaparliament.gd/wp-content/uploads/2021/08/Cap149-INCOME-TAX-ACT.pdf).

## Ordinary calculation

For an otherwise fully taxable gross fee of EC$10,000 under section 50, without relief, the deduction is **EC$1,500** and the recipient receives **EC$8,500**. A contractual promise to deliver EC$10,000 net is a different price arrangement; do not label EC$1,500 as its automatically correct withholding. Review the contract and applicable gross-up treatment first. [Third Schedule, paragraphs 2–3](https://grenadaparliament.gd/wp-content/uploads/2021/08/Cap149-INCOME-TAX-ACT.pdf).

## Timing conflict requiring resolution

The published text contains materially different instructions:

| Provision | Printed timing |
|---|---|
| Section 50(1) | Within seven days after payment or credit to the payee |
| Section 96(a) | Within seven days after the end of the month in which section 50 tax was deductible |
| Section 50(2), unpaid year-end accrual | Advance tax at submission of the business return |
| Third Schedule paragraph 1(3), accrued expense | Treat payment as made on the final day of the basis period |

These are not interchangeable deadlines. A month-end rule gives a later date than a transaction-based rule for many payments. Obtain the operative amendment or written IRD clarification before relying on the later date, and retain both transaction and accounting dates. This Guide has not resolved precedence. The 2016 Tax Administration Act's Schedule 2(4) repeals numerous Income Tax Act provisions, including section 97, but does **not** list section 96 among them; that Schedule alone does not resolve the conflict. [Income Tax Act, sections 50 and 96, Third Schedule](https://grenadaparliament.gd/wp-content/uploads/2021/08/Cap149-INCOME-TAX-ACT.pdf); [Tax Administration Act, Schedule 2(4)](https://grenadaparliament.gd/wp-content/uploads/2021/08/Act-No.-14-of-2016-Tax-Administration-Act.pdf#page=89).

## Certificates, records and reconciliation

Third Schedule paragraph 4 requires a certificate to the recipient showing gross payment and tax deducted. Keep a calendar-year record of payment nature, gross amount, deduction, and recipient name/address. Paragraph 5 requires a remittance return with certificate copies and an annual return within **two months after year-end**, unless the Comptroller allows further time. Confirm the current approved submission method before filing. Reconcile deductions to actual remittances and account for any difference. [Third Schedule, paragraphs 4–5](https://grenadaparliament.gd/wp-content/uploads/2021/08/Cap149-INCOME-TAX-ACT.pdf).

Section 50(3) provides expense disallowance for unpaid withholding. Third Schedule paragraph 6 makes the payer personally liable for tax it failed to deduct, subject to its provisions. Do not copy the old section 97 interest rate into a current calculation: the 2016 administrative legislation expressly repeals that section. The administrative Act commenced on 1 May 2016 under [SRO 34](https://grenadaparliament.gd/wp-content/uploads/2021/08/S.R.O.-34-of-2016-Tax-Administration-Act-Commencement-Order-2016.pdf); later amendments and current interest/penalty calculations require separate verification. [Act, section 50(3) and Third Schedule paragraph 6](https://grenadaparliament.gd/wp-content/uploads/2021/08/Cap149-INCOME-TAX-ACT.pdf); [Tax Administration Act, Schedule 2(4)](https://grenadaparliament.gd/wp-content/uploads/2021/08/Act-No.-14-of-2016-Tax-Administration-Act.pdf#page=89).

## Investment relief is narrower than the property-transfer waiver

Act 8 of 2016 added section 50(4A)–(4C), effective **1 May 2016** under [SRO 26 of 2016](https://grenadaparliament.gd/wp-content/uploads/2021/08/S.R.O.-26-of-2016-Income-Tax-Amendment-Act-Commencment-Order.pdf). The relief concerns qualifying investments in **tourism accommodation or health and wellness**, qualifying financing interest and royalties, with the statutory unrelated-person condition referencing VAT Act section 5. It is not a general waiver for every priority sector or every payment. [Amendment, section 4](https://grenadaparliament.gd/wp-content/uploads/2021/08/Act-No.-8-of-2016-Income-Tax-Amendment.pdf#page=5).

| Qualifying costs | Waiver of covered withholding |
|---|---:|
| Over EC$30 million, up to EC$80 million inclusive | 50% |
| Over EC$80 million | 100% |

Exactly EC$30 million does not meet either band; exactly EC$80 million meets the 50% band. The amendment does not introduce the property-transfer waiver's parish distinction. Use the qualifying-cost rules and retain proof of recipient relationship and financing purpose. The [property-transfer Guide](gd-property-transfer-tax.md) explains the retrieved investment regulations and outstanding cost questions. [Amendment, section 4](https://grenadaparliament.gd/wp-content/uploads/2021/08/Act-No.-8-of-2016-Income-Tax-Amendment.pdf).

If the required cost threshold is not met on completion or expiry of the investment-agreement period, whichever occurs first, the waived withholding is payable within **two months**, after which statutory interest and penalties apply. The Comptroller, consulting the investment agency, can grant an extension of no more than **twelve months** for meeting the relevant requirement. It is not automatic. [Section 50(4B)–(4C), inserted by Act 8](https://grenadaparliament.gd/wp-content/uploads/2021/08/Act-No.-8-of-2016-Income-Tax-Amendment.pdf#page=6).

## Treaty and completion checks

Section 50(5) permits a lower deduction directed by the Comptroller where an applicable section 43 international agreement provides a lower rate. No treaty rate or entitlement is certified here. Obtain the agreement, recipient residence evidence and applicable direction before reducing tax. [Act, section 50(5)](https://grenadaparliament.gd/wp-content/uploads/2021/08/Cap149-INCOME-TAX-ACT.pdf).

Remaining work includes later amendment reconciliation, the deadline/accrual conflicts, current forms and filing route, section 52 prescriptions, interest exemptions, dividend classification, treaty eligibility and independent review. Guide presence does not establish comprehensive country coverage.
