# Switzerland Company Formation & Entity Choice

## Entity types, capital and compliance

The two main Swiss capital companies are the GmbH/Sàrl (limited liability company) and the AG/SA (stock corporation); sole proprietorships and partnerships are also common. These are the rules in force for 2026, taken from the federal acts on fedlex.admin.ch.

- **GmbH / Sàrl (limited liability company)**: members hold capital contributions (Stammanteile), each with a nominal value above zero. Founded by public deed _(OR arts. 774(1), 777(1))_
- **AG / SA (stock corporation)**: capital divided into shares (registered or bearer). Founded by public deed _(OR arts. 622(1), 629(1))_
- **Other forms**: sole proprietorship (Einzelunternehmen), general partnership (Kollektivgesellschaft) and limited partnership (Kommanditgesellschaft). A sole proprietor whose business had turnover of at least CHF 100,000 in the last business year must register it in the commercial register; liberal professions and farmers not run on commercial lines are exempt, and anyone may register voluntarily _(OR art. 931(1), (3))_
- **GmbH minimum share capital**: CHF 20,000, or the equivalent in a foreign currency essential to the business. On formation, a contribution equal to the issue price must be paid in full for each capital contribution _(OR arts. 773(1), (2), 777c(1))_
- **AG minimum share capital**: CHF 100,000, or a foreign-currency equivalent at formation. At least 20% of the nominal value of each share must be paid in at formation, and at least CHF 50,000 in total _(OR arts. 621(1), (2), 632(1), (2))_
- **Capital deposit**: cash contributions are deposited with a Swiss bank for the company's exclusive use. The bank releases them only after the company is entered in the commercial register. The same rules apply to a GmbH _(OR arts. 633(1), (2), 777c(2))_
- **Incorporation steps**: 1) check the name and draft the articles; 2) deposit the cash contributions with a bank; 3) the founders sign the public deed, which adopts the articles and appoints the officers; 4) file the application with the commercial register office with the deed, the articles, the officers' acceptances, the auditor's acceptance where needed and the bank's deposit certificate (unless the deed names the bank); 5) the bank releases the capital after registration _(OR arts. 629, 633, 777; HRegV arts. 43(1), 71(1))_
- **Commercial register fees**: new registration of a capital company CHF 420; general or limited partnership CHF 160; sole proprietorship CHF 80. Changes to the articles cost CHF 200; registering an opt-out from the limited audit costs CHF 30. Where the schedule gives no flat fee, the office charges CHF 100 to 250 an hour, and may add up to 50% for unusual, difficult or urgent work _(GebV-HReg art. 3 and Annex items 1.1 to 1.3)_
- **Notary fees**: the public deed is drawn up by a notary under cantonal law; the fee depends on the canton (see Not yet confirmed)
- **Swiss residency requirement**: the company must be able to be represented by a person resident in Switzerland. For an AG this person must be a board member or a director; for a GmbH a managing officer or a director. The person must have access to the share register (AG) or the register of contributions (GmbH); the wording of that sentence was last amended with effect from 1 October 2026 _(OR arts. 718(4), 814(3))_
- **Capital issue stamp duty**: 1% federal issuance stamp duty on the amount the company receives for new shares or contributions, at least the nominal value. Shares issued for consideration on the formation or capital increase of an AG or GmbH are exempt as long as the members' contributions do not exceed CHF 1 million in total _(StG arts. 5(1)(a), 6(1)(h), 8(1)(a))_
- **VAT registration**: an enterprise is exempt while its turnover from supplies in Switzerland and abroad is under CHF 100,000 a year. It may waive the exemption and register voluntarily, for at least one tax period. Rates, methods and returns are in [switzerland-vat](switzerland-vat.md) _(MWSTG arts. 10(2)(a), 11)_
- **Bookkeeping and annual accounts**: companies (legal persons), and sole proprietorships and partnerships with turnover of at least CHF 500,000 in the last business year, must keep full books and prepare annual accounts under the OR. Smaller sole proprietorships and partnerships keep only income, expenses and the asset position _(OR art. 957(1), (2))_
- **Annual meeting**: the shareholders' meeting of an AG, and the members' meeting of a GmbH, takes place within six months of the end of the business year _(OR arts. 699(2), 805(2))_
- **Audit requirement**: ordinary audit for listed companies, companies that must prepare group accounts, and companies that exceed two of: balance sheet total CHF 20 million, revenue CHF 40 million, 250 full-time positions, in two consecutive business years. Otherwise a limited audit. With the consent of all shareholders, a company with no more than ten full-time positions on annual average can opt out of the limited audit. Since 1 January 2025 the opt-out applies only to future business years and must be filed with the commercial register before the year begins, together with the last annual accounts. These rules apply to a GmbH too _(OR arts. 727(1), 727a(1), (2), (2bis), 818(1))_
- **Annual tax filing**: each canton names one office to assess companies for the direct federal tax, and the canton collects it with its own cantonal and communal profit and capital tax. A company must file a return even if it received no form. Rates, deadlines and payment are in [ch-corporate-income-tax](ch-corporate-income-tax.md) and [ch-cantonal-tax](ch-cantonal-tax.md); VAT returns in [ch-vat-return](ch-vat-return.md); social security in [ch-ahv-iv](ch-ahv-iv.md) _(DBG arts. 104(2), 124(1))_

For the whole system on one page, see [ch-tax-overview](ch-tax-overview.md).
