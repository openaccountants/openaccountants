# Puerto Rico: federal residence, income source and filing

## Three separate decisions

For the section 933 exclusion, establish **bona fide residence**, identify the **source of each income item**, and then determine **which returns are required**. A Puerto Rico address or local decree does not replace the federal tests in [26 USC 937](https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/119-121/t26/s937/). The exclusion in [26 USC 933](https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/119-121/t26/s933/) applies to qualifying Puerto Rico-source income, not automatically to every receipt of someone living there.

This Guide uses the [2025 IRS Publication 570](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p570) and federal sections fetched in October 2026. It focuses on US citizens and resident aliens. Nonresident aliens and military families have distinct rules that need separate treatment.

## Bona fide residence

The ordinary framework requires all three: satisfying the presence test, no tax home outside Puerto Rico during the tax year, and no closer connection to the United States or a foreign country than to Puerto Rico. Special move-year provisions and other exceptions must be applied on their own terms. [26 USC 937(a)](https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/119-121/t26/s937/); [Publication 570, chapter 1](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p570).

For a US citizen or resident alien, the IRS lists five alternative ways to meet the presence component:

- At least **183 days** in Puerto Rico in the year.
- At least **549 days** across the current and preceding two years, with at least **60 days in each**.
- No more than **90 days** in the United States in the year.
- No more than **USD 3,000 US earned income**, and more days in Puerto Rico than in the United States.
- No significant connection to the United States under the applicable definition.

Meeting one presence alternative does not dispense with tax home or closer connection. Special day-counting rules cover travel, medical treatment and emergencies; this list is not permission to count every absence as a Puerto Rico day. The alternatives above do not apply unchanged to nonresident aliens. [Publication 570, Presence Test](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p570).

Tax home ordinarily follows the main place of business, employment or duty; it is not necessarily the family home. Closer connection examines facts such as homes, family, belongings, business and social connections, banking, voting and licensing. Spending 183 days locally does not cure a failed closer-connection test. [Publication 570, Tax Home and Closer Connection](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p570).

## Source of income

For ordinary personal services, the place where work is performed is central to source. A customer or employer being in the mainland United States does not, by itself, make work performed in Puerto Rico US-source; equally, payment into a Puerto Rico bank account does not turn mainland work into Puerto Rico-source income. Services performed in several locations need an appropriate allocation. Military and other special sourcing rules require separate attention. [Publication 570, chapter 2, Compensation for Labor or Personal Services](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p570).

Investment income, pensions, real estate and gains have different source rules. In particular, pre-move appreciated property requires the special gain rules; this Guide does not treat selling an investment after moving as sufficient proof of Puerto Rico source. [26 USC 937(b)](https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/119-121/t26/s937/); [Publication 570, chapter 2](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p570).

## Federal exclusion and return distinction

Under the ordinary section 933(1) rule, an individual who is a bona fide Puerto Rico resident for the **entire taxable year** excludes Puerto Rico-source income from federal gross income. Compensation as an employee of the United States or its agencies is expressly excepted. Deductions and credits allocable to excluded income are restricted. [26 USC 933(1)](https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/119-121/t26/s933/).

For US citizens and resident aliens, Publication 570 describes a Puerto Rico return reporting worldwide income and, where a federal filing requirement exists, a federal return reporting worldwide income with qualifying Puerto Rico-source income excluded. If all income is qualifying Puerto Rico-source income, a federal income-tax return is not required solely for that excluded income. Self-employment tax and other federal reporting must still be checked. Income outside Puerto Rico can create a federal filing requirement; foreign-tax credits are subject to their own limits. [Publication 570, Puerto Rico and chapter 4](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p570).

A US citizen who leaves after at least **two years** of bona fide Puerto Rico residence has a separate statutory rule for qualifying local income attributable to the pre-departure residence period. That is not a general exemption for the entire departure year. Arrival-year treatment also requires the specific move-year rules; do not automatically apply the full-year exclusion to a midyear move. [26 USC 933(2)](https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/119-121/t26/s933/); [Publication 570, Year of Moving](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p570).

## Reporting the change and retaining evidence

Section 937(c) requires notice of a position that bona fide residence began or ended. Publication 570's Form 8898 instructions apply where **worldwide gross income exceeds USD 75,000** and one of its specified residence-change positions applies. Gross income includes excluded income before deductions; the threshold is not taxable income and is not USD 75,000 or more. Check the prior-year filing position as well as the move. [26 USC 937(c)](https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/119-121/t26/s937/); [Publication 570, Reporting a Change in Bona Fide Residence](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p570).

Retain a dated travel calendar, work-location records, home and family facts, evidence of the main business location, income-by-source schedules and the relevant prior returns. These are practical evidence categories derived from the tests, not a claim that one document conclusively proves residence.
