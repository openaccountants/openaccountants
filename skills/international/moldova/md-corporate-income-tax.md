# Moldova corporate income tax, 2026 v0.12

## Who this applies to

Compute ordinary resident-company profit tax and the separate eligible non-VAT SME income regime for 2026. Establish the taxpayer, elected regime and applicable period before calculating. Resident business entities include income from Moldova and abroad, less the deductions and exemptions they actually qualify for. Legal persons use accrual accounting. This is a source-cited writer draft, without accountant attestation or completed whole-country review. [Fiscal Code arts. 13–14, 44][code].

A company is resident if its activity is organised or managed in Moldova, or Moldova is its principal place of activity. The ordinary income-tax period is the calendar year; a newly created business starts at registration. A lawful noncalendar accounting period has Code art. 12^1(4)–(6)'s separate eligibility, notification and transition requirements; liquidation, reorganisation and IT-park entry/exit have their own short-period rules. Do not use the excise article 121 in place of income-tax article 12^1. [Code arts. 5(5)(b), 12^1][code].

The 7% rate belongs to the statutory **gospodărie țărănească (de fermier)** category. It is not a rate for every agricultural company. Individual entrepreneurs use the 12% annual taxable-income rule and their own personal/entity procedures; the corporate Guide does not replace the [individual-income Guide](moldova-income-tax.md). IT-park, free-zone, financial-sector and other special regimes require their own legal eligibility tests. Use [transfer pricing](md-transfer-pricing.md), [cross-border](md-cross-border.md), [VAT](moldova-vat.md), [payroll](moldova-payroll.md) and [social contributions](moldova-social-contributions.md) for the connected subjects, with each file's stated currency and gaps. [Arts. 15, 49, 54^1, 87(1^1)][code].

## The base

1. **Choose and document the regime.** Article 54^1 excludes VAT-registered taxpayers, peasant farms, individual entrepreneurs and the specified consultancy-heavy businesses from the SME class. For the election cases, test prior 31 December income from exempt-without-credit supplies alone or together with taxable supplies, the applicable threshold and the requirement that exempt-without-credit supplies exceed 50% of total supplies. No-prior-income and newly registered entities have separate election routes. Record the choice in accounting policy by 25 April for the first two routes, or by the 25th of the month after the registration quarter for new entities. Test the shareholding/number-of-SMEs limit. VAT registration moves the taxpayer to general taxation from registration; cancellation uses the art. 113(4) date. Preserve each regime's actual tax period. [Arts. 54^1–54^1/1][code].
2. **Reconcile ordinary profit tax.** Start with accounting revenue and expenses, not bank deposits. Include the taxable art. 18 receipts, including business receipts, rent, interest, royalties, covered debt releases and non-exempt subsidies/penal receipts. Remove only income demonstrably exempt under art. 20 or another controlling rule. Resident-company dividends received from another resident company have art. 20(z^19)'s exemption except the specified undistributed 2008–2011 profits. Recovery/reversal of an expense or provision never deducted has art. 20(z^17)'s rule, with its exception for provisions created in the stated 2023–2026 deferral periods. Do not exempt every grant, compensation or revaluation without its precise statutory category. Reconcile timing, unpaid related-party amounts and prior-year returns/discounts under art. 44. Add back income tax, statutory fines/penalties, private or exempt-income expenditure, unsupported related payments and other disallowed costs. Do not deduct a capital-asset purchase as an ordinary expense when depreciation rules apply. [Arts. 18, 20, 24, 30, 44][code].
3. **Apply expense-specific limits.** Ordinary necessary business expenses qualify under art. 24(1). Mixed business/private costs require its actual greater-business-than-private test and only the directly business portion. Travel, representation and business insurance have Government limits; do not assume the accounting amount qualifies. One-car restrictions apply to the specified occupation groups 112/121. Related-party losses need the arm's-length exception or other stated exception. Undocumented ordinary expenses have the 0.2%-of-taxable-income route; business-card purchases have a separate rule. Patent-holder/independent-retailer payments have their own restriction. Membership/subscription and qualifying union allocations have the separate 0.15%-of-payroll or two-forecast-average-salaries limit; the exact applicable alternative and recipient must be documented. [Art. 24(1)–(11)][code].
4. **Check new service-entrepreneur payments.** The art. 24(11^1) route covers the specified businesses with at most 9 employees and turnover or assets up to 18 million, subject to its 10%-of-annual-payroll payment limit, or the separate main-activity route above 70% for CAEM 59.1, 90.01, 90.02, 93.11 or 93.12. Verify the exact route rather than deducting every freelancer invoice. Employment reclassification and the recipient's separate single-tax regime must also be checked in the payroll/individual work. [Art. 24(11^1)][code]; [Finance Ministry 2026 explanation][changes].
5. **Reconcile employee-related expenditure.** Article 24(19^3) provides: annual in-kind gifts/vouchers up to 10% of the forecast monthly average salary per employee; specified training/team-building up to 5% of the defined payroll fund after excluding the stated management groups; sport subscriptions and qualifying rural-tourism/vacation-voucher costs together up to 50% of the forecast average salary per employee; medical services up to one forecast average salary per employee. Article 24(20) separately covers qualifying voluntary medical-insurance premiums up to one forecast average salary. The 2026 forecast is 17,400, giving 1,740, 8,700 and 17,400 for those respective per-employee caps. Taxed compensation above those limits must be tested under art. 24(19^2), not automatically disallowed or treated as exempt remuneration. Childcare has its separate 2,500-per-month-per-child limit for children under 3. Preserve the actual provider, benefit, cumulative-period and payroll facts. [Art. 24(19)–(20), (26)][code]; [CNAS 2026 instructions, point 12.1, citing Government Decision 773/2025][average].
6. **Financing, depreciation and other deductions.** For loans from the specified non-financial individuals/entities, compare the actual contractual interest with the BNM weighted banking rate for the matching currency and term, up to or above 12 months. Keep that limit distinct from transfer pricing. Maintain each tangible asset separately; ordinary straight-line depreciation starts the month after commissioning, using the Government useful life. Financial versus operating leasing determines the depreciating party, with the stated IFRS right-of-use exception. For qualifying repairs to rented/leased assets outside the ownership test, the ordinary limit is 15% of accrued rent/operating-lease/concession consideration; international aircraft have the separate 100% operating-lease cap, and qualifying own taxi vehicles have the stated full-repair deduction. Free assets are not depreciated unless their value entered taxable income; non-taxable partial financing limits the depreciable part, subject to the statutory post-investment subsidy exception. The non-financial-sector accelerated option permits at most 50% of entry cost in the first year, followed by remaining value over remaining useful life. Financial/operating leases, IFRS right-of-use assets, rented-asset repairs, grants and disposals each have separate rules. Limited-life intangibles amortise straight-line. Ordinary provisions and revaluation/impairment do not become deductible merely because booked; a legally established business bad debt has its own rule. [Arts. 25, 26^1, 28, 31][code].
7. **Losses and donations.** Keep a loss ledger by origin year, the five following periods, amounts already used and statutory ordering. Apply art. 32's remaining-balance rules; do not invent an indefinite carryforward or automatically deduct an expired amount. Qualifying charitable/sponsorship donations have the 5%-of-taxable-income limit and eligible-recipient/document requirements; statutory food-donation treatment is separate. The ordinary cash-donation cap is reconciled to approved VEN12 row 040 below; noncash/food and other special cases require their own applicable method. [Arts. 32, 36][code].
8. **Compute the SME base separately.** Use financial-accounting income for the SME period. Subtract current returns/discounts in the period they occur, including returns of prior-period sales. Exclude art. 20 income and income from exchange-rate or sum differences. Do not deduct ordinary operating expenses or use the ordinary 12% profit-base calculation. Apply 4%. Keep the short periods on entering/leaving VAT registration separate. [Arts. 54^1/1–54^4][code].
9. **Settle the actual liability.** For ordinary profit tax, apply the legal rate to the positive reconciled taxable income after eligible losses. Match qualifying foreign-tax credit to the same taxed foreign income and period, capped at Moldova's tax on that income. Credit evidenced instalments and applicable advance payments, avoiding double credit. The dividend-linked payment deferral below changes payment timing; it does not make all retained profit exempt. [Arts. 15–16, 32, 81–84, 87][code].

Article 24 also has specific stock-loss, environmental, infrastructure-transfer, audit/sector, stipend and liquidation rules. Their full special procedures are not certified by this draft. The ordinary Government procedures below resolve the identified representation, business-insurance, transport/food, development and donation-documentation gaps; the travel/asset procedures below are sourced; remaining annex applications, capped-base and actual-return implementation gaps stay explicit. Do not classify a payment from a vendor name or an assumed expense percentage.

### Company disposals and the individual capital-gain boundary

For an ordinary resident company, recognise the business disposal income and the admissible tax cost under the applicable company rules. **Do not remove half of a company’s share-disposal gain under art. 40(7).** Article 37 limits that capital-gains chapter to resident/nonresident **individuals who do not carry on business**; article 18(e)'s reference to art. 40(7) does not expand that taxpayer scope. The company's ordinary worldwide business-income and deduction rules remain arts. 14(1)(a), 18(a)/(o) and 24; depreciable property follows its separate tax-undepreciated-value method below. [Code arts. 14, 18, 24, 37–40][code].

Keep the nonresident rules distinct as well. Article 71(1)(c) refers to art. 40(7) for the specified nonbusiness **individual** gains; article 71(1)(d) expressly refers to art. 40(1)–(4), excluding paragraph (7), for a nonresident **legal entity** disposing of the specified Moldovan assets. Determine source, tax basis, withholding and any treaty entitlement under the cross-border method; neither route creates a universal 50% company exclusion. [Code arts. 37, 40, 71(1)(c)–(d), 91][code]; [cross-border Guide](md-cross-border.md).

### Apply the ordinary deduction procedures

Government Decision 693/2018 annex 1 supplies the operating rules below. Its latest displayed 2026 version is effective 18 June. The January/current comparison and the actual amending Decision 321/2026 show that the June changes to this business annex concern points 30 and 31; the January transport, meal and representation caps below are also retained directly. The Code prevails where a general regulatory statement is narrower or broader than the statute. [Regulation 693, annex 1][corpreg]; [January regulation][corpregjan]; [Decision 321/2026 point 2.1][corpregamend].

For an ordinary expense, record the business purpose, period, actual supplier/payee, source document, accounting treatment and any specific cap. Points 24–30 require the business connection and correct period, and permit only the business portion of mixed expenditure where business use predominates, above 50%. Recovered costs/losses/bad debts previously deducted enter gross income in the recovery year; costs never deducted use the statutory exclusion. **Do not read point 30 as overriding the specific Code art. 20(z^17) exception for provisions created in 2023–2026 when art. 87(1^1) was applied.** [Regulation points 24–30][corpreg]; [Code arts. 20(z^17), 24 and 44][code].

The membership/qualifying union alternatives in Code art. 24(15),(15^1) already applied from **1 January 2026** under Law 318/2025. The January regulation's union point 31 mentioned only 0.15% of payroll; June's regulatory update adds the alternative of up to two forecast monthly average salaries. Follow the January Code rather than incorrectly starting that statutory alternative in June, or adding both limits together. Keep the eligible organisation, actual dues/allocation and collective-agreement purpose distinct. For the two-salary alternative, the verified 17,400 forecast input gives **34,800**. [January Code art. 24(15),(15^1) and commencement annotations][jan]; [Regulation point 31][corpreg]; [Decision 321/2026 point 2.1.2][corpregamend]; [forecast input][average].

### Employee development, recreation and required records

Business-specific professional education under point 45 must be needed for the employee's actual role/activity and incurred exclusively for business. This differs from other development/team-building under point 45^1 and Code art. 24(19^3)(b), whose **5%** base is the selected previous/current year's payroll less the specified management-group 112/121 payroll. The Code's complete base restriction controls; the regulation's general definition of payroll as salary, other work rewards and material aid does not restore excluded management amounts. Keep internal recipient/training and activity orders, contract, invoice and supporting cost records under point 46. Do not charge the same activity to both an uncapped role-required training route and the capped recreation/development route. [Regulation points 45–46][corpreg]; [Code art. 24(19),(19^3)(b)][code].

For rural-tourism reimbursement/vacation vouchers, points 44^1–44^13 require the qualifying Moldovan rural facility/authorised operator and the relevant Code cap. Accrual taxpayers need the tax invoice; cash-method taxpayers additionally need evidence of actual payment. Aggregate multiple vouchers, reimbursements and the connected sports costs within the Code's shared **50%-of-forecast-average-salary per-employee annual cap**, rather than creating separate allowances for each purchase. The current 2026 input gives **8,700**. Above-cap amounts enter the stated taxable-benefit/taxed-deduction route. [Regulation points 44^1–44^7][corpreg]; [Code art. 24(19^3)(c)][code]; [forecast input][average].

The paper voucher must show the rural facility's name/IDNO, nominal value, issue date and validity period. Employer purchase/award can cover both main and concurrent jobs under the stated rule; unused-value handling follows the operator/employer contract. A value returned by the operator after expiry enters the employer's gross income in the receipt period. The employer cannot replace a voucher returned by the employee with cash compensation. Authorisation of the actual operator/facility requires the Ministry of Culture list mentioned in point 44^7; this Guide does not certify any particular provider. [Regulation points 44^7–44^13][corpreg].

Meal vouchers are a different statutory scheme under Law 166/2017, not the organised-food 70-per-day rule or rural-vacation voucher. Points 42–44 refer to that law's actual nominal limit and the taxed-excess deduction route. The current nominal meal-voucher amount and scheme implementation remain an explicit gap; do not substitute an invented figure. [Regulation points 42–44][corpreg]; [Code art. 24(19^1),(19^2)][code].

### Donation documentation, values and losses

For an ordinary philanthropic/sponsorship deduction, verify the Code art. 36(2) eligible recipient and statutory purpose, obtain the written beneficiary request and donation/sponsorship contract, and retain the actual transfer/payment evidence. Regulation points 58–66 permit monetary and nonmonetary forms but do not remove the **5%-of-taxable-income** ceiling or recipient/purpose requirements. For cash, the contract plus payment document is required. Noncash documentation includes a contract specifying the assets/purpose, notarisation for immovable property, the relevant recipient authorisation and the prescribed tax-invoice evidence. [Code art. 36][code]; [Regulation points 58–66][corpreg].

A business gift of property has a **deemed-disposal income adjustment as well as any donation deduction**. Code art. 21(5) uses the greater of tax-undepreciated value and market value, with its explicit qualifying food-donation exception under Law 299/2022. Regulation point 64 addresses the residual-value/market comparison and nondepreciable-property book/market rule. Reconcile disposal proceeds, tax basis and the eligible donation separately; do not simply deduct the accounting expense and ignore deemed income. The food-donation exception and precise capped-base/return-line calculation need their own applicable procedure; no result for those open computations is certified here. [Code arts. 21(5), 36][code]; [Regulation points 64–66][corpreg].

Maintain loss-origin years and the version that governed each origin. Regulation points 76–78 support a tax loss based on allowed deductions exceeding gross income and require the origin-period art. 32 rules, with the legacy 2017–2022 table. Use current Code art. 32 for the applicable five-period/ordering method. The approved return and any particular historic-origin exception still require reconciliation; an old table's “unlimited” entry does not establish unlimited carryforward years. [Regulation points 76–78][corpreg]; [Code art. 32][code].

### Foreign currency and connected-party values

For ordinary income tax, Code art. 21(3) converts foreign-currency receipts/income/expenses at the official MDL rate for the transaction date. Its date rule uses the bank/payment-company statement or, in other cases, primary documents under the applicable SNC/IFRS accounting method; do not automatically use a single invoice/payment-date shortcut for every accrual. At fiscal year-end, retranslate foreign-currency debts due by/to the taxpayer at the final-day official rate, **excluding advances for assets/services**. Recognise the stipulated current/year-end exchange differences under the Code's period rules. For a currency not quoted by BNM, the provision prescribes conversion through a BNM-quoted currency using the direct relationship, then into MDL. Retain the actual rate, date, method and source evidence. [Code art. 21(3), points 1–4 including 2^1 and 3^1–3^3][code].

The SME base's specific FX/sum-difference exclusion remains separate; do not move ordinary-company exchange treatment into the 4% base. For below-market business dealings with nonbusiness individual founders/other related individuals, art. 21(6) requires its market adjustment, with the stated arm's-length/art. 19 exceptions. Ordinary currency conversion does not settle treaty residence, foreign-tax-credit timing, VAT conversion or the controlled-transaction price. [Code arts. 21(6), 54^2][code]; [cross-border](md-cross-border.md) and [transfer pricing](md-transfer-pricing.md).

### Business travel: authorise, evidence and settle each trip

Government Decision 693/2018 annex 1 point 54 directs business-travel deductions to the norms in Decision 10/2012. Use its latest displayed consolidation, effective **1 December 2024**, including the actual amending Decision 783/2024. The regulation's personnel definition includes employment/civil-contract personnel and administrators, founders and governing-body members; this is not a conclusion that every recipient has the same employee tax exemption. Domestic permanent mobile/itinerant work is excluded from its trip definition. Record the actual business mission and recipient category. [Point 54][corpreg]; [travel regulation points 1–4][travelreg]; [amendment][travelamend].

The authorised manager's order states the purpose, duration and host country. Keep the domestic order/travel authorisation, destinations, actual departure/arrival dates and supporting tickets/border evidence. A separate travel authorisation is not compulsory for a same-day return or travel between subdivisions in different localities of the same entity, but an order is still required. Reimburse actual authorised days, including departure/arrival as established by the records. [Points 6–8, 10–11][travelreg].

### Asset life, depreciation, repairs and disposals

The current life-selection rules are in Decision 941/2020, **2022 consolidation**, rather than the older repealed catalogue. For a business, use the financial-accounting useful life, subject to the annex's minimum rather than its maximum. Ordinary straight-line depreciation starts in the **month after commissioning**, and capitalised subsequent costs start in the month after capitalisation. The final depreciation month includes expiry, withdrawal from service or disposal. Compute `depreciable value × (1 / useful life in years) × eligible months / 12`; equivalently use a life in months consistently. Reconcile the last-period total with the available basis. The regulation also prorates months when a business first becomes entitled to deductions on moving from a special regime; it has particular adjusted-basis rules for that change and withdrawal of IT-park residence. Finance/operating leases and IFRS right-of-use assets follow their distinct statutory owner/deduction rules. [Code art. 26^1(4)–(7)][code]; [regulation points 2, 10–19][assetreg].

For a business outside CAEM section K financial/insurance/reinsurance activities, accelerated depreciation is an option: up to **50% of entry cost in the first year of use**, then the undepreciated value divided by remaining useful life. That first year is **12 consecutive months of useful life**, not automatically the calendar year of purchase. Record the election, actual qualifying months, tax depreciation already taken and remaining life; do not take a full first-year amount twice across two calendar returns. [Code art. 26^1(6^1)][code]; [regulation point 16^4, amendment effective 1 January 2025][assetreg].

Separate servicing/maintenance from repair and capital improvement. SNC/IFRS current expenses follow Code art. 24(1); capitalised improvements increase entry cost and depreciation uses the adjusted undepreciated basis over the remaining life from the next month. Life extensions/reductions follow catalogue points 10–11, including the minimum-life safeguard. For qualifying rented/leased/concession assets whose repairs are borne by the user, the Code normally caps current deduction at **15% of accrued annual rent/operating-lease/concession charges**; the specified international-aircraft route uses 100% of operating-lease charges, and own taxi-vehicle repairs have their separate 100% rule. Regulation point 28 permits full current deduction of the specified leased/rented/other-use-contract repairs where the contract lasts **up to 12 months inclusive**. Do not apply a universal 15% cap to owned-asset maintenance. [Code arts. 24(1), 26^1(3), (11)–(11^1)][code]; [regulation points 20–28][assetreg]; [catalogue points 10–11][assetcatalogue].

An applicable repair excess becomes a separately registered tax asset when the work is complete. Its starting life is the shorter of remaining contract term and the underlying asset's useful life; the entity can elect a longer life. Multiple repairs can be one investment asset or separate ones under point 29. If the contract is terminated, point 30 retains the investment in the register and continues depreciation over its originally fixed duration. Obtain the actual contract, repair allocation and completion records before deciding the route. [Points 24–30][assetreg].

On disposal, determine tax undepreciated value from entry/capitalised cost less tax depreciation **through the disposal month**. A sale's tax result is proceeds less that value. A donation produces the positive excess of market value over tax undepreciated value, or zero where the latter is higher, subject to the Code's specific controlling exceptions. An exchange uses the received asset's contractual value adjusted for extra cash receivable/payable, less the transferred asset's tax undepreciated value. A capital contribution uses the participation interest's value less the transferred asset's tax undepreciated value. **Forced loss or early scrapping does not automatically deduct the remaining tax value**: where qualifying insurance/co-insurance compensation is taxable under Code art. 18(i), point 36 permits deduction only up to that taxable income. Historical capped-car and transition assets have separate points 33^1, 35^1 and 38–45; keep their original-period schedules instead of resetting their tax basis to book value. [Code arts. 18(i), 21(5), 26^1(17)][code]; [regulation points 31–45][assetreg].

## Penalties and interest

**Calendar-2026 ordinary tax late charge.** Code art. 228(3) and the BNM decision of 6 November 2025, point 1, supply the calculation: the retained **6.00%** short-term policy rate, rounded up to a whole percent, plus **5 percentage points**, divided by **365 days**, gives **0.0301% per delayed day** after rounding the daily percentage to four decimal places. The decimal multiplier is **0.000301**. Apply it to the established outstanding principal for each chargeable day; split changes in principal and calendar year. This calculation concerns debts governed by art. 228 and does not replace a separate statutory regime's rate or establish case-specific relief. [Code art. 228(2)–(3)](https://www.legis.md/cautare/getResults?doc_id=157050&lang=ro); [BNM decision, point 1](https://bnm.md/en/node/69226).

Late tax accrues from the day after its due date through actual payment inclusive. Article 228(3) sets the annual daily rate from the preceding November BNM short-term policy rate rounded up to a whole percentage point, plus 5 percentage points, divided by days in the year and mathematically rounded to four decimal places. Do not compound the debt automatically. Apply the actual account movements, statutory exceptions and the corrected-return cap in art. 228(2^1). [Art. 228][code].

For corporate taxpayers within art. 232(a), failure to follow return preparation/submission rules carries 500–1,000 per return, with the 10,000 total cap in art. 260(1). Understatement through false return data has the separate 20%–30% sanction; qualifying self-correction is excepted under art. 261(4^1). Evasion has its separate 80%–100% rule. Understatement in the art. 87(1^1) deferred-payment class has the specific 1,000–10,000 rule; distinguish its commencement/application from ordinary understatement. [Arts. 232, 260(1), (6), 261(4)–(5)][code]; [Law 41 arts. I(7), X(2)][amend].

Instalment underpayment has art. 261(6)'s own comparison with preceding-year tax and 80% of final current tax after the specified credits, its below-5,000 exception, dates and art. 228(5) exceptions. It is not an automatic percentage of every quarterly shortfall. Obtain the actual prior/current tax and credits before calculating a sanction. [Art. 261(6)][code].

## Cross-border points

A resident business's worldwide income is not a blanket exemption for foreign receipts. For art. 82 credit, obtain proof of actual payment/withholding certified by the competent foreign authority; translate to Romanian unless issued in English or Russian. Credit only Moldova's tax on the same income, in the period Moldova taxes it. Actual treaty entitlement and refund procedures require the operative treaty and residence/beneficial-owner facts. [Arts. 13–14, 82][code].

Separate company profit tax, dividend-related advance/deferred company tax and recipient withholding. Resident-individual dividends use art. 90^1's 6%/historic 15% split; nonresident dividends use art. 91's ordinary 6% and specified 15% exceptions. Nonresident interest/royalty payments require actual Moldova-source classification, treaty relief and the art. 91 exclusions; do not assert a universal reduced treaty range. PE-linked receipts and covered employment have separate routes. Use the cross-border Guide for the full source/PE/treaty method and the TP Guide for affiliated transactions. [Arts. 80^1, 90^1–92][code].

## Two worked examples that recompute

### 1. Ordinary company reconciliation, loss and foreign credit

Assume a resident ordinary company outside the special/deferral regimes. Accounting revenue is 1,000,000 and accounting expenses 700,000, including a nondeductible statutory fine of 5,000 and book depreciation 50,000. The separately verified asset schedule supports tax depreciation 40,000; this example does not determine asset lives. An unexpired earliest loss of 100,000 is fully usable under the origin-year schedule. The reconciled profit includes 50,000 of net foreign income taxable in Moldova at 12%, with certified qualifying foreign tax 8,000. No other adjustments/credits; evidenced instalments are 18,000. Taxable income is 215,000, tax 25,800, allowable foreign credit 6,000 and final balance 1,800. [Arts. 15, 26^1, 30, 32, 44, 82–87][code].

```oa-check
name: Moldova 2026 ordinary corporate loss and capped foreign credit
inputs:
  accounting_revenue: 1000000
  accounting_expenses: 700000
  statutory_fine: 5000
  book_depreciation: 50000
  verified_tax_depreciation: 40000
  eligible_unexpired_loss: 100000
  same_foreign_net_income: 50000
  certified_foreign_tax: 8000
  evidenced_instalments: 18000
steps:
  accounting_profit: accounting_revenue - accounting_expenses
  profit_before_losses: accounting_profit + statutory_fine + book_depreciation - verified_tax_depreciation
  taxable_income: max(0, profit_before_losses - eligible_unexpired_loss)
  gross_tax: taxable_income * 0.12
  foreign_tax_cap: same_foreign_net_income * 0.12
  allowed_foreign_credit: min(certified_foreign_tax, foreign_tax_cap)
  final_tax: max(0, gross_tax - allowed_foreign_credit)
  balance_due: final_tax - evidenced_instalments
expected:
  accounting_profit: 300000
  profit_before_losses: 315000
  taxable_income: 215000
  gross_tax: 25800
  foreign_tax_cap: 6000
  allowed_foreign_credit: 6000
  final_tax: 19800
  balance_due: 1800
```

### 2. Eligible SME accounting-income base

Assume the taxpayer has established eligibility, a timely 2026 SME election, no VAT registration/change and no prohibited consultancy/shareholding facts. The full-year financial-accounting income is 1,000,000 before recording current returns of 20,000, and includes 50,000 of verified art. 20 income and 10,000 of exchange-rate income. These exclusions do not overlap; no return has already been deducted. Ordinary operating expenses do not reduce this base. The resulting 920,000 base gives 36,800 tax at 4%; evidenced quarterly payments of 30,000 leave 6,800. [Arts. 54^1–54^4][code].

```oa-check
name: Moldova 2026 SME defined accounting income
inputs:
  accounting_income_before_returns: 1000000
  verified_article20_income: 50000
  exchange_rate_income: 10000
  current_returns_not_already_recorded: 20000
  quarterly_payments: 30000
steps:
  sme_income_base: accounting_income_before_returns - verified_article20_income - exchange_rate_income - current_returns_not_already_recorded
  annual_sme_tax: sme_income_base * 0.04
  balance_due: annual_sme_tax - quarterly_payments
expected:
  sme_income_base: 920000
  annual_sme_tax: 36800
  balance_due: 6800
```

## Ask the client first

- What is the legal/taxpayer category, residence, tax period, VAT registration history and elected tax regime? Supply the actual accounting policy and prior-year income/classification tests.
- Who holds participation/voting rights, in how many SMEs, and from which dates? What are the actual last-day employee, turnover/assets and activity figures for any deferral claim?
- Supply the trial balance, invoices, bank reconciliation, related-party contracts and all accounting-to-tax adjustments; identify private/exempt-income expenditure and benefits separately.
- Supply each asset's acquisition, commissioning, legal life, grant/lease/use history and disposal facts, plus loss-origin/utilisation schedules.
- Which foreign receipts, certified foreign taxes, treaties/residence evidence and distributions by profit-origin year exist? Supply instalments, withholding and company-tax payments separately.
- Supply the actual approved return, SFS account/notices, corrections and filing/payment receipts before concluding an outstanding liability or sanction.
