# Moldova: personal income tax (2026) v0.2

Moldova taxes individuals at one flat rate of 12% under Title II of the Fiscal Code. The State Tax Service (Serviciul Fiscal de Stat, SFS) collects the tax. The tax year is the calendar year (Fiscal Code art. 12^1(1)). Figures below are from the consolidated Fiscal Code on legis.md as amended to Law LP235 of 2 October 2026 (in force 30 October 2026), and from the amending laws of 2025 that took effect on 1 January 2026.

## Who this applies to

- **Residents.** An individual is resident if he or she has a permanent home (domiciliu permanent) in Moldova, including while abroad for treatment, rest, study or a business trip, or is in Moldova for at least 183 days in the tax year (art. 5 pct. 5 a)).
- **Who is taxed on what** (art. 13, 14):
  - Resident individual entrepreneurs, people providing professional services, and justice and health professionals: income from all sources, in Moldova and abroad.
  - Resident Moldovan citizens without a business: taxable income from Moldovan sources and from foreign sources for their work in Moldova, plus investment and financial income from abroad.
  - Resident foreigners and stateless persons working in Moldova: Moldovan-source income and foreign income for their work in Moldova, but not foreign investment and financial income.
  - Non-residents without a business in Moldova: only the Moldovan-source income listed in chapter 11 (art. 71).
- **Separate regimes inside Title II.** Justice and health professionals (chapter 10^1, art. 69^1 to 69^5), independent retail traders (chapter 10^2), plant-product buyers (chapter 10^3) and independent entrepreneurs (chapter 10^4, from 1 January 2026) have their own rules, shown below.
- **Out of this skill.** Companies (see [md-corporate-income-tax](md-corporate-income-tax.md)), the month-by-month payroll run (see [moldova-payroll](moldova-payroll.md)), social and health contributions (see [moldova-social-contributions](moldova-social-contributions.md)), VAT (see [moldova-vat](moldova-vat.md)), and IT park residents, whose staff salaries are taxed inside the 7% single tax (art. 370, 377; see [moldova-tax-optimization](moldova-tax-optimization.md)).

## The taxable base

**Gross income.** Gross income includes business and professional income, pay for work and services (salaries, employer benefits, fees, commissions, bonuses), rent, capital gains under art. 40(7), interest, royalties, annuities and insurance payouts not reinvested, debts written off by a creditor, state prizes not exempted, contract penalties received, and any other income not made non-taxable (art. 18). The non-taxable list is in art. 20. Each taxpayer must declare gross income from all sources (art. 13(2)).

**Business deductions.** An individual entrepreneur deducts the expenses of the business under chapter 3 of Title II (art. 24 onward). Justice and health professionals deduct business expenses under the same chapter and keep the exemptions in chapter 4 (art. 69^3). A fixed asset is depreciable when its value is above MDL 12,000 and its life is over one year, by the straight-line method; most businesses may instead deduct up to 50% of cost in the first year (art. 26^1(2), (6), (6^1)). Donations to public bodies, non-profits and unions are deductible up to 5% of taxable income (art. 36(1)-(2)).

**Losses.** A business loss is carried forward and spread over the next five tax years, oldest loss first (art. 32).

**Personal exemptions.** A resident whose annual taxable income is below MDL 360,000 (not counting income taxed finally under art. 90^1) gets the personal exemption of MDL 29,700, or MDL 34,620 for the groups in art. 33(2) (art. 33(1)-(2)). Income under the retail and plant-buyer regimes counts toward the MDL 360,000 test (art. 33(1^1)). The spouse exemption (art. 34(2)) and dependant exemptions (art. 35) start in the month after the right arises. A dependant is a parent or child of the taxpayer or spouse (including adoptive), or a second-degree collateral relative disabled from birth or childhood, whose own income is not above MDL 12,400 a year (art. 35(2)). Curators of minors aged 14 to 18 and legal guardians get the dependant exemption for each person in their care (art. 35(3)).

**Who loses the exemptions.** A person whose income was taxed for part of the year under the special regime in chapter 1 of Title X (IT park) cannot use the exemptions for that period; exemptions apply pro rata to the months under the standard regime (art. 35^1).

**Personal deductions.** An individual also deducts (art. 35^2):
- compulsory health insurance premiums he or she paid;
- compulsory state social insurance contributions he or she paid;
- voluntary health insurance or medical services from one provider, up to one forecast average monthly wage (MDL 17,400 for 2026);
- premiums under life, health, fire and natural-disaster insurance contracts, up to one average monthly wage for all contracts together;
- interest on a loan for a first home (not under state programmes), up to one average monthly wage, shared by co-owners in proportion to their shares;
- from 1 January 2026, own education or professional training and children's education at institutions under art. 15 of the Education Code, up to MDL 20,000 in total.

**Final withholding.** Dividends, bank interest, rent, royalties, winnings, donated money, plant and livestock produce sold in natural form (6%) and consignment sales (12%) taxed under art. 90^1 are final. They are not added to gross income and do not go on the return (art. 90^1(4)). The SFS may check plant and livestock sales and certain non-taxable income above MDL 100,000 a year (art. 90^1(5)).

**Rent to tenants who do not withhold.** An owner without a business who lets real estate to a person who is not a withholding agent (for example another individual) pays 7% of the monthly contract value himself. The contract must be registered with the SFS within 7 days of signing, and the tax is paid by the 25th of the current month. If the contract is not registered and the tax not paid, the SFS estimates the income by indirect methods (art. 90^1(3^4)).

**Services by a person without a business.** Income of an individual without a business from services or works, and fees of board and audit-committee members, are treated as salary and taxed at 12% by the payer (art. 88(5)). A director's mandate is treated as employment (art. 88(6)). An independent entrepreneur working for a client outside the conditions of art. 24(11^1) is treated as an employee (art. 88(8)).

**Independent retail regime (chapter 10^2).** A resident trader without a legal form, selling retail (no excise goods) from an outlet up to 50 m2, with income up to MDL 1,200,000 a year, may opt in by application to the SFS. Tax is 1% of sales, at least MDL 3,000 a year; on stopping mid-year, at least MDL 250 for each month of activity. The SFS sends quarterly payment notices by the 25th of the month after each quarter, at least MDL 750 for quarters I to III, with a recalculation in Q4. The first year's tax is reduced by the cost of the cash register, unless already compensated. A cash register connected to the SFS sales-monitoring system is mandatory (art. 69^6 to 69^12).

**Independent entrepreneurs (chapter 10^4, from 1 January 2026).** A resident individual registered with the Public Services Agency as an independent entrepreneur (antreprenor independent) may provide only the services listed in the annex to chapter 10^4 (40 activities, including software development 62.01, IT consulting 62.02, data processing and web portals 63.1, design 74.1, translation 74.3, architecture 71.11 and engineering 71.12). There is no bookkeeping and no return: the SFS records income from cash-register data and the dedicated bank accounts (art. 69^22). The single tax is 15% of income up to MDL 1,200,000 a year and 35% on the excess. It includes income tax, state social insurance contributions, compulsory health insurance premiums and local taxes (art. 69^25). Independent entrepreneurs are not required to register for VAT (art. 112(1)). Chapter 10^4 was added by Law LP228/2025, in force 1 January 2026.

**Who must file** (art. 83(2)): individuals with tax to pay; everyone in professional activity, even with no tax due; residents who used the personal exemption and had annual taxable income above MDL 360,000 (not counting art. 90^1 income); residents who direct a percentage of their tax to a beneficiary; non-residents with income under art. 74; and individual entrepreneurs and farms. A person who is not obliged to file may file to reclaim overpaid tax (art. 83(5)). Someone who moves his permanent home abroad files a return for the part of the year he was resident (art. 83(7)). An individual without a business may ask the SFS, before the deadline, to extend the filing date (art. 83(8)). Returns of individual entrepreneurs and professionals are filed electronically (art. 83(12), 69^5(6)). The form of the return is set by the Ministry of Finance (art. 83(4)); it is commonly called CET18.

**Information from payers.** By 1 March, every payer gives the individual a statement of income paid, exemptions and deductions applied and tax withheld (art. 92(4)).

The SFS may assess tax, interest and fines for 4 years from the filing or payment date, and up to 6 years if no return was filed or a refund is claimed (art. 264(1), (3)).

## Cross-border points

- **Non-residents.** A non-resident individual without a business in Moldova is taxed only on Moldovan-source income in art. 71, for example payments for services provided in Moldova, management and consulting fees paid by a resident, dividends and interest paid by residents, and capital gains on assets located or registered in Moldova (art. 71(1)). The payer withholds 12%, or 6% on dividends (art. 91(1)). Non-residents receiving income from payers who do not withhold are taxed under art. 74 and file a return (art. 74, 83(2) b1)). Real-estate rent received by a non-resident from such a payer is taxed at 12% of the monthly contract value, with the contract registered within 7 days (art. 74(1^1)).
- **Residents with foreign income.** Residents are taxed on worldwide income as set out in art. 13. Tax paid abroad is credited under art. 82 (for justice and health professionals, art. 69^5(3)).
- **Leaving Moldova.** A person moving abroad files a return for the resident part of the year (art. 83(7)).
- **Treaties.** Treaty rates and the documents needed to apply them are in art. 79^3 and the individual treaties; see the pack's cross-border skill, [md-cross-border](md-cross-border.md).

## Worked examples

**Example 1. Employee, 2026, standard exemption.** A resident employee earns MDL 240,000 gross in 2026 (MDL 20,000 a month) and has no other income. The 9% health insurance premium withheld by the employer, MDL 21,600, is deductible (art. 35^2(1)). Employees pay no state social insurance contribution, so there is nothing more to deduct. Income after the deduction is MDL 218,400, below MDL 360,000, so the MDL 29,700 personal exemption applies (art. 33(1)). Taxable income is MDL 188,700 and the tax at 12% is MDL 22,644. If the employer withheld exactly this over the year, no return is needed (art. 83(2)).

```oa-check
name: Moldova employee, MDL 240,000 gross, 2026
inputs:
  gross: 240000
  health_rate: 0.09
  personal_exemption: 29700
  pit_rate: 0.12
steps:
  - health: gross * health_rate
  - income_after_deductions: gross - health
  - taxable: income_after_deductions - personal_exemption
  - tax: taxable * pit_rate
expected:
  health: 21600
  income_after_deductions: 218400
  taxable: 188700
  tax: 22644
```

**Example 2. Individual entrepreneur above the exemption limit, with bank interest.** A resident individual entrepreneur has business profit of MDL 420,000 in 2026 after business expenses. She also receives MDL 10,000 of bank interest, from which the bank withheld the final 6%, MDL 600 (art. 90^1(3^7)); the interest stays off the return (art. 90^1(4)). She paid the fixed state social insurance contribution of MDL 22,878 (see moldova-social-contributions). As the founder of an individual enterprise she paid the fixed health premium by 31 March, so she paid the reduced MDL 2,527 rather than MDL 12,636. Both are deductible (art. 35^2(1)-(2)). Her taxable income is MDL 394,595. That is above MDL 360,000, so she gets no personal exemption (art. 33(1)). Tax at 12% is MDL 47,351.40. She has no staff and is not VAT-registered, so she files the unified return by 25 March 2027 (art. 83(11)).

```oa-check
name: Moldova individual entrepreneur, profit MDL 420,000, no exemption, 2026
inputs:
  profit: 420000
  social_fixed: 22878
  health_fixed_paid_by_31_march: 2527
  bank_interest: 10000
  pit_rate: 0.12
  interest_final_rate: 0.06
  exemption_limit: 360000
steps:
  - interest_tax_withheld: bank_interest * interest_final_rate
  - taxable: profit - social_fixed - health_fixed_paid_by_31_march
  - over_limit: taxable - exemption_limit
  - tax: taxable * pit_rate
expected:
  interest_tax_withheld: 600
  taxable: 394595
  over_limit: 34595
  tax: 47351.40
```

## Ask the client first

1. Where is your permanent home, and how many days were you in Moldova in 2026?
2. What kinds of income did you have: salary, business, professional fees, rent, dividends, interest, foreign income? Who paid each one, and was tax withheld?
3. Do you run a business, and under which form: individual enterprise, farm, justice or health practice, retail trader, independent entrepreneur, IT park company?
4. Is your annual taxable income near or above MDL 360,000?
5. Do you support dependants, or do you or your spouse fall into a group with the higher MDL 34,620 exemption?
6. Did you pay for education, voluntary health insurance, life insurance or first-home loan interest in 2026?
