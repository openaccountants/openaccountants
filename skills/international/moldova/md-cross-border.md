# Moldova cross-border income tax, 2026

## Who this applies to

This Guide covers ordinary resident foreign income and nonresident Moldova-source income: residence, permanent establishments (PEs), gross withholding, property and capital gains, treaty evidence and refunds, foreign-tax credits, and individual arrival/departure. It is a source-cited writer draft. The country pack remains partial pending the identified implementation gaps and independent whole-country review. A named accountant has not attested to this expansion.

Determine **the person, residence, income category, source, PE connection and payer** before choosing a rate. Citizenship, incorporation, an invoice address and the destination bank account do not answer all six questions. Use the Fiscal Code consolidated through the amendment effective 30 October 2026; selected operative articles were read, not every provision of the Code. [Code arts. 4–5, 13–15, 71–79³ and 91](https://www.legis.md/cautare/getResults?doc_id=157050&lang=ro), read 2026-10-30.

### Residence and the taxable population

An individual is domestically resident if either they have a permanent domicile in Moldova or they spend **at least 183 days during the fiscal year** in Moldova. The domicile limb includes specified absences for treatment, holidays, study or business travel and Moldovan officials exercising functions abroad. Article 5(6) gives nonresident exceptions for diplomatic/consular personnel and families, specified treaty-created international-organisation staff and families, persons present exclusively for treatment, holidays or study, and transit. A company or organisational form is resident where its activity is organised or managed in Moldova or its main place of activity is Moldova. Incorporation abroad does not by itself settle the domestic residence of a business managed in Moldova. Domestic residence does not decide a dual-residence treaty claim; establish the actual treaty and its operative residence article separately. [Code art. 5(5)–(6)](https://www.legis.md/cautare/getResults?doc_id=157050&lang=ro).

The ordinary income-tax scope is **not the same worldwide-income rule for every resident individual**:

| Population | Domestic scope under arts. 13–14 | Official source |
|---|---|---|
| Resident companies and persons carrying on business, professional services, and specified justice/health professions | Moldova-source and foreign-source income, with the applicable deductions/exemptions and special-regime screening | [Code arts. 13(1)(a), 14(1)(a)](https://www.legis.md/cautare/getResults?doc_id=157050&lang=ro) |
| Resident Moldovan citizens outside business | Moldova-source income, foreign-source income for activity in Moldova, and foreign investment/financial income | [Code arts. 13(1)(b),(c), 14(1)(b),(c)](https://www.legis.md/cautare/getResults?doc_id=157050&lang=ro) |
| Resident foreign citizens/stateless persons working in Moldova | Moldova-source income and foreign-source income for activity in Moldova; foreign investment/financial income is expressly excluded from this ordinary population | [Code arts. 13(1)(d), 14(1)(d)](https://www.legis.md/cautare/getResults?doc_id=157050&lang=ro) |
| Resident independent-activity taxpayers | The distinct scope in arts. 13(1)(b¹)/14(1)(b¹); identify the actual regime before applying ordinary tax | [Code arts. 13–14](https://www.legis.md/cautare/getResults?doc_id=157050&lang=ro) |
| Nonbusiness nonresident individuals | Moldova-source income under Title II Chapter 11, subject to its payer, income and treaty rules | [Code arts. 13(1)(e), 14(1)(e), 71–74](https://www.legis.md/cautare/getResults?doc_id=157050&lang=ro) |

Investment and financial income have statutory definitions; the references to investment, royalties, annuities, rent, exchange differences and other financial income depend on participation not being regular, permanent and substantial. Do not label an active foreign business as excluded passive income. Article 14(3) also separates the specifically listed special/final-tax bases. Use [individual income](moldova-income-tax.md) and [corporate income](md-corporate-income-tax.md) for the resident computation and regime tests. [Code arts. 12(5)–(6), 14(3)](https://www.legis.md/cautare/getResults?doc_id=157050&lang=ro).

## The base

### Classify the nonresident's Moldova-source income

Article 71 applies whether receipt is in Moldova or abroad. Read the specific source condition before moving to art. 73/74/75 and the rate. The categories below preserve the whole ordinary statutory source map; they are not a statement that every category always bears 12% of an invoice. [Code art. 71](https://www.legis.md/cautare/getResults?doc_id=157050&lang=ro).

| Art. 71(1) | Moldova-source category and operative distinction |
|---|---|
| (a) | Goods sales. This category is expressly excluded from the ordinary art. 91(1) 12% withholding rule; that exclusion does not settle PE, VAT or customs. |
| (b), (b¹) | Management or consultancy in any field paid by a resident, or borne as a Moldovan PE expense, is source income regardless of performance location. Other services under (b¹) are sourced where performed in Moldova. A remote invoice is not enough to choose between them. |
| (c), (d) | Specified gains on art. 39 assets located, registered or issued in Moldova, with different individual/legal-person computations. The nonbusiness individual clause excludes gains on state securities/local-authority bonds. |
| (e), (f) | Resident-paid dividends; resident-paid interest or interest borne by a Moldovan PE. Nonbusiness nonresident individuals have the specified state-security/local-authority-bond interest exclusion. |
| (g), (h) | Assignments of claims; contractual fines/penalties, including works/services and foreign-trade goods contracts, paid by a resident or borne by a Moldovan PE under the stated conditions. |
| (j) | Royalties paid by a resident or borne by a Moldovan PE. Apply the actual royalty definition and exclusions below. |
| (k), (k¹) | Leasing, letting/subletting, usufruct and superficies of property in Moldova; separately, the stated ship, aircraft, rail/road vehicle and related movable-property leasing/use income, including international-traffic equipment. |
| (l), (m) | Insurance/reinsurance premiums collected from a resident or borne by a Moldovan PE; transport services except transport solely between points outside Moldova. |
| (n), (o) | Individual salary and similar employment remuneration, stated administrator/founder/board/governing-body payments irrespective of where the administrative duties are actually exercised, and art. 19 employer benefits, under the resident/PE payer conditions. These enter the art. 88 salary mechanism. |
| (p) | Payments by resident voluntary-pension funds. |
| (q), (r) | Moldova-performed entertainer/sportsperson income regardless of the person receiving the payment; separately, professional, sporting, entertainment and other stated Moldova-performed services. |
| (s), (t), (t¹) | Prizes at Moldova contests; commissions for Moldova activities with the specified resident/PE payer connection; gambling/promotional winnings with that connection. |
| (u) | Liquidation proceeds from a resident: the excess of monetary/nonmonetary distributions over the recipient's capital contribution/participation. Do not withhold on an assumed entire capital return without determining this base. |

Article 71(2) separately excludes income from regular international air services earned by a nonresident without a Moldovan PE from the Moldova-source definition. Article 72 is repealed; it supplies no additional current exemption. [Code arts. 71(2), 72](https://www.legis.md/cautare/getResults?doc_id=157050&lang=ro).

**Software classification matters.** Article 12(1) defines royalty by the right being used or transferred. Its exclusions cover software acquired exclusively for operation, including installation, implementation, storage, customisation and updates; full copyright acquisition or limited copying for the user's own use/distribution; distribution rights without reproduction; specified nonpossessory transponder/cable/pipeline access; and specified roaming/radio-frequency/operator communications. A product labelled SaaS or software is not automatically a royalty or automatically outside Moldova tax. Examine the rights and services, then the relevant art. 71 category and treaty. [Code art. 12(1)](https://www.legis.md/cautare/getResults?doc_id=157050&lang=ro).

### Apply the appropriate collection route

For a nonresident legal person, art. 73(1) subjects covered art. 71 income not connected to a Moldovan PE to art. 91 at source without expense deductions, excluding goods under 71(a) and the stated correspondent-bank account/settlement services. Redirecting income to third persons or foreign subdivisions does not change this withholding rule. For a nonresident individual, art. 73(3) has the corresponding goods and salary distinctions. Withholding applies irrespective of payment form or location. Identify any base specifically defined by art. 71, such as a gain or liquidation excess, before treating the gross statutory income as the withholding base. [Code art. 73](https://www.legis.md/cautare/getResults?doc_id=157050&lang=ro).

For covered ordinary income, calculate **statutory gross income × applicable domestic/treaty rate**, then reconcile gross contractual income, tax withheld and net paid. Preserve noncash valuation and tax-bearing contract terms for review; this draft does not certify a gross-up or foreign-currency rounding method. For dividends, establish the profit years, shareholder decision, monetary/nonmonetary distribution and historic capital movements before selecting 6% or 15%. Dividends are paid out of after-tax income under art. 80; the withholding is not a deduction from the company's corporate tax bill. [Code arts. 71, 73, 80, 91](https://www.legis.md/cautare/getResults?doc_id=157050&lang=ro).

If the payer is outside art. 90, use art. 74 instead of assuming no tax. Its nonresident self-calculation/cessation document covers the enumerated income categories. Its special monthly 12% rental/use route covers art. 71(k),(k¹) income from these other payers. The remaining approved form and procedural details are listed under Sources. [Code arts. 74, 90](https://www.legis.md/cautare/getResults?doc_id=157050&lang=ro).

### Establish the PE and calculate its profit

Domestic art. 5(15) covers a fixed business place through which the nonresident carries on business directly or through a dependent agent: management, branch/office, factory/workshop, mines/extraction and agricultural cultivation; the construction/project limb; sales from owned or rented Moldova warehouses; the other-services/activity limb; and qualifying dependent-agent activities or agent-maintained stock from which goods are delivered on the nonresident's behalf. A legal registration label alone does not establish or remove this tax status. [Code art. 5(15)](https://www.legis.md/cautare/getResults?doc_id=157050&lang=ro).

Article 5(15¹) excludes the specified preparatory/auxiliary or other activities **in the absence of the art. 5(15) PE criteria**. The list covers exclusively storage/display facilities or stock, processing by another person, purchasing, information/marketing/advertising/market research that is not the business's ordinary core activity, specified signing under detailed written instructions, and construction activity not exceeding six months. Do not detach a listed activity from its conditions or use a storage label for a warehouse actually making sales. Apply the relevant treaty separately. [Code art. 5(15)–(15¹)](https://www.legis.md/cautare/getResults?doc_id=157050&lang=ro).

For construction, maintain a dated project/site ledger. Article 77 includes preparatory works and access/infrastructure works, except infrastructure initially made for unrelated purposes. A general contractor counts subcontractor work entrusted to it; the direct-client-contract exception has a related-person qualification. The article separately addresses a nonresident subcontractor's PE position. Start with the earlier site handover/access or actual commencement. Completion follows the acceptance/actual-completion rules, including work completed after acceptance or without a formal acceptance act. Temporary interruption is not automatically a cessation; the public-authority conservation exceeding 90 days/force-majeure exception and the resumed-work conditions must be applied together. Related resumed work/new construction or extension can aggregate; the article distinguishes later repairs/reconstruction of an already handed-over object. Roads, viaducts, canals and communications with moving locations count as one site. Do not replace this method with invoice dates or a fixed days-to-months conversion. [Code art. 77(1)–(9)](https://www.legis.md/cautare/getResults?doc_id=157050&lang=ro).

For an established PE, art. 75 treats it as a resident business **only for its Moldova-source income in the listed art. 71 categories and directly connected, documented expenses**. Build this reconciliation:

1. Identify the Moldova-source income connected to the PE under art. 75(1), rather than importing all worldwide head-office revenue.
2. Deduct documented, directly related expenses allowed by Title II. Apply the ordinary corporate deduction, depreciation, loss and regime tests in [corporate income](md-corporate-income-tax.md).
3. Add back the nonresident head-office amounts prohibited by art. 75(4): royalties/similar IP-use charges, head-office service charges, interest/other head-office loan remuneration, expenses unrelated to Moldova business income, and unsupported expenses. A group recharge invoice does not override this list.
4. Apply the appropriate income-tax rate to the resulting taxable profit and reconcile any valid tax payments/credits and filing obligations. Art. 91(1) withholding does not apply to income connected to the PE under art. 91(2)(a).

These steps are grounded in [Code arts. 15(b), 75 and 91(2)](https://www.legis.md/cautare/getResults?doc_id=157050&lang=ro). Treaty attribution requirements and taxpayer facts must also be established. Employment or other civil-contract work performed by a nonresident individual does not itself create that individual's PE under art. 75(5); do not extend that rule to the foreign employer's fixed-place or agent position.

### Foreign income, credits, property and mobility

First establish that the foreign income is taxable in Moldova for this taxpayer under arts. 13–14. Compute its Moldova taxable amount using the applicable category and ordinary deductions/reliefs. Article 82 allows the foreign income tax actually paid/withheld only with evidence certified by the competent foreign authority, translated into the state language unless issued in English or Russian. The credit cannot exceed the Moldova tax calculated on **that income** and belongs to the period in which it is taxed in Moldova. Reconcile the eligible income, foreign tax, Moldova tax attributable to it and allowed credit. Do not offset foreign VAT or social insurance as income tax, assume an automatic carryforward/refund of excess foreign tax, or credit tax against income excluded from the Moldova tax base. The worked example below applies one identified income stream; multi-country allocations, foreign tax assessed later and timing differences remain procedure gaps. [Code arts. 13–15, 82](https://www.legis.md/cautare/getResults?doc_id=157050&lang=ro).

For the ordinary income-tax computation, convert foreign-currency income/receipts/expenses using the official MDL rate for the transaction date under art. 21(3). Its transaction-date rule uses the bank/payment-company statement or, in other cases, the primary documents under the applicable SNC/IFRS method. At the fiscal year-end, retranslate foreign-currency debts at the final-day official rate, excluding advances for assets/services, and recognise the prescribed exchange differences. A currency not quoted by BNM uses the stated two-stage conversion through a BNM-quoted currency. Retain the actual rate, date and category evidence; this income-tax method does not decide VAT conversion, the foreign-tax-credit payment/timing allocation or a contractual gross-up. [Code art. 21(3), including points 2^1 and 3^1–3^3](https://www.legis.md/cautare/getResults?doc_id=157050&lang=ro).

For Moldova property and capital disposals, distinguish rent/use, capital gain and liquidation. Nonbusiness individuals fall within Chapter 5 under art. 37. Article 39 lists shares/participations, bonds and other business ownership titles, private nonbusiness property, land and options, with the stated nonrecognition cases. Article 40 computes disposal proceeds less documented basis, including market value for noncash proceeds, and contains special gifts, qualifying car, basic-home, jointly owned-home and stock-option rules. Article 41 limits capital losses to capital gains. Article 42 specifies acquisition/creation, inheritance/gift/exchange and other bases, including zero where confirming documents are absent and its specific construction exception. A foreign buyer or overseas payment does not remove the art. 71 Moldova location/registration/issue test. Obtain the actual title, acquisition/capital-contribution records, consideration, valuation, basis evidence and treaty property/gains article before filing. [Code arts. 37, 39–42, 71(c),(d),(k),(u)](https://www.legis.md/cautare/getResults?doc_id=157050&lang=ro).

A main-home exemption is not any home someone once occupied. Article 5(46) requires ownership for at least three years and domicile/residence during the last three years up to disposal, with its specified cancellation no more than 90 days before disposal. Article 40(6) excludes the qualifying main-home gain/loss from recognition. For a nonresident claim, establish the full past facts and treaty position; do not substitute the person's current mailing address. [Code arts. 5(46), 40(6)](https://www.legis.md/cautare/getResults?doc_id=157050&lang=ro).

**Departure and arrival have an actual domestic property rule.** When an individual ceases to be resident, art. 78(1) deems a sale of all property **except immovable property**, at market value when residence ceases. Identify the cessation date, asset inventory, classification, documented/adjusted basis, market values and applicable recognition/exemption rules before computing the resulting taxable income. Do not apply an invented standalone exit-tax percentage or assume every deemed disposal has the same capital-income treatment. On becoming resident, art. 78(2) gives a nonresident individual the right to establish the market value of their property at that date as the basis for determining income on a later sale. Preserve the entry valuation and date rather than treating the foreign original cost as automatically the only basis. The departure filing and unresolved implementation evidence appear below. [Code arts. 37–42, 78, 83(7)](https://www.legis.md/cautare/getResults?doc_id=157050&lang=ro).

## Penalties and interest

Incorrect treaty application that leaves income tax unpaid or partly paid leaves the resident withholding payer responsible under art. 79³(8). A residence document or adviser instruction does not transfer that statutory responsibility. [Code art. 79³(8)](https://www.legis.md/cautare/getResults?doc_id=157050&lang=ro).

Article 260(1) penalises noncompliance with preparation/submission of a tax report: for the art. 232(b) nonbusiness individual population, **200–400 per report, capped at 2,000 for all reports**; for art. 232(a),(c) legal persons and the specified business/professional individual populations, **500–1,000 per report, capped at 10,000**. Establish the actual offence and responsible population before quoting a sanction. Article 261(4) provides **20%–30% of understated tax** for understatement through a return with untrue data; art. 261(4¹) has the stated truthful self-correction exception under art. 188. Article 261(5) separately provides **80%–100%** for the described evasion of calculation/payment of undeclared percentage-based liabilities. These are different offences, not three automatic additions to every missed withholding payment. [Code arts. 232, 260(1), 261(4)–(5), 188](https://www.legis.md/cautare/getResults?doc_id=157050&lang=ro).

Late-payment interest under art. 228 runs from the day after the due date through actual payment, inclusive. Its annual daily-rate method uses the preceding November BNM short-term monetary-policy base rate rounded up to the next whole percentage, plus five percentage points, divided by the number of days in the year and rounded to four decimal places under the statutory rule. The actual published 2026 daily percentage has not been retained here, so do not use an assumed fixed rate. Article 228(2¹) limits interest to the corresponding liability for the stated corrected/erroneous-calculation cases; art. 228(5)–(7) contains documented transfer/offset, budget-funded receivable, special-accounting and bank-timing exceptions. A corrected return does not automatically remove tax or interest. [Code art. 228](https://www.legis.md/cautare/getResults?doc_id=157050&lang=ro).

## Cross-border points

### Apply an actual treaty and retain evidence

Article 4 gives an applicable tax treaty precedence over conflicting domestic rules and denies the stated treaty-shopping use of a resident by an ineligible other person. Article 79³(1) provides the more favourable rate where domestic and treaty rates differ and directs use of OECD Model Commentaries for interpretation. This does not create a single universal treaty rate, residence tie-breaker or employment exemption. Retrieve the actual agreement, subsequent protocols, operative dates and any applicable multilateral modifications; determine residence, income article, PE attribution, beneficial-owner and anti-abuse conditions from those texts. [Code arts. 4, 79³(1)](https://www.legis.md/cautare/getResults?doc_id=157050&lang=ro).

The Ministry's linked inventory still states **20 June 2022**. Use the SFS treaty/protocol inventory as an additional discovery source; neither a country list nor a PDF filename substitutes for reading the relevant operative treaty. Treaty-by-treaty rates and MLI conclusions are not certified in this Guide. [MF inventory](https://mf.gov.md/ro/content/acorduriconven%C8%9Bii-pentru-evitarea-dublei-impuneri-la-situa%C8%9Bia-din-20062022); [SFS treaty inventory](https://www.sfs.md/ro/tratate-internationale?categories%5B%5D=3), read 2026-10-30.

Article 79³(2) distinguishes evidence by legal form:

- A nonresident **individual** supplies the competent residence-state authority's residence certificate, in original paper and/or electronic form, **before income payment**.
- A nonresident **legal person** supplies a copy of the residence certificate, or a document proving registration in the residence country, or an extract from that state's official authority website confirming fiscal residence or registration. This domestic documentary alternative does not itself establish substantive treaty entitlement.
- A foreign-language residence document needs a state-language translation unless issued in English or Russian. The certificate is valid for its stated year(s), unless residence conditions change. A certificate issued for a fiscal period also applies during the first **60 days** of the next period unless those conditions change. Without the residence certificate, the Code directs domestic treatment; apply the legal-person alternatives in the same provision rather than silently turning this into a certificate-only rule for companies.

Keep the actual evidence, date received, covered period, residence facts, treaty analysis, contract, rights/service-performance evidence, payment and withholding reconciliation. Moldova-residence certification itself is governed by the MF procedure under art. 79³(9); its current forms and service procedure remain an explicit gap. [Code art. 79³(2),(9)](https://www.legis.md/cautare/getResults?doc_id=157050&lang=ro).

### Late evidence, refunds and tax-paid certificates

A certificate arriving after payment is not permission to erase tax without a process. Under art. 79³(2¹), where the beneficiary presents it **during the calendar year**, they can make a free-form overpayment-refund application to the payer. The payer recalculates at treaty rates and files a corrected tax report with its SFS subdivision. Article 79³(3) separately provides refund to the payer or nonresident on the nonresident's request within the statutory limitation period. The exact limitation computation and implementing forms/documents have not been verified here. [Code art. 79³(2¹)–(4)](https://www.legis.md/cautare/getResults?doc_id=157050&lang=ro).

Under paragraph (4), the resident payer or another authorised person files the refund application acting for the nonresident. Refund to the applicant requires no budget arrears. This withholding refund route is not accepted for income earned through the nonresident's PE. Paragraph (5) provides a certificate of Moldova income tax paid on withheld tax. If that certificate has already been issued, paragraph (6) conditions refund execution on notifying the nonresident and the foreign authority of cancellation of the earlier certificate. If refund happened first, paragraph (7) permits the later certificate only for the unrefunded tax or after repayment of the refunded amount. Reconcile refunds with foreign credits so a cancelled/full-tax certificate is not used twice. [Code art. 79³(4)–(7)](https://www.legis.md/cautare/getResults?doc_id=157050&lang=ro).

### Interfaces that need their own test

For employment, connect the actual treaty employment/director/entertainer article to [payroll](moldova-payroll.md) and [social/medical contributions](moldova-social-contributions.md). An income-tax treaty is not evidence of a social-security exemption or a posting certificate; the relevant agreement and insurance facts require their own verification. For goods, property, digital services and foreign suppliers, test VAT place of supply, import/self-assessment and customs separately under [VAT](moldova-vat.md). A withholding exclusion or treaty exemption is not a VAT determination. Domestic legal bases for these separate computations are supplied in those source-cited drafts; their independent review and explicit country gaps remain open.

Related-party dealings also need the actual controlled-transaction, aggregation, file-request and adjustment analysis in [transfer pricing](md-transfer-pricing.md). Do not treat a treaty reduced rate as proof that an intra-group charge is deductible or arm's length. Entity migration, reorganisations, CFC rules, information-exchange/CbCR status and prospective EU-alignment measures require separate enacted-law/effective-date work; this Guide makes no unverified assertion that they are absent or already applicable.

## Two worked examples that recompute

### 1. Distinguish income categories before withholding

Assume an ordinary Moldovan company pays nonresidents without a Moldovan PE, without applicable treaty relief: management consultancy **100,000** within art. 71(b), a qualifying copyright reproduction royalty **50,000**, current-profit dividends **200,000**, and dividends attributable to 2008–2011 profits **20,000**. Separately it pays **80,000** for goods under art. 71(a) and **40,000** for residual services performed wholly outside Moldova that are established not to fall within another Moldova-source category. These are hypothetical classified facts, not conclusions inferred from invoice labels.

Withholding is **12,000 + 6,000 + 12,000 + 3,000 = 33,000**. Total contractual payments are **490,000** and net paid is **457,000**. The goods and the specified offshore residual services contribute zero to this particular withholding computation for different reasons; no PE, VAT/customs or other-country exemption follows. IPC21 maps royalties to 51, dividends to 54 and the consultancy to the applicable other-payment entry 56. Do not assume a payment excluded from withholding is therefore omitted from every reporting requirement. [Code arts. 71(a),(b),(b¹),(e),(j), 73, 91](https://www.legis.md/cautare/getResults?doc_id=157050&lang=ro); [IPC21 annex 2 Table 1](https://www.legis.md/cautare/getResults?doc_id=154168&lang=ro).

```oa-check
name: Moldova nonresident income categories and net payments
inputs:
  consultancy: 100000
  copyright_royalty: 50000
  current_profit_dividend: 200000
  historic_profit_dividend: 20000
  goods: 80000
  offshore_residual_service: 40000
  ordinary_rate: 0.12
  dividend_rate: 0.06
  historic_dividend_rate: 0.15
steps:
  consultancy_tax: consultancy * ordinary_rate
  royalty_tax: copyright_royalty * ordinary_rate
  current_dividend_tax: current_profit_dividend * dividend_rate
  historic_dividend_tax: historic_profit_dividend * historic_dividend_rate
  withheld: consultancy_tax + royalty_tax + current_dividend_tax + historic_dividend_tax
  gross_payments: consultancy + copyright_royalty + current_profit_dividend + historic_profit_dividend + goods + offshore_residual_service
  net_paid: gross_payments - withheld
expected:
  consultancy_tax: 12000
  royalty_tax: 6000
  current_dividend_tax: 12000
  historic_dividend_tax: 3000
  withheld: 33000
  gross_payments: 490000
  net_paid: 457000
```

### 2. Cap the foreign credit at Moldova tax on that income

Assume an ordinary resident company subject to 12% has **300,000** of Moldova taxable profit and one identified foreign-income stream with **200,000** of Moldova taxable profit after all allowed deductions. That foreign income is taxable in Moldova in the same period, the competent foreign authority's acceptable evidence confirms **30,000** of foreign income tax paid, and there are no other reliefs, losses or credits. The 12% regime and all bases are stipulated; this is not a turnover-tax or IT-park example.

Moldova tax before credit is **60,000**. The tax on the foreign income is **24,000**, so the art. 82 credit is **24,000**, leaving **36,000** Moldova tax. The **6,000** foreign-tax excess is not treated as a Moldova refund/carryforward or a credit against domestic income. This computation does not decide a timing mismatch, mixed tax base, multiple-stream allocation or foreign treaty-refund claim. [Code arts. 13–15, 82](https://www.legis.md/cautare/getResults?doc_id=157050&lang=ro).

```oa-check
name: Moldova resident company foreign income credit ceiling
inputs:
  moldova_taxable_profit: 300000
  foreign_taxable_profit: 200000
  ordinary_company_rate: 0.12
  certified_foreign_income_tax: 30000
steps:
  total_taxable_profit: moldova_taxable_profit + foreign_taxable_profit
  tax_before_credit: total_taxable_profit * ordinary_company_rate
  foreign_income_moldova_tax: foreign_taxable_profit * ordinary_company_rate
  allowed_foreign_credit: min(certified_foreign_income_tax, foreign_income_moldova_tax)
  moldova_tax_after_credit: tax_before_credit - allowed_foreign_credit
  foreign_tax_above_ceiling: certified_foreign_income_tax - allowed_foreign_credit
expected:
  total_taxable_profit: 500000
  tax_before_credit: 60000
  foreign_income_moldova_tax: 24000
  allowed_foreign_credit: 24000
  moldova_tax_after_credit: 36000
  foreign_tax_above_ceiling: 6000
```

The predecessor's three decision cases remain: a remote supplier needs service/source classification; a fixed-place business needs domestic/treaty PE and profit attribution; and interest/royalty payments need domestic classification, timely legal-form-specific evidence and the operative treaty before relief. They have been expanded into the method above rather than converted into invented treaty-rate examples. All examples assume their stated legal facts; arithmetic checks do not independently verify those facts.

## Ask the client first

- Who legally earns each payment, what is their legal form/citizenship where relevant, and what is their domestic and treaty residence for the actual period? Obtain domicile, travel/day records, management-location facts and dated residence evidence.
- What exactly is supplied: goods, management/consultancy, other services, IP rights, software operation/distribution/reproduction, financing, rent, gains, salary/director remuneration, pension, prizes or liquidation? Obtain the contract, rights, service location and payment terms.
- Is the payer within art. 90, and is the payment connected to a Moldova PE? Obtain sites, offices, warehouses/sales, agents/stock, signing authority, related contracts, project start/stop/acceptance and subcontractor records.
- For dividends/capital/liquidation, what profit years, historic capital increases, contributions, distributions and noncash values support the base and rate? For property/gains, obtain title, cost, improvements/valuation, disposal proceeds, main-home and loss evidence.
- Which treaty and protocol apply, what substantive entitlement/beneficial-owner/anti-abuse facts support relief, when was the acceptable residence document received, and has residence changed? Is a refund, corrected return or tax-paid certificate already in progress?
- For foreign income, is it taxable in Moldova for this population and period? Obtain the Moldova-category computation, foreign authority's tax-paid evidence, translation, currency/timing records, refund entitlement and any prior credit.
- Has the individual arrived, ceased residence or changed permanent domicile? Obtain the exact dates, property inventory, arrival/departure market valuations, basis documents and departure filing history.
- Obtain actual payroll/insurance/posting facts, VAT/customs and related-party records, prior filings/payment receipts, current form access and treasury details. Which unclosed ordinary procedure gap below is relevant to the planned filing?
