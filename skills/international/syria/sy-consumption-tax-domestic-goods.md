# Syria: consumption tax on domestic manufactured goods

## Scope

This source-cited draft covers the producer-level goods rules in Finance Ministry Decision 938/Q.W of 29 June 2026, implementing Decree 11/2015 as amended by Law 15/2024. Read the [official PDF](https://docs.mof.gov.sy/consumption_tax_on_imports_table.pdf): instructions occupy pages 1–20; Table 3 occupies pages 233–234 and Table 4 page 235. Its import-oriented filename does not describe its entire contents.

This is not a general VAT, retail-sales or import-rate schedule. Jewellery, precious-metal articles and vehicles have separate collection rules and are outside this Guide. The base decree/amendment chain, later changes and territorial implementation still require verification. Section XXII, p.19 expresses instruction amounts in **new Syrian pounds**; section XXIV, p.20 replaces the listed older instructions. Do not convert those amounts a second time or use an older collection method.

## Start with the actual product

For goods within the relevant domestic schedules, the levy is collected on the producer's sale. Use by the producer itself, its establishment or its projects is also treated as a sale. These are the instructions' rules for the covered goods, not a statement that every domestically produced item is taxable (III(c), p.4; IV(a–b), p.9).

| Covered domestic product | Rate | Primary locator |
|---|---|---|
| Alcoholic products/beverages within customs chapter 22 | 20% | III(c)(3), p.4; Table 3, p.233 |
| Domestic tobacco | 20% on local producer's sale value; the tobacco institution is instructed to add the levy to relevant wholesale/retail invoices and remit it | III(c)(3), p.4; Table 3, p.233 |
| Covered cosmetic preparations under headings 3303, 3304, 3305 and 3307 | 10% | III(c)(3), pp.4–5; Table 3, pp.233–234 |
| Covered marble/granite under headings 2515, 2516 and 6802 | 15% | III(c)(3), p.5; Table 3, p.234 |
| Finished cement, packaged or bulk, heading 2523 | 0.5% | III(c)(3), pp.5–6; Table 3, p.234 |
| Carbonated water/drinks and non-alcoholic energy drinks | 5% | IV(c)(1), p.9; Table 4, p.235 |
| Natural fruit/vegetable juices and juice mixtures | 5% | IV(c)(2), p.9; Table 4, p.235 |
| Covered household electrical appliances/equipment | 1% | IV(c)(3), p.9; Table 4, p.235 |
| Locally manufactured finished chocolate | 2% | IV(c)(4), p.10; Table 4, p.235 |

Classify against the actual composition, use, tariff heading where specified and product documentation. A rate in the imported-goods table must not be applied to a domestic sale merely because the product name is similar. The table above does not settle whether a specific transaction falls within an exemption or a separate collection rule. [Instructions, pp.4–10 and schedules pp.233–235](https://docs.mof.gov.sy/consumption_tax_on_imports_table.pdf).

## Valuation and important exclusions

**Cement.** For domestic cement, use the product value at sale from the factory, including packaging bags for packaged cement. Transport and loading charges are excluded from that stated value. Finished bulk/packaged cement is covered; primary/intermediate materials such as clinker are excluded from the finished-product rule. Cement consumed by the producing company is taxable and the company remits the levy. The separate imported-cement base includes customs-related amounts and must not be substituted for the domestic factory-gate base (pp.5–6).

**Marble/granite.** The domestic base is the sale invoice value, regardless of whether the purchaser is a trader or a consumer. Producers collect and remit on their full covered sales. Do not assume that a business buyer removes the levy (p.5).

**Cosmetics.** Covered preparations include perfumes/toilet waters, makeup, non-medicinal skincare/sunscreen, manicure/pedicure preparations, shampoo and hair preparations, shaving preparations, personal deodorants and specified bath/personal-care preparations. The instructions exclude room deodorising preparations, ritual incense, air/floor fresheners, hand/body cleaning liquids, and contact-lens/artificial-eye cleaning, disinfecting or soaking solutions **from this category**. These exclusions are not proof that the product is free of every tax or import levy (pp.4–5).

**Household appliances.** The rule covers ordinary household-use equipment, including refrigerators, washing machines, domestic air conditioning/heating and cooking equipment. It distinguishes industrial, commercial and service-use equipment; basic electrical installation items, batteries, chargers and water-lifting pumps; devices used only as a lighter or lamp, without another electrical use; and separately manufactured components. A component is not taxed under this finished-appliance rule unless it is independently usable as a standalone electrical device. The instructions specifically discuss a water-heating coil: fitting it as a spare part does not by itself make it an independently usable final product (p.9). Document actual use and configuration rather than relying on a shop's label.

**Juices.** Natural-juice status follows the applicable Syrian product standard, the approved manufacturing licence and the product label (p.9).

**Chocolate.** The 2% rule uses the value of locally manufactured finished chocolate at the factory warehouse, including the stated liquid, solid and filled forms. Foods dipped in or filled with chocolate/cocoa are outside this particular heading regardless of their chocolate percentage. The instructions give chocolate-coated/filled biscuits, chocolate ice cream and chocolate milk as examples, with the qualification that the product is not frozen or chilled chocolate itself. Finished chocolate does not become excluded merely because it is chilled or frozen. This is not a general exemption from all taxes (p.10). [Instructions, pp.4–10](https://docs.mof.gov.sy/consumption_tax_on_imports_table.pdf).

## Producer compliance

Section V, pp.10–11 requires a commencement declaration within 30 days of starting the covered activity, stock/transaction records and an invoice or contract for each taxable sale. File the previous month's sales declaration with the competent finance office during the first **10 days of the following month** and pay within that period. The special 15-working-day airline rule does not apply to these goods.

Record product classification, invoice base, exclusions, levy, own-use movements and payment evidence. The instructions' treatment of own use does not justify inventing a transfer-pricing or valuation formula: establish the applicable value with supporting records before computing the levy. General tax-exempt status does not automatically remove collection duties where the levy is borne by the customer; XIII, pp.16–17 contains specific qualifications. For foreign-currency receipts, XIV, p.17 requires levy remittance in the stated currency. Record disposal is controlled by XV, pp.17–18; do not treat it as a blanket one-year retention allowance. See the [services and common compliance Guide](sy-consumption-tax-services.md) for the shared procedural detail and its disclosed gaps. [Instructions, pp.10–19](https://docs.mof.gov.sy/consumption_tax_on_imports_table.pdf).

## Example 1: domestic cement valuation

Assume covered finished cement has a factory value of 100,000 new pounds including bags, with separately established transport of 8,000 and loading of 2,000. The levy base remains 100,000 and the levy is 500. Do not apply 0.5% to the combined 110,000 merely because all charges appear on one commercial invoice.

```oa-check
name: Syria domestic cement factory value
inputs:
  factory_value_including_bags: 100000
  transport: 8000
  loading: 2000
  rate: 0.005
steps:
  - commercial_charges: factory_value_including_bags + transport + loading
  - levy: factory_value_including_bags * rate
expected:
  commercial_charges: 110000
  levy: 500
```

## Example 2: marble sold to a business

Assume a domestic producer sells covered marble with an established taxable invoice value of 80,000 new pounds to a trader. The levy is 12,000. The buyer's business status does not reduce the covered sale to zero.

```oa-check
name: Syria domestic marble business purchaser
inputs:
  invoice_base: 80000
  rate: 0.15
steps:
  - levy: invoice_base * rate
expected:
  levy: 12000
```

## Example 3: chocolate classification boundary

Assume separately classified finished chocolate has factory-warehouse value 50,000 new pounds, while chocolate-coated biscuits worth 30,000 fall within the explicit exclusion from this heading. The chocolate-heading levy is 1,000 on the first product and zero under this heading on the biscuits. This example establishes no exemption from other taxes.

```oa-check
name: Syria chocolate heading boundary
inputs:
  finished_chocolate_value: 50000
  coated_biscuit_value: 30000
  chocolate_rate: 0.02
  biscuit_rate_under_this_heading: 0
steps:
  - chocolate_levy: finished_chocolate_value * chocolate_rate
  - biscuit_levy_under_this_heading: coated_biscuit_value * biscuit_rate_under_this_heading
expected:
  chocolate_levy: 1000
  biscuit_levy_under_this_heading: 0
```
