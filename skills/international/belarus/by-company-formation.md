# Belarus Company Formation & Entity Choice

## Entity types, capital and incorporation

- **Overview**: Foreign and local investors most commonly form a limited liability company (OOO). The company sets its own charter capital; there is no statutory minimum for an OOO. State registration is entered in the Unified State Register of Legal Entities and Individual Entrepreneurs (EGR), and tax and Social Protection Fund (FSZN) registration follow automatically from the registrar's data. Figures below are from the Law on Business Companies, the Civil Code rules inserted by Law No. 365-Z, the Tax Code, the Law on Audit and the Law on FSZN contributions, read on 31 October 2026.
- **Forms of business company**: a business company is formed as a joint-stock company (open or closed), a limited liability company or an additional liability company. It may be founded by one person and may have a single participant _(Law No. 2020-XII on Business Companies, art. 10)_
- **Limited liability company (OOO)**: the most common form; at most 50 participants; the charter capital is divided into shares (doli) of sizes fixed by the charter _(Law on Business Companies, art. 91)_. Participants are not liable for the company's debts and bear the risk of loss only up to the value of their contributions; a participant who has not fully paid in is jointly liable up to the unpaid part _(art. 95)_
- **Additional liability company (ODO)**: like an OOO (at most 50 participants), but participants are jointly and subsidiarily liable for the company's debts with their own property up to the amount set in the charter, and at least the minimum set by legislative acts, in proportion to their contributions _(Law on Business Companies, art. 112)_
- **Joint-stock company (OAO / ZAO)**: open or closed joint-stock company; its charter capital is the nominal value of its shares and must be at least the statutory minimum _(Law on Business Companies, arts. 10, 65)_. The BYN amount of that minimum is on the Not yet confirmed line.
- **Individual entrepreneur (IP)**: an individual may carry on business without a legal entity from state registration as an IP. An IP may carry on only the activities on the list set by the Council of Ministers and may engage at most three individuals under employment or civil-law contracts for work, services or intellectual property _(Civil Code art. 22(1), as worded by Law No. 365-Z of 22.04.2024, art. 1)_. The list is Annex 1 to Council of Ministers Resolution No. 457 of 28.06.2024 (for example, OKED code 62, computer programming and related consultancy, is on it) _(Resolution No. 457, para. 1 and Annex 1)_
- **IP transition (now closed)**: since 1 October 2024 an IP is not registered for an activity outside the list; IPs registered before that date could continue unlisted activities only until 31 December 2025 _(Law No. 365-Z, arts. 12, 23)_. From 2026 every IP must be on the list.
- **Single-founder company created by an IP**: an IP may create a commercial organisation founded by one person under the procedure annexed to Law No. 365-Z. No selective (planned) inspection of that company may be held for two years from its registration. Territorial tax reliefs that run from registration date may be used for their full period only by such a company created before 1 January 2026; one created from 1 January 2026 counts the period from the IP's own registration _(Law No. 365-Z, arts. 14-16)_
- **Without registration: independent professional activity**: an individual who has no employer for that activity and engages no one may work on the separate list in Annex 2 to Resolution No. 457 after notifying the tax authority that they use a special tax regime (professional income tax, or the single tax for listed activities) _(Civil Code art. 22(1)(2) as worded by Law No. 365-Z; Law No. 365-Z, art. 13; Resolution No. 457, Annex 2)_. Detail: [belarus-income-tax](belarus-income-tax.md).
- **Minimum share capital (OOO)**: no statutory minimum; the OOO determines the size of its charter capital itself _(Law on Business Companies, art. 91)_
- **Charter capital rules**: contributions may be things (including money and securities) or other property rights with a valuation; property rights may not exceed 50% of the charter capital; a non-cash contribution must be valued; no founder may be released from paying in _(Law on Business Companies, art. 29)_. If net assets fall below the charter capital at the end of the second or any later financial year, the company must reduce its capital to no more than net assets within six months of year end _(art. 28)_; if an OOO's net assets are zero or negative at the end of the second or a later year, it must be liquidated _(art. 91)_
- **Capital contribution deadline**: the Law leaves the deadline for forming the charter capital to other legislation _(Law on Business Companies, art. 28)_. The old 12-month figure is on the Not yet confirmed line.
- **Registration authority**: the registering body under Presidential Decree No. 1 of 16 January 2009 on state registration and liquidation of business entities. The pravo.by register card shows Decree No. 1 limited in effect to 1 July 2027 _(National Register card, Decree No. 1, Pd0900001)_. Which body registers which entity is on the Not yet confirmed line.
- **Registration law changes on 1 July 2027**: Law No. 150-Z of 1 June 2026 on state registration and liquidation of business entities repeals Decree No. 1 and its main provisions take effect on 1 July 2027; until then Decree No. 1 governs registration _(Law No. 150-Z, arts. 45(1), 48)_. From 21 June 2026 a registration made on an application with knowingly false statements that the applicant (or a liquidated non-state company it owned) has no overdue debts for damages or wages can be declared invalid by the economic court on the application of the Labour Inspectorate _(Law No. 150-Z, arts. 46(6), 48)_
- **Incorporation timeline and tax registration**: the tax authority registers a new company or IP within two working days of receiving the registrar's information card, and the UNP equals the EGR registration number; no fee is charged for the UNP _(General Part of the Tax Code, art. 69(1), (4))_. The FSZN registers companies and IPs as payers from the date of the EGR entry, on the same information cards _(Law No. 118-Z, art. 7(1))_
- **Core incorporation steps**: agree the name and legal address, adopt the charter (and hold the founders' meeting where there are several founders) _(Law on Business Companies, arts. 12, 14)_, pay the state duty unless filing on the EGR web portal, file for state registration, receive the UNP and FSZN registration from the registrar's data, then open a bank account. Detail of the bank and name steps is on the Not yet confirmed line.
- **State registration duty**: 1 basic unit for a commercial organisation; 0.5 basic unit for an IP; 2 basic units for registering charter changes _(Special Part of the Tax Code, Annex 22, items 1.1, 1.3, 1.7)_. The basic unit is BYN 45 from 1 January 2026 (Council of Ministers Resolution No. 651 of 20.11.2025), so BYN 45 for a company and BYN 22.50 for an IP _(pravo.by news, 21.11.2025)_. No duty is due when the registration documents are filed electronically through the EGR web portal _(Special Part, art. 285(10.3))_
- **Annual compliance**: keep accounts under the Law on Accounting and Reporting, which applies to every organisation unless a legislative act exempts it _(Law No. 57-Z, art. 2)_, and give the annual individual financial statements to the participants and other recipients set by law no later than 31 March of the following year _(art. 16(1)-(2))_; file profit tax returns quarterly (by the 20th, paid by the 22nd) with the annual return by 20 March, and VAT returns by the 20th _(Special Part, arts. 136, 186; see [by-corporate-income-tax](by-corporate-income-tax.md) and [belarus-vat](belarus-vat.md))_; pay FSZN contributions and file FSZN reports (see [belarus-social-contributions](belarus-social-contributions.md)); hold the annual general meeting of participants, which approves the annual report, the annual financial statements and the distribution of profit, by the date in the charter and no later than three months after year end _(Law on Business Companies, art. 36)_
- **Statutory audit**: annual mandatory audit for joint-stock companies that must disclose information under securities law, banks, insurers and insurance brokers, High-Tech Park residents and other listed entities, and for any other legal entity whose revenue for the previous year exceeds 500,000 basic units (on 31 December of that year). The audit must be done by 30 June of the following year _(Law No. 56-Z on Auditing Activity, art. 22(3), (10))_
