# Saint Lucia: company annual returns, accounts and audit decisions

## Scope and required facts

This Guide covers domestic companies under the Companies Act, concentrating on ordinary share-capital companies. Identify incorporation or continuance date, public/private status, financial year, last annual meeting, shareholder classes including non-voting holders, auditor appointments, articles and any unanimous shareholder agreement. External companies, international business companies, regulated entities, insolvency and beneficial-ownership filings need separate checks; do not apply this ordinary-company workflow to them without establishing the governing regime.

## Three obligations with different dates

| Obligation | Rule | Authority |
|---|---|---|
| Registry annual return | Company having share capital: by **1 April** each year after incorporation/continuance; information made up to the preceding **31 December**, with prescribed fee | [Section 194(1)](https://attorneygeneralchambers.com/laws-of-saint-lucia/companies-act/section-194) |
| Annual shareholder meeting | First within **18 months** after the company comes into existence; subsequent meetings within **15 months** after the preceding annual meeting | [Section 107](https://attorneygeneralchambers.com/laws-of-saint-lucia/companies-act/section-107) |
| Financial statements | Prepare the comparative statements required for the annual meeting; the financial period described in section 149 cannot exceed **12 months** | [Section 149](https://attorneygeneralchambers.com/laws-of-saint-lucia/companies-act/section-149) |

A director or officer certifies the registry return. A late return exposes the company and each defaulting director/officer to the prescribed penalty for each month or part-month. This draft does not state the penalty amount because the current fee/penalty schedule has not been fully reconciled. Filing the registry return does not satisfy the separate [company income-tax return](lc-company-tax-filing-and-payments.md). [Section 194(2)–(3)](https://attorneygeneralchambers.com/laws-of-saint-lucia/companies-act/section-194).

For example, a domestic share-capital company incorporated in July 2025 has its first section 194 return due **1 April 2026**, made up to **31 December 2025**. The first-meeting allowance does not defer that registry return. A different accounting year-end does not replace section 194's December reporting date.

## Financial-statement preparation and approval

At the annual meeting the directors present the prescribed comparative statements, the auditor's report if any, and further financial information required by the articles, bye-laws or unanimous shareholder agreement. Section 149(2) permits omission of the preceding-year comparative only with the reason stated in the statements or notes; it is not an unexplained omission. The Registrar can adjust the comparative period under subsection (3). [Section 149](https://attorneygeneralchambers.com/laws-of-saint-lucia/companies-act/section-149).

Directors approve the statements and one or more directors sign to evidence approval. Copies must not be issued, published or circulated without that approval/signature and the auditor's report, if any. [Section 152](https://attorneygeneralchambers.com/laws-of-saint-lucia/companies-act/section-152).

Send the section 149 documents to shareholders at least **21 days before** the annual meeting or signing of the qualifying written resolution, except shareholders who have declined a copy in writing. The listed-public-company summary-statement exception has separate conditions and is not a general shortcut. [Section 153](https://attorneygeneralchambers.com/laws-of-saint-lucia/companies-act/section-153).

Section 130 allows a written resolution dealing with every required meeting matter and signed by all shareholders entitled to vote at that meeting, subject to its exception for a director's or auditor's written statement. Retain the resolution with shareholder minutes. This route does not eliminate section 153's advance circulation rule. [Section 130](https://attorneygeneralchambers.com/laws-of-saint-lucia/companies-act/section-130); [section 153](https://attorneygeneralchambers.com/laws-of-saint-lucia/companies-act/section-153).

## Auditor appointment and dispensation

Section 162 requires an ordinary-resolution auditor appointment at the first and subsequent annual meetings, subject to section 163. An incumbent continues if no successor is appointed; mere silence is not evidence of a valid auditor dispensation. [Section 162](https://attorneygeneralchambers.com/laws-of-saint-lucia/companies-act/section-162).

Section 163 permits a company outside section 154(1)'s public-company category to resolve not to appoint an auditor. **Every shareholder must consent, including shareholders without voting rights**, and the resolution lasts only until the next annual meeting. Do not invent a turnover threshold or treat private status alone as an automatic audit exemption. Other applicable sector rules still require checking. [Section 163](https://attorneygeneralchambers.com/laws-of-saint-lucia/companies-act/section-163).

Example: two voting shareholders consent but one non-voting shareholder does not. The section 163 resolution is invalid. If all consent and the company qualifies, retain evidence from all three and revisit the decision at the next annual meeting. A valid dispensation does not remove accounting-record, financial-statement or annual-return obligations.

## Public-company and subsidiary boundaries

Public companies have an additional Registrar-copy requirement under section 154 and an audit-committee requirement under section 156, subject to the specified reliefs. Section 154 includes both meeting-related and outer-limit timing; obtain a separate filing calendar rather than assuming the ordinary annual return supplies the required financial statements. Section 154(4)'s subsidiary exception requires both inclusion in holding-company consolidated/combined accounts and delivery of those accounts by the holding company in compliance with the section. [Section 154](https://attorneygeneralchambers.com/laws-of-saint-lucia/companies-act/section-154); [section 156](https://attorneygeneralchambers.com/laws-of-saint-lucia/companies-act/section-156).

Keep at the registered office the financial statements of subsidiaries consolidated in the company's statements. Section 151 gives shareholders and their agents/representatives inspection rights, with a court application mechanism for detrimental disclosure. Registrar permission under section 150 to omit particular information is a separate relief requiring an application; management cannot simply decide that disclosure is undesirable. [Section 151](https://attorneygeneralchambers.com/laws-of-saint-lucia/companies-act/section-151); [section 150](https://attorneygeneralchambers.com/laws-of-saint-lucia/companies-act/section-150).

## Records and practical workflow

Maintain adequate accounting records and directors' and committee minutes/resolutions at the registered office or another directors-designated place in Saint Lucia, available to directors at reasonable times. When accounting records are abroad, retain local records adequate to ascertain the financial position with reasonable accuracy quarterly. This is a local-record availability requirement, not a quarterly Registrar return. [Section 187](https://attorneygeneralchambers.com/laws-of-saint-lucia/companies-act/section-187).

Electronic records are permitted if the required information can be reproduced in intelligible written form within a reasonable time. Take reasonable precautions against loss/destruction and falsification, and enable detection/correction of inaccuracies. A cloud subscription alone does not demonstrate those controls or satisfy the local-record requirement. [Section 188](https://attorneygeneralchambers.com/laws-of-saint-lucia/companies-act/section-188); [section 189](https://attorneygeneralchambers.com/laws-of-saint-lucia/companies-act/section-189).

1. Classify the company and build separate registry, meeting, financial-statement and tax calendars.
2. Reconcile shares, shareholder details and corporate changes to the prescribed annual-return form and the year-end snapshot.
3. Prepare statements and confirm auditor appointment or a valid unanimous dispensation.
4. Obtain directors' approval/signature and deliver documents within the statutory lead time.
5. Hold the meeting or execute a qualifying resolution; retain minutes and all consents.
6. File the certified registry return with the verified fee and retain filing acknowledgement.
