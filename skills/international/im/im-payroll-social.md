# Isle of Man: payroll and National Insurance (2026/27)

Isle of Man payroll has two parts. Employers deduct income tax through the Income Tax Instalment Payments (ITIP) scheme under the Income Tax (Instalment Payments) Act 1974. They also deduct and pay National Insurance under the Social Security Contributions and Benefits Act 1992 and the Social Security Administration Act 1992 (as applied to the Island) and the Social Security (Contributions) Regulations 2001. The Income Tax Division of the Treasury collects both. The tax year runs from 6 April 2026 to 5 April 2027. Amounts are in pounds sterling (GBP).

Related skills: [im-income-tax](im-income-tax.md) for the employee's annual income tax, allowances and rates, [im-non-resident-income-tax](im-non-resident-income-tax.md) for non-resident employees (code HR), [im-corporate-income-tax](im-corporate-income-tax.md) for the company, and [isle-of-man-vat](../isle-of-man/isle-of-man-vat.md) for VAT.

## Who this applies to

- **Age band.** National Insurance is paid from age 16 up to state pension age (Income Tax Division, National Insurance Contributions page).
- **Class 1** is paid by employed earners and their employers. **Class 2** is paid by the self-employed. **Class 3** is voluntary, for people not liable to another class. **Class 4** is paid by the self-employed on profits above set levels, on top of Class 2 (Paying National Insurance page).
- **Employers after pension age.** An employee over state pension age pays no Class 1 primary contributions, but the employer still pays (table letter C, with a Certificate of Age Exception) (Paying National Insurance page; PN 227/26 para 5).
- **State pension age rises from 6 April 2026.** For people born between 6 April 1960 and 5 March 1961 it moves in steps from 66 years and 1 month to 66 years and 11 months, on the way to 67. Employees keep paying until their own state pension age (PN 227/26 para 5).
- **ITIP** covers everyone who receives remuneration: employees, office holders such as directors, and pensioners. Remuneration means any salary, wages, fees, pensions or annuities, including bonuses, commission, holiday pay, pay in lieu of notice, termination payments, tips and round sum expenses (Income Tax (ITIP) page).
- **Employment status** is decided by the Income Tax Division, not by agreement between the parties (Employers page).

## The taxable base

### Step 1: Class 1 for each pay period

For each pay period, take gross earnings and compare them with the period thresholds:

- **Employee (primary):** 11% of earnings between the Primary Threshold and the UEL, plus 1% of earnings above the UEL.
- **Employer (secondary):** 12.8% of all earnings above the Secondary Threshold. There is no upper limit (rates and thresholds page).

The Division publishes deduction tables (CF391 for table letters A and J, CF393 for table C) for 2026/27 (National Insurance Contributions page). Table letters: A is the standard rate; J is the deferred rate (needs a Certificate of Deferment); C is over state pension age, employer only; B is the married woman's reduced rate, available from the Division on request (Paying National Insurance page).

### Step 2: directors

A person who is a director at the start of the tax year has an annual earnings period for that year, so bonuses cannot be timed to avoid contributions. Either work out contributions on cumulative pay each time the director is paid, or, for regular pay, deduct per period and recalculate on the annual figure in the last pay period of the year. A director appointed during the year has a pro-rata earnings period to the year end (Paying National Insurance page).

### Step 3: ITIP income tax

Deduct income tax on each payday according to the employee's tax code. A code is one to four numbers followed by F, M or S. The non-numeric codes are NT (no tax), SB (no allowances, tax at the lower rate using table D and table C if needed) and HR (no allowances, higher rate, table C) (Tax codes explained for employers page). The employer acts on form T6 (codes for the new year, issued in mid-March) or form T21 from a previous employer, never on the employee's own T1. Failing to apply the correct code makes the employer liable for the ITIP not deducted. The 2026/27 free pay tables and tables C and D apply from 6 April 2026 (Employers page).

### Step 4: self-employed contributions

- **Class 2:** GBP 6.75 a week, paid monthly, normally by direct debit (form CF351). A person whose net self-employed earnings are below GBP 9,152 can apply for a Small Earnings Exception certificate. It runs from the date of application and can be backdated up to 13 weeks. Opting out of Class 2 can reduce benefit and state pension entitlement (Small Earnings Exception page; Paying National Insurance page).
- **Class 4:** 8% of profits between GBP 9,152 and GBP 56,264 and 1% above. It is charged through the annual income tax assessment (and possibly payment on account), is paid with the income tax, and stops if the person is over state pension age at the start of the tax year. Class 4 does not count towards benefits (Paying National Insurance page).

Self-employed people send form R133 when they start, so the Division can confirm their status, and pay Class 2 monthly (Paying National Insurance page). Class 4 is assessed with the income tax return: see [im-income-tax](im-income-tax.md) for return and payment dates.

## Penalties and interest

- **GBP 250** each for failing to register as an employer, notify a starter or leaver, give payslips, apply the correct tax code, keep records, or otherwise comply with the ITIP Act and regulations. A wrong code also makes the employer liable for the ITIP underpaid (Compliance and Penalties page).
- **Late T35:** 5% of the amount due (which the Division may estimate), and a further 5% if still outstanding after six months.
- **Late annual return:** GBP 250 and GBP 50 a day until it is submitted. A return that is incomplete may be sent back and treated as outstanding; continued failure may lead to prosecution.
- **Online services:** an employer with 5 or more employees who does not register can be fined up to GBP 5,000 on summary conviction.
- **Wrong NI deduction:** the employer is liable for the underpaid employee and employer contributions. Under the Social Security Contributions and Benefits Act 1992 Schedule 1 paragraph 3(1), as applied to the Island, an employer who fails to deduct at the right time may have to pay the employee's share too. Directors can be held personally liable for unpaid NI under the Social Security Administration Act 1992, as applied (Compliance and Penalties page; Reciprocal Agreement with the UK page).
- **Late notice of self-employment:** GBP 100, which can be charged within 6 years (Social Security (Contributions) Regulations 2001 reg 87(3), as cited on the Paying National Insurance page).

## Cross-border points

- **UK.** Under the Social Security (United Kingdom) Order 1977 the Island and the UK are a single area for National Insurance liability. An employer in one territory with staff in the other stays liable as if both were in the same territory. An Island employer was held liable for employer NI on staff working in the UK (Staff of Government Division, July 2012) (Reciprocal Agreement with the UK page; Detached Workers page).
- **New UK agreement.** The Social Security (National Insurance Contributions Reciprocal Agreement) (United Kingdom) Order 2026 (SD 2026/0113) is going through ratification. When in force, workers pay in one territory at a time, generally where the work is done. Certificates of coverage allow temporary postings of up to 36 months and cover a few other groups. It has effect from the tax year starting on the 6 April after it enters into force (UK/Isle of Man Agreement page). It was still going through ratification when read, so the 1977 rules apply for 2026/27.
- **Postings abroad.** An Island employee sent abroad by an Island employer keeps paying Island NI for the first 52 weeks. For reciprocal agreement countries other than the UK, a certificate of detachment may extend that (Detached Workers page). The Island has reciprocal agreements with, among others, Ireland, France, Germany, the Netherlands, Spain, Switzerland, Canada, the United States, Japan, Jersey and Guernsey (Detached Workers page).
- **Workers sent to the Island** from an agreement country can keep paying at home with a certificate of detachment or an A1 form (Detached Workers page).
- **Non-resident employees** get no personal allowance; the employer uses code HR unless the Division gives clearance. See [im-non-resident-income-tax](im-non-resident-income-tax.md).
- **Returning students.** The National Insurance Holiday Scheme no longer applies to new residents (it closed to new residents whose qualifying employment started after 5 April 2025). An Island student who completes a full-time degree-level course off-Island on or after 6 April 2019 and returns to permanent Island employment within 5 years can reclaim Class 1 primary contributions for the first 12 months, up to GBP 4,400, on form R247(RS) within 6 months after those 12 months (National Insurance Holiday Scheme page).

## Worked examples

### Example 1: monthly employee, GBP 3,500 a month

An employee aged 35 on table letter A earns GBP 3,500 in May 2026. Earnings above the monthly Primary Threshold of GBP 763 are GBP 2,737, all below the monthly UEL of GBP 4,689. Employee NI is 11% of GBP 2,737, GBP 301.07. Employer NI is 12.8% of GBP 2,737, GBP 350.34 after rounding. Both, with the ITIP deducted under the employee's code, go to the Division with form T35 by 19 June 2026. This is the exact percentage method; the CF391 tables can differ by a few pence.

```oa-check
name: IM Class 1 monthly, earnings 3,500 (2026/27)
inputs:
  pay: 3500
  pt_month: 763
  uel_month: 4689
steps:
  - main_band: max(min(pay, uel_month) - pt_month, 0)
  - above_uel: max(pay - uel_month, 0)
  - employee_ni: main_band * 0.11 + above_uel * 0.01
  - employer_ni: round(max(pay - pt_month, 0) * 0.128, 2)
expected:
  main_band: 2737.00
  employee_ni: 301.07
  employer_ni: 350.34
```

### Example 2: self-employed with profits above the upper profits limit

A self-employed resident has profits of GBP 70,000 for 2026/27 and paid Class 2 every week. Class 4 is 8% of GBP 47,112 (GBP 56,264 less GBP 9,152), GBP 3,768.96, plus 1% of GBP 13,736 above the upper limit, GBP 137.36. Class 4 is GBP 3,906.32, payable with the income tax on the assessment. Class 2 for 52 weeks is GBP 351.00, paid monthly. Total contributions are GBP 4,257.32.

```oa-check
name: IM self-employed Class 2 and Class 4, profit 70,000 (2026/27)
inputs:
  profit: 70000
  lpl: 9152
  upl: 56264
  class2_week: 6.75
  weeks: 52
steps:
  - class4_main: max(min(profit, upl) - lpl, 0) * 0.08
  - class4_upper: max(profit - upl, 0) * 0.01
  - class4: class4_main + class4_upper
  - class2: class2_week * weeks
  - total: class4 + class2
expected:
  class4_main: 3768.96
  class4_upper: 137.36
  class4: 3906.32
  class2: 351.00
  total: 4257.32
```

## Ask the client first

1. Is the worker an employee, a director or self-employed, and has the Division confirmed the status?
2. What is each worker's age and table letter (A, J, C, B), and is anyone near state pension age?
3. What tax code has the Division issued on T6 or T21, and is any worker non-resident?
4. When did the employer first pay staff, and are the T30, T20 and T21 notifications in?
5. Does anyone work in the UK or another country, or come from abroad, and for how long?
6. For the self-employed: what are the net profits, and is there a Small Earnings Exception certificate?
