# Bulgaria corporate income tax (2026)

> **Source-cited draft.** Drafted from the consolidated Corporate Income Tax Act (Закон за корпоративното подоходно облагане, ZKPO, last amended ДВ бр. 55/2026), the Euro Introduction Act and the Law on Interest on Taxes, Fees and Other Similar State Receivables, all on lex.bg, read on 2026-10-23. No accountant has reviewed it. Do not file from it without review by a qualified Bulgarian professional.

Tax year: the calendar year (ZKPO art. 21). Authority: the National Revenue Agency (NRA, НАП, nra.bg). Currency: the euro from 1 January 2026. Many ZKPO amounts are still printed in leva. Under the Euro Introduction Act they are read as euro, converted at BGN 1.95583 per euro and rounded to the cent (arts. 11(2), 12, 13). Each converted figure below shows the lev amount next to it. For withholding tax, treaties, foreign tax credit, CFC detail and exit tax, see `bg-cross-border`. For arm's length pricing and documentation, see `bg-transfer-pricing`.

## Who this applies to

- **Resident companies** (ZKPO art. 3): legal entities incorporated under Bulgarian law, and SEs and SCEs with their seat in Bulgaria. They are taxed on profits from all sources. There is no place-of-management test.
- **Foreign companies** with a Bulgarian place of business (permanent establishment) are taxed on the profit of that establishment and file the same annual return (arts. 1(3), 2(1)(2)). Their other Bulgarian-source income is taxed by withholding (see `bg-cross-border`).
- **Sole traders** (ЕТ) and non-profit legal entities on their business profit are also ZKPO taxpayers (arts. 1(2), 2(1)(3)). This skill is written for companies.
- **Out of the corporate tax**: activities taxed by an alternative tax instead (gambling, budget enterprises' commercial income, shipping under the tonnage regime; ZKPO Part Five, art. 218). The company still pays corporate tax on its other activities (art. 218(2)).

## The taxable base

**Start from the accounts.** The tax result (данъчен финансов резултат) is the accounting result adjusted by permanent differences, temporary differences and other amounts the ZKPO names (art. 22). A permanent difference changes the result once and never reverses (art. 23). A temporary difference is an item recognised for tax in a different year from the accounts; it is added back now and deducted when the condition is met, or the other way round (art. 24). Taxable profit is the positive tax result less tax losses carried forward (art. 19).

**Common add-backs (permanent).** ZKPO art. 26 disallows:
- expenses not connected with the business, and expenses without proper documents;
- fines, confiscations and other sanctions, and late-payment interest on public debts;
- donations beyond art. 31;
- withholding tax the payer bears itself;
- hidden profit distributions (item 11), and bribes.
Shortages and scrap of assets are disallowed except force majeure and normal technological loss (art. 28).

**Common deductions (permanent).** Dividends from Bulgarian companies and from companies resident in another EU or EEA state are taken out of the result (art. 27(1)(1)). The exclusion does not apply to hidden profit distributions or to amounts the payer deducted (art. 27(2)). Gains on financial instruments traded on a regulated market or growth market are taken out, and losses on them added back (art. 44).

**Hidden profit distribution** (§ 1, item 5 of the additional provisions). Amounts not connected with the business or above market levels paid in any form to shareholders, partners or their related parties; and interest on a shareholder-type loan meeting at least three of four tests (loan above the payer's equity, no fixed term, repayment or interest tied to profits, repayment subordinated to other creditors or to dividends). It is not deductible (art. 26(11)), it is not exempt as a dividend at the recipient (art. 27(2)(2)), and a company that does not declare it pays a sanction of 20% of the amount (art. 267). For prices between related parties, see `bg-transfer-pricing`.

**Temporary differences.** Impairments and revaluations of assets and liabilities, and write-offs of receivables, count for tax only when the asset is disposed of or the receivable is collected or time-barred (arts. 34, 35, 37). From 2026, revaluations of cash items in another currency at the BNB euro reference rate are recognised as they arise (art. 34(2), as amended by ДВ 30/2026). Accrued unused leave and unpaid amounts to individuals are deducted when paid (arts. 41, 42).

**Depreciation.** Accounting depreciation is always added back and tax depreciation deducted instead (art. 54). Every company keeps a tax depreciation plan listing each asset (art. 52). Annual tax depreciation = depreciable value x annual rate x months in use / 12, starting from the month the asset is put into use or the next month (art. 58). The company picks its rate once a year, up to the category maximum in the table (art. 55(2)). Goodwill from a business combination is not depreciable for tax (art. 49).

**Losses.** A tax loss is carried forward and set against positive tax results for the next five years, oldest first, until used (arts. 70 to 72). There is no carry-back and no cap other than the positive result for the year (art. 71(1)). Losses from a foreign source under a treaty exemption, or under the credit method, are set only against later profit from that same source (arts. 73, 74).

**Interest: two rules, one result.**
1. **Thin capitalisation** (art. 43). Applies only if average debt (1 January and 31 December) is more than three times average equity (art. 43(6)). Disallowed interest = interest costs - interest income - 75% x accounting result before all interest (art. 43(1)). Interest on bank loans and finance leases from unrelated parties is left out, unless a related party guarantees, secures or directs it (art. 43(4)). Disallowed interest is recognised in later years within the same formula (art. 43(2)).
2. **Interest limitation** (art. 43a, the ATAD rule). Exceeding borrowing costs = borrowing costs (interest and its economic equivalents, art. 43a(4)) less taxable interest income. Where they exceed EUR 3,000,000, the excess over 30% of tax EBITDA is disallowed (art. 43a(1), (7)). Tax EBITDA = tax result before losses and before arts. 43 and 43a, plus tax depreciation, less interest income, plus borrowing costs (art. 43a(3)). The disallowed excess carries forward with no time limit (art. 43a(5)).
Where both rules bite, the larger disallowance applies (art. 43a(9)). Banks are outside both rules (arts. 43(7), 43a(10)).

**CFC.** The undistributed profit of a controlled foreign company (over 50% control and low actual foreign tax) is added to the Bulgarian company's result, unless the CFC has substantive economic activity (arts. 47в, 47г). Detail is in `bg-cross-border`.

**Expenses tax** (pointer). Representation costs, social benefits in kind and assets given to staff for personal use are deductible in full (art. 206(1)) but bear a separate final expenses tax of 10% or 3% (art. 216), declared in the annual return and paid by 30 June (art. 217). Exemptions apply to voluntary insurance up to BGN 60 (EUR 30.68) a month per employee (art. 208) and electronic food vouchers up to BGN 200 (EUR 102.26) a month per employee (art. 209a), on conditions.

**Alternative taxes** (pointer). Gambling (arts. 219 to 247), budget enterprises' commercial income at 3% (arts. 248 to 253) and shipping under the elective tonnage tax (arts. 254 to 259) replace corporate tax for those activities only.

**Pillar Two** (pointer). Groups at or above EUR 750,000,000 of consolidated revenue in two of the four preceding years fall under ZKPO Part Five "a" (arts. 260a onwards): the income inclusion rule, the undertaxed profits rule and the Bulgarian domestic top-up tax. Treat any in-scope group as a separate engagement.

Evidence of foreign tax paid goes with the return (art. 92(6)).

## Cross-border points

- **Dividends paid out.** Dividends paid to foreign companies bear 5% final withholding. Dividends to a company resident in another EU or EEA state are exempt, **except hidden profit distributions** (ZKPO art. 194(3)(3)). The older version of this skill said the EU/EEA exemption had no exception; that was wrong. Withholding detail, treaty relief and the DOPK procedure are in `bg-cross-border`.
- **Dividends received.** Dividends from EU or EEA resident companies are taken out of the result (art. 27(1)(1)). Dividends from other foreign companies are taxable, with a foreign tax credit (art. 14; see `bg-cross-border`).
- **Interest to related non-residents.** The thin capitalisation and interest limitation rules above apply on top of the 10% withholding and the arm's length rule.
- **Foreign permanent establishment of a Bulgarian company.** Its profit is in the Bulgarian result; relief is by treaty exemption or credit. Foreign-source losses are ring-fenced (arts. 73, 74).
- **Moving assets or residence out of Bulgaria.** Exit tax at 10% on market value less tax value, with a five-year deferral inside the EU or EEA (arts. 155 to 155г): see `bg-cross-border`.

## Worked examples

### Example 1: trading company, ordinary year with a loss brought forward

Struma Trade OOD has an accounting profit for 2026 of EUR 200,000. Its accounts include EUR 30,000 of accounting depreciation and a EUR 2,000 traffic fine. Its tax depreciation plan gives EUR 45,000 for 2026. It has a EUR 50,000 tax loss from 2023, still within five years. It spent EUR 10,000 on representation (client entertainment).

The fine is added back (art. 26(6)); accounting depreciation is replaced by tax depreciation (art. 54). The tax result is EUR 187,000. After the 2023 loss, taxable profit is EUR 137,000 and corporate tax is EUR 13,700. Representation costs stay deductible but bear 10% expenses tax, EUR 1,000.

Its 2024 net sales were BGN 1,800,000, which is EUR 920,325.39: above EUR 153,387.56 and below EUR 1,533,875.64, so it pays quarterly advances. It declared a forecast profit of EUR 120,000 by 15 April 2026, so each of the three quarterly advances is EUR 3,000 (EUR 9,000 in all). The balance of EUR 4,700 is due by 30 June 2027 with the expenses tax. No interest under art. 89: 75% of the annual tax is EUR 10,275, below 125% of the advances (EUR 11,250).

  loss_used: 50000
  taxable_profit: 137000
  cit: 13700
  expenses_tax: 1000
  net_sales_2024_eur: 920325.39

  balance_due: 4700
  interest_base: 0

### Example 2: holding company with a large shareholder loan, both interest rules tested

Balkan Holding EAD is financed by a EUR 60,000,000 loan from its foreign parent; its equity averages EUR 10,000,000. In 2026 it books EUR 6,000,000 of interest to the parent and EUR 200,000 of interest income. Its accounting profit is EUR 3,500,000. Its tax result under the general rules, before losses and before arts. 43 and 43a, is EUR 4,000,000, and its tax depreciation is EUR 1,000,000.

Debt is six times equity, so thin capitalisation is tested. The accounting result before interest is EUR 9,300,000; 75% of that is EUR 6,975,000, which exceeds net interest of EUR 5,800,000, so art. 43 disallows nothing. Exceeding borrowing costs of EUR 5,800,000 are above EUR 3,000,000, so art. 43a applies. Tax EBITDA is EUR 10,800,000; 30% is EUR 3,240,000. EUR 2,560,000 is disallowed this year and carried forward. Because the art. 43a amount is the larger, only it applies (art. 43a(9)(1)). Taxable profit is EUR 6,560,000 and corporate tax EUR 656,000.

## Ask the client first

1. **Net sales for 2024 (and 2025 for next year), and the year the company was formed.** They decide no advances, quarterly or monthly (arts. 83, 84).
2. **Tax losses by year since 2021.** Only the last five years can be used, oldest first (arts. 70 to 72).
3. **The fixed asset register and the tax depreciation plan.** Category and rate chosen for each asset, and any electric cars bought from 2026 (art. 55).
4. **Loans: lender, relation to the company, guarantees, interest, and opening and closing debt and equity.** They decide whether thin capitalisation or the EUR 3,000,000 interest limitation bites (arts. 43, 43a).
5. **Payments to shareholders or their related parties beyond salary and declared dividends.** Possible hidden profit distribution (§ 1, item 5; arts. 26(11), 267).
6. **Representation costs, staff benefits in kind and assets used privately.** Expenses tax base (art. 204).
