# Cyprus transfer pricing (tax year 2026)

Source-cited draft: not reviewed by an accountant, not for filing.

Cyprus transfer pricing rules sit in article 33 of the Income Tax Law 118(I)/2002 (arm's length rule and documentation), article 33C (advance pricing agreements), the Income Tax (Transfer Pricing Documentation File and Advance Pricing Agreement) Regulations of 2022 (K.D.P. 273/2022), the Tax Commissioner's notice on the content of the file (K.D.P. 314/2022) and article 50Z of the Assessment and Collection of Taxes Law 4/1978 (penalties). The documentation regime applies from tax year 2022. The Income Tax (Amendment) (No. 4) Law 244(I)/2025 raised the local file thresholds from 1 January 2026.

Related skills: `cy-corporate-tax` (the 15% rate, the taxable base, the IR4 deadline, interest limitation, payments to associated low-tax companies, SDC, withholding), `cyprus-income-tax` (individuals), `cyprus-tax-optimization` (planning), `cy-non-dom`. This skill does not repeat those rules.

## Who this applies to

**The arm's length rule (article 33(1) and (2)).** Where a business in Cyprus takes part, directly or indirectly, in the management, control or capital of another person's business, or the same persons take part in two or more businesses, and their commercial or financial relations carry terms that differ from those between independent businesses, the Commissioner may add to the Cyprus business's profits the profit it would have made on arm's length terms. The rule also covers any transaction between connected persons, domestic or cross-border. It can only increase the profits of the person adjusted; the other side gets relief under article 33(5) (see "The taxable base").

**Connected persons (article 33(3) and (4)).**
- An individual is connected with a spouse and with relatives up to the third degree, with the spouse of such a relative, and with relatives of their own spouse (article 33(3)(a), 33(4)(c)).
- A person is connected with their business partners and with a partner's spouse or relatives (article 33(3)(b)).
- Two companies are connected where the same person, alone or with persons connected to them, holds directly or indirectly at least 25% of the voting rights or share capital, or is entitled to at least 25% of the income, of both companies; the same applies where groups of two or more persons that are, or can be treated as, the same persons hold 25% of each (article 33(3)(c)).
- A company is connected with a person holding directly or indirectly at least 25% of its votes, capital or income, alone or with connected persons (article 33(3)(d)).
- Two or more persons acting together to secure 25% of a company's votes, capital or income are connected with each other and with anyone acting on their instructions (article 33(3)(e)).
- From 2026, a director or adviser who, alone or with connected persons, holds at least 50% of the voting rights over board decisions under the articles or a shareholder authorisation is treated as connected with the company (article 33(4)(c), proviso added by Law 244(I)/2025).

**Documentation duty (article 33(7)).** Connected persons that are Cyprus tax resident, or non-residents with a Cyprus permanent establishment, keep a transfer pricing documentation file and a Summary Information Table for their controlled transactions. "Controlled transactions" means transactions between connected persons as defined in article 33(3) (article 2). The file has two parts: a master file and a Cyprus local file (article 33(8)).

**Shareholder use of company assets (article 33(2), proviso added for 2026).** The arm's length rule does not apply to a shareholder's (or connected individual's) use of company assets to the extent the shareholder has paid 10% SDC on a disguised dividend distribution for that use under article 3A(2)(a) of the SDC Law. See `cy-corporate-tax` for the 10% charge.

## The taxable base

1. **Identify controlled transactions.** List every transaction with a connected person (the 25% test above, relatives, partners, the 50% board director). Domestic transactions count.
2. **Price each one at arm's length.** Article 33(1) may be applied and read in line with the OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines as amended from time to time (article 33(6)). The notice K.D.P. 314/2022, para. 7, repeats that the principle is the one in article 9 of the OECD Model Tax Convention and that the OECD Guidelines may be taken into account.
3. **Primary adjustment.** Where the terms differ from arm's length terms, the profit the Cyprus business would have made is added to its profits and taxed at 15% (article 33(1)). The adjustment is upward only.
4. **Corresponding adjustment (article 33(5)).** If the Commissioner increases the profits of a Cyprus resident (or a non-resident with a Cyprus permanent establishment) because of its dealings with another Cyprus resident or Cyprus permanent establishment, the other person gets a deduction equal to the increase. The deduction is an expense subject to the normal limits on deductions. If the increase was computed on a loan, credit facility or debit balance, the deduction is treated as interest and article 11 applies, including the interest limitation rule described in `cy-corporate-tax`. No corresponding deduction arises under article 33(5) when the counterparty is abroad; relief there depends on the other country, a treaty MAP or the EU procedure.
5. **Interest-free and below-market loans.** An interest-free loan from a Cyprus company to a connected company is a financing transaction. The Commissioner can add arm's length interest to the lender's income (worked example 1). A Cyprus-resident company does not pay SDC on interest: article 3B of the SDC Law charges interest received by resident individuals and certain public bodies and funds, not companies.
6. **Local file categories.** The Summary Information Table template (annex to K.D.P. 314/2022) splits controlled transactions into five categories: goods; services; royalties and other intangibles; financing transactions; other. Each column records sales or provision and purchases or receipt, with a "total per category" row. The local file thresholds apply per category (article 33(9)(a)).
7. **What the Cyprus local file contains** (K.D.P. 314/2022, para. 2(b)): the group's activities and structure; the local organisation and who management reports to; the business and strategy, including changes from the previous year; restructurings and transfers of intangibles; key competitors; a detailed description of each controlled transaction, the amounts per category per connected person, the group entities involved, copies of the contracts, and the analysis and documentation for each category. The master file content follows para. 2(a).
8. **Quality review.** A holder of a practising certificate from the Institute of Certified Public Accountants of Cyprus (ICPAC) or another recognised audit body in Cyprus carries out a quality assurance review of the Cyprus local file, no later than the return filing date for the year (K.D.P. 273/2022, reg. 3(3)).
9. **Below the threshold.** A taxpayer under the local file threshold for a category still has to price that category at arm's length; article 33(1) applies regardless. The Tax Department has issued guidance on the documentation it expects below the threshold and optional simplified pricing for some transactions. That guidance was not read from an official copy for this skill, so no safe-harbour figure is given here.

The Summary Information Table is not proof that prices are at arm's length (K.D.P. 314/2022, para. 5(b)). It records, per connected person (tax number, name, tax residence), the euro amounts in each of the five categories.

## Penalties and interest

- **Summary Information Table not filed:** EUR 500 (Assessment and Collection of Taxes Law, article 50Z(1)).
- **File not produced within 60 days of a request:** EUR 5,000 if produced between day 61 and day 90; EUR 10,000 between day 91 and day 120; EUR 20,000 after day 121 or if never produced (article 50Z(2)).
- **Tax on an adjustment.** Extra tax from a transfer pricing adjustment is tax due for the year it relates to. Interest at the public interest rate (3.50% for 2026) and the 5% plus 5% late payment charge follow the general rules set out in `cy-corporate-tax`, which this skill does not repeat.

## Cross-border points

- **Treaties and MAP.** Double tax treaties have effect under article 34 of the Income Tax Law. A person facing a transfer pricing adjustment abroad can ask the Cyprus competent authority for relief under the mutual agreement article of the relevant treaty. The Income Tax Law refers to the EU Arbitration Convention 90/436/EEC on the elimination of double taxation in connection with the adjustment of profits of associated enterprises, and APA regulation 4(6)(c) names it alongside treaty MAP.
- **EU dispute procedure (Part IX, articles 36B to 36D, transposing Directive 2017/1852).** An affected person may file a complaint on a question in dispute with the Commissioner and with each competent authority of every other Member State concerned, with the same information, within 3 years of first notice of the measure that gives rise to the dispute. The Commissioner accepts complaints in Greek or English, acknowledges receipt within 2 months and decides within 6 months; if no decision is made in time, the complaint is treated as accepted (article 36C). It applies to complaints filed from 1 July 2019 on (article 36D).
- **APA (article 33C; K.D.P. 273/2022, reg. 4).** A Cyprus resident or a Cyprus permanent establishment may apply for an advance pricing agreement on specific ongoing or future controlled transactions. A bilateral request must be filed in parallel with the other treaty states' authorities. While the agreement's terms and critical assumptions hold, prices within it are treated as arm's length. It can be revised, revoked (treated as never issued) or cancelled in the cases listed in reg. 4(6) to (9).
- **Related rules elsewhere.** Payments of interest or royalties to an associated company in a low-tax jurisdiction are not deductible (article 11(17)); SDC and withholding on outbound dividends, interest and royalties; the exit tax (article 33B); and the general anti-abuse rule (article 33A). All are in `cy-corporate-tax`.

## Worked examples

### Example 1: interest-free loan to a foreign subsidiary, tax year 2026

Delta Holdings Ltd is Cyprus resident. On 1 January 2026 it lends EUR 2,000,000 to its 100% subsidiary in Greece, interest free, repayable on demand. The loan is outstanding all year. Delta's own benchmarking study (not an official rate) supports an arm's length interest rate of 4.5% a year for a loan of this kind to a borrower with this credit profile.

The subsidiary is connected with Delta (25% test, article 33(3)(d)). Independent lenders would have charged interest, so the Commissioner can add the interest Delta would have earned: 2,000,000 x 4.5% = EUR 90,000. Corporate tax at 15% on that is EUR 13,500. Delta is a company, so no SDC arises on the deemed interest (SDC Law, article 3B). No Cyprus corresponding deduction arises because the borrower is in Greece; any relief there is a matter for Greek law, the Cyprus-Greece treaty MAP, or the EU dispute procedure. If the borrower had been a Cyprus-resident company, it would get a deduction of EUR 90,000 treated as interest under article 33(5), subject to the interest limitation rule, saving it up to EUR 13,500 at 15%.

The financing category is below EUR 10,000,000 whether measured by principal or by interest, so Delta has no Cyprus local file for it, but it must still be able to show the arm's length rate.

```oa-check
name: Delta Holdings Ltd, interest-free loan EUR 2m, 2026
inputs:
  principal: 2000000
  arm_length_rate_pct: 4.5
  months_outstanding: 12
  cit_rate_pct: 15
  financing_threshold: 10000000
steps:
  - deemed_interest: principal * arm_length_rate_pct / 100 * months_outstanding / 12
  - extra_cit: deemed_interest * cit_rate_pct / 100
  - sdc_on_interest_company: 0
  - total_extra_tax: extra_cit + sdc_on_interest_company
  - cyprus_borrower_tax_saving_if_domestic: deemed_interest * cit_rate_pct / 100
  - financing_excess_by_principal: max(0, principal - financing_threshold)
expected:
  deemed_interest: 90000
  extra_cit: 13500
  sdc_on_interest_company: 0
  total_extra_tax: 13500
  cyprus_borrower_tax_saving_if_domestic: 13500
  financing_excess_by_principal: 0
```

### Example 2: does the company need a Cyprus local file, and for which category? (tax year 2026)

Epsilon Trading Ltd, Cyprus resident, is a subsidiary in a group with consolidated revenue of EUR 300m. Its 2026 controlled transactions are: goods sold to connected companies EUR 4,800,000; services provided to connected companies EUR 1,500,000; services received from connected companies EUR 1,200,000; a loan of EUR 6,000,000 received from its parent; no royalties.

The test is per category per year (article 33(9)(a)). Reading the Summary Information Table, which totals each category across both directions, services come to 1,500,000 + 1,200,000 = EUR 2,700,000, which is EUR 200,000 over the EUR 2,500,000 limit. Goods (EUR 4,800,000) are EUR 200,000 under the EUR 5,000,000 goods limit. Financing (EUR 6,000,000) is under EUR 10,000,000. So Epsilon needs a Cyprus local file for its services transactions, with the ICPAC quality review, by 31 January 2028. It is not the ultimate or surrogate parent, so it keeps no master file; the group is under EUR 750m, so there is no country-by-country report. It files the Summary Information Table with its 2026 return. Under the 2022 to 2025 rule (EUR 750,000 per category), all three categories would have needed a local file.

```oa-check
name: Epsilon Trading Ltd, local file test by category, 2026 thresholds
inputs:
  goods_sold: 4800000
  services_provided: 1500000
  services_received: 1200000
  loan_received: 6000000
  limit_general: 2500000
  limit_goods: 5000000
  limit_financing: 10000000
  old_limit: 750000
steps:
  - services_total: services_provided + services_received
  - services_excess: max(0, services_total - limit_general)
  - goods_headroom: limit_goods - goods_sold
  - goods_excess: max(0, goods_sold - limit_goods)
  - financing_excess: max(0, loan_received - limit_financing)
  - categories_over_old_limit_count: min(1, max(0, services_total - old_limit)) + min(1, max(0, goods_sold - old_limit)) + min(1, max(0, loan_received - old_limit))
expected:
  services_total: 2700000
  services_excess: 200000
  goods_headroom: 200000
  goods_excess: 0
  financing_excess: 0
  categories_over_old_limit_count: 3
```

## Ask the client first

1. Who owns the company, directly and through relatives, partners or other companies, and does any director hold 50% or more of board votes? This decides who is connected.
2. What are the 2026 controlled transactions, by category (goods, services, intangibles, financing, other), in each direction?
3. Are there loans to or from connected persons at no or low interest, and on what terms? Does the counterparty sit in Cyprus or abroad?
4. Is the company the ultimate or surrogate parent of a group, and what was consolidated group revenue in the previous year?
5. Does any shareholder use company assets privately, and has 10% SDC on a disguised distribution been paid for that use?
6. Has any other country adjusted the price of these transactions, or is an APA or MAP request open?
