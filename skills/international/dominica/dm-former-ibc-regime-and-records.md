# Dominica: former IBC regime and surviving records

## The regime was repealed

Act 6 of 2021 repealed the International Business Companies Act, Chapter 78:01. Section 1(2) brings the repealing section 3 into operation on **1 January 2022**. An old IBC incorporation certificate or an old registry web page is not evidence that the IBC formation regime remains available. [Repeal Act, sections 1–3](https://www.dominica.gov.dm/laws/2021/international_business_companies_ibc_repeal_act_2021_6_of_2021.pdf#page=2).

For an ordinary company application, use the separate [registration Guide](dm-business-registration-and-tax-onboarding.md). Do not infer a current tax exemption from historical IBC status. This Guide does not establish the historical tax treatment of any particular company or the full sequence of earlier tax amendments.

## Historical transition was an application process

Section 5(1) allowed a company carrying on business under the former Act to apply for registration under the Companies Act, Chapter 78:04, without a fee during the statutory transition. Section 5(2) provides for a certificate of incorporation with a unique identification number on registration, with permission to retain the former name. It does not say every IBC automatically became an ordinary company. [Section 5(1)–(2)](https://www.dominica.gov.dm/laws/2021/international_business_companies_ibc_repeal_act_2021_6_of_2021.pdf#page=2).

**The retrieved printed text omits the year after “31st day of December” in section 5(1).** Do not silently fill that gap or treat the historic provision as an open fee-free conversion route in 2026. If a transition date is material to a case, obtain the authoritative correction or determination and the actual registration record. The repeal commencement date is separately explicit and unaffected by this missing year. [Printed section 5(1)](https://www.dominica.gov.dm/laws/2021/international_business_companies_ibc_repeal_act_2021_6_of_2021.pdf#page=2).

The former company's agent was required to notify it of the pending repeal and transition option within **21 days of commencement of section 5**. This is a historical duty, not a fresh notice period starting when someone reads this Guide. [Section 5(3)](https://www.dominica.gov.dm/laws/2021/international_business_companies_ibc_repeal_act_2021_6_of_2021.pdf#page=2).

## Obligations and records survive

Section 4 preserves obligations and penalties incurred by an IBC while the former Act operated. Related investigations, legal proceedings and remedies can be instituted, continued or enforced, and penalties imposed, as if repeal had not occurred. Repeal is therefore not evidence that historical liabilities were discharged. [Section 4](https://www.dominica.gov.dm/laws/2021/international_business_companies_ibc_repeal_act_2021_6_of_2021.pdf#page=2).

Section 5(4) requires the agent to retain all records required under the former Act or any other law for **at least five years after section 3 commenced**. Section 5(5) expressly includes companies formerly carrying on business that were struck off or dissolved. On 22 October 2026, five years from 1 January 2022 have not elapsed. The minimum is not permission to destroy records when another retention duty or proceeding requires preservation. [Section 5(4)–(5)](https://www.dominica.gov.dm/laws/2021/international_business_companies_ibc_repeal_act_2021_6_of_2021.pdf#page=2).

## Evidence needed for an existing entity

As a practical review workflow, obtain the old incorporation record, any transition application, the issued Companies Act certificate and identifier, current registry evidence, agent-held records, and documents concerning outstanding obligations or proceedings. These are evidence requests, not a statutory list added by this Guide. Assess the actual entity before concluding that it is active, dissolved, restored or entitled to transact.

## Remaining checks

Current entity-specific status, late registration or restoration eligibility, asset and contract continuity, historical tax amendments and any later changes to the repeal provisions require further verification. The 2021 Companies Act amendment separately addresses restoration; its existence does not prove that a particular former IBC can use that process. Independent review remains pending.
