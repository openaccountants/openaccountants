# Timing of deductions: when an expense is incurred in New Zealand

This Guide covers the allocation question that follows deductibility: once an amount passes the deduction tests, which income year does it belong to? Inland Revenue's position is that 
a deduction must be allocated to the income year in which it is incurred
, and that a separate rule then claws back the part of that expenditure that has not been used up by balance date. It applies to any person claiming a deduction for expenditure in an income year, and covers the income year up to and including the person's balance date. Figures are for tax year 2026. The threshold figures below are not stated by Inland Revenue as annual figures; each table note records the date or year the source page itself gives, and Determination E12 is stated to apply until it is cancelled by the Commissioner.

## Who this is for

This Guide is for a person who is allowed a deduction for expenditure and has to decide the income year the deduction falls in — most often a business, sole trader or partnership preparing accounts to a balance date. 
Inland Revenue's summary of the settled principles is that expenditure is incurred in an income year even if there is no actual disbursement, and that expenditure is incurred if the taxpayer has "definitively committed" itself to that expenditure.


It is not a deductibility Guide: it assumes the amount already satisfies the deduction rules. 
A person is denied a deduction for an amount of expenditure or loss to the extent to which it is of a capital nature (the capital limitation), and to the extent to which it is of a private or domestic nature (the private limitation)
 — those questions must be settled before timing is considered.

For partnerships, the determination in the first table below sets its own rule: 
for the purpose of applying this determination to partners and partnerships, section HG 2 of the Income Tax Act 2007 must be ignored, and expenditure must be treated as incurred by the partnership and not by the partners
.

## The method, step by step

1. **Confirm the amount is deductible before dating it.** Apply the general permission and then the general limitations, because 
expenditure of a capital nature is denied under the capital limitation and expenditure of a private or domestic nature is denied under the private limitation
. Source: https://www.taxtechnical.ird.govt.nz/-/media/project/ir/tt/pdfs/interpretation-statements/is1403.pdf
2. **Allocate the deduction to the income year in which it is incurred.** 
A deduction must be allocated to the income year in which it is incurred.
 Source: https://www.taxtechnical.ird.govt.nz/case-summaries/2007/timing-of-deduction-for-accounting-services
3. **Apply the "incurred" tests to the facts at balance date.** 
Expenditure is incurred in an income year even if there is no actual disbursement; it is incurred if the taxpayer has "definitively committed" itself to that expenditure; and it is not sufficient that the expenditure be merely "impending, threatened or expected".
 
There must be an "existing obligation"
, tested in light of all the surrounding circumstances. Source: https://www.taxtechnical.ird.govt.nz/case-summaries/2007/timing-of-deduction-for-accounting-services
4. **Do not substitute the accounts for the statutory test.** The Taxation Review Authority 
confirmed that it is settled law that expenditure can only be deducted if it can be brought within the terms of the Tax Acts and, referring to both the Privy Council and Court of Appeal in Mitsubishi Motors Ltd v The Commissioner of Inland Revenue, accounting principles and good commercial practice cannot be substituted for the statutory test of deductibility
. Source: https://www.taxtechnical.ird.govt.nz/case-summaries/2007/timing-of-edduction-for-accounting-services
5. **Identify the unexpired portion at balance date and add it back.** 
The unexpired amount is income of the person in the income year.
 Source: https://www.taxtechnical.ird.govt.nz/-/media/project/ir/tt/pdfs/interpretation-statements/is1403.pdf
6. **Test whether Determination E12 excuses the add-back.** A person allowed a deduction 
is excused from complying with section EA 3 to the extent to which the expenditure is described by a row in column 1 of the schedule, the unexpired portions of all expenditures described by that row do not in total exceed the maximum total amount in column 2, the length of time between balance date and the subsequent expiry date does not exceed the period in column 3, and, for rows (d) and (k), the goods are in the possession of the person at balance date
. Source: https://www.taxtechnical.ird.govt.nz/determinations/miscellaneous/det-e12-persons-excused-from-complying-with-section-ea-3-of-the-income-tax-act-2007
7. **For consumable aids, aggregate before comparing to the threshold** in the consumable aids table above, including stock not in possession at balance date and deferred expenditure. Source: https://www.taxtechnical.ird.govt.nz/-/media/project/ir/tt/pdfs/interpretation-statements/is1403.pdf
8. **For prepaid travel and accommodation, apply the amount and time triggers** in the third table; where they are met, 
the deduction is spread by treating part of the prepayment (the "unexpired portion") as income
, with the deduction following in the later year. Source: https://www.taxtechnical.ird.govt.nz/-/media/project/ir/tt/pdfs/tib/volume-34---2022/tib-vol-34-no9.pdf
9. **Repeat the test at each balance date.** As Inland Revenue puts it for a deferred amount, 
"Section EA 3 and DET E12 will apply again at the end of the following income year to determine whether any of the expenditure ..."
 is again unexpired. Source: https://www.taxtechnical.ird.govt.nz/-/media/project/ir/tt/pdfs/interpretation-statements/is1403.pdf
10. **Partnerships:** apply step 6 at partnership level, ignoring section HG 2 and treating the expenditure as incurred by the partnership. Source: https://www.taxtechnical.ird.govt.nz/determinations/miscellaneous/det-e12-persons-excused-from-complying-with-section-ea-3-of-the-income-tax-act-2007

The pages used for this Guide do not name a return form, a registration step, a records requirement or a filing or payment deadline for these adjustments. Those steps are not stated here because the sources read are silent on them.

## Ask the client first

- What is the balance date, and on what date did the obligation to pay become an existing obligation rather than something merely impending, threatened or expected?
- Was there a contract, order or other commitment in place at balance date that definitively committed the client, even though nothing had been paid?
- For each prepayment, when does the benefit expire, and how many months after balance date does that expiry date fall?
- What is the total of unexpired portions across all expenditure of the same kind, not just the one invoice being queried — including goods not held at balance date and amounts deferred in the accounts?
- For goods, were they in the client's possession at balance date?
- Is the client a partnership, so that the expenditure must be tested at partnership level?

## When to refuse or refer

- The question is whether the expense is deductible at all — capital, private or domestic character, apportionment, or a specific denial rule — rather than which year it falls in.
- The expenditure falls in a schedule row of Determination E12 whose column 2 amount and column 3 period are not reproduced in this Guide; the determination itself must be read before advising.
- Trading stock rather than consumable aids is involved: Inland Revenue treats goods held for sale to customers as falling under 
the definition of "trading stock" in s EB 2
, which is outside this Guide.
- The client wants the accounting treatment to drive the tax year, or is relying on a provision or accrual in the financial statements.
- Financial arrangements, leases, employment-related accruals, depreciation or amortisation timing, or any spreading regime other than the unexpired portion rule.
- Any deadline, return form or payment date is needed: the sources used here do not state them.
- The client is disputing an Inland Revenue assessment on timing, or wants a binding ruling.
