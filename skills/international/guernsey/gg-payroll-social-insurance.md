# Guernsey payroll and social contributions v0.8

> **Source-cited draft.** Professional review is pending. Figures are limited to the periods and assumptions below. This Guide prepares working papers; it is not authority to file or pay.

## Who this applies to

Prepare ordinary 2026 Class 1 payroll, a Class 2 review checklist and Class 3 classification/assessment working papers. Includes ordinary benefit valuation, vehicle and housing scales, and ETI reporting. Secondary pensions and mixed-status cases still need further work.

Sources were accessed on 22 October 2026. A dated rate table controls its stated period; an undated guidance page does not prove that all later amendments have been incorporated.

## Rates, bands and thresholds

The operative Revenue Service leaflet, effective **1 January 2026**, states:

| Class | Rate | Lower earnings limit | Upper earnings limit |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Employee, Class 1 | 7.5% | £192/week or £832/month | £3,780/week or £16,380/month |
| Employer, Class 1 | 7.1% | Same | Same |
| Self-employed, Class 2 | 12.4% | £9,984/year | £196,560/year |

[Benefit Payment & Contribution Rates 2026, pp19–20](https://gov.gg/CHttpHandler.ashx?id=102818&p=0). The lower limit is a liability threshold, not a deductible allowance. Confirm age, class, period and exception before applying this table.

## The base and ordinary computation

For an ordinary full month at earnings above the lower limit, apply the Class 1 rates to gross earnings capped at £16,380. Employee contributions reduce net pay; the employer share is an additional cost.

- £3,000 gross: employee £225; employer £213; gross plus employer SI £3,213, before pensions and other costs.
- £20,000 gross: capped base £16,380; employee £1,228.50; employer £1,162.98.
- At £832, rates apply to the whole qualifying base: £62.40 employee and £59.07 employer after rounding to pennies. Do not calculate only on earnings above £832.
- For pay below the limit, a partial period or multiple employers, obtain the applicable classification instructions before assigning zero or pro-rating a cap.

## Classification, distributions and special earnings

### Classification before calculation

The Revenue Service describes the scheme as covering Guernsey, Alderney, Herm and Jethou, generally above school-leaving age. Class 1 covers employment; Class 2 self-employment; Class 3 non-employment. Do not infer a Class 3 assessment from the Class 1 earnings formula. [Contribution guidance](https://gov.gg/SScontributions).

For construction labour-only contracts, the guidance requires employed classification, including workers not under an ordinary contract of service. Identify the contracting party, labour supplier or agency that is the employer. Substantial materials proportionate to the contract distinguish the self-employed case; supplying tools or negligible materials does not. A worker can have employed labour-only contracts and separate self-employed contracts. The guidance excludes a private householder engaging the worker in their own home from these classification regulations. Keep contract-by-contract evidence. [Contribution guidance, labour-only sections](https://gov.gg/SScontributions).

Controlling employee-directors became employed for contributions on **1 January 2025**. A historic election allowed a further five years of self-employed classification; obtain the election and Revenue Service confirmation rather than treating this as a new 2026 option. The webpage says receipt by 31 December 2024, while SIC1 says prior to that date: a disputed last-day election needs confirmation rather than an assumed outcome. [Contribution guidance](https://gov.gg/SScontributions); [SIC1, introduction](https://gov.gg/CHttpHandler.ashx?id=196680&p=0).

Self-employed individuals are assessed at the maximum unless they apply for assessment on earnings. Do not replace an issued assessment with a self-calculated lower payment without resolving that application. [Class 2 guidance](https://gov.gg/SScontributions).

### Class 3: classification and application before payment

Non-employed classification includes specified low-paid employees, unpaid workers, people between jobs and part-time self-employed people below the annual lower earnings limit. Confirm class rather than assuming no contribution obligation from low wages. Apply for income-related assessment where appropriate; without an application, the published guidance charges the maximum. Pension-age health/long-term-care contributions remain distinct from social insurance. [Class 3 guidance](https://gov.gg/non-employedcontributions).

The exemption test uses income **before** the non-employed allowance. Only after testing that lower limit is the allowance subtracted to calculate liability. Do not deduct the allowance first to create an exemption. The guidance also distinguishes special-rate, voluntary and overseas-voluntary contributions; eligibility for those routes requires separate review. [Class 3 guidance](https://gov.gg/non-employedcontributions).

| Annual lower income limit | £24,960 | £24,960 |

At £24,960 the below-limit exemption is not established: the calculation base after £11,122 allowance is £13,838. Multiplying by 11.8% gives £1,632.884 as annual-rate arithmetic, not a payment demand. The official weekly minimum is £31.40; reconcile the assessed weeks and rounding to Revenue Service's notice rather than paying a self-generated annual figure. [Exemption and allowance](https://gov.gg/non-employedcontributions); [2026 table](https://gov.gg/CHttpHandler.ashx?id=185595&p=0).

### Dividends and qualifying loans

Employee dividends from the employer private company or an associated company enter contribution earnings. SIC1 distinguishes payment in the ordinary week/month from apportionment: the dividend is paid less frequently than wages, normal wages are below the upper limit, and adding the dividend crosses that limit. For dividends declared from **1 January 2025**, Revenue Service interprets the allocation periods as those of the **calendar year of declaration**, not the company's financial period. Report the dividend and estimated annual earnings to the Compliance Inspectors; provisional calculations are revised where necessary with the quarter-four return. SIC1 also identifies an anti-avoidance power under regulation 9. [SIC1, clarification, published 22 December 2025](https://gov.gg/CHttpHandler.ashx?id=196680&p=0).

Loans require a separate qualifying-loan analysis. The Revenue Service describes low/interest-free loans or advances by a company with income taxed below 20% to a participator or associate, contribution liability when the loan qualifies, allocation when the relevant upper limit is exceeded, and a claim for repayment of tax/contributions on amounts repaid within six years. Do not treat every director loan as qualifying or every repayment as an automatic payroll credit. Confirm the qualifying date, classification, allocation and claim with the source rules. [Contribution guidance, loans](https://gov.gg/SScontributions).

### Pension-age change

Employee Class 1 contributions stop from the **week or month following pension age**; employer Class 1 continues. The employer must change the employee from full-rate blue to employer-only orange status. The 2026 leaflet sets the employer-only rate at **7.1%**. This does not establish exemption from tax or another class of contributions. [Employer Guide, p9](https://gov.gg/CHttpHandler.ashx?id=175677&p=0); [2026 leaflet, p19](https://gov.gg/CHttpHandler.ashx?id=102818&p=0).

### Benefits: classification and valuation before payroll

The Revenue Service's **2026 Benefits in Kind Guide** covers employment-related non-cash benefits received by the employee or family, including from a third party because of the job. A benefit merely being available can be taxable; an offer refused or disclaimed so it is not received is different. The guide is administrative guidance and expressly does not replace the legislation. [Sections 1, 2 and 5](https://gov.gg/CHttpHandler.ashx?id=202739&p=0).

Use this sequence for each item:

1. Identify the benefit, recipient, employment connection, provision dates, ownership and any employee reimbursement. Distinguish a cash allowance from an actual business-expense reimbursement; do not apply the non-cash exemption to ordinary cash remuneration. [Section 4 closing paragraph; SoP E13](https://gov.gg/CHttpHandler.ashx?id=202739&p=0).
2. Test a specific exemption and retain its conditions. Group medical-cost cover differs from an individual policy and from critical-illness cover. The narrow individual-policy concession requires an ongoing serious condition that would lose cover on switching to the employer scheme, with supporting information. [Sections 4.23 and 5.10–5.11; SoP E46](https://gov.gg/CHttpHandler.ashx?id=202739&p=0).
3. Select the valuation rule below before applying any exemption. Employee contributions generally reduce the benefit, subject to the particular category's rules. Do not treat every employee-paid cost as a contribution to the benefit. [Sections 6 and 12](https://gov.gg/CHttpHandler.ashx?id=202739&p=0).
4. Track the **£900 exemption for eligible benefits**, not separately for each item. It excludes benefits valued under the benefit regulations, including vehicles/accommodation, and share-option/share-award emoluments. SoP E12 also excludes specified relief-eligible payments and allowed payments on behalf of controlling directors. Establish eligibility before applying the balance. Multi-employer allocation remains unverified here. [Section 4.1; SoP E12](https://gov.gg/CHttpHandler.ashx?id=202739&p=0).
5. Include the taxable benefit in gross pay in the relevant period and calculate ETI, contributions and secondary pensions using their applicable rules and limits. The benefit value is an income addition, not the tax itself. An ongoing benefit is processed monthly; a one-off benefit is included when provided. [Sections 8–9](https://gov.gg/CHttpHandler.ashx?id=202739&p=0).

| Benefit type | Guidance valuation | Important boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Use of property without ownership transfer | Generally 20% of market value at the year's start or later first availability | Special vehicle/accommodation rules and Director-agreed reductions require separate treatment |
| Transfer of a non-stock item | Market value at transfer | Check specific practice exceptions; do not apply this universally |
| Transfer of employer trading stock | Employer's acquisition cost | Employee payment reduces the resulting benefit; retail discount alone is not the base |
| Employer's ordinary customer service/advice | Average cost to employer | Not automatically the customer selling price |
| Other benefit | Cost to provider | Establish actual cost and any special rule |

Sources: [sections 6–7 and example 1](https://gov.gg/CHttpHandler.ashx?id=202739&p=0). One specific exception is the guidance treatment of transferred mobile phones/IT equipment and associated desks/chairs at **20% of market value** under SoP E35. Where the employer retains ownership and use is solely for duties, that practice states no benefit; additional services with private use require cost apportionment. Do not extend that exception to every asset gift. [SoP E35](https://gov.gg/CHttpHandler.ashx?id=202739&p=0).

**Employer-paid tax:** SoP E4 requires the tax reimbursement itself to enter taxable pay. Its 20% example grosses up the established taxable benefit using **1.25**, and the employer reimburses the employee while operating ETI normally. This is an income-tax calculation, not a promise that the employer has also covered employee contributions. [SoP E4, p19](https://gov.gg/CHttpHandler.ashx?id=202739&p=0).

**Homeworking:** where the employer requires homeworking, the practice permits up to **£8 per week**, proportionately reduced for less than a week, without tax. Above that amount, the guidance requires the sums to be returned through ETI and the employee to claim expenses on their return. If homeworking is a choice, the full subsidy is taxable. Do not tax only the excess over £8 or infer an automatic £8 deduction. [SoP E48](https://gov.gg/CHttpHandler.ashx?id=202739&p=0).

### Employer vehicles: choose the category first

Apply Fact Sheet 1's exemption tests before valuing a vehicle. Business-only use is exempt. Its second exemption includes business use and commuting where the vehicle is required for work outside normal employer hours, such as call-outs or going directly on site. Ordinary commuting alone does not meet that second test. Retain use records. [Fact Sheet 1, section 1](https://gov.gg/CHttpHandler.ashx?id=202739&p=0).

For non-exempt use, the **limited benefit** category covers cars with private use confined to commuting, cars with **more than 75%** business use, qualifying pool cars, and other motor vehicles available privately. Within that category the 2026 annual benefit is **£1,565 for cars**, and **nil for motorcycles, vans, lorries and other commercial vehicles**. A qualifying pool car must be available to and used by multiple employees, not used by one to the exclusion of others, with private use secondary to business use; calling a car a pool car is not sufficient. Commercial vehicles are defined by their main design for carrying goods/business items. [Fact Sheet 1, sections 2.1 and 4](https://gov.gg/CHttpHandler.ashx?id=202739&p=0).

Other non-exempt car use has a 2026 annual benefit of **20% of cost**. Cost is gross acquisition cost including extras, without deducting a trade-in. For a leased car use the purchase-equivalent amount in the Director's opinion. The published example deducts the employee's contribution to extras from the resulting cost basis; retain that contribution evidence. These figures are benefit values, not percentages of tax due. [Fact Sheet 1, sections 2.2 and 4](https://gov.gg/CHttpHandler.ashx?id=202739&p=0).

The scale covers fuel and other running costs even where the employer pays only some of them. Employee payment for private fuel is not itself a deductible reimbursement against the car benefit. For the **limited-charge** case, the Director accepts no taxable benefit where the employer certifies a record of all private mileage and the employee reimburses at least **77p per mile**, even if total reimbursement is below £1,565. Do not apply that concession to an ordinary 20%-of-cost car. [Fact Sheet 1, sections 2–3; SoP E18 and E20](https://gov.gg/CHttpHandler.ashx?id=202739&p=0).

An employee using their **own** vehicle on business is a separate rule. SoP E17 gives 2026 accepted mileage rates of **77p for an ordinary car, 69.6p hybrid, 59.6p electric, 38.5p motorcycle, 17p e-bike and 12p pedal bicycle** per mile. Keep business-mileage and vehicle-type evidence; this is not the private-mileage reimbursement test for an employer car. [SoP E17](https://gov.gg/CHttpHandler.ashx?id=202739&p=0).

### Accommodation: identify the employee and type of property

For employees outside the hotel/guesthouse schedule, Fact Sheet 2 values unfurnished accommodation/dwellings or land at **15% of assessable emoluments**, furnished accommodation/dwellings at **17.5%**, and proprietary director/employee accommodation at **open-market rental value**. The latter status includes direct or indirect control of **more than 15%** of shares in the employing company. This is a specific housing classification, not a replacement definition for all director rules. [Fact Sheet 2, sections 1.3 and 2](https://gov.gg/CHttpHandler.ashx?id=202739&p=0).

An ordinary non-proprietary employee can elect for the benefit not to exceed a lower open-market rental value. The election must be made within **two years after the end of the relevant year**: for 2026, by **31 December 2028**. Do not automatically choose the lower number without the election. Establish the assessable-emoluments base separately rather than assuming cash salary is always the whole base. [Fact Sheet 2, section 2](https://gov.gg/CHttpHandler.ashx?id=202739&p=0).

Employee contributions reduce the housing benefit subject to the detailed rules. Under SoP E24, utilities are separate additional benefits for the percentage/open-market housing category; paying personal utility bills does not reduce the accommodation benefit. The hotel schedule charges under regulations 3(1) and 3(2) include those utilities. [SoP E24](https://gov.gg/CHttpHandler.ashx?id=202739&p=0).

For ordinary hotel/guesthouse employees, the 2026 schedule charges **£30 per week or part-week for accommodation** and separately **£30 for food**. It does not add spouses/dependants unless they too are employees. The employed-manager category adds the same charge for the first spouse/dependant; proprietary directors/employees instead require the detailed annual establishment/family schedule. Do not apply the ordinary £30 schedule to everyone. Non-hotel use requires the Director's agreement where Fact Sheet 2 provides for it. [Fact Sheet 2, section 1](https://gov.gg/CHttpHandler.ashx?id=202739&p=0).

SoP E28 includes emoluments from other employers when computing a percentage-based benefit. An employer reports on information reasonably available and Revenue Service handles any further recalculation. Where spouses work for the same employer, their own use/emoluments must be considered separately; accommodation is not automatically taxed once for the household. [SoP E28](https://gov.gg/CHttpHandler.ashx?id=202739&p=0).

### Salary sacrifice is not an automatic benefit saving

Under SoP E15, deductions from salary for a benefit do not reduce gross emoluments; the employee contribution instead enters the benefit calculation. If salary sacrifice would otherwise produce a lower charge than the salary given up, the stated treatment preserves the tax charge on that salary. Review the contract and both calculations before describing a saving. [SoP E15](https://gov.gg/CHttpHandler.ashx?id=202739&p=0).

### Partial periods and remaining special cases

Section 11 contains an annual days/365 approach and a final-month example using days in that month. Their reconciliation for all partial-year cases has not been established here. Controllers' company-deduction interaction, share schemes, relocation and preferential-loan computations require additional work. No generic benefit formula resolves those cases.

## Deadlines and filing

Register with the Revenue Service and obtain separate tax and contribution references. Use these in Returns Creator. Coding/direction notices expire on **31 December** and become invalid when the employee leaves. An incorrect coding requires an updated notice; do not invent a replacement allowance. Without a coding notice, the employer guidance requires deduction on gross pay after approved pension payments. [Employer webpage](https://gov.gg/employers).

The ordinary quarterly return/payment deadlines are **15 April, 15 July, 15 October and 15 January**. Thus quarter four of 2026 ordinarily falls due on **15 January 2027**. The Employer Guide distinguishes employers with **more than 80 staff**, who pay deductions weekly or monthly while still returning quarterly. Establish the employer's payment arrangement; the quarterly return date is not universal permission to hold deductions until then. [Employer webpage](https://gov.gg/employers); [Employer Guide, p16](https://gov.gg/CHttpHandler.ashx?id=175677&p=0).

- Submit electronically using Returns Creator or supported payroll software. Before a replacement return, check whether the original has been processed.
- A no-pay quarter still requires a return or email notification. If employment ceases, notify employer references and final pay date; the guide then removes the nil-return requirement.
- For an incorrect return, send corrected details promptly. The employer webpage requests employer references, employee tax reference, corrected gross pay, pension amount where needed, and quarterly tax deductions. Retain the correction and response.
- Keep wage and deduction records for **six years after the end of the year in which the relevant return was submitted**, not simply six years after payday.

Sources: [Employer Guide, pp16–17](https://gov.gg/CHttpHandler.ashx?id=175677&p=0); [correction FAQ](https://gov.gg/employers). These are instructions for the responsible employer, not authorization for this workflow to email personal records.

A refund through payroll requires a Direction Notice. The Employer Guide limits refunds to tax deducted in the current quarter and prohibits a negative overall tax-deducted figure. Do not offset an independently estimated annual overpayment against current deductions. [Employer Guide, p10](https://gov.gg/CHttpHandler.ashx?id=175677&p=0).

### Benefits that cannot be processed through ETI

If there is no or insufficient cash pay to deduct the required tax, submit the separate benefit-in-kind return. For **2026 benefits the deadline is 15 February 2027**. A supporting spreadsheet does not replace the return or its certificate. Employees must also declare benefits not already included in ETI on their own return; do not double-count a benefit already returned through payroll. [Sections 2 and 9; SoP E38](https://gov.gg/CHttpHandler.ashx?id=202739&p=0). This exception does not establish cancellation of contribution or secondary-pension obligations: reconcile those separately.

### Statutory employment-benefit and record boundary

Section 8(1)–(3) of the current Income Tax Law confirms employment emoluments less authorised deductions and the benefit market-value/use/service/cost categories, reduced by the employee’s qualifying contribution. Section 8(2A)(a) excludes a controlling employee’s benefit **only if and to the extent** that the providing company has no relevant business deduction or Part IX/X/XIV relief for its provision or asset. Reconcile the company’s actual tax treatment before applying this exclusion; control alone is not sufficient. The detailed current benefit scales and concessions above continue to use the 2026 authority Guide. [Income Tax Law s8](https://www.guernseylegalresources.gg/CHttpHandler.ashx?fileid=253819).

## Penalties and interest

The Employer Guide currently linked by the Revenue Service is labelled **2025**. Its p18 states an initial late-payment surcharge of **5% for tax deductions** and **2.5% for contribution deductions**, with no surcharge where its amount is below **£50**. Part-payment leaves the unpaid balance exposed; a correction increasing liability runs from the original due date. It also states a **£300** automatic late-return penalty and possible **£50 per day** until submission. Additional surcharges apply at intervals to outstanding amounts, including earlier charges, but this Guide has not verified the intervals or a complete statutory calculation. [Employer Guide, p18](https://gov.gg/CHttpHandler.ashx?id=175677&p=0).

The same guidance describes a written surcharge appeal to the Director within **30 days**, limited to legal liability or calculation, not an employer's error or oversight. Check the assessment notice and statutory appeal provisions for the exact time trigger and procedure. Current section 193A now confirms statutory **ceilings of £300 plus £50 per continuing day after the original penalty is imposed** for the stated ETI return/record/document failures. For a materially incorrect/incomplete form, section 193A(2A) first requires a written correction notice allowing at least **21 days**; do not apply that trigger to every late return or call the ceiling an automatic assessment. Section 76 provides the issue-date written-grounds appeal route, and section 199 supplies income-tax surcharge mechanics with further charges every six months; the current rate/minimum regulations and social-insurance penalty machinery remain unreconciled. The older employer manual’s tax/contribution surcharge percentages are attributed guidance, not a complete current statutory penalty opinion. [Income Tax Law ss76, 193A, 199](https://www.guernseylegalresources.gg/CHttpHandler.ashx?fileid=253819).

## Secondary pensions

Use the [secondary-pensions Guide](gg-secondary-pensions.md) for 2026 eligibility, enrolment, minimum contributions, opt-outs and provider payments. These obligations are separate from Class 1 social insurance; specialist cases and statutory reconciliation remain open.

## Cross-border points

The Employer Guide requires a certificate of continuing liability to be forwarded when work starts for a claim to be excused Guernsey contributions based on compulsory coverage elsewhere. Foreign payment alone does not prove an exemption. Agreement coverage and the certificate's validity remain case-specific checks. [Employer Guide, p19](https://gov.gg/CHttpHandler.ashx?id=175677&p=0).

Income-tax treaty relief and social-insurance coverage are separate. Consult the [UK treaty Guide](gg-uk-treaty-employment-and-relief.md) for its tax scope; it does not establish contribution relief. Secondary pensions, special benefit categories, partial periods and multiple-employer cases remain open in this payroll pack.

## Worked examples that recompute

**Ordinary month.** At £3,000 qualifying gross pay, below the upper cap and above the lower threshold, employee SI is £225 and employer SI £213. Employer cost before pensions/other costs is £3,213. Net pay still needs tax and other deductions.

```oa-check
name: Guernsey ordinary monthly Class 1 contributions 2026
inputs:
  gross: 3000
  cap: 16380
  employee_rate: 0.075
  employer_rate: 0.071
steps:
  - base: min(gross, cap)
  - employee_si: round(base * employee_rate, 2)
  - employer_si: round(base * employer_rate, 2)
  - employer_cost: gross + employer_si
expected:
  employee_si: 225
  employer_si: 213
  employer_cost: 3213
```

**Upper-cap case.** At £20,000 gross in an ordinary full month, the contribution base is £16,380, giving employee SI £1,228.50 and employer SI £1,162.98. Gross pay still remains £20,000 for the separate tax-base analysis; do not cap wages reported for income tax at the SI limit.

```oa-check
name: Guernsey monthly Class 1 upper cap 2026
inputs:
  gross: 20000
  cap: 16380
  employee_rate: 0.075
  employer_rate: 0.071
steps:
  - base: min(gross, cap)
  - employee_si: round(base * employee_rate, 2)
  - employer_si: round(base * employer_rate, 2)
expected:
  base: 16380
  employee_si: 1228.50
  employer_si: 1162.98
```

Both examples use the [2026 leaflet, p19](https://gov.gg/CHttpHandler.ashx?id=102818&p=0). They assume established ordinary Class 1 status, no pension-age transition, allocation or other exception.

Following section 9's example, allocate £75 exemption monthly, leaving a £25 monthly taxable benefit. Annual taxable benefit is £300. Add £25 to the applicable monthly payroll base; do not deduct £900 every month. [Section 9, example 3](https://gov.gg/CHttpHandler.ashx?id=202739&p=0).

### Vehicle and housing boundary examples

**Gross car cost.** A car costs £30,000 before a £5,000 trade-in, plus £3,000 extras. The employee contributes £2,000 to the extras. Following Fact Sheet 1's example, the benefit cost basis is £31,000. If no exemption or limited category applies, the annual benefit is **£6,200**. The cash paid after trade-in is not the benefit cost basis. [Fact Sheet 1, sections 2.2 and 4](https://gov.gg/CHttpHandler.ashx?id=202739&p=0).

[Supplementary regression control retained byte-exact](../../research/guernsey-2026/original-controls.md).

**Strict business-use boundary.** Assume a £30,000 employer car, 10,000 total miles, no exemption, no qualifying pool arrangement, and private use beyond commuting. At 7,500 business miles the fraction is exactly 75%, which fails the **more-than-75%** test; the ordinary annual value is £6,000. At 7,510 miles it is 75.1%, satisfying that limb and giving the £1,565 limited value. Mileage evidence and the other assumptions decide classification; arithmetic alone does not. **Furnished housing election.** Assume a full-year ordinary non-proprietary employee with an independently established £40,000 assessable-emoluments base, no contributions and a supported £6,000 annual open-market rental value. ## Ask the client first

- Where is the work performed, and is a continuing-liability certificate in force?
- What are the contracts, materials supplied and actual employer relationships?
- What are the pay frequency, pension-age date, start/leave dates and valid notices?
- Are there dividends, loans, a historic director election or non-cash benefits, and how much benefit exemption has been used?
- How many staff are employed and what payment arrangement has Revenue Service set?
- Which returns, payments or corrections have already been submitted?
