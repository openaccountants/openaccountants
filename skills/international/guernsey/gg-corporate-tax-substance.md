# Guernsey corporate tax, distributions and substance v1.0

> **Source-cited draft.** Professional review is pending. Figures are limited to the periods and assumptions below. This Guide prepares working papers; it is not authority to file or pay.

## Who this applies to

Prepare calendar-year 2026 corporate tax classification and compliance working papers. This draft does not calculate Pillar Two, regulated-sector special rules, exemption applications or every company-law obligation.

Sources were accessed on 22 October 2026. A dated rate table controls its stated period; an undated guidance page does not prove that all later amendments have been incorporated.

### Territorial and entity scope

The company guidance uses Guernsey for income-tax purposes to include **Alderney and Herm**. Do not import the different island lists used for social insurance or pensions. It also explains that the tax definition of company includes incorporated and unincorporated bodies other than partnerships; clubs/associations and foundations therefore need corporate-tax analysis. [Company guidance, introduction, clubs and foundations](https://gov.gg/RevenueService/Companies).

A club's member receipts are treated differently from non-member income, with **rental income expressly excepted** from the member-income treatment. Guernsey property income is in the higher-rate category. Keep membership and income-source evidence rather than treating a non-profit label as a blanket exemption. [Company guidance, clubs and associations](https://gov.gg/RevenueService/Companies).

### Residence: evidence and application, not a form-based shortcut

The current non-tax-resident request form is **Form 707 (02/26)**. It asks for central management/control location, directors' residence, board-meeting location and a foreign residence certificate or authority confirmation. If that evidence is delayed, it asks for the notification/application sent to the foreign authority. It also asks about the foreign highest company rate, the rate applicable to this company, and any treaty tie-break position. A low tax bill or foreign incorporation alone is not the complete evidence requested. [Form 707, sections 1–3](https://gov.gg/CHttpHandler.ashx?id=118096&p=0).

The form says acceptance produces a letter detailing future responsibilities. It separately screens Guernsey-source income, a Guernsey PE, local staff, resident beneficial members, participator loans and international reporting. It expressly retains ETI returns where staff exercise functions in Guernsey. Keep that acceptance letter and each continuing-obligation assessment. **Apply the current statutory residence/exclusion decision below as well as the form’s evidence requirements.** For the UK corridor, also consult the [treaty Guide](gg-uk-treaty-employment-and-relief.md). [Form 707, introduction and sections 4–5](https://gov.gg/CHttpHandler.ashx?id=118096&p=0).

### Current statutory residence decision

Section 4(1) treats a company as resident for a year if it is controlled in Guernsey, centrally managed and controlled there, or registered there without the specified section 40A exemption. The section 4(2) exclusion requires proof satisfactory to the Director of **all** the following: tax residence under another territory’s domestic law; central management and control in that territory; either the applicable approved treaty/international-agreement tie-break route **or** that territory’s highest company-tax rate being at least 10%; and no tax-avoidance, reduction or deferral motivation within the statutory condition. The 10% limb concerns the territory’s **highest company rate**, not proof that this company actually paid 10%. Record the foreign law, residence certificate, real decision-making evidence, treaty route where used and commercial reasons. Section 4(3) preserves other obligations even where the exclusion applies. Form 707 is the application/evidence route, not a substitute for these conditions. [Income Tax Law, s4](https://www.guernseylegalresources.gg/CHttpHandler.ashx?fileid=253819).

A resident company is within the worldwide-income rule in section 5(1)(c); a non-resident company uses Part IV Chapter IV, including the specific disregarded-income provisions. Registration, corporate-tax residence, a treaty tie-break and substance status are distinct workpapers. A migration no-objection letter does not by itself settle the tax treatment of an actual disposal, distribution or retained Guernsey activity. [Sections 4–5](https://www.guernseylegalresources.gg/CHttpHandler.ashx?fileid=253819).

## Rates, bands and thresholds

Revenue Service publishes standard 0%, intermediate 10% and higher 20% rates. The intermediate categories include specified financial/fiduciary, custody, fund administration, investment, compliance and aviation-registry activities. Higher-rate categories include regulated trading, hydrocarbon/gas supply, large retail above the stated £500,000 taxable-profit threshold, Guernsey property income/development and specified cannabis/controlled-drug business. Classify the income stream, not merely the entity's name. [Company guidance, income tax rates](https://gov.gg/RevenueService/Companies).

The published tax-reform programme describes changes from 2027, including prescribed professional services. Do not backdate them to 2026 or assume a policy announcement alone establishes the final enacted scope. [Reform timetable](https://gov.gg/taxreform).

The intermediate list expressly includes banking, domestic insurance, fiduciary business, insurance intermediaries/managers, controlled-investment administration, custody, individual-client investment management, investment-exchange operation, compliance-related activities and aviation-registry income. Match the actual activity and statutory category; do not place all financial income at 10%. [Company guidance, rates](https://gov.gg/RevenueService/Companies).

The section 209 definition of **large retail business** tests taxable profits **exceeding £500,000**, reduced proportionately for a business carried on for part of the year. It excludes a retail business with more than 95% of customers outside Guernsey, excludes services to that extent and wholesale trade, and includes online retail. Aggregate the profits of associated Guernsey retail companies: control of one by the other or common control, construed under section 122(1). The resulting liability is apportioned by their respective profits even if one company individually falls below the threshold. Section 7BA and the Fifth Schedule put the qualifying retail-business income in the 20% class; this is not a 20% charge only on the excess over the threshold. At exactly £500,000 a full-year unassociated business does not meet this definition, although another higher-rate category can still apply. Keep customer-location, association, period and income-class evidence. [Sections 7BA, 209 and Fifth Schedule](https://www.guernseylegalresources.gg/CHttpHandler.ashx?fileid=253819).

## The base and computation

1. Confirm the company's residence, accounting period, legal form, ownership and activities.
2. Build a transaction-supported bridge from accounting profit to each taxable income stream. Record source and deduction decisions separately.
3. Apply the established rate to each stream; isolate regulated or special categories for review.
4. Compute distributions and qualifying-loan liabilities separately from company profit tax.
5. Reconcile the return and certified accounts to payments and the bookkeeping ledger.

**Rate illustration:** established ordinary taxable income £100,000 at 0% produces £0 corporate income tax; separately established Guernsey rental taxable income £20,000 at 20% produces £4,000. This does not waive returns, distribution tax, substance obligations or minimum-tax review. Do not use this split if a rule requires the company's wider profit base to be taxed at another rate.

**Established mixed income.** Assume review has established separate ordinary taxable income £100,000, intermediate-category income £30,000 and Guernsey rental taxable income £20,000, with no overriding wider-base or minimum-tax rule. At 0%, 10% and 20%, ordinary company tax is **£7,000**. The example does not establish deductions, stream classification, loss offsets or Pillar Two treatment. [Company rates](https://gov.gg/RevenueService/Companies).

### Ordinary business-profit bridge

Start with profits computed on ordinary commercial principles for the relevant business accounting period. For a business, section 6 normally uses the accounting period ending in the year of charge; the first-period rules permit at most eighteen months, subject to the Director’s determination and the stated investment-company exception. A period split uses months or fractions of months unless that does not fairly represent the profits and the Director directs another allocation. Record commencement, accounting dates and any statutory rate-change split separately. [Sections 6–7](https://www.guernseylegalresources.gg/CHttpHandler.ashx?fileid=253819).

For each item charged in the starting result, retain invoice, purpose, accrual and business/private allocation evidence. Section 7(2) denies capital expenditure, expenditure outside the wholly-and-exclusively business test, appropriations to reserves, the owner’s/family’s maintenance and private expenditure, liabilities under the Income Tax Law, amounts recoverable under insurance/indemnity, the specified exempt-body payments and unlawful payments. Do not add an expense back twice if it was never deducted in the starting accounts. A payment label or bank transfer is not evidence that its purpose qualifies. [Section 7(1)–(2)](https://www.guernseylegalresources.gg/CHttpHandler.ashx?fileid=253819).

Section 7(3) permits qualifying replacement of implements, current machinery/plant/furniture repairs, specified insurance, business borrowing interest, evidenced bad/doubtful debts within the amount written off, business-premises rent and qualifying retirement-benefit contributions under Part XIII. A partial-premises rental deduction follows the business portion; capital improvements do not become current repairs. A later recovery of a previously relieved bad debt enters business receipts under the statutory rule. Ownership-of-land/buildings income uses sections 9–16 rather than automatically importing the trading-profit method. [Section 7(3)–(6)](https://www.guernseylegalresources.gg/CHttpHandler.ashx?fileid=253819).

### Capital-allowance working paper

Separate capital asset expenditure from current costs and reconcile book depreciation to the statutory allowance. Section 84 requires qualifying ownership and business use at the basis-period end, with its patent-rights extension. Sections 85–86 provide the published rates and a proportionate reduction where the business operates for only part of the year. Keep acquisition/disposal dates, cost, previous allowances, private use, unallowed expenditure and any balancing allowance/charge; a sale or cessation is not permission to leave the old asset pool unchanged. Special hotel, glasshouse and ship/aircraft incentives require their own provisions. [Sections 84–86 and Part IX](https://www.guernseylegalresources.gg/CHttpHandler.ashx?fileid=253819).

| Ordinary asset class | Published method/rate | Source |
|---|---|---|
| General machinery and plant where no special rate applies | 20%, reducing balance | [Annual Allowances, A(3)](https://gov.gg/CHttpHandler.ashx?id=2086&p=0) |
| Motor cars, buses, lorries and motorcycles | 25%, reducing balance | [A(4)(k)](https://gov.gg/CHttpHandler.ashx?id=2086&p=0) |
| Computer hardware | 20%, straight line | [B(vi)](https://gov.gg/CHttpHandler.ashx?id=2086&p=0) |
| Substantial stone/brick/concrete buildings | 1.25%, reducing balance | [A(1)(i)](https://gov.gg/CHttpHandler.ashx?id=2086&p=0) |

These are the rates in the Revenue Service’s currently linked table, read on 27 October 2026; the PDF carries Form 651 (12/01), so its historic form date is not represented as a 2026 enactment date. Match the actual asset class and statutory entitlement before applying a rate. For an established full-year qualifying general-plant tax balance of £40,000, 20% gives £8,000 allowance and £32,000 remaining before other movements. Computer hardware’s 20% applies on its straight-line basis, not to the diminishing balance. No private-use or disposal outcome is inferred from these arithmetic illustrations.

### Losses: current use, carryforward and cessation

Compute a tax loss using the same rules as profits, not merely the accounting loss. Section 133 allows eligible losses between distinct businesses, section 134 between sources of the same class, and section 135 supplies the further current-year set-off order. **Section 133A permits cross-class set-off only where the loss and income bear the same rate.** Track each business, class, rate, accounting period, amount claimed and prior utilisation. An ordinary 0%-class loss is not automatically deductible against 20% rental income. [Sections 133–135 and 147](https://www.guernseylegalresources.gg/CHttpHandler.ashx?fileid=253819).

An unrelieved business loss can be claimed forward under section 139 against profits of the **same business**, from year to year. The provision’s rate-change transition exceptions require the specified historic class/year and continuing-business facts; do not assume that a rate change generally frees all old losses. Sections 141 and 143 prohibit relief beyond the actual loss and double use. Unless another provision sets a different period, section 144 requires the loss-relief claim within six years following the relevant year of charge. Record a valid claim and the surviving balance; do not mistake that claim deadline for a six-year automatic expiry of an already valid same-business carryforward. [Sections 139, 141, 143–144](https://www.guernseylegalresources.gg/CHttpHandler.ashx?fileid=253819).

For example, an established £30,000 same-business, same-rate carried loss against current tax-adjusted profit £50,000 leaves £20,000 chargeable profit and no surviving loss, assuming no other restriction. At 10% that is £2,000 company tax; at 0% the tax remains nil but the loss ledger still changes. The example assumes the class and entitlement rather than proving them from the arithmetic.

Section 142’s cessation carryback is narrower than a general terminal-loss election: permanent discontinuance and a **balancing allowance causing the unrelieved loss** are required. It reaches the business’s two preceding years, later year first, subject to the same-rate condition. Group surrender under section 142A has its own group/Guernsey incorporation-or-PE and overlapping-period conditions, consent and two-year claim deadline; test those separately instead of offsetting another company’s losses through the ordinary ledger. [Sections 142–144](https://www.guernseylegalresources.gg/CHttpHandler.ashx?fileid=253819).

### Ordering relief without losing the loss ledger

Revenue Service practice M24 applies current-year trading losses through sections 133–135, subject to section 133A’s same-rate restriction. Its normal section 135 allocation is proportional across eligible classes, but a taxpayer can request the most advantageous allocation, including to preserve double-tax relief. The practice permits specified charges on income to be used before current-year trading losses, leaving the unused loss for carryforward. **Brought-forward section 139 losses go against that business’s income before charges on income.** Reconcile the claim, ordering election, foreign-tax credit limitation and surviving loss separately; do not treat a discretionary allocation as automatic. [M24, reviewed 30 December 2024](https://gov.gg/CHttpHandler.ashx?id=185346&p=0).

## Deadlines, registration and filing

The **2025 company return is due 30 November 2026**. Annual online filing and accountant-certified accounts are required; audit exemption does not itself remove tax certification. Use the correct return certificate and retain the qualification/authority of the certifying accountant. [Company guidance](https://gov.gg/RevenueService/Companies).

### Statutory assessment payment dates

Subject to the separate withholding/distribution/qualifying-loan routes, section 81(1A) makes tax for 2023 and later years payable in four equal instalments on **15 April, 15 July, 15 October and 15 January of the following year**. For 2026 these are April/July/October 2026 and January 2027. If the relevant assessment is not made before the corresponding **17 March, 16 June, 16 September or 17 December** statutory cut-off, that instalment is due within thirty days of issue of the assessment notice. Reconcile the actual assessments and withholding credits; the annual-return deadline is not an instruction to postpone all payments until filing. Section 81(2) separately makes a penalty payable within thirty days of the communicating order, subject to the Director’s permitted further time. [Section 81](https://www.guernseylegalresources.gg/CHttpHandler.ashx?fileid=253819).

### Registration and first return

Guernsey-formed companies have their tax reference created from Registry information. Foreign-incorporated companies must register with Revenue Service; the presently linked **Form 706 (08/21)** is also used for branches and PEs. It instructs registration by **14 July following the first calendar year requiring a return**; its example is a 2025 return requiring registration by 14 July 2026. Its date is old despite current linkage, so retain the form version and confirm case-specific requirements before reliance. Guernsey-incorporated companies do not complete that foreign-company form. [Company guidance](https://gov.gg/RevenueService/Companies); [Form 706, opening instructions](https://gov.gg/CHttpHandler.ashx?id=183549&p=0).

The ordinary first return covers the incorporation calendar year. The guidance allows the company to confirm in writing that its first accounting year ends in the following year of charge, subject to a maximum **18 months**. Do not omit the first return merely because accounts have not yet been prepared. [Company guidance, company tax return](https://gov.gg/RevenueService/Companies).

### Accounts and return certificates

The guidance lists ICAEW, ICAS, Chartered Accountants Ireland and ACCA membership, or an equivalent qualification approved by the Director, for accounts certification. Audit exemption does not remove this tax requirement. An authorised corporate service provider can submit the return; that authority does not itself establish accounts-certification competence. [Company guidance, company tax return](https://gov.gg/RevenueService/Companies).

Select certificate 1, 2 or 3 from the actual return and facts. Certificate 1 is the residual category. The webpage describes dormant/no-income circumstances for certificate 2 and conditions concerning employees, beneficial members, regulated income, participator loans and section 62AB requests for certificate 3. Its prose/example conditions are not a sufficient replacement for the current certificate wording; this draft does not turn them into an automated eligibility rule. Retain the completed certificate and evidence. [Company guidance, certificate type](https://gov.gg/RevenueService/Companies).

## Penalties and interest

The Revenue Service penalty page states an initial corporate late-return penalty of **£300**, then **£50 per day**. It says no daily penalties are charged if the return is submitted within **30 days of the initial penalty being imposed**; if later, daily penalties run from the initial penalty date. This is not a 30-day extension of the filing deadline. The page's introductory deadline relates to 2023 returns and is stale for current filing dates; use the company page's **30 November 2026** deadline for the **2025** return. [Penalty FAQ, calculation](https://gov.gg/taxpenalties); [company page](https://gov.gg/RevenueService/Companies).

An appeal must be submitted within **30 days of the Order imposing the penalty**. The FAQ says appeal does not suspend penalties: submit the return or evidence of prior submission to stop further daily charges. Retain the final online submission number and confirmation. Late filing, non-filing and inaccurate-return matters require their own analysis; payment surcharges and a complete statutory penalty opinion remain unverified. The personal-allowance £50 restriction on the same webpage is not a corporate exemption. [Penalty FAQ, evidence, appeal and suspension](https://gov.gg/taxpenalties).

**Daily-penalty catch-up.** Assume no successful appeal/reduction and a corporate return submitted after the 30-day concession period, with **35 chargeable days established from the Order and submission dates**. The guidance calculation is £300 + 35 × £50 = **£2,050**, not merely five daily penalties. A return qualifying for the within-30-day concession would retain the £300 initial penalty without daily penalties. This example does not decide inclusive date counting or the Order's validity. [Penalty FAQ](https://gov.gg/taxpenalties).

## Substance and cross-border distributions

Screen the relevant activities and document direction/management, people, premises, expenditure and local core income-generating activities. Pure equity holding and high-risk IP require their own tests. [Economic substance guidance](https://gov.gg/economicsubstance).

### Statutory substance scope

Revenue Service currently links the **2021 Implementation Regulations** as the consolidation of the original rules and partnership amendments. Regulation 40 commenced them on **30 June 2021**, and regulation 38 revoked the listed 2018–2020 instruments. Read the 2021 text rather than combining the old instruments as though all remained independently operative. This source establishes the provisions below; a full search for subsequent amendments remains outstanding. [Regulations 38–40](https://gov.gg/CHttpHandler.ashx?id=141223&p=0); [official source index](https://gov.gg/economicsubstance).

Regulation 1 includes resident companies and the specified exempt companies that would otherwise be resident. It excludes foundations and equivalent foreign bodies, and excludes collective investment vehicles **unless self-managed**. A self-managed collective investment vehicle is treated as carrying on fund management and receiving income from it. Thus tax exemption or a fund label alone is not a substance exemption. The foundation exclusion is specific to these regulations; it does not remove the corporate-tax treatment described above. [Regulation 1](https://gov.gg/CHttpHandler.ashx?id=141223&p=0).

Regulation 3 identifies income from banking, insurance, fund management, financing/leasing, headquarters, shipping and distribution/service centres, with separate IP and pure-equity holding categories. Classification requires the defined activity and income facts. [Regulation 3](https://gov.gg/CHttpHandler.ashx?id=141223&p=0).

### Ordinary relevant activities: evidence for each accounting period

Regulation 4 requires the activity to be directed/managed locally, local core income-generating activity, appropriately qualified people, operating expenditure and physical presence proportionate to the activity. It gives no universal minimum headcount or expenditure safe harbour. Evidence must support each applicable accounting period. [Regulation 4(1)](https://gov.gg/CHttpHandler.ashx?id=141223&p=0).

For direction and management, the governing board must meet in Guernsey sufficiently often for the decisions required; a quorum must be **physically present**. Strategic decisions must be taken at those meetings and recorded in minutes; the board collectively needs the expertise to decide; relevant minutes and records must be kept in Guernsey. A local registered office or a signed resolution alone does not establish all these tests. [Regulation 4(2)](https://gov.gg/CHttpHandler.ashx?id=141223&p=0).

Local outsourcing can satisfy the CIGA requirement only where the activity is performed **in Guernsey** and the body demonstrates adequate supervision. Retain the service agreement, actual work locations, responsible personnel, deliverables and supervision records. An offshore outsourced function is not converted into local CIGA merely by a Guernsey invoice. [Regulation 4(3)](https://gov.gg/CHttpHandler.ashx?id=141223&p=0). The activity map below develops regulation 8; specialist IP rules and a complete subsequent-amendment review remain open.

### Activity classification and the work that produces the income

Apply the **regulation 37 definition first**, then map the actual functions to regulation 8. The list of core income-generating activities is inclusive; it is not a checklist that can replace the actual business facts. The local-performance and supervision conditions above continue to apply. [Regulations 4, 8 and 37](https://gov.gg/CHttpHandler.ashx?id=141223&p=0).

| Relevant activity | Core functions identified in regulation 8 | Suggested evidence to retain |
|---|---|---|
| Banking | Raising funds; managing credit/currency/interest risks; hedging; customer loans/credit/financial services; regulatory capital and reporting | Decision records, risk/treasury records and regulatory filings |
| Insurance | Predicting/calculating risk; insurance/reinsurance; client services | Underwriting, pricing, reinsurance and service records |
| Fund management | Holding/selling investment decisions; risk/reserve calculations; currency/interest and hedging decisions; regulatory/investor reports | Investment committee decisions, portfolio/risk work and reports |
| Financing/leasing | Funding terms; identifying/acquiring leased assets; financing/lease terms and duration; monitoring/revising agreements; risk management | Credit approvals, executed terms, asset records and monitoring |
| Headquarters | Relevant management decisions; expenditure on behalf of group members; coordination of group activities | Authority matrix, management decisions and expenditure/coordination records |
| Shipping | Crew management; hauling/maintenance; delivery tracking; goods-order/delivery decisions; voyage organisation/oversight | Crew, vessel, delivery and voyage-management evidence |
| Distribution/service centre | Transport/storage; inventory; orders; consulting/administrative services | Goods flows, stock/order systems and actual service deliverables |

Legal functions: [regulation 8(a)–(g)](https://gov.gg/CHttpHandler.ashx?id=141223&p=0). The evidence column is a working-paper recommendation, not a prescribed form or guaranteed proof of compliance. Record who performed each material function, where, when, with what authority and under whose supervision. A provider invoice proves a payment; it does not by itself prove where the underlying function occurred.

### Do not overextend the category names

- **Financing/leasing** requires provision of credit facilities for consideration, including interest. It includes the specified separately charged instalment arrangements and treats an assignee of customer credit as providing the facility. Its financial-leasing limb excludes land/interests in land; banking, insurance and fund-management activities are excluded from this category. Do not classify every asset rental as financing/leasing.
- **Distribution/service centre** is a sole/main business involving the specified purchases from non-resident group members for resale, or services to non-resident group members. Ordinary services to unrelated customers do not satisfy that group condition merely because the company calls itself a service centre. The definition excludes the other listed relevant activities.
- **Headquarters** concerns senior management, material-risk assumption/control or substantive risk advice for non-resident group members. Check the exclusions for the other named relevant activities and IP holding. Merely calling a registered office a headquarters does not decide classification.
- **Intra-group** uses the financial-consolidation relationship specified in regulation 37, including the hypothetical publicly traded-equity test. Do not substitute the UK treaty's ownership percentages or assume common directors alone settle this definition.

All four boundaries: [regulation 37](https://gov.gg/CHttpHandler.ashx?id=141223&p=0). Banking, insurance and fund management also use licensing and statutory cross-references. The 2021 document cites older sector statutes; confirm amendments or replacement-law mappings before applying those licence definitions to a 2026 entity.

### IP requires an additional analysis

Regulation 8(h) identifies research/development for patent-type assets and marketing, branding and distribution for marketing intangibles. Its exceptional alternative functions include the stated strategic decisions, risk management and underlying exploitation activity, but that exception is unavailable to high-risk IP bodies. Do not treat board-level risk discussion as a universal substitute for local development or marketing functions. [Regulation 8(h)](https://gov.gg/CHttpHandler.ashx?id=141223&p=0). The specific IP and high-risk requirements follow; full enforcement calculations and subsequent amendments remain to be reconciled before accepting an IP case.

### IP governance and high-risk classification

Regulation 5 applies its own substance requirements to IP bodies: local direction/management and CIGA, with proportionate qualified people, expenditure and physical presence, demonstrated for each applicable accounting period. Its board test requires sufficiently frequent Guernsey meetings, a physically present quorum, locally made and minuted strategic decisions, collective expertise and local records. Periodic decisions of non-resident board members are not counted for the meeting-frequency test. Outsourced CIGA must be performed in Guernsey under adequate supervision. [Regulation 5](https://gov.gg/CHttpHandler.ashx?id=141223&p=0).

For a company, apply the **cumulative** high-risk definition in regulation 37. The body must hold IP that it did not create; have acquired it either from an intra-group person **or** in consideration of funding research/development by another person outside Guernsey; **and** license it to non-resident intra-group persons or otherwise earn income from it through their activities. Retain the creation history, acquisition/funding agreements, group relationship, licence terms and actual income-generating arrangements. Partnership application also needs regulation 2 and is outside this corporate classification. [Regulation 37, high risk IP body](https://gov.gg/CHttpHandler.ashx?id=141223&p=0).

**Classification boundaries:** assuming all other definition conditions are established, purchased group IP licensed to a non-resident group company meets the stated high-risk limbs. Purchase from an unrelated seller does not by itself establish the acquisition limb: check the alternative overseas-R&D funding route. A sale to an unrelated customer does not by itself rule out high-risk status either, because income can result from non-resident group activities facilitating the sale. These are applications of the definition, not findings about an unidentified client's facts. [Regulation 37](https://gov.gg/CHttpHandler.ashx?id=141223&p=0).

### Rebuttal evidence and the return

Regulation 17 presumes that a high-risk IP body does **not** meet the substance requirements, even when relevant CIGA occurs in Guernsey. The body must provide evidence satisfying the Director that the presumption is rebutted. A local office, board minutes or a service-provider invoice alone is not the complete evidential case. [Regulation 17(1)–(2)](https://gov.gg/CHttpHandler.ashx?id=141223&p=0).

Build the evidence file around each statutory item:

- Current **and historical** high control over the IP's development, exploitation, maintenance, enhancement and protection, exercised by adequate qualified full-time employees permanently resident and working in Guernsey.
- Detailed business plans explaining the commercial rationale for holding the IP in Guernsey.
- Employee experience, contract type, qualifications and duration of employment.
- Evidence that the body's decisions take place within Guernsey.
- Any additional information required by the Director.

These are regulation 17(2)'s evidence categories, not an automatic approval checklist. The ordinary outsourcing permission does not erase the high-risk employee/control evidence requirement. [Regulations 5(3) and 17(2)](https://gov.gg/CHttpHandler.ashx?id=141223&p=0).

For each applicable regulation 9 return, regulation 17(3) requires either a declaration that regulation **5(1)(b)** is met, with sufficient rebuttal evidence included or accompanying it, **or** a declaration that the body cannot rebut the presumption and that 5(1)(b) is not met. Do not reduce this to an unsupported compliance tick. The provision refers to company periods beginning after **31 December 2018**, with the separate partnership commencement cross-reference; it is not a new universal filing deadline. [Regulation 17(3)](https://gov.gg/CHttpHandler.ashx?id=141223&p=0).

Regulation 10 requires the Director to disclose high-risk IP information to a competent authority under the applicable approved international agreement or international tax measure for spontaneous exchange, **whether or not substance requirements are met**. Successful rebuttal therefore does not remove that exchange provision. Regulation 17(4) also modifies enforcement: regulation 14's third-default-period references become second-period references and regulation 15's fourth-period references become third-period references. The company penalty and appeal provisions are mapped below, subject to their stated gaps; do not use the ordinary corporate late-return penalty example below as a substance penalty calculation. [Regulations 10 and 17(4)](https://gov.gg/CHttpHandler.ashx?id=141223&p=0).

### Substance default notices, penalties and appeals

The following maps the **2021 text**, whose subsequent-amendment review remains open. Regulations 13–15 operate following the regulation 9 return and the Director's opinion that applicable substance requirements were not met. Their monetary figures are **ceilings**, not automatic invoices. Keep the notice and the accounting-period history; do not count isolated failures across non-consecutive periods as one continuous escalation. [Regulations 13–15](https://gov.gg/CHttpHandler.ashx?id=141223&p=0).

| Statutory stage for a company | Ordinary body | High-risk IP body | Penalty ceiling in the cited provision |
|---|---|---|---|
| Regulation 13 | First accounting period of default, as defined in 13(4) | Same first-period provision | £10,000 |
| Regulation 14 | Third consecutive accounting period of default | Second consecutive accounting period of default | £50,000 |
| Regulation 15 | Fourth and subsequent consecutive accounting periods of default | Third and subsequent consecutive accounting periods of default | £100,000 for the relevant period |

Sources: [regulations 13(1)/(4), 14(1)/(4), 15(1)/(4)/(5), with high-risk substitutions in 17(4)](https://gov.gg/CHttpHandler.ashx?id=141223&p=0). The ordinary text labels regulation 14 as the **third**, not second, default period. This table does not invent an ordinary second-period amount or declare that period penalty-free. Reconcile that treatment and any later amendments before calculating a client's exposure. The first-period definition in 13(4) also needs the actual preceding-period scope/compliance facts; it is not simply the first notice received.

For companies registered under the stated Guernsey or Alderney company laws, regulations 13 and 14 permit a Registrar notice for striking-off purposes if the Director decides there is no realistic possibility of compliance. At the regulation 15 stage the stated Registrar notice is mandatory. **A notice to the Registrar is not itself completed dissolution**; the applicable company-law procedure must also be checked. Each stage also provides for information exchange under the specified international agreement/measure framework. [Regulations 13(1), 14(1) and 15(1)](https://gov.gg/CHttpHandler.ashx?id=141223&p=0).

The penalty notice must state the grounds, identify the applicable and unmet substance requirements, explain the sanctions and set out applicable appeal rights. It can also require further documents/information. Build the response around those identified failures and retain proof of submission. [Regulations 13(2)–(3), 14(2)–(3) and 15(2)–(3)](https://gov.gg/CHttpHandler.ashx?id=141223&p=0).

Regulation 16's **£150,000 enhanced penalty** concerns specified non-registered partnerships' subsequent default periods, not companies. Regulation 18 separately imports section 193 penalties for missing/false information, in addition to substance penalties. The current section 193 amounts and machinery have not been established here; neither the table nor the ordinary late-return example is a total exposure estimate. [Regulations 16 and 18](https://gov.gg/CHttpHandler.ashx?id=141223&p=0).

For a penalty under these regulations, regulation 19 provides an appeal to the Tribunal (now the **Guernsey Revenue Service Tribunal**; the 2021 text uses its former name, **Guernsey Tax Tribunal**) by written notice **to the Director**, stating grounds, within **30 days beginning on the date of the notice imposing the penalty**. Do not substitute receipt date or the accounting-period end. Late admission requires the Tribunal to be satisfied that exceptional circumstances prevented timely notice; it is not an automatic extension. [Regulation 19(1)–(2)](https://gov.gg/CHttpHandler.ashx?id=141223&p=0). The name changed on 1 January 2022. [Tribunal guidance, page 4, footnote 1](https://gov.gg/CHttpHandler.ashx?id=122165&p=0).

The sole stated grounds are that the body is outside regulation 3's substance scope, complies with its applicable requirements, or is not subject to the penalty under the regulations. Regulation 19(4) permits confirmation or annulment; it does not state a general power to reduce a penalty because it is unaffordable. Sections 76 and 79 are excluded, while sections 77, 78, 78A and 80 apply through the stated cross-reference. Full procedural rules, onward appeal and any effect on collection require those current provisions; this draft does not assume that filing an appeal suspends enforcement. [Regulation 19(3)–(7)](https://gov.gg/CHttpHandler.ashx?id=141223&p=0).

**Boundary applications:** for established consecutive third-period defaults, an ordinary company reaches regulation 14's £50,000 ceiling while a high-risk IP company reaches regulation 15's £100,000 ceiling. Neither is necessarily charged the maximum. A company outside the specified Guernsey/Alderney registration categories is not shown by this text alone to receive the same Registrar notice, even if its substance penalty applies. A missed appeal deadline requires the exceptional-circumstances application rather than an invented fresh 30-day period. [Regulations 14, 15, 17 and 19](https://gov.gg/CHttpHandler.ashx?id=141223&p=0).

### Responding to a substance inspection

Regulation 20 permits entry to business premises and inspection for the specified regulation 9 purposes during normal business hours or another reasonable time. There are **three alternative entry routes**: occupier consent; at least **seven days’ written notice** specifying when entry/inspection will take place; or Bailiff approval under regulation 21 with regulation 22 notice. Seven days is therefore not a universal minimum where another route applies. For approved entry, notice goes to the occupier if present, otherwise someone apparently in charge, otherwise a prominent place at the premises. [Regulations 20–22](https://gov.gg/CHttpHandler.ashx?id=141223&p=0).

The regulation 20 inspection power excludes parts used **solely as a dwelling**, legally privileged items within the stated statutory definition, and documents concerning conduct of a tax appeal. Items held to further a criminal purpose are excluded from the privilege protection. Do not assume that an entire mixed home/business property is exempt, or that every accountant-client record is legally privileged. Identify the actual protected material and obtain advice on contested scope. [Regulation 20(3), including its statutory privilege cross-reference](https://gov.gg/CHttpHandler.ashx?id=141223&p=0).

For entry approved by the Bailiff, regulation 23 permits reasonable assistance requirements directed at the specified investigated person, tax adviser, relevant workers and persons concerned with document systems or storage **who are present on the premises**. This particular power does not itself establish a duty for an absent adviser or remote worker. This expressly reaches electronically accessible records. The Director must give the person written information that contravening the assistance requirement is an offence. Do not transpose this particular assistance power to all consent/notice inspections without checking the applicable authority. [Regulation 23](https://gov.gg/CHttpHandler.ashx?id=141223&p=0).

Regulation 24 permits copies/extracts, removal at a reasonable time and retention for a reasonable period, preservation measures, explanations and enquiries about missing documents. Electronic records can be required in a portable, visible/legible form or a form readily convertible to it. The owner can request a **receipt** for removed records and, if reasonably needed for an immediate purpose while the Director retains the original, a **free copy**. Loss/damage before return attracts compensation for reasonable replacement/repair costs. This is not a promise of compensation for every claimed business loss. [Regulation 24](https://gov.gg/CHttpHandler.ashx?id=141223&p=0).

**Suggested response record:** log the authority and entry route, notice/approval, scope, staff involved, documents requested/provided/removed, any privilege issue, receipt/copy requests and follow-up dates. Preserve originals and electronic records. These are working-paper recommendations, not an additional statutory form or an invented retention period.

Regulation 25 makes obstruction or non-compliance without reasonable excuse an offence, with its fine linked to the current uniform scale. Regulation 26 separately imports falsification and related provisions of the Income Tax Law. Regulation 27 can make directors and other specified officers personally liable where the offence is proved to involve their consent, connivance or neglect; office-holding alone is not that proof. Current fine-scale amounts and the full incorporated provisions remain unverified here, so do not estimate criminal exposure from the civil penalty table. [Regulations 25–27](https://gov.gg/CHttpHandler.ashx?id=141223&p=0).

### Pure-equity holdings and periods without income

The regulation 37 definition is narrower than ownership of any investment portfolio. It requires the specified legal-person status, a primary function of acquiring/holding shares or equitable interests, **no commercial activity**, and one of three control routes: majority voting rights; membership plus the right to appoint/remove a majority of the governing board; or membership plus an agreement giving sole control of a majority of votes. A minority portfolio is not automatically in this definition. [Regulation 37, pure equity holding body](https://gov.gg/CHttpHandler.ashx?id=141223&p=0).

For a qualifying pure-equity holding body, regulation 6 requires compliance with applicable company/partnership legislation and adequate people and physical presence in Guernsey proportionate to holding/managing the interests. Do not impose the entire ordinary regulation 4 test as though it were identical, or infer that reduced requirements mean no requirements. [Regulation 6](https://gov.gg/CHttpHandler.ashx?id=141223&p=0).

Regulation 7 removes the requirements in **regulations 4–6** for a period with **no income from the relevant business/activity**. It does not use a zero-taxable-profit or loss test and does not repeal regulation 9 reporting. For example, activity receipts fully offset by expenses are not established to be 'no income' merely because the profit is nil. Preserve the income analysis and respond to required reporting notices. [Regulations 7 and 9](https://gov.gg/CHttpHandler.ashx?id=141223&p=0).

Regulation 9 distinguishes reporting commencement for companies from partnerships and permits Director notices to set form, timing and electronic submission requirements. This corporate Guide does not apply a single company start date to all partnerships or infer a universal standalone substance-return deadline. [Regulation 9](https://gov.gg/CHttpHandler.ashx?id=141223&p=0). The official page also records withdrawal of temporary COVID-19 guidance for accounting periods commencing on or after **1 November 2022**; do not apply that historic relief to ordinary 2026 periods. [Official guidance index](https://gov.gg/economicsubstance).

### A verified residence amendment, with the remaining gap explicit

Regulation 30 replaced section 4(1)(a) with a test covering control in Guernsey **or** central management and control in Guernsey in the year of charge, and changed 'incorporated' to 'registered' in section 4(1)(b). This is direct evidence of that amendment, not the complete current section 4 or its non-residence exclusions. The complete current section 4 decision is now supplied above; retain this regulation as its amendment history rather than a competing test. [Regulation 30](https://gov.gg/CHttpHandler.ashx?id=141223&p=0).

A distribution from income taxed below 20% to a Guernsey-resident beneficial member requires a separate tax/reporting calculation. Keep evidence of non-residence before treating a payment to a non-resident member as outside the stated distribution charge. Loans are not automatically exempt alternatives. [Company distributions and qualifying loans](https://gov.gg/RevenueService/Companies).

### Distributions: identify the income pool and beneficial recipient

Sections 57–61, 62AA–62D and 81B require a separate distribution ledger. Trace the actual and beneficial recipient through intervening companies, trusts and arrangements; retain any section 62AB request for treatment at the receiving-company level. Section 81B(17) treats a distribution as coming from **undistributed income first**, then other income and capital only to the extent that pool is insufficient. Keep profits by source, tax rate, year, previous distributions and credits. A label such as capital repayment does not override that ordering. Tax-cap cases also require the underlying-income year allocation under section 39D, explained in [practice C44](https://gov.gg/CHttpHandler.ashx?id=191282&p=0). [Income Tax Law, ss57–62D and 81B](https://www.guernseylegalresources.gg/CHttpHandler.ashx?fileid=253819).

For an individual recipient, section 81B(3)(a) specifies deduction at the **difference between the company rate on the underlying income and the individual standard rate**; the section 58 gross income and section 61 credit include company tax plus the distribution deduction as directed there. For a company recipient the statutory deduction rate is the company standard rate, subject to beneficial-recipient tracing. Use the underlying pool and actual certificate; do not simply tax the same net cash a second time at 20%. A distribution from an established 0%-taxed pool with £10,000 gross income gives £2,000 deduction and £8,000 cash to a resident individual before that person’s final assessment and credits. Mixed-rate, capped and exempt-company pools require their separate statutory adjustments. Sections 59 and 81B(9) impose a net-payment gross-up where required deduction was omitted; an agreement to pay without deduction is void under 81B(11). [Sections 58–61 and 81B(3)–(11)](https://www.guernseylegalresources.gg/CHttpHandler.ashx?fileid=253819).

Evidence establishing a non-resident individual’s disregarded distribution income is required under sections 47B–47E; this is not a universal exemption for every recipient or all Guernsey income. Practice C37 distinguishes resident-member withholding, non-resident disregarded income and property-company tax credits. Section 61 restricts refunds until the recipient’s current and earlier liabilities are determined; do not promise immediate repayment of a credit from an interim assessment. [Sections 47B–47E and 61](https://www.guernseylegalresources.gg/CHttpHandler.ashx?fileid=253819); [C37, updated 1 September 2025](https://gov.gg/CHttpHandler.ashx?id=193346&p=0).

Give the recipient the section 60 certificate showing gross amount, tax rate/amount and net cash. The quarterly return identifies recipient name/address, distribution date/amount and tax date/amount. Return and payment are due **within 15 days after 31 March, 30 June, 30 September or 31 December**, including reportable distributions on which no tax was deducted. Keep the deduction record, certificate and payment reconciliation. The 2008 regulations allow a Director’s written variation/no-deduction direction and particular collection arrangements; obtain that direction rather than making a unilateral variation. [Sections 60, 81B(12)–(13), 209](https://www.guernseylegalresources.gg/CHttpHandler.ashx?fileid=253819); [Tax Deductions from Company Distributions Regulations 2008, rr1–6](https://www.guernseylegalresources.gg/CHttpHandler.ashx?fileid=267621).

### Qualifying loans: separate charge, exemptions and repayment claim

Section 66A covers a company with income taxed below the higher of the company higher rate and individual standard rate lending to a participator, officer, connected person or a third party for their benefit. Its connected-person and attribution rules can move the charge to the underlying person; an unrelated name on the loan agreement does not settle the test. A loan can become qualifying when the borrower’s status later changes. It is not deductible in computing company profits. [Section 66A(1)–(8)](https://www.guernseylegalresources.gg/CHttpHandler.ashx?fileid=253819).

Check the statutory exceptions separately: a licensed money-lender’s normal-commercial-rate loan; ordinary-course goods/services credit unless it exceeds six months or the normal customer credit period; and qualifying officer/employee loans unless the borrower and connected persons control more than 5% of the relevant issued share capital. Regulation 2 of the 2008 No.3 exemptions also excludes a loan where aggregate outstanding loans by the company/connected lenders to the borrower/connected persons **do not exceed £2,500**. Aggregate first; this is not a £2,500 deduction from a larger loan. **The old general commercial-interest exemption was repealed from 1 January 2014**: charging Bank Rate plus 2% alone does not exempt an ordinary shareholder loan in 2026. Tax actually paid under section 66C and tax actually deducted under section 81B are excluded from a further qualifying-loan charge under regulations 3–4, with the stated deemed-deduction exception. [Section 66A(4)](https://www.guernseylegalresources.gg/CHttpHandler.ashx?fileid=253819); [2008 No.3 rr2–4](https://www.guernseylegalresources.gg/CHttpHandler.ashx?fileid=267761), [2012 amendment r1](https://www.guernseylegalresources.gg/CHttpHandler.ashx?fileid=266936), [2013 amendment rr1,3](https://www.guernseylegalresources.gg/CHttpHandler.ashx?fileid=266902).

Section 66C treats the qualifying advance as **net of tax** in the quarter it becomes qualifying. The applicable rate is the individual standard rate for an individual borrower or company higher rate for a company borrower, both 20% for 2026: gross income = net advance / 0.80 and tax = gross income × 20%. Report borrower/address, advance, tax, repayments and quarter-end outstanding balance. Return and company payment are due within 15 days after the relevant calendar-quarter end. [Section 66C and Fifth Schedule](https://www.guernseylegalresources.gg/CHttpHandler.ashx?fileid=253819).

If repaid within six years, section 66B permits **the person who paid the tax** to claim the tax attributable to the repaid portion, **within 15 days after the reporting date for that repayment quarter**. Apply repayments to earlier qualifying loans, including the specified pre-2008 loans, first. A write-off or balance not repaid within six years produces the section 66B deemed-income/credit treatment; it does not create a second charge or a second section 81B deduction where section 66C tax was already paid. Keep loan-by-loan dates, net/gross amounts, tax payer and timely claim evidence. [Section 66B](https://www.guernseylegalresources.gg/CHttpHandler.ashx?fileid=253819).

### Loan direction matters for 2026 repayments

From **1 January 2026**, section 62AA(1)(b) includes a company’s repayment of sums advanced by a member or a person connected to a member in the distribution definition. The same paragraph excludes repayment of a **commercial loan made to a trading company**, with definitions in section 62AA(1)(b)(i)–(ii): terms must be those that would apply between independent parties, interest must be at a commercial rate, and the company must derive income from business within the definition. Record original lender, member connection, advance and repayment, business status, agreement and comparable terms. This direction is the company repaying shareholder borrowing; it is distinct from a company receiving repayment of its qualifying loan. Do not transfer the section 66B six-year repayment claim to it. [Section 62AA, including Budget Ordinance 2025 s6 commencement note](https://www.guernseylegalresources.gg/CHttpHandler.ashx?fileid=253819).

### Migration and continuing source income

Revenue Service practice C52, updated **23 July 2026**, explains that accepted section 4(2) non-residence applies for the whole year, while Guernsey-source income remains subject to sections 47G–47H. Use Form 707, foreign tax-residence evidence and the Director’s acceptance. If section 4(2) is not established, section 5(4) governs overseas income on cessation: distinguish a source held throughout the year, a source ceased before departure and a new source acquired before departure; do not impose one blanket daily fraction on all income. Section 6’s accounting-period basis still applies to business income. [C52, company residence and arrivals/departures](https://gov.gg/CHttpHandler.ashx?id=202425&p=0); [sections 4–6](https://www.guernseylegalresources.gg/CHttpHandler.ashx?fileid=253819).

A company migrating its registration submits **Form 708** for a Revenue Service letter to the Registrar. C52 requires review of outstanding tax and reporting obligations, including CRS, CbCR and substance. A non-calendar accounting year can require the last accounting period ending in the year plus the appropriate proportion of the next period up to migration; reconcile this with each source’s statutory treatment. Preserve the letter and continuing obligations. This ordinary income and administrative method is not a statement that unrealised gains or an actual disposal always have a nil exit charge. [C52, migration](https://gov.gg/CHttpHandler.ashx?id=202425&p=0).

### Reporting and exit are separate screens

Form 707 asks about CbCR, Pillar Two, FATCA, CRS and CARF. These questions do not establish that every applicant is subject to each regime, nor that a non-resident acceptance cancels them. Determine each regime's scope separately. Likewise, the company page's migration procedure requires a Revenue Service no-objection letter for the Registrar; it does not establish that migration has no tax cost. [Form 707, section 5](https://gov.gg/CHttpHandler.ashx?id=118096&p=0); [company guidance, migration](https://gov.gg/RevenueService/Companies).

## Two worked examples that recompute

**Business base, allowance and carried loss.** A full-year company’s independently established intermediate-rate business has accounting profit £200,000 after £10,000 capital expenditure charged as a current expense. There are no other adjustments. Its qualifying general-plant tax balance is £40,000, with no disposal/private use and a full 20% reducing-balance allowance. A valid unused £30,000 same-business, same-rate loss is brought forward. Add back £10,000, deduct £8,000 allowance and £30,000 loss: taxable profit **£172,000**, tax **£17,200**. Rates/classification and qualifying ownership/use are facts requiring evidence; this example does not establish them by arithmetic. [Sections 7, 84–86, 139 and Fifth Schedule](https://www.guernseylegalresources.gg/CHttpHandler.ashx?fileid=253819); [allowance table A(3)](https://gov.gg/CHttpHandler.ashx?id=2086&p=0).

```oa-check
name: Guernsey ordinary corporate base allowance and loss
inputs:
  accounting_profit: 200000
  capital_expensed: 10000
  qualifying_plant_balance: 40000
  carried_same_business_loss: 30000
steps:
  - capital_allowance: qualifying_plant_balance * 0.20
  - profit_before_loss: accounting_profit + capital_expensed - capital_allowance
  - loss_used: min(carried_same_business_loss, profit_before_loss)
  - taxable_profit: max(0, profit_before_loss - loss_used)
  - company_tax: taxable_profit * 0.10
  - loss_remaining: carried_same_business_loss - loss_used
expected:
  capital_allowance: 8000
  profit_before_loss: 202000
  loss_used: 30000
  taxable_profit: 172000
  company_tax: 17200
  loss_remaining: 0
```

**Qualifying loan and timely partial repayment.** A 0%-income company advances £8,000 cash to its resident individual participator. There are no earlier/connected loans, the £2,500 aggregate exemption is exceeded, and no other exception applies. The £8,000 is net: gross income **£10,000**, section 66C tax **£2,000**. A £3,000 repayment within six years, allocated to this loan, supports a **£750** tax repayment to the original tax payer if claimed within 15 days after that repayment quarter end. £5,000 net principal remains; no second loan-tax charge is created by the £2,000 tax payment. [Sections 66A–66C](https://www.guernseylegalresources.gg/CHttpHandler.ashx?fileid=253819); [2008 exemptions rr2–3](https://www.guernseylegalresources.gg/CHttpHandler.ashx?fileid=267761).

```oa-check
name: Guernsey qualifying loan net gross-up and partial repayment
inputs:
  net_advance: 8000
  tax_rate: 0.20
  timely_principal_repayment: 3000
steps:
  - gross_income: net_advance / (1 - tax_rate)
  - loan_tax: gross_income * tax_rate
  - refundable_tax: loan_tax * timely_principal_repayment / net_advance
  - principal_remaining: net_advance - timely_principal_repayment
  - tax_remaining: loan_tax - refundable_tax
expected:
  gross_income: 10000
  loan_tax: 2000
  refundable_tax: 750
  principal_remaining: 5000
  tax_remaining: 1250
```

The original mixed-income £7,000 and corporate daily-penalty £2,050 explanations remain in their main rate/penalty sections, and the original executable controls remain in [supplementary research](../../research/guernsey-2026/corporate-original-controls.md). The penalty method above continues to apply; replacing its main control does not remove that topic.

## Ask the client first

- Where is the company incorporated, managed and controlled, and what residence evidence exists?
- What are the incorporation date, accounting period and first-return arrangements?
- Which income streams, property interests and regulated activities exist?
- Who are the beneficial members, and what distributions/loans have been made?
- What local staff, relevant substance activities and group-reporting obligations exist?
- What returns, certificates, payments and penalty orders have already been issued or submitted?
