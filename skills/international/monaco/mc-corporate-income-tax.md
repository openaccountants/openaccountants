# Monaco business profits tax (ISB)

> **Source-cited draft; partial scope.** Checked 24 October 2026. No named Monaco professional has reviewed this Guide. It produces a working paper, not a filing or a final tax position. Read the [country gap register](README.md) before use.

## Who this applies to

Tax year 2026, ordinary Monaco impôt sur les bénéfices (ISB). Despite this inherited filename, the tax applies to industrial or commercial enterprises **whatever their legal form**, including an individual proprietor. Article 1(1)(a) brings an enterprise operating in Monaco into scope when **at least 25%** of its turnover arises from operations outside Monaco, directly or through an intermediary. Exactly 25% is inside the statutory boundary. A separate article 1(1)(b) limb covers companies receiving specified patent, trademark, manufacturing-process or literary/artistic rights income. Do not apply the turnover gate to that separate limb. [Ordinance 3.152, arts. 1–4](https://legimonaco.mc/tnc/ordonnance/1964/03-19-3.152).

Article 3 tests goods destinations and where services, rights or rented property are used. A customer's address alone is insufficient. On-site retail cash sales in Monaco are expressly excluded from foreign sales under that article. A government summary uses “more than 25%”; this Guide follows the inclusive statutory wording.

## The base

Build an accounting-to-tax reconciliation, not 25% of receipts. Articles 6–9 address the period, net business result, expenses, depreciation and provisions. Check all special deduction limits separately. Article 9 restricts shareholder interest and financial expenses; this draft does not compute those limits. Article 12 requires both independence and genuine payment evidence for specified fees, royalties and commissions to Monaco persons. Article 13 requires effective, non-excessive executive work and applies additional caps. The current small-turnover executive scale has not been established here. Article 14 requires normal foreign commercial and financial conditions. [Arts. 6–14](https://legimonaco.mc/tnc/ordonnance/1964/03-19-3.152).

Separate foreign-operation net profits **and losses** excluded by article 1(2): foreign permanent establishments, habitual complete business cycles abroad and qualifying foreign representatives. Do not remove all foreign-customer profit. Article 7 ordinary loss use is the minimum of available loss, positive profit and the ceiling above; carry the unused loss forward. Insolvency increases, carryback under article 7 bis and reorganisation conditions require separate review. [Arts. 1(2), 7–7 bis](https://legimonaco.mc/tnc/ordonnance/1964/03-19-3.152).

New-activity relief runs through the end of the twenty-third month following the creation month, then three successive twelve-month periods with taxable fractions of one quarter, one half and three quarters. Timely declaration is a condition. Banking, finance, insurance, property management/rental, corporate ownership above the statutory limit (including deemed indirect ownership) and reconstruction, extension or takeover exclusions must be checked. Do not describe this as an automatic two-year exemption for every new company. [Arts. 1–3](https://legimonaco.mc/tnc/ordonnance/1991/10-17-10.324).

## Deadlines and filing

File the article 23 declaration and supporting reconciliations with the Direction des Services Fiscaux (DSF) within three months after year end. If no financial year closes in a calendar year, file before 1 April of the following year. Article 23 lists turnover, remuneration, accounts, adjustments and foreign-tax evidence. The current form and live submission route have not been tested. [Art. 23](https://legimonaco.mc/tnc/ordonnance/1964/03-19-3.152).

Ordinary advances fall in February, May, August and November. Each is one fifth of tax on the statutory reference result, with annualisation for non-twelve-month periods and the prior-result/true-up rule where the latest declaration is not yet due. The balance is due by the declaration deadline. A reduction declaration has its own fifteen-day advance notice and error conditions; do not simply stop payments. Cessation has a different return timetable. [Arts. 29, 31–32](https://legimonaco.mc/tnc/ordonnance/1964/03-19-3.152).

## Penalties and interest

Article 34 sets a EUR 15–75 late-results-declaration fine and EUR 1–15 for specified missing information. Article 35's late-payment rule, where the required declarations/formalities are complete, is 3% if paid within the month after the due date, plus 1% for each additional month or fraction. Other good-faith contraventions and assessment adjustments can attract penalties up to 150% of omitted or jeopardised tax; article 37 prescribes a fine equal to four times the tax for bad faith or fraud, with further consequences. These are different triggers, not a single penalty to apply to every late return. [Arts. 34–37](https://legimonaco.mc/tnc/ordonnance/1964/03-19-3.152).

## Cross-border points

Article 22 permits evidenced foreign-income-tax credit against Monaco tax on the same income, with gross-up. Article 15 excludes credit on the participation income removed under that provision. Establish entity, holding, income and treaty conditions first. See [cross-border](mc-cross-border.md) and [transfer pricing](mc-transfer-pricing.md). Large-group minimum-tax enactment and implementation remain an open research item; the retrieved project 1.129 is not proof of operative law. [Arts. 15, 22–23](https://legimonaco.mc/tnc/ordonnance/1964/03-19-3.152); [Project 1.129](https://legimonaco.mc/projet/1.129).

## Two worked examples that recompute

### Ordinary business and advances

An in-scope enterprise has EUR 100,000 of reconciled taxable profit, no relief, loss or credit. ISB is EUR 25,000. Assume the correctly established reference profit for all four advances is EUR 80,000: reference tax EUR 20,000, each advance EUR 4,000, total EUR 16,000, balance EUR 9,000. Articles 21 and 31 control; the example assumes no interim reference change.

```oa-check
name: Ordinary ISB and four unchanged-reference advances
inputs:
  profit: 100000
  reference_profit: 80000
steps:
  - tax: profit * 0.25
  - reference_tax: reference_profit * 0.25
  - advance: reference_tax / 5
  - paid: advance * 4
  - balance: tax - paid
expected:
  tax: 25000
  advance: 4000
  balance: 9000
```

### Loss ceiling edge

An in-scope business has EUR 2,000,000 profit before ordinary carried losses and EUR 2,000,000 eligible losses. The ceiling is EUR 1,500,000, leaving taxable profit EUR 500,000, ISB EUR 125,000 and EUR 500,000 losses carried forward. This assumes no special insolvency increase or continuity restriction. Article 7 supplies the ceiling.

```oa-check
name: Ordinary loss ceiling above EUR one million
inputs:
  profit: 2000000
  loss: 2000000
steps:
  - ceiling: 1000000 + max(0, profit - 1000000) * 0.5
  - used: min(loss, profit, ceiling)
  - taxable: profit - used
  - tax: taxable * 0.25
  - remaining_loss: loss - used
expected:
  used: 1500000
  tax: 125000
  remaining_loss: 500000
```

## Ask the client first

- What is the legal form, activity, rights income and financial year end?
- Where are goods delivered and services or rights used, and what is the evidenced turnover split?
- What foreign operations, group payments and director remuneration exist?
- What losses, relief conditions, foreign taxes and prior advances are documented?
- What is the group's size and structure for the unresolved large-group rules?
