# Statutory audit workflow v1.0

## What this file is

This is the **shared workflow base** for statutory audit content skills. It supplies an ISA-based working-paper method, not a country audit exemption determination. The country threshold matrix below separates confirmed UK figures from held country questions. It contains:

- The audit lifecycle runbook aligned to ISA (IAASB)
- The country audit threshold matrix (when an audit is required)
- The engagement acceptance and continuance protocol
- The ISA risk assessment and significance-determination framework
- The reviewer-oriented audit file structure
- The opinion formulation decision tree
- The 21 self-checks
- The global refusal catalogue
- The slot contract for country statutory audit skills

**Use this base with the country audit job and applicable adopted standards.** This guide never signs an opinion or replaces a licensed auditor's judgement.

## Quick reference and standard dates

The reporting anchor is **ISA 700 (Revised), Forming an Opinion and Reporting on Financial Statements**, effective for audits of periods ending on or after **15 December 2016**. This is the metadata anchor, not a common commencement date for every ISA. Source reading is dated 14 November 2026. Apply the effective version of every other relevant standard, national adoption and any permitted early adoption to the actual engagement.

The [2026 IAASB handbook contents](https://www.iaasb.org/publications/2026-handbook-international-quality-management-auditing-review-sustainability-and-other-assurance) identify standards becoming effective in December 2026. Do not treat publication as immediate commencement. [ISA 570 (Revised 2024)](https://www.iaasb.org/publications/isa-570-revised-2024-going-concern) applies to audits of financial statements for periods beginning on or after 15 December 2026, subject to local adoption. The earlier ISA 570 assessment rule below must not be silently replaced for an earlier period. ISA 240 revisions and PIE-related amendments require their own effective-version check.

## Ask the client first

- What are the jurisdiction, legal form, group structure, public-interest/listing status and reporting period?
- Is an audit required by statute, member demand, constitution, regulator, financing agreement or a voluntary appointment, and which exemptions are potentially available?
- Which accounting framework and auditing/ethical standards, editions, national additions and early-adoption decisions apply?
- Who is responsible for the financial statements, engagement acceptance, independence, component work and any required engagement quality review?
- What are the opening balances, risk evidence, controls, estimates, going-concern assessment, subsequent events and uncorrected misstatements?
- What are the report, approval, issue, filing and documentation dates, restrictions on evidence and unresolved integrity concerns?

## The method, step by step

1. Determine the statutory/contractual audit obligation and applicable standards; document each exemption condition separately.
2. Establish acceptance preconditions, ethical compliance, independence, competence, resources and written engagement terms.
3. Understand the entity and controls, assess risks, set justified materiality and plan responses to the assertions and significant risks.
4. Perform and document the required controls/substantive work, specific ISA procedures, component work and follow-up of contradictory evidence.
5. Resolve estimates, misstatements, going concern, subsequent events, representations and the required engagement quality review before dating the report.
6. Form and document the opinion using the nature, materiality and pervasiveness of the issue; separately consider going-concern reporting, KAMs and other information.
7. Communicate, obtain approval for issue, file under local law and assemble/retain the final file. Later administrative assembly does not permit completing missing pre-report audit work.

The detailed lifecycle, opinion paths, refusal catalogue and country handoff below implement this sequence. Paragraph citations refer to the [public IAASB handbook text](https://www.iaasb.org/publications/2025-handbook-international-quality-management-auditing-review-other-assurance-and-related-services), with the later effective-date boundary checked against the current handbook announcement.

## Section 1 :  Audit lifecycle (ISA-aligned)

### Phase 1 :  Engagement acceptance / continuance (ISA 220, 220R, 210)

**Before accepting or continuing tests**  _(ISA 220, 220R, 210)_

| Test | Reference |
| --- | --- |
| Independence (firm-level + engagement team) | Applicable ethical/independence standards, including the adopted IESBA Code and national additions; ISA 220 (Revised) |
| Competence to perform the audit | ISA 220 (Revised), competence and resource requirements |
| Acceptance approval by appropriate partner | Firm acceptance policies; ISA 220 (Revised) |
| Terms agreed and recorded in a letter or suitable written agreement, subject to the prescribed-law exception | ISA 210 paragraphs 9-11 |
| Predecessor auditor communication | Initial-engagement/opening-balance work under ISA 510; predecessor access/communication under the applicable ethical and local requirements |
| Audit fee not impaired (no contingent fees on the audit opinion) | Applicable ethical fee/contingent-fee provisions; do not assume a section number replaces the local rule |

### Phase 2 :  Risk assessment (ISA 315 (Revised 2019))

**[T1] Required activities:**
- Understand the entity, its environment, and its system of internal control
- Identify and assess Risks of Material Misstatement (RMM) at financial statement and assertion levels
- Determine significant risks
- Identify controls relevant to the audit

**Significant risks:** ISA 330 paragraph 21 requires specifically responsive substantive procedures; where the response consists only of substantive procedures, include tests of details. Select any control testing required by the assessed risks and proposed reliance; do not infer a universal procedure mix from a risk label.

### Phase 3 :  Audit plan and materiality (ISA 320, 330)

- **Overall materiality:** document the benchmark, qualitative factors and justified amount for the financial statements as a whole; no percentage in this global guide is a mandatory ISA rate.
- **Performance materiality:** determine an amount below overall materiality to address aggregation risk, using the engagement facts rather than a fixed universal proportion.
- **Specific materiality:** identify transaction classes, balances or disclosures for which a lower amount could influence users, including relevant related-party or executive disclosures.
- **Clearly trivial items:** establish and document the threshold and qualitative considerations. "Clearly trivial" is not synonymous with "not material". Reassess materiality when evidence or actual results change.

Authority: ISA 320 paragraphs 10-14 and application material; ISA 450 paragraphs 5, 10-11 and A2-A4 in the [IAASB handbook](https://www.iaasb.org/publications/2025-handbook-international-quality-management-auditing-review-other-assurance-and-related-services). The previous benchmark, performance-materiality and trivial-item percentages are held as unsupported global defaults in Sources.

### Phase 4 :  Fieldwork / procedures (ISA 330, 500, 501-540)

- **Audit evidence requirement** :  Audit evidence must be sufficient (quantity) and appropriate (relevance + reliability). Per assertion (existence, completeness, valuation, rights/obligations, classification, presentation/disclosure, accuracy, cut-off), select procedures from the procedures table.  _(ISA 330, 500, 501-540)_

**Procedures and Types**  _(ISA 330, 500, 501-540)_

| Procedure | Type |
| --- | --- |
| Inspection of records or documents | Substantive / control |
| Inspection of tangible assets | Substantive (existence) |
| Observation | Purpose/assertion dependent; evidence is limited to the observation time |
| Inquiry of management / others | Substantive / control / inquiry |
| External confirmation | Substantive (existence/valuation) |
| Recalculation | Substantive |
| Reperformance | Classify by the purpose of the procedure, not its name alone |
| Analytical procedures | Substantive (when designed appropriately) |

- **Specific procedures and their conditions:** ISA 240 addresses fraud risks and management override, including journal-entry procedures. ISA 250 distinguishes direct-effect laws from other laws and requires the applicable evidence, inquiries, inspection and response to suspected non-compliance; it does not impose a legal letter on every audit. ISA 501 addresses material inventory, litigation/claims and segment information. Litigation communication with external counsel is conditional on the assessed risk or indications of material claims, with alternatives and reporting consequences where appropriate. ISA 540 addresses estimates, ISA 550 related parties, ISA 560 subsequent events, ISA 570 going concern and ISA 600 group work under their effective versions. Identify which requirements apply and obtain the specific standard before designing the work; this list is not a substitute for the complete conditions.

### Phase 5 :  Completion (ISA 700-720)

- **Completion checklist** :  Completion checklist: - Going concern assessment (ISA 570) - Subsequent events review (ISA 560) - Written representations from management (ISA 580) - Quality control review (ISA 220, 220R) - Engagement Quality Review (EQR) where the applicable standards, law or firm policies require it (ISA 220 (Revised), ISQM 1 and ISQM 2) - Key Audit Matters (KAM) selection (ISA 701 :  listed entities) / equivalent for unlisted in some jurisdictions - Audit opinion formulation (ISA 700, 705, 706) - Annual report or other information reading (ISA 720)  _(ISA 700-720)_

### Phase 6 :  Reporting (ISA 700/705/706/701/710)

- **Opinion options** :  Opinion options: - **Unmodified** :  financial statements give a true and fair view - **Qualified** :  material but not pervasive misstatement OR scope limitation - **Adverse** :  material and pervasive misstatement - **Disclaimer** :  material and pervasive scope limitation - **Emphasis of matter** :  adds emphasis without modifying opinion (e.g., subsequent event after issuance) - **Other matter** :  additional matters not addressed in financial statements - **Key Audit Matters** :  significant matters in current period audit (listed and certain unlisted) - **Going concern reporting**: apply the required separate section or opinion modification; a KAM is not a substitute for a required material-uncertainty section  _(ISA 700/705/706/701/710)_

### Phase 7 :  Post-issuance (ISA 220R, ISQM 1)

- **Post-issuance activities** :  - Follow-up of completed pre-report engagement quality review matters, without replacing the required pre-report review - Firm monitoring and remediation - Inspections by regulators (PCAOB, FRC, AOB, etc.) - Subsequent discovery of facts (ISA 560 ¶14-17)  _(ISA 220R, ISQM 1)_

## Section 2 :  When is a statutory audit required?

Each country sets size thresholds (typically two-of-three test on balance sheet total, revenue, employees) above which an audit is mandatory.

### United Kingdom: confirmed size gateway, not an automatic exemption

For financial years beginning on or after 6 April 2025, GOV.UK states the following small-company gateway:

| Condition | Value | Authority |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Annual turnover | No more than GBP 15 million | [GOV.UK audit exemption](https://www.gov.uk/audit-exemptions-for-private-limited-companies) |
| Balance sheet assets | No more than GBP 7.5 million | [GOV.UK audit exemption](https://www.gov.uk/audit-exemptions-for-private-limited-companies) |
| Average employees | 50 or fewer | [GOV.UK audit exemption](https://www.gov.uk/audit-exemptions-for-private-limited-companies) |
| Size gateway | Meet at least two of those conditions; then establish all other exemption requirements | [GOV.UK audit exemption](https://www.gov.uk/audit-exemptions-for-private-limited-companies) |

The same page lists the old values for financial years beginning before the change; they are not the current default. Size alone does not remove an audit required by excluded status, group rules, members or the company's articles. Obtain the current Companies Act/group and period-transition analysis, the necessary balance-sheet statement and any member request before advising exemption.

### Country threshold and regulator handoff

Keep a separate country sheet for Germany, France, Italy, Spain, the Netherlands, Ireland, Australia, Canada, the United States, India, Japan, Brazil and Singapore. The old thresholds, employee tests, private-company waiver assertions, group conditions and dates in this guide were not supported by current country sources. They remain expressly held in Sources. For EU countries, distinguish the Accounting Directive's size categories from each country's audit exemption and implementation; the two are not automatically identical.

Record local public-interest entity status and any separate listed/bank/insurance requirement, audit committee, rotation, EQR and report obligations. Do not infer that every entity called a PIE globally has identical KAMs, partner rotation, firm rotation or exemption rules.

## Section 3 :  Engagement structure

### 3.1 Roles

**Roles and Responsibilities**

| Role | Responsibility |
| --- | --- |
| Audit engagement partner | Overall accountability; signs the report (ISA 220) |
| Engagement Quality Reviewer (EQR) | Independent objective evaluation where required by applicable law/standards or firm policies; completed before the report is dated (ISA 220 (Revised), ISQM 1/2) |
| Manager | Day-to-day engagement leadership |
| Senior / In-charge | Field execution |
| Staff | Detailed testing |
| Specialists | Tax, IT, valuation, actuarial :  internal or external (ISA 620) |
| Group auditor (parent) | Responsibility for group direction, supervision, review and the group opinion; component work does not transfer that responsibility (ISA 600 (Revised)) |
| Component auditor | Component audit work for inclusion in group audit |

### 3.2 Independence (IESBA Code Part 4A)

- **Threats and prohibitions:** identify self-interest, self-review, advocacy, familiarity and intimidation threats under the actual adopted code. Evaluate bookkeeping, internal audit outsourcing, valuations, tax advocacy, corporate finance and other non-audit services separately; some are prohibited and others depend on status, threats, safeguards and local rules. Fee dependence, business relationships and contingent audit fees require their own check. Do not assume every service is permitted after a safeguard or that every tax service is always prohibited.
- **Long association and rotation:** identify the partner's role, years served, entity's public-interest status, cooling-off restrictions, national audit-firm rotation and tender rules. No universal years-on/years-off period is supplied here. The legacy rotation percentages/periods and code-specific independence detail are held pending the actual ethical/local text.

## Section 4 :  Reviewer brief (audit working paper file)

Every audit produces a file containing:

```
1. Engagement acceptance / continuance file
   - Independence declarations
   - Risk acceptance
   - Engagement letter
   - Predecessor communications

2. Risk assessment file
   - Entity understanding documentation
   - Industry / regulatory environment
   - Internal control walkthroughs and testing
   - Risk identification and significance matrix
   - Significant risks and response plan

3. Planning file
   - Materiality determination (overall, performance, specific, trivial)
   - Audit plan with timing and resources
   - Component auditor instructions (group audits)
   - Internal control reliance plan

4. Fieldwork file
   - By assertion: procedures performed and conclusions
   - Confirmations sent and received
   - Inventory observation working papers
   - Going concern assessment
   - Estimates testing (ECL, pensions, goodwill, fair value)
   - Related party identification and testing
   - Litigation legal letters and management responses
   - Subsequent events review

5. Completion file
   - Final analytical procedures
   - Management representation letter
   - Going concern conclusion
   - Subsequent events through report sign-off
   - Completed engagement quality review where required, before report dating

6. Reporting file
   - Final financial statements signed by management
   - Audit opinion, required going-concern section and KAMs where applicable
   - Letter to those charged with governance / management letter
   - Audit committee communications (ISA 260, 265)
```

## Section 5: opinion formulation and going concern

First establish whether sufficient appropriate evidence exists. Then distinguish a known material misstatement from the possible effects of missing evidence. ISA 705 paragraphs 6-10 decide the modified opinion; an uncertainty is not automatically a scope limitation.

| Finding | Opinion path | Authority |
| --- | --- | --- |
| No material misstatement and sufficient appropriate evidence | Unmodified, subject to the reporting framework and all other requirements | ISA 700, paragraphs 10-17; [IAASB handbook](https://www.iaasb.org/publications/2025-handbook-international-quality-management-auditing-review-other-assurance-and-related-services) |
| Known misstatement, material but not pervasive | Qualified | ISA 705, paragraph 7(a); [handbook](https://www.iaasb.org/publications/2025-handbook-international-quality-management-auditing-review-other-assurance-and-related-services) |
| Known misstatement, material and pervasive | Adverse | ISA 705, paragraph 8; [handbook](https://www.iaasb.org/publications/2025-handbook-international-quality-management-auditing-review-other-assurance-and-related-services) |
| Insufficient evidence, possible effects material but not pervasive | Qualified | ISA 705, paragraph 7(b); [handbook](https://www.iaasb.org/publications/2025-handbook-international-quality-management-auditing-review-other-assurance-and-related-services) |
| Insufficient evidence, possible effects material and pervasive | Disclaimer, subject to the standard's requirements and exceptional cases | ISA 705, paragraphs 9-10; [handbook](https://www.iaasb.org/publications/2025-handbook-international-quality-management-auditing-review-other-assurance-and-related-services) |

An emphasis-of-matter or other-matter paragraph does not cure a necessary opinion modification. A corrected error also does not by itself prove the rest of the audit supports a clean opinion. Accumulate and evaluate errors unless clearly trivial, and assess qualitative effects and aggregation.

### Going concern: the period and the report are separate tests

For the earlier ISA 570 (Revised), paragraph 13 requires evaluating the period used by management under the framework/law; if that is shorter than twelve months from the financial-statement date, request extension to at least that date-based period. Inquire about known events beyond management's assessment. This is not a blanket twelve months from the audit report date. Local requirements may require longer.

Under that standard, inappropriate use of the going-concern basis requires an adverse opinion. Where the basis is appropriate but a material uncertainty exists and disclosure is adequate, the opinion is unmodified and the report includes a separate **Material Uncertainty Related to Going Concern** section. Inadequate disclosure requires a qualified/adverse opinion as appropriate. Refusal to assess or provide evidence requires evaluating the resulting reporting consequences, not assuming uncertainty equals a disclaimer. See ISA 570 paragraphs 13-25 in the [handbook](https://www.iaasb.org/publications/2025-handbook-international-quality-management-auditing-review-other-assurance-and-related-services).

For periods beginning on or after 15 December 2026, the [revised 2024 standard](https://www.iaasb.org/publications/isa-570-revised-2024-going-concern) changes the requirements, including the management-assessment period to at least twelve months from approval of the financial statements. Check adoption and the applicable edition before applying the new reporting model. The founder's date or this guide's update date does not elect early adoption.

### Worked case: accumulate errors without turning materiality into a formula for an opinion

Fabricated audit workpaper amounts in currency units. The engagement partner has already justified overall materiality of 100,000 and a clearly trivial amount of 5,000. Three isolated errors are 2,000, 12,000 and 35,000. On the explicit assumptions that the first is clearly trivial in nature/circumstances as well as size, it is excluded from the accumulated schedule. The other two total 47,000. That is 53,000 below overall materiality quantitatively, but it does not establish an unmodified opinion: qualitative factors, aggregation, other errors and evidence sufficiency remain to be evaluated. A small fraud-related or disclosure error must not be excluded solely because of size. These assumed amounts are not ISA-prescribed benchmarks.

```oa-check
name: Audit misstatement accumulation with supplied judgement
inputs:
  overall_materiality: 100000
  clearly_trivial_error: 2000
  error_two: 12000
  error_three: 35000
steps:
  accumulated: error_two + error_three
  quantitative_headroom: overall_materiality - accumulated
  total_identified: clearly_trivial_error + accumulated
expected:
  accumulated: 47000
  quantitative_headroom: 53000
  total_identified: 49000
```

## Section 6 :  21 self-checks

Before signing the opinion, verify:

1. [ ] Engagement acceptance / continuance documented and approved
2. [ ] Engagement terms agreed and recorded under ISA 210, including any applicable prescribed-law exception and recurring-engagement review
3. [ ] Independence :  firm and engagement team :  declared and threats assessed
4. [ ] Risk assessment per ISA 315 (Revised) :  entity understanding, RMM, significant risks
5. [ ] Materiality :  overall, performance, specific, trivial :  determined and documented
6. [ ] Audit plan responsive to identified risks
7. [ ] Significant risks addressed responsively; substantive-only responses include tests of details
8. [ ] Going-concern assessment covers the applicable standard/framework period; old financial-statement-date and new approval-date requirements are distinguished
9. [ ] Subsequent events review through report sign-off
10. [ ] Written representation letter from management received before report sign-off
11. [ ] Fraud risk assessment per ISA 240 with required journal entry testing
12. [ ] Estimates tested per ISA 540 (Revised)
13. [ ] Related parties identified and tested per ISA 550
14. [ ] Litigation/claims procedures and external legal communication assessed under ISA 501 conditions; no universal letter checklist substituted for the evidence requirement
15. [ ] Group audit direction, supervision, review, component communication and evidence documented under the effective ISA 600
16. [ ] Required EQR completed and engagement partner notified before report dating; do not defer the review to post-issuance
17. [ ] Annual report read and other information consistent with audited financial statements (ISA 720)
18. [ ] Communications to TCWG / management letter prepared
19. [ ] Opinion type confirmed via decision tree (Section 5)
20. [ ] KAMs identified and documented for listed entities (ISA 701)
21. [ ] Audit file assembled and retained under ISA 230/ISQM 1 and any longer local rule; no post-report completion of missing audit work

## Section 7 :  Global refusal catalogue

**Global refusal catalogue**

| Refusal | Trigger |
| --- | --- |
| **R-AUDIT-1** | Independence cannot be established or threats cannot be reduced to acceptable level |
| **R-AUDIT-2** | Predecessor auditor will not communicate or hostile transition |
| **R-AUDIT-3** | Management refuses to provide written representations |
| **R-AUDIT-4** | Material misstatement detected and management refuses to correct |
| **R-AUDIT-5** | Limitation on scope imposed by management |
| **R-AUDIT-6** | Suspected fraud :  escalate per firm fraud protocols and ISA 240 ¶42 |
| **R-AUDIT-7** | Suspected non-compliance with laws and regulations affecting financial statements :  ISA 250 ¶27 escalation |
| **R-AUDIT-8** | Going concern material uncertainty with inadequate disclosure that management refuses to enhance |
| **R-AUDIT-9** | Component auditor refuses cooperation in group audit |
| **R-AUDIT-10** | Regulatory inspection finding against firm requires re-issuance of opinion |

These identifiers are stop/escalation flags, not a rule to abandon every engagement. Determine whether to decline, withdraw where permitted, obtain evidence, communicate or modify the report under the applicable standards and law. A management refusal to correct a misstatement can require a qualified or adverse opinion; it does not authorise a clean opinion. Missing required management-responsibility representations invokes ISA 580 paragraphs 19-20 and its disclaimer requirement in the specified cases. A hostile predecessor or component limitation requires the applicable acceptance, evidence and reporting analysis. A regulator's finding does not automatically require reissuance without evaluating facts and law.

## When to refuse or refer

- Do not accept where essential engagement preconditions or independence cannot be established; assess the precise legal/ethical response.
- Do not sign without sufficient appropriate evidence, required representations, completed required EQR and the necessary opinion evaluation.
- Escalate fraud, suspected non-compliance, management integrity concerns, group-evidence restrictions, disputed standards/adoption or post-issuance facts to the responsible qualified auditor.
- Do not provide a country audit exemption from this global matrix if its local threshold, group/status condition or period is held below.

## File assembly and retention

ISA 230 A21 describes final file assembly as ordinarily no more than sixty days after the report, with A22 distinguishing administrative assembly from new audit work. A23/ISQM 1 A85 describe an audit retention period ordinarily no shorter than five years from the auditor's report or, if later, the group auditor's report. Establish any longer local obligation, confidentiality/access requirements and documentation changes before setting the actual file policy. [IAASB handbook, ISA 230 paragraphs 14-16 and A21-A24](https://www.iaasb.org/publications/2025-handbook-international-quality-management-auditing-review-other-assurance-and-related-services).
