# Residency regimes for wealthy families compared (2026, v1.0): Malta, Italy, Portugal, Switzerland, UAE, Singapore, UK, Ireland and Cyprus

Figures are for tax year 2026. This Guide compares the special residence regimes that internationally mobile families most often weigh: Malta's remittance basis and Global Residence Programme, Italy's flat tax for new residents, Portugal's IFICI, Swiss expenditure-based taxation (federal tax only), the UAE residence rules, Singapore's treatment of overseas income, the UK's four-year foreign income and gains regime, Ireland's remittance basis and domicile levy, and Cyprus's non-dom rules. Each country section links its source provisions. This is a source-cited comparison draft, with source versions and execution limits stated below. The UK tax year is 6 April 2026 to 5 April 2027; the other countries use the calendar year. The operative scopes were personally read on 16 November 2026; prior exact-source UAE reading is reused within its recorded scope. No accountant attestation is claimed.

This Guide compares regimes. It does not replace the country Guides. For the detail, use `mt-non-dom` and `moving-to-malta-tax-residence` (Malta), `uk-to-italy-flat-tax-relocation` (Italy), `uk-to-uae-relocation-tax` and `ae-income-tax` (UAE), `uk-non-dom` (UK), `ie-non-dom` (Ireland) and `cy-non-dom` (Cyprus). Malta's residence programmes are set out in full in `mt-residence-programmes-for-wealthy-families`. What happens when the family leaves its current country is in `exit-taxes-when-a-family-moves`. Inheritance, gift and forced-heirship rules are in `succession-and-forced-heirship-compared`.

## Inputs and output

Read the existing record before asking the client. Obtain each person's nationality, ordinary and treaty residence, domicile, arrival dates, historic residence years, work and business facts, income sources and remittances, qualifying property and dependants, prior regimes/elections, assets and trust arrangements, and the expected departure date. Separate tax residence from immigration permission and eligibility for a special income-tax treatment.

Produce a person-by-person eligibility schedule, the source version/date, covered and excluded income, annual minimum or fixed payment, remaining duration, entry/continuing evidence, application/return/payment calendar and unresolved country questions. Calculate the old-country exit charge and destination inheritance, wealth and social-security consequences separately.

## The method, step by step

1. Fix the family's current position first: where each member is tax resident and domiciled today, and what the old country charges on departure. Use `exit-taxes-when-a-family-moves` before choosing a destination.
2. Test each person, not the family, against each regime's entry conditions in the country sections below. Each regime has its own look-back: nine of ten years non-resident for Italy ([TUIR art. 24-bis](https://www.normattiva.it/uri-res/N2Ls?urn:nir:stato:decreto.del.presidente.della.repubblica:1986-12-22;917~art24bis!vig=)), five years for Portugal ([EBF article 58.º-A](https://info.portaldasfinancas.gov.pt/pt/informacao_fiscal/codigos_tributarios/bf_rep/Pages/EBF58A.aspx)), ten years for Switzerland ([Federal Direct Tax Act, art. 14](https://www.fedlex.admin.ch/filestore/fedlex.data.admin.ch/eli/cc/1991/1184_1184_1184/20260101/de/pdf-a/fedlex-data-admin-ch-eli-cc-1991-1184_1184_1184-20260101-de-pdf-a.pdf)) and ten consecutive tax years for the UK ([HMRC, 4-year FIG regime](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/check-if-you-can-claim-the-4-year-foreign-income-and-gains-regime)).
3. Map the family's income by type and by source country: work income, dividends, interest, rent, gains on shares, gains on property. A regime that relieves foreign investment income may still tax local work income in full, and Portugal's IFICI covers qualifying work income only ([EBF article 58.º-A](https://info.portaldasfinancas.gov.pt/pt/informacao_fiscal/codigos_tributarios/bf_rep/Pages/EBF58A.aspx)).
4. For each candidate regime, read the cost line in the country's source table and the duration. Where a regime has an annual minimum (Malta, Italy, Switzerland), a family with modest foreign income can pay more than under ordinary rules. Compare like with like; this Guide does not compute any family's total.
5. Separately test inheritance, gift, wealth, property taxes and social security, including any specific relief attached to the regime. The income-tax amount is not a complete family tax assessment. Use `succession-and-forced-heirship-compared`.
6. Diary the conditions that end each regime (days spent elsewhere, property kept, payment made on time, years counted) and the filing or application deadline in the first year. Record the evidence for each year.

## Malta

Malta has two routes for a wealthy family. Both are set out in more detail in `mt-non-dom`, `moving-to-malta-tax-residence` and `mt-residence-programmes-for-wealthy-families`.

**Remittance basis.** A person who is not ordinarily resident in Malta or not domiciled in Malta pays Malta tax on foreign income only on the amount received in Malta. Foreign capital gains of such a person are not taxed in Malta, even if brought in. Neither rule is available to everyone. From year of assessment 2019 they do "not apply to an individual who is a long-term resident, or who holds a permanent residence certificate or a permanent residence card, in respect of any income derived by such individual in the year of being granted long-term resident status or the right of permanent residence and in subsequent years". They also "shall not apply to an individual whose spouse is ordinarily resident and domiciled in Malta". The full test is in `mt-non-dom`. ([Income Tax Act, Cap. 123, article 4(1), provisos (i) and (ii)](https://legislation.mt/eli/cap/123/eng/pdf))

**Minimum tax.** Article 56(27) applies to an ordinarily resident but non-domiciled individual who still qualifies under article 4(1)(i)–(ii), is not already taxed under a minimum-tax scheme, and derives the specified foreign income of at least EUR 35,000 which is not or not fully received in Malta. Article 49 couples aggregate both spouses' income. The minimum is EUR 5,000; tax already paid under the Act, including withholding, counts, but article 5A property-transfer tax does not. The statutory floor is before double-tax relief. If the person proves that worldwide-basis tax would be lower, the liability is capped at that lower amount. [Cap.123 article56(27)](https://legislation.mt/eli/cap/123/eng/pdf).

The old draft reserved whether the EUR 35,000 gate meant all foreign income or only its unremitted part. The actual MTCA remittance guidance has now been read: section6.3 uses foreign income, aggregates a married couple's total income, and applies one minimum to the couple; section6.2 restricts double-tax relief to actually remitted income taxed abroad. Use that published administrative reading together with the statute's not-or-not-fully-remitted condition. Keep the source/remittance records; do not compute the gate only from the unremitted balance. [MTCA guidance sections1/5/6](https://mtca.gov.mt/docs/default-source/documents/mtca-guidelines-on-the-remittance-under-the-income-tax.pdf?sfvrsn=d2f39d05_1).

**Global Residence Programme (GRP).** Open only to third-country nationals: a Maltese, EEA or Swiss national cannot apply, and nor can a long-term resident or anyone already under another Malta tax scheme listed in rule 4. The rule2 long-term-resident definition also includes an applicant for that status. The applicant applies through an authorised registered mandatary and must, among other conditions, hold a qualifying owned or rented property as the main home, have stable and regular resources sufficient without Maltese social assistance, a valid travel document, sickness insurance covering the EU for self/dependants, adequate communication in an official language and fit-and-proper status ([S.L. 123.148, rules 3 and 4](https://legislation.mt/eli/sl/123.148/eng/pdf)).

EU, EEA and Swiss nationals (not Maltese nationals) cannot use the GRP but have a parallel scheme, the Residence Programme Rules, with the same flat rate on foreign income received in Malta and the annual minimum in the table below ([Residence Programme Rules, S.L. 123.160, rules 4 and 5](https://legislation.mt/eli/sl/123.160/eng/pdf)). **Malta traps.** The GRP is lost by a calendar year of more than 183 days in any other country, so a family that splits time across several homes must count the beneficiary's days in each other country. The minimum tax is due in full in the first and last year, however short. The GRP rules speak of foreign income received in Malta; foreign capital gains are free of Malta tax under article 4(1) proviso (ii) only if the person is not ordinarily resident or not domiciled in Malta; the GRP rules do not exempt gains themselves. Record residence and domicile evidence. Succession questions are not part of either regime; see `succession-and-forced-heirship-compared`.

**Enacted transition, not a 2026 replacement.** L.N.195/2026 rule1(2) commences the Individual Tax Programme on 1 January 2027. Rule3(3) provides a five-year term, renewal provisions, and continuation until 31 December 2031 for status granted before or up to 31 December 2026, expressly including applications received up to that date. The old GRP/TRP rules alone therefore do not establish an indefinite future entitlement. This comparison's EUR15,000 and property/fee figures are the existing-programme figures, not the new-applicant 2027 minima. Obtain the applicable transition/grant before execution; this Guide does not compute the new programme. [Actual enacted rules1–3, read with4–6](https://legislation.mt/eli/ln/2026/195/eng/pdf).

## Italy

Under article 24-bis of the TUIR, a person who moves tax residence to Italy may opt to pay a fixed substitute tax on foreign income instead of IRPEF on that income, if they were not Italian tax resident for at least nine of the ten tax periods before the option starts.

The literal text of article 24-bis(3) refers to a favourable ruling and the return deadline for the move year, including disclosure of the last residence jurisdictions. Read it with the actual published administrative procedure: Circular 17/E of 23 May 2017, Part III sections 3.1 and 4, says the ruling is optional; the option may be exercised directly in the return, or before the requested ruling arrives, with eligibility/checklist evidence retained. The agency describes the move-year return or the following-period return in its specified nine-of-ten situation. Do not make a favourable ruling a universal entry gate, or use the circular's historical filing dates/old tax amounts as 2026 deadlines or rates. [Current24-bis](https://www.normattiva.it/uri-res/N2Ls?urn:nir:stato:decreto.del.presidente.della.repubblica:1986-12-22;917~art24bis!vig=); [actual Finance Ministry-hosted Circular17/E, PartIII3–4](https://def.finanze.it/DocTribFrontend/getPrassiDetail.do?id=%7BDF6F5518-4D04-4718-B611-8AC67E8C08BC%7D).

The fixed amount is due for each tax period, whatever the income, in one payment by the balance date for income tax. It can be extended to family members within article 433 of the civil code who themselves meet the nine-of-ten test, at a lower amount for each. Gains on qualifying shareholdings (article 67(1)(c)) realised in the first five tax periods of the option are NOT covered and are taxed under the ordinary rules. The person may exclude one or more foreign countries: income from those countries is then taxed normally, with a foreign tax credit ([TUIR art. 24-bis, commi 1, 2, 5 and 6](https://www.normattiva.it/uri-res/N2Ls?urn:nir:stato:decreto.del.presidente.della.repubblica:1986-12-22;917~art24bis!vig=)).

The option can be revoked and in any case ends after fifteen years from the first tax period of the option. It also ends if the substitute tax is not paid, or paid only in part, on time. Revocation or loss bars a new option. Revocation or loss for the main person also ends the regime for the family members; a family member's own non-payment does not end it for the main person ([TUIR art. 24-bis, commi 4 and 6](https://www.normattiva.it/uri-res/N2Ls?urn:nir:stato:decreto.del.presidente.della.repubblica:1986-12-22;917~art24bis!vig=)).

## Portugal

The pages on the Portal das Finanças describe IFICI, the incentive for scientific research and innovation in article 58.º-A of the Estatuto dos Benefícios Fiscais. The allowed pages read for this Guide bar anyone who benefits or has benefited from the non-habitual resident (NHR) regime, and anyone who opted for the "Regressar" regime in article 12.º-A of the IRS Code. They do not state, in words, that IFICI replaced NHR. ([EBF article 58.º-A, n.º 10](https://info.portaldasfinancas.gov.pt/pt/informacao_fiscal/codigos_tributarios/bf_rep/Pages/EBF58A.aspx))

IFICI covers a person who becomes Portuguese tax resident, was not resident in Portugal in any of the previous five years, AND carries on one of the listed activities: higher-education teaching and scientific research, qualified jobs under contractual investment benefits, highly qualified professions in qualifying companies, other qualified jobs in entities recognised by AICEP or IAPMEI, R&D staff, jobs in certified startups, or jobs in the Azores and Madeira. The special rate applies to net employment and self-employment income (categories A and B) from those activities, for 10 consecutive years from the year of registration as a resident, and only in years in which the person is resident and still earns income from a qualifying activity. It can be used only once. EBF58-A(4) permits a new eligible activity begun within six months after the former activity; missed years do not restart the ten-year clock, and late registration only gives the remaining period (5)/(7). Jobs covered by CFI22(2)(c) are excluded (11); each activity also has its own employer/qualification rules and registration authority (1)/(6). ([EBF article 58.º-A, n.os 1 to 3 and 12](https://info.portaldasfinancas.gov.pt/pt/informacao_fiscal/codigos_tributarios/bf_rep/Pages/EBF58A.aspx))

## Singapore

IRAS states that, generally, overseas income received in Singapore, including overseas income paid into a Singapore bank account, is not taxable, and need not be declared. Overseas income IS taxable if: it is received through a partnership based in Singapore; overseas employment is incidental to a Singapore employment; an overseas trade or business is incidental to a Singapore trade; the person works in Singapore for a foreign employer; or, for a Singapore citizen or tax resident, overseas employment on behalf of the Singapore Government is deemed Singapore-source, subject to any Minister-approved remission of tax on overseas allowances. ([IRAS, income received from overseas](https://www.iras.gov.sg/taxes/individual-income-tax/basics-of-individual-income-tax/what-is-taxable-what-is-not/income-received-from-overseas))

**Singapore traps.** The page covers individuals' overseas income. Singapore-source income, Singapore work and Singapore partnership income follow the ordinary rules. Residence conditions, immigration status and any family-office incentive are not on the page read here; refer them.

## UK

On 6 April 2025 the four-year foreign income and gains (FIG) regime replaced the remittance basis. A UK resident under the statutory residence test can claim it only within their first 4 years of UK residence that follow at least 10 consecutive tax years of non-UK residence. Those whose 4 years began before 6 April 2025 can use the years left from 2025 to 2026. Years spent non-resident inside the window cannot be claimed or rolled forward. The claim is made on the Self Assessment return, source by source. Eligible items include profits of a trade carried on wholly outside the UK, overseas property profits, dividends from non-UK companies and foreign interest. Foreign employment income is NOT eligible (Overseas Workday Relief may apply instead). A claim removes the income-tax and capital-gains allowances and the listed marriage/blind-person allowances; foreign income still enters adjusted-net-income calculations for relevant childcare/child-benefit rules. ([HMRC, 4-year FIG regime](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/check-if-you-can-claim-the-4-year-foreign-income-and-gains-regime))

Former remittance-basis users can designate foreign income and gains from before 6 April 2025 under the Temporary Repatriation Facility at the rate in the table below. Designation requires UK residence in the designation year and former remittance-basis use. Designation and remittance are different: designated qualifying capital can be remitted later without another remittance charge; undesignated old income/gains retain their ordinary remittance rules. No foreign tax credit is given against the TRF charge. Retain origin, designation and mixed-fund records. ([HMRC, HS264](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/remittance-basis-hs264-self-assessment-helpsheet/hs264-remittance-of-pre-6-april-2025-foreign-income-and-gains-and-the-temporary-repatriation-facility-trf))

**UK traps.** From 6 April 2025 inheritance tax follows long-term UK residence, not domicile. A person is long-term UK resident in a tax year if UK resident for the previous 10 consecutive years or for a total of 10 years or more within the previous 20 years. Long-term residence can continue for up to 10 tax years after leaving, less for shorter stays (3 years after leaving for 10 to 13 years of residence). The general tail is qualified by transitional rules: a person who had neither UK domicile nor deemed domicile on 30 October 2024, is non-resident in 2025 to 2026 and does not return is excluded. A person with deemed domicile on 30 October 2024 who is non-resident in 2025 to 2026 and does not return has the separate 3-year non-residence rule. After 10 consecutive non-resident years, the 10-out-of-20 test resets on return and counts only the return year and future years. Inheritance tax is charged on overseas assets in a trust the person set up or added to, even when the person was not long-term UK resident. HMRC lists trust assets on which there is no inheritance tax on the person's death: assets placed in the trust while the person was non-UK domiciled, overseas on 30 October 2024, and overseas on the date of death or when the person's rights in the trust ended. Separate trust charges may still apply ([HMRC, Inheritance Tax if you're a long-term UK resident](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/inheritance-tax-if-youre-a-long-term-uk-resident)). A family leaving the UK should read this with `exit-taxes-when-a-family-moves`; one arriving should note that the FIG window is short and fixed. Details are in `uk-non-dom`.

## Ireland

An individual resident in Ireland but not domiciled there pays Irish tax on Irish source income in full and on foreign income only to the extent remitted into Ireland ([Revenue, domicile and the domicile levy](https://www.revenue.ie/en/jobs-and-pensions/tax-residence/domicile-domicile-levy.aspx)). The remittance basis rests on domicile: since tax year 2010 Irish citizenship plus non-ordinary residence alone no longer qualifies a domiciled individual; a non-domiciled citizen may still meet the domicile test. Case IV income is outside the remittance basis. Accumulated income does not automatically become capital; mixed funds, indirect remittances and spouse transfers need tracing. It also applies to foreign chargeable gains of non-domiciled individuals. Pay for duties performed in Ireland, even under a foreign employment, is taxed in full and is NOT on the remittance basis. ([Revenue, Tax and Duty Manual Part 05-01-21, the remittance basis of assessment as regards UK source income and gains](https://www.revenue.ie/en/tax-professionals/tdm/income-tax-capital-gains-tax-corporation-tax/part-05/05-01-21.pdf); [Tax and Duty Manual Part 05-01-21A](https://www.revenue.ie/en/tax-professionals/tdm/income-tax-capital-gains-tax-corporation-tax/part-05/05-01-21a.pdf))

**Domicile levy.** The levy is the opposite case: it is charged on an individual who IS domiciled in Ireland, whatever their tax residence, if ALL of these apply in the tax year: worldwide income above the income test, Irish income tax below the tax test, AND Irish property on 31 December worth more than the property test, with no deduction for debts. Irish income tax paid for the year can be set against the levy; USC, PRSI and RZLT cannot. The credit is limited to Irish income tax paid no later than the levy; the income-tax liability gate is before foreign-tax credits. Worldwide income has a statutory computation, not simply net cash receipts; Irish-property market value ignores debts, has trading-company exceptions and anti-avoidance rules. The levy is normally paid and filed by 31 October of the following year, with the published ROS pay-and-file extension where applicable. ([Revenue, Tax and Duty Manual Part 18C-00-01](https://www.revenue.ie/en/tax-professionals/tdm/income-tax-capital-gains-tax-corporation-tax/part-18c/18c-00-01.pdf); [Revenue, domicile and the domicile levy](https://www.revenue.ie/en/jobs-and-pensions/tax-residence/domicile-domicile-levy.aspx))

## Cyprus

An individual is Cyprus tax resident by spending more than 183 days in Cyprus in the tax year, or under the 60-day rule, which needs ALL of: at least 60 days in Cyprus, not spending more than 183 days in any one other state, a Cyprus business/employment or office in a Cyprus tax-resident person (with the statutory termination-in-year exclusion), and a permanent home in Cyprus owned or rented. An individual who is NOT domiciled in Cyprus (or not resident) pays no Special Defence Contribution (SDC) on dividends and interest; the current 2026 SDC charging provisions concern dividends and interest, not rental receipts. The former 2025 rental charge is an old historical claim, not a 2026 rate; the historic rental return remains outside current execution here. Whatever the domicile of origin, a person resident for at least 17 out of the last 20 years before the tax year is deemed domiciled, and stays so until completing 20 years of non-residence. ([IncomeTaxLaw2, actual CyprusBar consolidation](https://www.cylaw.org/nomoi/enop/non-ind/2002_1_118/full.html); [SDCLaw2/3/3B, actual CyprusBar consolidation](https://www.cylaw.org/nomoi/enop/non-ind/2002_1_117/full.html); [Tax Department, tax residency and domicile](https://www.gov.cy/mof-tax/en/documents/tax-residency-domicility/))

The 2026 reform keeps the non-dom status and adds an option (new article 3D of the SDC Law) for people who do NOT have a Cypriot domicile of origin and become deemed domiciled: they may pay a lump-sum SDC instead, for two 5-year periods. The election is irrevocable, is not automatic, needs an application approved by the Commissioner for each 5-year period, and must be filed by 30 June of the first year of that period. The lump sum for the whole period is paid by the end of the month after approval; it is not refundable and covers all SDC for those five years. Article 3D(4) is stricter than the old draft: late payment makes the article unavailable for ANY tax year, with ordinary SDC under articles3–3C; an application must separately meet3D(3). There is no set-off against other liabilities/credits or foreign-tax deduction, and no refund. The native 3D(1)–(9) has been read; the government form/circular URLs remained inaccessible, so current submission-channel/form execution remains unconfirmed. ([SDCLaw3D, actual statutory body](https://www.cylaw.org/nomoi/enop/non-ind/2002_1_117/full.html); [Tax Department, SDC presentation 2026](https://www.gov.cy/media/sites/167/2026/03/EEA-%CE%A6%CE%9A%CE%9A-%CE%9C%CE%95%CE%A4%CE%91%CE%A1%CE%A1%CE%A5%CE%98%CE%9C%CE%99%CE%A3%CE%97-06032026.pdf); [Application form, article 3D](https://www.gov.cy/media/sites/167/2026/06/Application-Extension-of-Non-Dom.pdf))

## Decision checklist for a family choosing a base

1. Does each adult meet the look-back test for the regime (years of non-residence before arrival)? Write the years down for each person.
2. Is the family's wealth producing investment income, or does a member work? Portugal's IFICI and Switzerland's expenditure tax turn on work in opposite ways: IFICI needs a qualifying activity, while the Swiss route forbids gainful activity in Switzerland.
3. Is the annual minimum (Malta GRP, Italy, Switzerland) higher than the tax the family would pay anyway? Compare the minimum in each source table with a normal-rules computation done by the adviser for that country.
4. How long does the family plan to stay? Compare with the regime's end: four years in the UK, ten in Portugal, fifteen at most in Italy, the 17-of-20 deemed-domicile clock in Cyprus, continuing Malta remittance-basis and Swiss eligibility conditions, and the enacted GRP/TRP transition above.
5. Where will the family actually spend its days? Malta's GRP ends at more than 183 days in another country; Cyprus day-count routes require their other conditions, and the UAE also has a residence/interests route.
6. Malta's GRP excludes Maltese, EEA and Swiss nationals; EEA and Swiss nationals (not Maltese) can use the Residence Programme instead; Switzerland's expenditure tax excludes Swiss citizens.
7. What does the old country charge on leaving, and what inheritance, gift and wealth taxes follow the family? Use `exit-taxes-when-a-family-moves` and `succession-and-forced-heirship-compared`.
8. Who files what in the first year? The Italian optional-ruling/return option, the Cyprus article 3D application, the Malta GRP minimum normally by 30 April with the grant-year exception, and the UK FIG claim each have their own deadline.

## Ask the client first

- For each family member: nationality, current tax residence, domicile (and domicile of origin), and the tax years of residence in each country over the last 20 years.
- What income does the family have, by type and by source country, and which member owns each asset? Does any member work, and where?
- Where will each member live and spend days, year by year, and will the family buy or rent the main home?
- Has any member already used a special regime (for example NHR in Portugal, Italy's flat tax or a UK remittance-basis claim)?
- Which country does the family leave, and does it hold property, trusts or companies there?
- How long does the family intend to stay, and do they want the children to inherit under a particular law?

## When to refuse or refer

- Refer any computation of total tax under a regime: this Guide compares rules and costs; it does not compute a family's bill.
- Refer Swiss cantonal amounts, cantonal wealth and inheritance tax, and the cantonal negotiation of the expenditure base to a Swiss adviser.
- Refer Italian residence for anyone who moved before 1 January 2026, because the amount depends on the statutory move-date transition and the actual election, not a universally mandatory ruling.
- Refer any family with trusts, foundations or companies abroad: the anti-avoidance and attribution rules of each country are outside this Guide.
- Refer double residence and treaty tie-breaker questions; each regime assumes residence is settled.
- Refuse to state that a family "pays no tax" in a country. Each regime still taxes local-source income, and the UAE and Singapore pages describe specific exceptions.
- Refer disputed Malta source/capital/remittance classifications and any proposed departure from the now-recovered published MTCA section6.3 reading; do not treat the old all-income versus unremitted-only ambiguity as an unresolved source-access fact.

## Worked boundary checks
