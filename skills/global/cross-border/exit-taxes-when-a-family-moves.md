# Exit taxes when a wealthy family changes country (2026, v1.1)

Figures are for tax year 2026. This Guide compares what happens to unrealised gains when an individual stops being tax resident in Germany, France, the Netherlands, Norway, Spain, Italy, Canada, Australia, the United States or the United Kingdom. It covers the trigger, what is taxed, the holding or value threshold, the deferral or instalment options, when the tax falls away on a return, and the return or form that reports it. Each country section uses the wording of that country's tax authority or statute page, linked in the section. Amounts in the worked example are hypothetical and labelled as such. Use the exact event date and source version. A 2026 form for 2025 departures is not a final filing manual for 2026 departures. Dated limitations and unresolved execution points appear below; they are not permissions to assume an exemption.

Deeper corridor Guides by Michael Cutajar cover single departures: `leaving-france-exit-tax`, `norway-to-switzerland-wealth-tax-exit`, `leaving-australia-tax-residency-cgt`, `us-citizen-moving-abroad-tax` and `germany-to-switzerland-cross-border-tax`. The United States exit tax has its own Guide, `us-expatriation-exit-tax`. This Guide links to them and does not repeat them. Companion Guides on the same family-office theme: `family-office-residency-regimes-compared` and `succession-and-forced-heirship-compared`.

## Inputs and output

Read the complete existing record before asking the client. Collect citizenship and immigration history; each person’s domestic and treaty residence periods; exact departure and possible return dates; ownership chains, fiscal partners and household holdings; asset categories, acquisition dates, bases and market values; prior gifts, restructurings and deferred gains; proposed dividends and sales; destination; earlier elections, assessments and security; and all departure and follow-up returns.

Produce a person-by-person and asset-by-asset schedule showing the applicable legal version, trigger, valuation date, scope/threshold result, deemed gain, calculation limits, payment election, security, acceleration events, return relief and dated reporting calendar. Keep destination basis and treaty-credit questions open until their own applicable sources are established. A planning illustration is not a tax assessment.

## The method, step by step

1. Fix the departure date and the residence history for each family member who holds assets. Each exit tax tests years of past residence differently (Germany: [section 6(2) AStG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/astg/__6.html); France: [CGI 167 bis](https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/codes/article_lc/LEGIARTI000048806379); Spain: [article 95 bis Ley 35/2006](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-2006-20764)).
2. List every asset each person holds at the relevant statutory snapshot date, by type: company shares, fund units, pension and savings wrappers, real estate, receivables from an earn-out. The country sections below say which types each country catches ([Norway exit tax page](https://www.skatteetaten.no/en/person/to-from-norway/from/tax-when-you-move-abroad/exit-tax/); [Canada dispositions page](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/international-non-residents/individuals-leaving-entering-canada-non-residents/dispositions-property.html)).
3. Test each holding against the country's threshold in the tables below. A threshold is not an allowance unless the page says so: on a move, Norway's NOK amount is a deduction from the gain, but on a gift or other transfer Norway's NOK amount is a cliff (the whole gain is taxed once it is exceeded); Spain's and France's amounts decide whether the rule applies at all.
4. Value the holdings at the date the country names and compute the deemed gain. Then apply the country's rate on that gain. Only the rates printed in the tables below are stated here.
5. Choose payment: immediate, deferral, or instalments. Note what security is needed and what ends the deferral early (a sale, a gift, a dividend above a limit, a move to a non-EU or non-EEA state).
6. File the departure return with the country's exit form, then diarise the yearly follow-up filings and the date on which the tax falls away if the person comes back or keeps the shares.
7. Check whether the destination country gives a credit or a stepped-up cost for the exit charge. Treaty relief is outside this Guide; record it as an open question for the adopting accountant.

## Ask the client first

- For each person who will move: in which years were they tax resident in the country they are leaving, and on what exact date does residence end?
- Which company shares, fund units and pension or savings accounts does each person (and, for France and the Netherlands, the household or fiscal partner) hold, and what share of each company do they own?
- Is the destination inside the EU or EEA? Several deferrals depend on it; for France, whether it is on the tax authority's list.
- Is a sale, a dividend, a gift or a restructuring of the company planned within the next twelve years?
- Is a return to the country of departure possible, and within how many years?
- Is anyone in the family a US citizen or a US green card holder?

## Germany: section 6 AStG

Who is caught. A natural person who, within the last twelve years before the event, was unlimited liable to German income tax for at least seven years in total ([section 6(2) AStG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/astg/__6.html)). Periods of a gratuitous predecessor also count, with simultaneous periods counted only once; the special prior-return-relief deeming provision in6(2) must also be checked.

What triggers it. Three events are treated as a sale of the shares at fair market value (gemeiner Wert): the end of unlimited tax liability because the person gives up domicile or habitual abode; a free transfer to a person who is not unlimited liable to German tax; and any exclusion or restriction of Germany's right to tax the gain on the shares ([section 6(1) AStG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/astg/__6.html)).

What is taxed. Only shares within section 17(1) sentence 1 EStG: shares in a corporation where the person held, directly or indirectly, at least the percentage in the table below of the capital at any time within the last five years ([section 17 EStG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/estg/__17.html)). Fund units outside business assets have their own deemed sale under section 19(3) InvStG, but only if the holder held at least the percentage in the table of the issued units within the last five years, OR holds units whose acquisition cost is at least the amount in the table, per fund (units "an dem Investmentfonds"), and only if the sum of taxable gains is positive ("die Summe der ... steuerpflichtigen Gewinne insgesamt positiv") ([section 19 InvStG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/invstg_2018/__19.html)). Special-investment-fund units have a separate nonbusiness rule in [InvStG 49(5)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/invstg_2018/__49.html), including a positive-gain condition, different return treatment and no ordinary-fund 1%/EUR 500,000 gates. Both fund provisions incorporate the relevant AStG6(2)–(5) lookback/payment/reporting framework, with their own substitutions and the special-fund return mechanism. Do not transfer section 19’s thresholds to section 49. [Section 57(10)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/invstg_2018/__57.html) applies these fund exit provisions to events after 31 December 2024. EStG17(6)/(7) also extends the covered interests in its specified restructuring/cooperative cases; do not use1% as the only possible entry route. For qualifying section17 share gains, the partial-income method is grounded in EStG3No40(c) and3c(2): the prescribed exemption and corresponding deduction limits must be applied before the individual's relevant income-tax tariff under32a. That is not a universal flat exit-tax rate or the ordinary-fund calculation. [Exemption](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/estg/__3.html); [deduction limits](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/estg/__3c.html); [2026 tariff](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/estg/__32a.html). This Guide does not calculate the German income tax on the gain; see `germany-to-switzerland-cross-border-tax`.

Instalments. On application, the tax on the deemed gain can be paid in seven equal yearly instalments. The application is normally granted only against security. The first instalment is due within one month of the tax notice; the rest fall due on 31 July of the following years; no interest is charged. Collection accelerates within one month for missed instalments, reporting default or insolvency, and to the statutory extent of a sale/transfer or distributions/capital repayments beyond the aggregate one-quarter limit ([section 6(4) AStG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/astg/__6.html)). A transfer on death to a natural person is disregarded for the sale or transfer event. The law prints no special EU or EEA deferral for a departure under the current text.

Return within seven years. If the departure was only a temporary absence and the person becomes unlimited liable again within seven years, the tax falls away, but only to the extent that: the shares were not sold, transferred or put into business assets in the meantime; distributions or capital repayments did not exceed one quarter of the value at departure; AND Germany's right to tax a sale of the shares is restored at least to the extent it existed at departure. On application the tax office can extend the seven years by at most five years in total if the intention to return still exists ([section 6(3) AStG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/astg/__6.html)).

Where the departure is a temporary absence under section 6(3), the payment is deferred for the period the tax office grants and, on application, no instalments are collected; if the tax does not then fall away, interest is charged for the deferral ([section 6(4) AStG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/astg/__6.html)).

Reporting. The taxpayer must report any of the events that end the instalments within one month, electronically, to the tax office that was competent at departure. Every year by 31 July the taxpayer must report the current address and confirm that the shares are still attributed to them ([section 6(5) AStG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/astg/__6.html)).

Older departures. Where a trigger under the version in force until 30 June 2021 happened before 1 January 2022, that older version continues to apply to the case, with the modifications in section 21(3) AStG ([section 21 AStG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/astg/__21.html)).

## France: article 167 bis CGI

Use [current CGI 167 bis](https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/codes/article_lc/LEGIARTI000048806379), effective 2024-01-01 and displayed current on 2026-11-16. The [2026 ETD notice](https://www.impots.gouv.fr/sites/default/files/formulaires/2074-etd/2026/2074-etd_5518.pdf) is expressly for 2025 departures; its specific statement about 2026 social levies is identified separately below. Do not treat the old ETS2 notice as the current-departure manual: it concerns departures in 2013.

Who and what is caught. Article I requires at least 6 of the preceding 10 years of French tax domicile for latent gains and earn-out receivables. The household’s directly or indirectly held securities trigger latent-gain taxation if they represent at least 50% of a company’s profits OR their aggregate value exceeds EUR 800,000. Those holding/value gates do not turn previously deferred gains under II into the same category: II independently brings its listed deferred gains into charge on departure. The deemed transfer is the day before worldwide French taxation ceases. Value less tax acquisition value gives the latent gain, with the exchange-basis and applicable allowance provisions in I(2)–(3). Latent losses cannot offset those gains or other gains. [CGI I–III](https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/codes/article_lc/LEGIARTI000048806379).

The progressive election is global for the household’s relevant capital income/gains, using 2OP; it is not a selective election for one favourable asset. Historic-rate deferred gains under article 150-0 B ter require their separate calculation. [CGI II bis](https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/codes/article_lc/LEGIARTI000048806379); [notice II, p4](https://www.impots.gouv.fr/sites/default/files/formulaires/2074-etd/2026/2074-etd_5518.pdf).

Suspension. IV grants automatic suspension for the EU or a destination with BOTH qualifying administrative and recovery assistance that is not noncooperative. The notice’s list for transfers from 2025 includes the UK, US, Australia, Norway and Monaco; it does not list Switzerland or the UAE. Test the current legal agreements and status for the actual destination/date rather than treating the dated list as immutable. V allows an express request for other destinations or a later onward move outside IV, with declaration, a representative in France and predeparture guarantees. It sets a 12.8%-of-gross provisional guarantee rule, a special historic-rate rule and possible adjustment within 1 month after the notice. No work-related security waiver is stated in this current V. [CGI IV–VI](https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/codes/article_lc/LEGIARTI000048806379); [notice III, pp4–5](https://www.impots.gouv.fr/sites/default/files/formulaires/2074-etd/2026/2074-etd_5518.pdf).

Acceleration and relief. Sale, redemption, repayment or cancellation can end suspension, subject to the express qualifying-exchange/contribution exceptions. Gifts and death differ between latent gains, the listed historical deferred-gain regimes and earn-out claims; never apply one category’s exception to all categories. Qualifying latent gains are relieved/refunded after the 2- or 5-year period if the required securities remain held, or on an earlier qualifying return. The statute says the 5-year period applies only above EUR 2.57 million; equality therefore stays on the 2-year side, despite inconsistent equality shorthand in the notice. This time relief does not discharge every deferred gain or receivable. On return, VII(3) restores the predeparture tax position for retained deferred-gain securities; VII(4) limits earn-out relief to the retained claim, after intervening receipts. Donation, death, lower actual gains/losses and foreign tax can also change the charge under their own VII–VIII conditions. [CGI VII–VIII bis](https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/codes/article_lc/LEGIARTI000048806379).

Historic periods remain important: 2014–2018 departures have the 15-year latent-gain retention period; the historical 8-year period belongs to the 2011–2013 regime, not every departure before 2014. The ETS1 notice covers 3 March 2011–2012; ETS2 covers 2013. Relief still needs the appropriate evidence and follow-up, including the separately dated extension of social-levy relief. [ETS3 notice pp1–4/§430](https://www.impots.gouv.fr/sites/default/files/formulaires/2074-ets3/2026/2074-ets3_5526.pdf); [ETS1 notice p1](https://www.impots.gouv.fr/sites/default/files/formulaires/2074-ets1/2026/2074-ets1_5522.pdf); [ETS2 notice p1](https://www.impots.gouv.fr/sites/default/files/formulaires/2074-ets2/2026/2074-ets2_5524.pdf).

Forms and timing. IX normally requires the initial declaration in the year AFTER departure, within the income-return deadline. The 2026 notice for 2025 transfers uses paper 2074-ETD with 2042/2042 C; an express suspension request instead requires an initial ETD at least 90 days BEFORE departure and a second filing the next year. The deadline comes from [annex III, article 41 tervicies A](https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/codes/article_lc/LEGIARTI000038950368/): “au plus tard quatre-vingt-dix jours avant”. This controls over the inconsistent “dans les quatre-vingt-dix jours précédant” wording on notice p5. Follow-up since 2014 uses ETS3: latent-gains-only cases after 2018 are generally event-based; deferred gains/receivables and mixed cases retain annual reporting, with ETSL available in specified no-event cases. Subsequent changes of tax domicile must be notified within 2 months; the move may itself end automatic suspension. Do not invent a calendar deadline or a final 2027 form for a 2026 departure. [CGI IX–X](https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/codes/article_lc/LEGIARTI000048806379); [ETD notice V–IX, pp7–9](https://www.impots.gouv.fr/sites/default/files/formulaires/2074-etd/2026/2074-etd_5518.pdf); [ETS3 notice I–III](https://www.impots.gouv.fr/sites/default/files/formulaires/2074-ets3/2026/2074-ets3_5526.pdf). The authority’s 2026 simulator labels 8TN for tax on gains in payment suspension; that confirms the old code only for its stated 2025-income version, not universal 2026-departure filing instructions. [Actual simulator field](https://simulateur-ir-ifi.impots.gouv.fr/calcul_impot/2026/complet/). For depth use `leaving-france-exit-tax`.

## Netherlands: the conserverende aanslag

Scope and trigger. [Wet IB 2001 4.16(1)(h)](https://wetten.overheid.nl/BWBR0011353) treats cessation of domestic tax liability otherwise than death as a deemed disposal of a substantial interest. It is not restricted to Dutch-incorporated companies. The 4.18 exception for a non-Dutch-established company requires departure within 8 years after arrival AND no more than 10 total years of Dutch residence in the preceding 25 years. Check its full facts before charging foreign-company shares. Article 4.6 includes at least 5% held directly/indirectly, alone or with a partner, in shares, acquisition rights, profit certificates and specified voting interests; class rules and family attribution can extend scope under the adjacent provisions. Article 4.3 separately equates a person entitled only to benefits from shares (rights of enjoyment, genotsrechten) with a shareholder and treats that entitlement as a share; it does the corresponding thing for benefits from profit certificates. These are distinct from acquisition rights. [Statutory equivalence 4.3](https://wetten.overheid.nl/BWBR0011353). [Statute 4.6–4.7/4.16–4.18](https://wetten.overheid.nl/BWBR0011353); [2026 authority substantial-interest explanation](https://www.belastingdienst.nl/wps/wcm/connect/fisin/fisin2026/aanmerkelijk_belang).

The protective assessment can also concern relieved Dutch pension accrual, deducted annuity premiums and specified own-home savings products. Those categories have their own scope and relief; do not apply the substantial-interest rule to them. [Authority emigration assessment](https://www.belastingdienst.nl/wps/wcm/connect/nl/buitenland/content/conserverende-aanslag-bij-emigratie).

Payment and security. The authority gives automatic deferral for EU/EEA moves; elsewhere request it using the assessment procedure, with security for a substantial interest (the Norway/Iceland/Liechtenstein exception belongs to the EEA treatment). A bank guarantee, mortgage or pledge can provide security. An onward move outside the protected area must be reported and may require a new request/security. Current Invorderingswet 25(8) regulates the deferral and its termination, including disposal, distributions and capital repayments, with specific continuation/credit limits; it is not an unconditional lifelong nonpayment promise. [Current collection statute](https://wetten.overheid.nl/BWBR0004770); [2026 authority preserved-income section 26.2.1](https://www.belastingdienst.nl/wps/wcm/connect/fisin/fisin2026/te_conserveren_inkomen).

Interest requires care. Invorderingswet 28(3) excludes collection interest during specified qualifying deferral; 28(4) makes an exception when the assessment is collected after termination or late payment. The authority’s general emigration page says no interest during deferral, whereas its 2026 substantial-interest section expressly identifies an exception. Use the statutory 28(3)/(4) distinction; this Guide neither guarantees an interest-free lifetime nor calculates the delegated interest period. [Actual 28](https://wetten.overheid.nl/BWBR0004770); [current substantial-interest guidance](https://www.belastingdienst.nl/wps/wcm/connect/fisin/fisin2026/te_conserveren_inkomen).

Duration and acceleration. The authority distinguishes its normal 10-year protective assessment from the unlimited substantial-interest assessment. For departure from 15 September 2015 at 15:15, no ordinary remission after 10 years applies; pre-cutoff substantial-interest cases can request remission subject to continued ownership/company conditions. Report sale, distributions and cessation; only the amount required by the applicable collection provisions becomes payable. [Authority dated transition](https://www.belastingdienst.nl/wps/wcm/connect/nl/buitenland/content/conserverende-aanslag-bij-emigratie); [25(8)](https://wetten.overheid.nl/BWBR0004770).

Return. The former source gap is resolved: UBIB 16(5) reduces the protective assessment for an individual becoming a domestic taxpayer, but only to the extent its tax is still deferred under 25(8). Wet IB 4.25/UBIB 16 separately regulate arrival acquisition value, including prior domestic/foreign tax and Dutch-company exceptions. This is not a blanket refund of every amount already collected or an unconditional market-value step-up. [UBIB 16](https://wetten.overheid.nl/BWBR0012066); [Wet IB 4.25](https://wetten.overheid.nl/BWBR0011353).

## Norway: exit tax on shares, after the 2024 changes

What triggers it. Moving out of Norway and becoming resident in another country under a tax treaty, being registered as emigrated for tax purposes, or becoming tax resident in Svalbard, after having been tax resident in Norway under both the Taxation Act and any relevant treaty. Gifts and gift sales to persons resident abroad, and inheritance passing to heirs abroad (inheritance from a Norwegian estate from 1 January 2025), are also caught ([Skatteetaten, exit tax](https://www.skatteetaten.no/en/person/to-from-norway/from/tax-when-you-move-abroad/exit-tax/)). Registration of a residential address in Norway is not relevant.

Which rules apply depends on the date of the move. The page splits moves on or before 19 March 2024 from moves on or after 20 March 2024, with further changes from 7 October 2024 and 1 January 2025. This section states the rules for moves on or after 20 March 2024. For a move on or before 28 November 2022, the conditional liability lasted five years and then lapsed.

What is taxed. Latent gains on shares in Norwegian or foreign companies, units in securities funds, share savings accounts, investment fund accounts, employment options, interests in companies assessed as partnerships, financial instruments with such underlying assets, and assets in foreign pension savings schemes when considered a regular investment account (the page names IRAs and 401(k)s). Exit tax is calculated only on values accumulated while the person was tax resident in Norway. On a move there is a basic deduction, shown below; only the total gain or loss above it is subject to exit tax. On a move within the EEA or to Svalbard, gains and losses are netted before the deduction. On a move outside the EEA, only gains are added up; losses are not included. For gifts and other transfers, the NOK 100,000 net-gain threshold is an annual aggregate cliff, not a per-gift deduction: above it the whole net gain is caught. Coordinate transfers and an actual move in the same year under 10-70(4); do not double-use the move deduction. EEA loss relief also has later recapture/return conditions. [10-70(2)–(4)](https://lovdata.no/lov/1999-03-26-14/%C2%A710-70).

## Spain: article 95 bis LIRPF

Who is caught. A taxpayer who loses Spanish taxpayer status by change of residence, and who was a taxpayer in at least ten of the fifteen tax periods before the last period that must be declared ([article 95 bis Ley 35/2006](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-2006-20764)). For a person who used the special regime for workers posted to Spain, the ten periods are counted from the first period in which that regime no longer applied.

Article 95 bis is the law of common-regime Spain. The Basque historical territories and Navarre apply their own income tax law ("sin perjuicio de los regímenes tributarios forales de concierto y convenio económico en vigor, respectivamente, en los Territorios Históricos del País Vasco y en la Comunidad Foral de Navarra", [article 4 Ley 35/2006](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-2006-20764)); this Guide does not cover them.

What is taxed. The positive difference between market value and acquisition value of shares and units of any type of entity, if EITHER the market value of all such holdings together exceeds the first amount in the table, OR, where that test is not met, the person holds more than the percentage in the table in one entity on the accrual date of the last period and the market value of that holding exceeds the second amount. Under the second test only the gain on that holding is taxed. The gain is savings income, imputed to the last period that must be declared, by a complementary self-assessment without penalty, late-payment interest or surcharge. Listed shares use their quotation on accrual; unlisted shares use the higher of the last closed balance-sheet equity and the average results of the last 3 closed years capitalised at 20%, unless another market value is proved. Investment-fund interests use the applicable net-asset-value rule and fallback under 95 bis(3)(c).

Move to another EU or EEA state. Where the new residence is in another EU member state, or an EEA state with effective exchange of tax information, the taxpayer can opt for special rules: the gain is self-assessed only if, within the ten periods after the last one declared, the shares are transferred inter vivos, the person stops being resident in an EU or EEA state, or the person fails to make the required communication. The option, the gain, the new state and address, later changes, and continued ownership must be communicated to the tax authority. If the person becomes a Spanish taxpayer again before any of those events, the rule has no effect.

Temporary move elsewhere. On the taxpayer's request, payment is deferred where the change of residence is a temporary move for work to a country or territory that is not a tax haven, or a temporary move for any other reason to a country with a double tax treaty with Spain containing an exchange of information clause. Interest accrues and guarantees are required; the guarantees can be given over the shares. If the person becomes a Spanish taxpayer again within the five periods after the last one declared without having transferred the shares, the deferred debt and the interest are extinguished. For a work move the five periods can be extended, by no more than five more periods.

Return after paying. A person who becomes a Spanish taxpayer again without having transferred the shares can ask for rectification of the self-assessment and a refund, with interest from the date of payment.

Tax havens. The article also applies where a person moves to a tax haven but keeps Spanish taxpayer status under article 8.2, with the gain imputed to the last period of habitual residence in Spain.

## Italy

Current TUIR 166 applies to persons carrying on commercial businesses. Its events cover residence transfer and specified asset/business transfers or restructurings that remove Italian taxing rights; assets retained in the Italian permanent establishment are treated under the relevant exceptions. The taxable value uses the event-specific market-value rules, including goodwill where applicable, less the tax basis; losses have separate limitations. An individual’s migration does not by itself establish that privately held company shares are a commercial business, and paragraph 14 says the company’s transfer does not itself tax its shareholders. ## Canada: departure tax

What triggers it. A person who ceased to be resident in Canada in the year is deemed to have disposed of certain property at fair market value when leaving and to have immediately reacquired it for the same amount ([CRA, dispositions of property for emigrants](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/international-non-residents/individuals-leaving-entering-canada-non-residents/dispositions-property.html)). This applies to most property.

What is not caught. The exceptions the page lists include: Canadian real or immovable property, Canadian resource property and timber resource property; Canadian business property (including inventory) where the business is carried on through a permanent establishment in Canada; pension plans, annuities, registered retirement savings plans, registered retirement income funds, registered education and disability savings plans, tax-free savings accounts and the other plans and rights the page lists as excluded rights or interests; and property owned on last becoming resident (or inherited afterwards) by an individual who was resident in Canada for 60 months or less in the ten years before emigrating. A person can elect to report a deemed disposition of the first two kinds of property.

The expatriation tax provisions of sections 877 and 877A of the Internal Revenue Code apply to US citizens who relinquish citizenship under the statutory event rules (including renunciation) and to long-term residents who have ended their US resident status for federal tax purposes. ## United Kingdom: departure and temporary non-residence

No UK charge on the unrealised gains of an individual who leaves is printed on the HMRC page linked in this section. The rule HMRC sets out for departures is temporary non-residence: a person who returns may pay tax in the year of return on certain income and gains received during the absence ([HMRC RDR3, section 7](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/rdr3-statutory-residence-test-srt/guidance-note-for-statutory-residence-test-srt-rdr3)). A person is temporarily non-resident if, after a residence period with sole UK residence, they have one or more residence periods without sole UK residence; AND in 4 or more of the 7 tax years before the year of departure they had sole UK residence for the tax year, or the year was a split year with a residence period of sole UK residence; AND the period of non-residence lasts 5 years or less. The statutory period is defined using the end of the preceding sole-UK-residence period and the start of the next one; raw travel dates alone are insufficient. HMRC’s dated example leaves on 4 May 2015 and returns on 4 May 2020 and says the rules do not apply. Preserve that illustration in its stated residence facts; those calendar dates are 1,827 days apart and do not themselves prove a universal “5 years plus 1 day” safe harbour. [FA 2013 Schedule 45 paragraphs 109–115](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2013/29/schedule/45/part/4/data.xht?wrap=true). Capital gains are among the items in scope. Current TCGA 1M/1N govern the gains rule and the conditional new-assets exclusion; old sections 10A/10AA are repealed. The current 2026–27 amendment separates specified carried-interest amounts, so this is not an all-income rule. Trust and company charges remain outside this Guide. [Current 1M](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1992/12/section/1M/data.xht); [1N](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1992/12/section/1N/data.xht).

## Planning checklist

1. Timing. Fix the departure date against each test: Germany's seven of twelve years, France's six of ten, Spain's ten of fifteen, and the United Kingdom's 4 of 7 years and five-year absence. Do not prescribe an automatic “leave before signing” saving: establish the actual disposal, residence and taxing-right dates. A later disposal can accelerate collection in France, Germany, the Netherlands, Norway and Spain, subject to their specific rollover/continuation exceptions.
2. Which assets. Map each holding against the tests: Germany's section 17 percentage and the fund rules; France's household tests; the Dutch substantial-interest percentage including the fiscal partner; Norway's wide asset list (including share savings accounts and some foreign pension accounts); Spain's two value tests; Canada's exceptions.
3. Getting the deferral. Germany: apply for the seven instalments and agree the security before the first notice. France: check that the destination is on the list for automatic suspension, or prepare a representative and guarantees. Netherlands: deferral is automatic only inside the EU or EEA. Norway: choose the payment route in the return; security for an EEA move requires the actual collection-risk condition. Spain: exercise and communicate the EU or EEA option, or request the work-move deferral with guarantees. Canada: file Form T1244 by April 30 of the following year.
4. Dividends and distributions. Germany makes the unpaid tax due to the statutory extent when distributions/capital repayments exceed the aggregate one-quarter departure-value limit; Norway takes the share of a distribution in the Norway table towards the deferred claim; the Netherlands makes the assessment (partly) payable on a dividend. Plan the dividend policy before leaving.
5. Coming back. Germany: seven years, extendable. France: apply the separate retained latent-gain, deferred-gain restoration and earn-out relief rules. Norway: before twelve years. Spain: five periods on the deferral route, or a refund claim after paying. Canada: the unwind election. Write the deadline into the family calendar.
6. Yearly filings. Germany by 31 July; Norway by 30 April; France category-appropriate ETS follow-up; Spain the communication of changes. Missing them can end the deferral.
