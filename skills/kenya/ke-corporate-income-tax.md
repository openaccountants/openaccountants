# Kenya corporate income tax (2026)

> **General reference only.** This skill is general tax/accounting reference material for AI-assisted workflows. It has not been reviewed for any specific person's facts, documents, elections, deadlines, residency, filing status, or local procedures. Do not rely on it to file, pay, amend, or take a tax position without review by a qualified professional in the relevant jurisdiction.

Ordinary corporation tax applies to adjusted taxable income, not bank receipts or a vague worldwide-income label. Confirm residence, source, permanent establishment and any special status before selecting a rate.

## Who this applies to

Resident companies and non-resident companies carrying on business through a Kenyan permanent establishment. Use ITA ss2-4,15-16 and the Second Schedule to move from accounting profit to taxable income. A business carried on partly inside and partly outside Kenya needs s4 analysis. This file does not replace the pack's transfer-pricing, VAT or payroll skills, or determine treaty residence.

## The base

Start with accounting profit. Exclude exempt/non-chargeable income, add back disallowed and capital expenses, deduct qualifying revenue expenditure wholly and exclusively incurred to produce income under s15(1), and claim current Second Schedule allowances rather than accounting depreciation. Separate capital gains and final-withholding income. Test losses under s15(4) and its conditions; do not presume unrestricted utilisation. Refer special-rate, EPZ/SEZ, NIFCA and cross-border cases.

For a branch, repatriated income uses s7B: opening net assets + (accounting net profit - tax on chargeable income) - closing net assets. It is a separate tax question from the ordinary branch rate. Accounting profit and taxable profit are different inputs.

| WHT remittance and certificate | Within 5 working days after deduction, s35(5), in words on the page; replaces following-month 20th | https://www.kra.go.ke/images/publications/Income-Tax-Act-2026.pdf |

Instalment amount is the lesser of the current-year basis and 110% of preceding-year assessment, less specified withholding/advance credits under s12(2)-(3), in words on the page. Reconcile credits before splitting payments; do not treat the prior-year figure alone as the safe-harbour amount.

Log into iTax (https://itax.kra.go.ke), select the company's income-tax obligation and accounting period, complete the company self-assessment with accounts and tax adjustments, validate, submit and retain acknowledgement. Generate the payment registration/slip for the correct obligation and period and match receipts to the ledger. Live box names and attachment requirements must be checked before submission; these statutes do not supply screen labels.

## Penalties and interest

[TPA ss38,83(1)(d)(i),83A](https://www.kra.go.ke/images/publications/Tax-Procedures-Act--2026.pdf): a late ordinary company return attracts the higher of 5% of tax payable or KES 20,000, with tax reduced by amounts paid and WHT credits; late tax attracts a 5% penalty and simple interest at 1% per month or part (in words on the page). Special returns have separate minima. Refer deliberate shortfalls, assessments and contested instalment penalties.

## Cross-border points

Non-resident dividend WHT is ordinarily 15%, not the old file's blanket 10%. A qualifying EAC-citizen dividend is governed by the actual s2 definition and para5(e), not a blanket listed-share assumption. Source, beneficial ownership, treaty, exemption and documentation must be established before applying a reduced WHT rate. Interest is not universally 15%: identify the instrument and recipient. Preserve the dividend exemption voting-power test; shareholding percentage alone may not prove it.

## Two worked examples that recompute

An ordinary resident company has hypothetical KES 1,000,000 accounting profit, KES 100,000 non-deductible expense and KES 50,000 capital allowances. Taxable profit is KES 1,050,000, tax KES 315,000; KES 200,000 credited instalments leave KES 115,000. No other exemption or loss is assumed.

```oa-check
name: Company adjustment and instalment credit
inputs:
  profit: 1000000
  addbacks: 100000
  allowances: 50000
  credits: 200000
steps:
  - taxable: profit + addbacks - allowances
  - tax: taxable * 0.30
  - balance: tax - credits
expected:
  taxable: 1050000
  tax: 315000
  balance: 115000
```

Branch decision: with hypothetical accounting and taxable profit both KES 1,000,000, opening and closing net assets both KES 2,000,000, ordinary tax is KES 300,000. Statutory repatriated income is KES 700,000 and its tax KES 105,000. Total KES 405,000 differs from both a subsidiary's retained-profit tax and the old blanket branch rate; choose structure on all facts, including distributions and liability.

```oa-check
name: Branch ordinary and repatriated tax distinction
inputs:
  profit: 1000000
  opening: 2000000
  closing: 2000000
steps:
  - ordinary: profit * 0.30
  - repatriated: max(0, opening + profit - ordinary - closing)
  - repatriation_tax: repatriated * 0.15
  - combined: ordinary + repatriation_tax
expected:
  ordinary: 300000
  repatriated: 700000
  repatriation_tax: 105000
  combined: 405000
```

## Ask the client first

- Company residence, ownership, Kenyan activity and any permanent establishment/treaty.
- Accounting period, accounts, expense support, asset register, exemptions and losses.
- NIFCA or other special-status certificates and exact effective years.
- Dividends, interest and royalties: recipient, instrument, gross payment, treaty evidence, voting control and withholding certificates.
- Instalment estimates, prior assessment, tax already paid and credited.
- For a branch: opening/closing net assets, accounting profit and chargeable-income tax.
