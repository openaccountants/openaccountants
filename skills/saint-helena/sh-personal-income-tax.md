# Saint Helena island: personal income tax v0.3

> **General reference only.** This skill is general tax reference material for AI-assisted workflows. It has not been reviewed for any specific person's facts. It covers Saint Helena island only, not Ascension or Tristan da Cunha, which have separate laws.

## Who this applies to

Individuals with income earned, accrued or derived in or from Saint Helena: employees, the self-employed, landlords and pensioners. The tax year runs 1 April to 31 March. For residence tests see [individual tax residence](sh-individual-tax-residence.md); for employers see [PAYE](sh-paye-compliance.md).

## The base

Chargeable income is taxable income less deductions, then allowances, floored at nil (s.7). The bands apply after deductions and allowances. Allowances come first off income other than dividends and gains, then dividends, then gains (s.7(2)). Do not grant a full year of allowance merely because the person meets a residence test at some point.

Mixed ordinary and production income: common deductions are apportioned, allowances come first off ordinary income, and if total chargeable income exceeds £18,000 the first ordinary band is reduced by the production income (Schedule 1 para 1(3)). Do not grant two independent £18,000 lower bands.

## Deadlines and filing

Who files. If your only taxable income is employment income and PAYE was correctly deducted and paid, no return is required (s.38), unless the Commissioner asks for one (s.37(2)). A return is required for self-employment, business, property or capital gains or losses, for untaxed income, or to claim a PAYE refund (Tax Guide 3).

Dates. For 1 April 2025 to 31 March 2026: 30 June 2026 for self-employment, business, property and capital gains or losses; 31 July 2026 for employees claiming a PAYE refund (official reminder). For income earned in 2026/27 the equivalents are 30 June 2027 and three months from receipt of the certificate (normally 31 July 2027). A refund claim made after three months is lost, and the deducted tax is treated as correct (s.39(2)). Tax falls due three months after the return date, normally 30 September (s.43). If you leave permanently, or will not return before the due date without arrangements, file at least 14 days before departure (s.37(3)).

How to file:
1. Collect your P5 or P60 certificate from each employer, and business or rental records.
2. Download the Individual Income Tax Return (P50 IND) from the Relevant Forms section of the [Income Tax page](https://www.sainthelena.gov.sh/public-services/income-tax/).
3. Enter income, deductions and allowances. The return self-assesses the tax.
4. Deliver it to the Income Tax Office, 1st Floor, Post Office Building, Jamestown. For help contact Norma Thomas or Pamela Joshua on 25880 (official reminder).
5. Pay any balance by the due date. A refund or balance follows from the return; enquiries come later (Tax Guide 3).

Extensions: a business or self-employed person may apply in writing before the due date (s.39(3)). This does not move payment (s.43(2)). There is no extension for an employee refund return. Partners each report their share, and the partnership files its own return (Tax Guide 3). Voluntary prepayments are allowed (Tax Guide 3). Instalments follow the rules in the [company skill](sh-company-income-tax.md), at 15 days after months 3, 6, 9 and 12.

Allowance through PAYE: employees receive it through their tax code; the self-employed claim it on the return (Tax Guide 12).

## Penalties and interest

Late return: £100 plus £10 for each complete month (s.66). Late payment: 10% plus 1% a month after the first month (s.67), which the Commissioner may remit (s.67(4)). False statement: 75% if knowing or reckless, 20% otherwise (s.68). No s.68(2)(b) penalty if the person could not reasonably have known, or took a reasonably arguable position (s.68(4)).

## Cross-border points

A non-resident is taxed on Saint Helena income, and gets the monthly allowance only on Saint Helena employment income (s.10(3)(b)). Tax Guides 1 and 12 say non-residents get no allowance; the statute wins. See [cross-border points](sh-cross-border.md).

## Two worked examples that recompute

Assume twelve months of allowance, ordinary income only and no other relief.

| Ordinary taxable income | Allowance used | Chargeable income | Annual tax |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| £6,000 | £6,000 | £0 | £0 |
| £7,000 | £7,000 | £0 | £0 |
| £20,000 | £7,000 | £13,000 | £3,380 |
| £25,000 | £7,000 | £18,000 | £4,680 |
| £30,000 | £7,000 | £23,000 | £6,230 |

Example 1: £30,000.

```oa-check
name: Annual tax
inputs:
  income: 30000
  allowance: 7000
  band: 18000
steps:
  - chargeable: income - allowance
  - tax: band * 0.26 + (chargeable - band) * 0.31
expected:
  chargeable: 23000.00
  tax: 6230.00
```

Example 2: six qualifying months on £10,000. These are annual liability examples, not payroll table instructions.

```oa-check
name: Part-year allowance
inputs:
  income: 10000
  allowance: 7000
  months: 6
steps:
  - part: allowance * months / 12
  - tax: (income - part) * 0.26
expected:
  part: 3500.00
  tax: 1690.00
```


## Ask the client first

- Income types: employment, business, property, dividends, gains?
- Months resident in the tax year?
- Is PAYE correct, and is there a P5 or P60?
- Approved pension contributions, and employer contributions?
- Any income from a listed production activity?
- Leaving the island?
