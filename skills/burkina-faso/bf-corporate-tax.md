# Burkina Faso corporate tax (IS) v1.1

> **General reference only.** This skill is general tax/accounting reference material for AI-assisted workflows. It has not been reviewed for any specific person's facts, documents, elections, deadlines, residency, filing status, or local procedures. Do not rely on it to file, pay, amend, or take a tax position without review by a qualified professional in the relevant jurisdiction.

## Who this applies to

This covers ordinary IS for 2026 profits in Burkina Faso. Under CGI arts. 42–44, IS applies by form to SA, SAS, SARL including single-member companies, SNC, SCS, sociétés en participation, GIE, cooperatives and assimilated bodies. Activity-based liability also covers the specified public bodies and civil companies. The listed SNC, SCS, participation/de facto companies and GIE members can elect IBICA, with all associates signing and notification within three months of the fiscal-year start (art. 44). This is an election out of the default IS treatment, not the reverse. Financial syndicates and professional civil companies have the separate irrevocable IS option in art. 43(3). Do not apply IS to a sole trader merely because turnover is high. [CGI arts. 42–44](https://dgi.bf/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/CGI-2026-v2.pdf).

CME can replace IS for an eligible microenterprise; determine the regime using [business income](bf-income-tax.md). Statutory exemptions in art. 45 and special sectors are outside this ordinary computation.

## The base

Start from accounts, reconcile to taxable profit, round that profit down to XOF 1,000, then apply IS, including the RSI CGA profit-tax reduction where art. 196 applies. Profit includes all operations and asset disposals; it is not cash received alone (arts. 48–51). A deductible expense must belong to the period, serve the direct business interest or normal management, be real, sufficiently documented and booked as an expense, reduce net assets, and generate non-exempt income. The specific conditions in arts. 54–84 still apply; the general test does not override them (art. 53). [CGI arts. 48–54](https://dgi.bf/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/CGI-2026-v2.pdf).

Remuneration must correspond to actual work. For managing associates of capital companies and their spouses, excessive or fictitious remuneration makes the entire payment a deemed distribution, not just the excess (art. 56). Personnel charges cannot be provisioned; required remuneration declarations must be timely (art. 61). Tax/social/customs and other statutory fines, confiscations, penalties and surcharges are not deductible (art. 74(3)). A provision is not deductible just because booked; its art. 79 conditions and arts. 80–82 restrictions require separate examination. [CGI arts. 54, 56, 61, 74 and 79–82](https://dgi.bf/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/CGI-2026-v2.pdf).

For art. 62, the overhead denominator means indirect operating/financial costs of ordinary management, excluding purchases, depreciation, provisions and exchange losses, not all expenses. Apply its combined fee cap only after proving the precise services and related-party scope. Do not treat the cap as permission to deduct unsupported fees.

Compute the MFP separately, with its rounding, floor, exclusive-activity rule, CGA reduction and first-period exemption. Pay the larger of ordinary IS and applicable MFP, then reconcile valid advances and credits. Art. 92 carries excess advances forward, permits refund on cessation, and allows allocation to other direct State taxes on request. IRCM withheld on investment income included in taxable profits is creditable under arts. 93–94; unused credit carries forward, with cessation refund/requested offset rules, but an IS-exempt company's IRCM credit is acquired by the Treasury. [CGI arts. 87–94](https://dgi.bf/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/CGI-2026-v2.pdf).

## Deadlines and filing

1. Close ordinary annual accounts at 31 December. A new company created no later than 30 June closes that year; one created later can close at the next year's 31 December. The first period rules affect both tax and exemption periods (art. 49).
2. Prepare the administration's annual taxable-result declaration and art. 97 attachments: applicable normal SYSCOHADA/special-framework financial statements; tax package; detailed expense/income accounts; annual salary statement; commissions, brokerage, rebates, fees, royalties and other third-party remuneration; associates' remuneration/profit shares and other income; depreciation/provisions; premises rents with landlords; and bad debts identifying debtor, origin date, initial/outstanding/write-off amounts, guarantee nature/value, transfer date and collection stage. Financial statements are supplied in three copies under the statute. For statutory-audit companies obtain certified statements and execution attestation; otherwise check the visa requirement. Arrêté 2025-0148 art. 19 confines the transitional visa procedure to RNI companies. Obtain an appropriately authorised local professional for this step; the Guide is not a visa report. [CGI art. 97 and reproduced order arts. 18–19](https://dgi.bf/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/CGI-2026-v2.pdf).
3. File by 30 April following the closed year; insurance/reinsurance companies use 31 May. A 2026 calendar-year profit return therefore falls in 2027. DGE taxpayers attach the transfer instruction where applicable. Pay the balance by the declaration deadline (arts. 92, 95).
4. During the year pay equal IS advances by 15 July, 15 October and 15 January; do not use the old Guide's 20th. Reconcile the payment against the taxpayer account. The statutory payee is the competent tax receiver; payment is governed by art. 671. [CGI arts. 91–92, 95](https://dgi.bf/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/CGI-2026-v2.pdf).

The DGI's public source index links eSINTAX, but current return IDs, box numbers, upload sequence, confirmation and payment-reference screens have not been verified. Do not describe these preparation steps as a completed filing workflow. Industrial companies, mobile operators, importers, wholesalers and semi-wholesalers also check the customer-list obligation in art. 100; the covered customer threshold is XOF 5 million excluding tax. [CGI art. 100](https://dgi.bf/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/CGI-2026-v2.pdf).

## Penalties and interest

For a missing/late profit declaration, art. 754(1) imposes 10% of duties, minimum XOF 50,000; recurrence raises it to 25% with the same minimum. After formal notice, the filed-return branch is 25%, rising to 50% for the stated recurrence/assessment conditions. No filing within thirty days after notice triggers 100%, minimum XOF 100,000 (art. 754(2)–(3)). Incorrect bases bring 25% for good faith, 50% for established bad faith or 100% for fraud, doubled on recurrence (art. 755). Late/insufficient payment brings 10% plus 1% per month or fraction (art. 756). For the specified missing annual attachments/obligations, art. 757(1) sets XOF 200,000; art. 757(2) raises that fine to XOF 500,000 for loss-making taxpayers and XOF 1,500,000 for convention businesses or businesses with a temporary exemption regime. Separate transfer-pricing branches in art. 757(2)–(4) are not this ordinary-attachment fine; art. 758 imposes 25% for late advance declarations or 50% on assessment. Do not assume these sanctions replace one another or add them without examining the facts. [CGI arts. 754–758](https://dgi.bf/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/CGI-2026-v2.pdf).

## Cross-border points

Art. 46 taxes Burkina-operated enterprise profits and profits allocated by treaty. Residence is based on seat/effective management; non-resident PEs and a complete commercial cycle are separate statutory routes, subject to treaty provisions. Detailed art. 47 PE tests and treaty-specific attribution remain outside this calculation. Use [transfer pricing](bf-transfer-pricing.md) for controlled transactions and [non-resident services withholding](bf-nonresident-services-withholding.md) for that withholding job. A dividend/interest rate in the table is not a treaty entitlement or a complete distribution-return procedure. [CGI arts. 46–47, 140–141](https://dgi.bf/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/CGI-2026-v2.pdf).

## Two worked examples that recompute

**Ordinary IS and balance.** An established non-CGA RNI company has adjusted profit XOF 12,345,678 and turnover excluding VAT XOF 200,099,999. Rounded profit is XOF 12,345,000; IS is XOF 3,394,875. MFP is XOF 1,000,000 after turnover rounding and floor. IS prevails. If prior-year IS was XOF 4 million, each current advance is XOF 1 million and the balance, with no other credits, is XOF 394,875.

```oa-check
name: Ordinary company IS and advances
inputs:
  profit: 12345678
  turnover: 200099999
  prior_tax: 4000000
steps:
  rounded_profit: floor(profit / 1000) * 1000
  is_tax: rounded_profit * 0.275
  mfp: max(floor(turnover / 100000) * 100000 * 0.005, 1000000)
  tax: max(is_tax, mfp)
  each_advance: prior_tax * 0.75 / 3
  balance: tax - each_advance * 3
expected:
  rounded_profit: 12345000
  is_tax: 3394875
  mfp: 1000000
  each_advance: 1000000
  balance: 394875
```

**Decision: which amount controls?** For an established non-CGA RNI company, turnover XOF 60 million and taxable profit XOF 1 million produce IS XOF 275,000 and MFP XOF 1 million. The MFP controls. For otherwise identical first-operating-period facts, art. 90 removes MFP, so IS XOF 275,000 controls. The XOF 725,000 difference is an exemption effect, not an optional tax election.

```oa-check
name: Established versus first-period minimum
inputs:
  profit: 1000000
  turnover: 60000000
steps:
  is_tax: profit * 0.275
  established_mfp: max(turnover * 0.005, 1000000)
  established_tax: max(is_tax, established_mfp)
  first_period_tax: max(is_tax, 0)
  difference: established_tax - first_period_tax
expected:
  established_tax: 1000000
  first_period_tax: 275000
  difference: 725000
```

## Ask the client first

- What is the legal form, election history and actual activity, including any special exemption?
- Which regime applies, and is CGA membership effective for this period?
- When did operations start and which accounting period is being closed?
- What are turnover excluding VAT, adjusted profit, related-party/head-office charges and gift evidence?
- Which advances, withholding credits and losses are supported by the tax account?
- Are there foreign operations, distributions or insurance/reinsurance activities?
