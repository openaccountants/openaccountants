# Brazil cross-border income tax in 2026 v0.1

> **General reference only.** This skill is general tax/accounting reference material for AI-assisted workflows. It has not been reviewed for any specific person's facts, documents, elections, deadlines, residency, filing status, or local procedures. Do not rely on it to file, pay, amend, or take a tax position without review by a qualified professional in the relevant jurisdiction.

**Quick reference.** Establish residence on the actual receipt/payment date before choosing a base or rate. A temporary visitor's day test is 184 days in a period of up to 12 months, with earlier residence for the employment/permanent-entry conditions below. Outbound Brazilian dividends generally attract 10% withholding from January 2026 without the resident individual's BRL 50,000 monthly threshold. Residents' qualifying foreign financial income has a separate annual 15% regime. A foreign invoice does not itself establish treaty exemption.

**Required inputs:** dated travel and immigration history; departure filings and payer notifications; recipient's beneficial ownership and tax residence; contract and payment/credit dates; income classification and gross/net tax clause; any foreign tax actually paid and recoverable. Currency: Brazilian reais (BRL). The year here is calendar 2026 income, not necessarily the year printed on a return filed in 2026.

## Who this applies to

This Guide covers Brazilian resident individuals with ordinary foreign income, people becoming resident/non-resident, Brazilian payers remitting income abroad and non-residents with ordinary Brazilian-source receipts. Companies also need their IRPJ/CSLL and transfer-pricing analysis; personal residence rules must never be applied to a company's permanent establishment.

[IN SRF 208/2002 arts. 2-4](https://normas.receita.fazenda.gov.br/sijut2consulta/link.action?idAto=15079) and [Receita's residence explanation](https://www.gov.br/receitafederal/pt-br/assuntos/meu-imposto-de-renda/preenchimento/dsdp/nao-residente) distinguish:

- Permanent residence in Brazil, entry with a permanent visa and a Brazilian non-resident's return with definitive intent: residence under the relevant arrival/permanence condition.
- Temporary entry for employment or the specified Mais Médicos scholarship: residence from arrival. Obtaining permanent status or employment before the day threshold can trigger residence earlier.
- Other temporary entry: residence on completing 184 days, consecutive or not, within up to 12 months. If that total is not reached, the regulation starts a new measuring period from the next entry after the arrival that began the previous count. This is not a calendar-year 183-day exemption.
- Permanent departure with the required communication: non-residence from departure. Temporary absence, or permanent departure without the required communication, generally retains residence for the initial 12 consecutive months, then changes from the following day. Keep the exact departure and intervening-return evidence.
- People sent abroad as employees of Brazilian government offices have a specific continuing-residence rule. That category does not automatically include a state-owned company's employee or locally hired embassy worker.

The acquisition/loss of status changes the treatment from that date. A CPF, bank account or annual return by itself is not a substitute for the statutory facts. Notify payers in writing so they use the correct status.

**Out of scope:** full resident IRPF computation, pensions/retirement judicial developments, diplomatic/international-organisation exemptions, foreign controlled-company and trust attribution, financial-market non-resident investor regimes, shipping/aircraft/lease special remittances, cross-border reorganisations, treaty-by-treaty PE and beneficial-owner analysis, social-security treaties, customs and import-tax calculations. Escalate those facts rather than defaulting to the general rate.

## The base

### Resident foreign receipts

Ordinary foreign-source remuneration, rent and similar receipts are taxable under the resident system even if never transferred to Brazil. [IN 208 art. 16](https://normas.receita.fazenda.gov.br/sijut2consulta/link.action?idAto=15079) says "transferidos ou não para o País" and uses monthly carnê-leão plus the annual return. Convert foreign receipts/tax under the applicable statutory exchange rule; the ordinary art. 16 conversion is not necessarily the same conversion as the foreign-financial-income credit below. Obtain the official Banco Central rate for the specified date and direction rather than using the bank's net cash conversion.

The ordinary monthly IRPF calculation must use the 2026 reduction introduced by [Lei 15.270](https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/_ato2023-2026/2025/lei/l15270.htm#art2). Do not copy the 2025 resident table or call the tax year 2026 merely because a 2025 return is filed then. Detailed resident bands, deductions and reduction calculations belong to `br-income-tax` and `br-estimated-tax`, whose currency must be checked before reuse.

**Foreign financial investments are a different route.** [Lei 14.754 arts. 2-4](https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/_ato2023-2026/2023/lei/l14754.htm#art2) separately taxes realised qualifying investment income annually. Its definition includes remunerated accounts/deposits, qualifying virtual assets/digital wallets, securities and non-controlled foreign shareholdings; gains include relevant currency appreciation on realisation. A transfer of principal is not itself the entire taxable return. Non-remunerated accounts at recognised authorised foreign financial institutions have the art. 2 §3 currency-variation exception. **Losses are distinct from base deductions:** [Lei 14.754 art. 9](https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/_ato2023-2026/2023/lei/l14754.htm#art9) allows documented realised foreign-financial losses against foreign-financial income in the same DAA period. Excess may offset qualifying foreign-controlled-entity profits/dividends included in that same period; remaining losses may offset income in that separate DAA component in later periods, once only. The law says "perdas realizadas" and "períodos de apuração posteriores". Track the loss origin, use and balance; this is different from the non-carryable unused foreign tax credit. A non-financial foreign asset disposal remains under separate capital-gains law, and a controlled entity/trust needs the specific attribution regime. Do not route all foreign receipts through carnê-leão or all through 15% annual investment tax.

For qualifying foreign-financial-income credits, art. 4 requires a treaty providing credit or reciprocity, caps relief at the corresponding Brazilian tax and excludes foreign tax that is refundable/recoverable/compensable. Convert foreign tax at Banco Central's purchase closing rate for its actual payment date. Unused foreign tax cannot be carried to earlier or later years under art. 4 §4. Do not net different countries or income categories without the statutory analysis. For ordinary foreign income use IN 208 art. 16 with its own credit and annual reconciliation rules, rather than importing this annual-investment rule wholesale.

### Non-resident Brazilian receipts and gains

Income is classified by the contract's substance, source and legal availability: payment, credit, employment, delivery or remittance may be relevant before cash reaches a foreign bank. For technical services, [RIR art. 765](https://www2.camara.leg.br/legin/fed/decret/2018/decreto-9580-22-novembro-2018-787360-normaatualizada-pe.html) states the rate is independent of where services were performed or contracted. Treaty business-profits/royalty/technical-service provisions and protocols may change the result; geography alone does not.

For a net-of-tax contract where the Brazilian payer bears the recipient's income tax, gross up the taxable amount under [RIR art. 786](https://www2.camara.leg.br/legin/fed/decret/2018/decreto-9580-22-novembro-2018-787360-normaatualizada-pe.html) before applying withholding. Do not subtract other import taxes from the IRRF base without the governing provision. Import services may separately require CIDE, PIS/Cofins-import, ISS and the CBS/IBS transition analysis; IRRF is not the full tax cost.

[IN 208 art. 26](https://normas.receita.fazenda.gov.br/sijut2consulta/link.action?idAto=15079) uses sale consideration less documented cost for Brazilian asset gains and denies resident-specific exemptions/reductions to a non-resident. [RIR art. 745](https://www2.camara.leg.br/legin/fed/decret/2018/decreto-9580-22-novembro-2018-787360-normaatualizada-pe.html) applies the progressive capital-gains bands and makes the Brazilian buyer, or the buyer's Brazilian attorney if the buyer is abroad, responsible for the tax on Brazilian assets. [Lei 8.981 art. 21 §§3-4](https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/leis/l8981.htm#art21) aggregates gains on partial disposals of the same asset through the end of the calendar year following the first disposal, crediting prior tax paid; shares/quotas of the same company count as the same asset. The law says "até o final do ano-calendário seguinte" and "ações ou quotas de uma mesma pessoa jurídica". Do not restart the lower band for every instalment or share sale. An old IN 208 flat-15% sentence is not authority to disregard later statutory bands. Favoured-tax-jurisdiction, listed-security and special-investment cases need separate rules before using the ordinary table.

### Outbound dividend conditions

From January 2026 [Lei 9.249 art. 10 §§4-5](https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/leis/l9249.htm#art10) imposes 10% on outbound dividends, subject to its exemptions. Section 5 II excludes qualifying reciprocal foreign-government receipts, statutory sovereign funds, and qualifying foreign pension-benefit administrators as defined in regulation. Verify the entity, not merely its public-sounding name.

The old-profit transition requires pre-2026 results, distribution approval by 31 December 2025 and an enforceable payment on the original approved terms. Do not label all old retained earnings exempt. [RFB's dividend Q&A Q19-20](https://www.gov.br/receitafederal/pt-br/centrais-de-conteudo/publicacoes/perguntas-e-respostas/dirf/manual_padrao_rfb_per_tributacao_cotin_v-19-12-2025.pdf/@@download/file) additionally describes payment through 2028; the outbound statute's §5 I does not itself contain that end-date. Record the statute/guidance difference and obtain case-specific review of the current rule and any court order before applying transitional exemption. The Guide does not resolve that discrepancy by inventing a deadline.

The optional foreign-beneficiary credit under art. 10-A addresses combined corporate/distribution taxation exceeding its statutory benchmark. It is not an automatic reduction of the initial 10% withholding. Its operational claim procedure is not confirmed here. Resident-individual dividends have a separate monthly threshold and annual minimum-income-tax analysis; neither supplies a foreign recipient exemption.

## Deadlines and filing

**Departure:**

1. Reconstruct the residence timeline from immigration/travel evidence. Decide whether this is a definitive departure or the end of the initial consecutive-absence period. Identify Brazilian payers and the representative.
2. In Receita's **Comunicação de Saída Definitiva do País** service, report the applicable departure/non-resident date and relevant dependants/representative. Submit and keep the receipt. IN 208 art. 11-A gives the February deadline in the table. This communication does not replace the final return.
3. Prepare **Declaração de Saída Definitiva do País (DSDP)** in the return program for the correct departure-income year. Enter the residence period, income and deductions for that period, assets/liabilities and payments using the departure-return option. IN 208 art. 9 §3 scales the monthly table by the resident months; it is not a full-year resident allowance by default. Apply the current annual instruction and program, including the 2026 changes when issued for 2027 filing.
4. Transmit, retain the return/receipt, and pay the single DARF by the applicable return deadline, reconciling any already-earlier tax obligations. Notify Brazilian banks and payers in writing of the non-resident status/date. Keep their acknowledgement. Reconcile pre-change resident income and post-change final withholding separately.

The exact 2027 filing deadline and program field numbers for a departure during 2026 are not yet confirmed. Do not reuse a filing date from the 2026 exercise or present the old general April date as a verified 2027 deadline. Official entry point: [Receita's departure hub](https://www.gov.br/receitafederal/pt-br/assuntos/meu-imposto-de-renda/preenchimento/dsdp/saida).

**Outbound dividends:** [RFB Q18,23](https://www.gov.br/receitafederal/pt-br/centrais-de-conteudo/publicacoes/perguntas-e-respostas/dirf/manual_padrao_rfb_per_tributacao_cotin_v-19-12-2025.pdf/@@download/file) states payment of non-resident dividend IRRF is due on the payment/credit/delivery/employment/remittance date and identifies DARF revenue code **1841, IRRF - Lucros ou Dividendos**. Quote: "na data do pagamento, crédito, entrega, emprego ou remessa" and "1841 - IRRF - Lucros ou Dividendos". Prepare the source transaction ledger and recipient status/exemption evidence before that event; save the payment confirmation and reconcile the applicable EFD-Reinf/DCTFWeb declaration. The current declaration event/field mapping has not been confirmed here. A general next-month withholding calendar must not postpone this same-day payment rule.

For other foreign remittances, identify the income-specific DARF code and statutory due date before payment/credit, then reconcile declarations and payment. Their codes/deadlines are not interchangeable with dividend code 1841. Resident ordinary foreign income uses monthly carnê-leão and annual reconciliation; foreign financial income uses its separate annual DAA component. Neither the recipient's foreign tax return nor a bank remittance receipt replaces Brazilian filing.

## Penalties and interest

The DSDP late-return penalty in IN 208 art. 13 is distinct from late tax payment. [Lei 9.430 art. 61](https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/leis/l9430.htm#art61) provides 0.33% daily late-payment penalty capped at 20%, plus SELIC from the month after the due month to the month before payment and 1% in the payment month. Use actual monthly factors, not an annual estimate. An assessed withholding shortfall can attract separate penalties under art. 44 and the applicable reporting provision.

Under [RIR art. 782](https://www2.camara.leg.br/legin/fed/decret/2018/decreto-9580-22-novembro-2018-787360-normaatualizada-pe.html), a payer remains responsible for withholding tax even if it failed to retain the money from the payee. Review treaty evidence and net/gross clauses before release. Declaration-specific penalties and special-case relief require the current official assessment rules, not an assumed universal fixed fine.

## Cross-border points

**Treaty workflow:** Open the applicable treaty and protocol through [Receita's treaty directory](https://www.gov.br/receitafederal/pt-br/acesso-a-informacao/legislacao/acordos-internacionais/acordos-para-evitar-a-dupla-tributacao). Verify entry into force and effective period, taxes/persons covered, residence/tie-breaker, beneficial ownership, PE, source and the exact income article. Technical services can be reclassified by a protocol; do not automatically apply business-profits exemption. Obtain the residence certificate and supporting ownership/substance evidence. Record both domestic tax and the treaty limitation, with the official article URL. An information-exchange or social-security agreement is not automatically an income-tax treaty.

**Companies:** A foreign company's Brazilian establishment or local business may require IRPJ/CSLL filing rather than only non-resident withholding. Analyse the domestic and treaty presence facts separately. Related-party prices, financing and royalties need the Lei 14.596 transfer-pricing and deduction tests. Resident companies' foreign profits/credits follow corporate law, not Lei 14.754's personal-investment 15% regime.

**Consumption taxes:** [LC 214 arts. 343,346,348](https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/leis/lcp/lcp214.htm#art343) has the 2026 CBS/IBS test and conditional payment dispensation; international supplies also need their import/export provisions. A cross-border income-tax exemption does not establish consumption-tax exemption. Detailed indirect-tax computations are outside this income-tax Guide.

## Two worked examples that recompute

### 1. Recipient-status decision: same amount, different withholding

Assume BRL 40,000 of post-2025 ordinary company profit distributed in one month, no treaty, recipient-specific exemption, old-profit transition or other distribution. To a resident individual, the amount does not cross the BRL 50,000 monthly threshold: no art. 6-A monthly dividend withholding on these facts. To a non-resident, BRL 4,000 is withheld and BRL 36,000 reaches the recipient. If the resident individual's same-company monthly total instead becomes BRL 60,000, withholding is BRL 6,000 on the whole total, not BRL 1,000 on the excess. Annual resident minimum tax is a separate check; this is not an election to change actual residence.

```oa-check
name: Dividend classification decision in 2026
inputs:
  distribution: 40000
  higher_month_total: 60000
steps:
  nonresident_irrf: distribution * 0.10
  nonresident_net: distribution - nonresident_irrf
  resident_below_limit: 0
  resident_above_limit: higher_month_total * 0.10
  wrong_excess_only: (higher_month_total - 50000) * 0.10
expected:
  nonresident_irrf: 4000
  nonresident_net: 36000
  resident_below_limit: 0
  resident_above_limit: 6000
  wrong_excess_only: 1000
```

### 2. Foreign financial income: foreign tax exceeds Brazilian capacity

Assume a resident's realised qualifying foreign investment income is BRL 80,000 after the applicable conversion, no losses or other relevant items, and eligible non-refundable foreign income tax of BRL 15,000 after conversion. Assume the required treaty-credit/reciprocity condition is established. Annual Brazilian tax before credit is BRL 12,000. Credit is limited to BRL 12,000, leaving zero Brazilian tax on this income. The BRL 3,000 unused foreign tax is not a carry-back or carry-forward under Lei 14.754 art. 4 §4. If the foreign amount were refundable, it would not qualify merely because cash was initially withheld.

```oa-check
name: Foreign financial income credit cap
inputs:
  income: 80000
  eligible_foreign_tax: 15000
steps:
  brazilian_tax: income * 0.15
  credit: min(eligible_foreign_tax, brazilian_tax)
  brazilian_balance: brazilian_tax - credit
  unused_foreign_tax: eligible_foreign_tax - credit
expected:
  brazilian_tax: 12000
  credit: 12000
  brazilian_balance: 0
  unused_foreign_tax: 3000
```

## Ask the client first

1. What are the exact arrival/departure/return dates, immigration and employment conditions, and evidence of definitive intent?
2. Were CSDP/DSDP filed, what dates do they record, and which Brazilian payers/banks were notified?
3. Who pays and beneficially receives the income, where are they tax resident, and what treaty/ownership evidence exists?
4. What do the contracts, invoices and actual work establish about services, royalties, dividends, JCP or asset/investment gains, and when did payment or credit occur?
5. What foreign tax was actually paid, can it be recovered, and which exchange rates and statutory dates apply?
6. Are there foreign entities/trusts, special investors, pensions, tax-favoured jurisdictions, old-profit distributions or a relevant court order?
