# Norway to Switzerland: the wealth-tax corridor, the 3-year tail, and the exit tax

## Scope

**Why this corridor needs a skill.** Norway levies an annual net wealth tax that falls on shareholdings, and the owner of a company usually funds it with dividends, which are taxed at an effective 37.84%. Switzerland also has a wealth tax, but it does not tax private capital gains on movable assets. The Norwegian exit tax on shares has been changed for moves from 29 November 2022, 20 March 2024 and 7 October 2024 _([Tax Act § 10-70, amendment history](https://lovdata.no/dokument/NL/lov/1999-03-26-14/*))_. The current text has no five-year expiry: the tax is settled within a 12-year period _([Tax Act § 10-70(7)](https://lovdata.no/dokument/NL/lov/1999-03-26-14/*))_. The residence rules have not changed: a person resident for 10 years or more (in words on the statute page) needs 3 full income years (in words on the statute page) abroad to stop being resident. Plan the move as a multi-year project.

**Who it is for.** Norwegian tax residents (owners, founders, investors) planning a move to Switzerland, and their advisers on both sides. Figures are for income year 2026.

---

## Ask the client first

- Exact move/treaty dates, residence years, all Norway days and every dwelling available to the person and close relatives?
- Covered assets, ownership/entry history, cost, market value, unused shield, losses and prior exit-tax assessments?
- Year-end residence/wealth facts, municipal rate, debt and the correct valuation base for each asset?
- Swiss private-investor status, canton, nationality, both spouses’ eligibility, work and household/living costs?
- Company management, distributions, planned sales, security and deferral elections, and treaty-residence evidence?

## The method, step by step

### Part 1: Why people leave (the arithmetic)

- **Wealth tax (formuesskatt), 2026.** Municipal tax up to 0.35% and state tax of 0.65% on net wealth above NOK 1,900,000, and state tax of 0.75% on the part above NOK 21,500,000. At the maximum municipal rate, the combined rates are 1.0% and 1.1% above NOK 21,500,000; use a lower municipal rate where adopted _(Stortingets skattevedtak 2026 §§ 2-1, 2-3; Skatteetaten, Formuesskatt 2026)_. Spouses taxed together have NOK 3,800,000 and NOK 43,000,000 _(skattevedtak § 2-1 second paragraph, § 2-3)_. [2026 tax decision §§ 2-1, 2-3](https://lovdata.no/dokument/STV/forskrift/2025-12-18-2747).
- **Valuation.** Listed shares are valued at 80% of the market price at 1 January of the assessment year. Unlisted Norwegian shares are valued at 80% of the share's part of the company's tax wealth value at 1 January of the year before _(skatteloven § 4-12 first and second paragraphs)_. Debt is reduced in proportion where assets are discounted _(skatteloven § 4-19)_. The primary home is valued at 25% of estimated market value, and 70% of the part above NOK 14,000,000; secondary homes at 100% _(skatteloven § 4-10 second and third paragraphs)_. [Tax Act §§ 2-1, 4-10, 4-12, 4-19, 10-11](https://lovdata.no/dokument/NL/lov/1999-03-26-14/*); [2026 share rate](https://www.skatteetaten.no/en/rates/factor-for-upward-adjustment-of-gainloss-or-dividend-on-shares/).
- **The funding loop.** Assume an owner living/taxable in Norway has an eligible undiscounted share valuation base of NOK 500,000,000, with the applicable municipal rate at its maximum. The discounted wealth value is NOK 400,000,000 (80%). Wealth tax is 1.0% x (21,500,000 - 1,900,000) = NOK 196,000, plus 1.1% x (400,000,000 - 21,500,000) = NOK 4,163,500, so NOK 4,359,500 in total (ignoring debt and other assets). Dividends to individuals are multiplied by 1.72 and taxed at 22%, an effective 37.84% _(skatteloven § 10-11 first paragraph; skattevedtak § 3-2 third paragraph)_. To keep NOK 4,359,500 after dividend tax, before any shielding deduction, the company must pay out about NOK 7,013,353. The tax is due in a loss year too. [Tax Act §§ 2-1, 4-10, 4-12, 4-19, 10-11](https://lovdata.no/dokument/NL/lov/1999-03-26-14/*); [2026 share rate](https://www.skatteetaten.no/en/rates/factor-for-upward-adjustment-of-gainloss-or-dividend-on-shares/).
- **New in 2026: deferral of wealth tax on business wealth.** An owner can defer wealth tax on shares, share savings accounts, partnership interests, business assets of a sole proprietorship that qualify for the statutory valuation discount, and commercial property for up to 3 income years (in words on the page) when the deferred tax exceeds NOK 30,000 in the year _(skattebetalingsloven § 9-3)_. Deferred tax and interest fall due on 31 May in the 3rd year after the assessment year (in words on the page) _(skattebetalingsloven § 10-51 fourth paragraph)_. The interest rate is 9% from 1 January 2026 and 9.25% from 1 July 2026 _(Skatteetaten, Renter ved utsatt betaling av formuesskatt)_. Deferral spreads the bill; it does not remove it. [Tax Payment Act §§ 9-3, 10-51(4)](https://lovdata.no/dokument/NL/lov/2005-06-17-67); [2026 interest rates](https://www.skatteetaten.no/satser/renter-ved-utsatt-betaling-av-fomuesskatt-beregnet-pa-virksomhetsformue/).
- **Who pays wealth tax.** From income year 2026 the duty to pay wealth tax depends on living in Norway at 31 December of the year before the assessment year _(skatteloven § 2-1 seventh paragraph, lov 22 December 2025 nr. 119)_. [Tax Act §§ 2-1, 4-10, 4-12, 4-19, 10-11](https://lovdata.no/dokument/NL/lov/1999-03-26-14/*); [2026 share rate](https://www.skatteetaten.no/en/rates/factor-for-upward-adjustment-of-gainloss-or-dividend-on-shares/).
- **Switzerland** also has a wealth tax: every canton must levy income and wealth tax on individuals _(StHG art. 2 para. 1 letter a)_, so this is not a zero-tax move. Switzerland does not tax capital gains on movable private assets, at federal or cantonal level _(DBG art. 16 para. 3; StHG art. 7 para. 4 letter b)_. [StHG arts. 2, 7(4)(b)](https://www.fedlex.admin.ch/filestore/fedlex.data.admin.ch/eli/cc/1991/1256_1256_1256/20250101/de/pdf-a/fedlex-data-admin-ch-eli-cc-1991-1256_1256_1256-20250101-de-pdf-a.pdf); [DBG art. 16(3)](https://www.fedlex.admin.ch/filestore/fedlex.data.admin.ch/eli/cc/1991/1184_1184_1184/20260902/de/pdf-a/fedlex-data-admin-ch-eli-cc-1991-1184_1184_1184-20260902-de-pdf-a.pdf).




### Part 2: The 3-year tail

Residence in Norway does not end when you board the plane _(skatteloven § 2-1 third paragraph)_. [Treaty arts. 4, 22](https://www.fedlex.admin.ch/filestore/fedlex.data.admin.ch/eli/cc/1989/1356_1356_1356/20201026/de/pdf-a/fedlex-data-admin-ch-eli-cc-1989-1356_1356_1356-20201026-de-pdf-a.pdf); [Tax Act § 2-1](https://lovdata.no/dokument/NL/lov/1999-03-26-14/*).

- **Resident at least 10 years (in words on the page) before the year you move:** residence ends only after the end of the 3rd income year (in words on the page) after the year you take up permanent residence abroad. In each of those 3 years you must show that you have not been in Norway for periods exceeding 61 days in the year, and that neither you nor a close relative has had a dwelling at your disposal in Norway _(§ 2-1 third paragraph letter b)_. [Treaty arts. 4, 22](https://www.fedlex.admin.ch/filestore/fedlex.data.admin.ch/eli/cc/1989/1356_1356_1356/20201026/de/pdf-a/fedlex-data-admin-ch-eli-cc-1989-1356_1356_1356-20201026-de-pdf-a.pdf); [Tax Act § 2-1](https://lovdata.no/dokument/NL/lov/1999-03-26-14/*).
- **Resident fewer than 10 years (in words on the page):** residence ends for the income year in which you show the same two things (61 days and no dwelling). It never ends before you and your close relatives stop having a dwelling at your disposal in Norway _(§ 2-1 third paragraph letter a)_. [Treaty arts. 4, 22](https://www.fedlex.admin.ch/filestore/fedlex.data.admin.ch/eli/cc/1989/1356_1356_1356/20201026/de/pdf-a/fedlex-data-admin-ch-eli-cc-1989-1356_1356_1356-20201026-de-pdf-a.pdf); [Tax Act § 2-1](https://lovdata.no/dokument/NL/lov/1999-03-26-14/*).
- **Close relatives** are a spouse, cohabitant or minor children _(§ 2-1 fourth paragraph)_. [Treaty arts. 4, 22](https://www.fedlex.admin.ch/filestore/fedlex.data.admin.ch/eli/cc/1989/1356_1356_1356/20201026/de/pdf-a/fedlex-data-admin-ch-eli-cc-1989-1356_1356_1356-20201026-de-pdf-a.pdf); [Tax Act § 2-1](https://lovdata.no/dokument/NL/lov/1999-03-26-14/*).
- **"Dwelling at your disposal"** means owning, renting or otherwise having a right to use a dwelling, directly or indirectly _(§ 2-1 fifth paragraph)_. A dwelling is a unit with year-round running water and drainage (unless planning rules bar its use as a home), and any unit actually used as a home. A unit acquired at least 5 years before (in words on the page) the year you move, and not used as a home by you or close relatives in that period, does not count _(§ 2-1 sixth paragraph)_. [Treaty arts. 4, 22](https://www.fedlex.admin.ch/filestore/fedlex.data.admin.ch/eli/cc/1989/1356_1356_1356/20201026/de/pdf-a/fedlex-data-admin-ch-eli-cc-1989-1356_1356_1356-20201026-de-pdf-a.pdf); [Tax Act § 2-1](https://lovdata.no/dokument/NL/lov/1999-03-26-14/*).
- **Until the tail ends** you remain resident under Norwegian law, with global income/wealth reporting under Skatteetaten’s current emigration instructions. Wealth tax additionally has the year-end condition in § 2-1(7), and the treaty can restrict Norway’s rights. Do not decide a specific post-move wealth assessment from physical departure alone. [current global reporting instructions](https://www.skatteetaten.no/en/person/to-from-norway/from/tax-when-you-move-abroad/tax-emigration/). The ordinary domestic worldwide rule remains _(skatteloven § 2-1 first and ninth paragraphs)_, subject to the treaty. [Treaty arts. 4, 22](https://www.fedlex.admin.ch/filestore/fedlex.data.admin.ch/eli/cc/1989/1356_1356_1356/20201026/de/pdf-a/fedlex-data-admin-ch-eli-cc-1989-1356_1356_1356-20201026-de-pdf-a.pdf); [Tax Act § 2-1](https://lovdata.no/dokument/NL/lov/1999-03-26-14/*).
- **The treaty overlay.** If you are resident in both states under domestic law, the treaty tie-breaker gives residence to the state where you have a permanent home; if you have one in both, to the state of your centre of vital interests; then habitual abode; then nationality; finally competent-authority agreement where both/neither nationality applies _(Norway-Switzerland treaty art. 4 para. 2)_. In the year you move, each state taxes you as a full resident only for the part of the year you are its resident _(art. 4 para. 3)_. Once you are treaty-resident in Switzerland, wealth other than Norwegian real estate and Norwegian business property can be taxed only in Switzerland _(art. 22 paras. 1, 2 and 4)_. [Treaty arts. 4, 22](https://www.fedlex.admin.ch/filestore/fedlex.data.admin.ch/eli/cc/1989/1356_1356_1356/20201026/de/pdf-a/fedlex-data-admin-ch-eli-cc-1989-1356_1356_1356-20201026-de-pdf-a.pdf); [Tax Act § 2-1](https://lovdata.no/dokument/NL/lov/1999-03-26-14/*).

**Practical points.** The 61-day test is per income year: count every day and keep evidence. Selling, letting on terms that do not let you return, or giving up a home is part of the exit. A holiday cabin with year-round water and drainage is a dwelling unless the statutory holiday-home exception protects it. [Treaty arts. 4, 22](https://www.fedlex.admin.ch/filestore/fedlex.data.admin.ch/eli/cc/1989/1356_1356_1356/20201026/de/pdf-a/fedlex-data-admin-ch-eli-cc-1989-1356_1356_1356-20201026-de-pdf-a.pdf); [Tax Act § 2-1](https://lovdata.no/dokument/NL/lov/1999-03-26-14/*).


### Part 3: The exit tax on shares (skatteloven § 10-70)

- **Trigger.** Gains on shares and similar assets you own when your Norwegian residence ends under § 2-1 third paragraph, or when you become resident in another state under a treaty, are taxed as if you had sold the assets on the last day before _(§ 10-70 first paragraph)_. The same applies when you transfer such assets to a person or company not taxable in Norway, for example a gift to a child living abroad _(§ 10-70 first paragraph, second sentence)_. [Tax Act § 10-70 and cited share rules](https://lovdata.no/dokument/NL/lov/1999-03-26-14/*); [dated exit regimes and exceptions](https://www.skatteetaten.no/en/person/to-from-norway/from/tax-when-you-move-abroad/exit-tax/).
- **What it covers.** Shares and equity certificates in Norwegian companies, share savings accounts, interests in Norwegian partnerships, corresponding foreign shares and interests, subscription rights and options over them, and certain capital insurance _(§ 10-70 second paragraph)_. [Tax Act § 10-70 and cited share rules](https://lovdata.no/dokument/NL/lov/1999-03-26-14/*); [dated exit regimes and exceptions](https://www.skatteetaten.no/en/person/to-from-norway/from/tax-when-you-move-abroad/exit-tax/).
- **Thresholds.** On a move to Switzerland, which is outside the EEA, add positive latent gains without deducting losses, then deduct NOK 3,000,000. For an EEA move, the corresponding test is net of deductible losses. The NOK 3,000,000 amount is in words on the statute page. On a transfer to a non-resident, the whole net gain is taxed when the net gain or loss exceeds NOK 100,000 in the income year _(§ 10-70 fourth paragraph)_. Skatteetaten computes each latent gain as market value less cost and unused shielding (risk-free return allowance), adds the gains up (on a move outside the EEA, losses are left out) and takes the NOK 3,000,000 basic deduction off that total; the 1.72 factor is not part of that sum _(Skatteetaten, Exit tax, worked examples)_. [Tax Act § 10-70 and cited share rules](https://lovdata.no/dokument/NL/lov/1999-03-26-14/*); [dated exit regimes and exceptions](https://www.skatteetaten.no/en/person/to-from-norway/from/tax-when-you-move-abroad/exit-tax/).
- **Losses.** Losses are deductible only on a move or transfer to another EEA state _(§ 10-70 third paragraph)_. Switzerland is not in the EEA, so a move to Switzerland gives no loss deduction. [Tax Act § 10-70 and cited share rules](https://lovdata.no/dokument/NL/lov/1999-03-26-14/*); [dated exit regimes and exceptions](https://www.skatteetaten.no/en/person/to-from-norway/from/tax-when-you-move-abroad/exit-tax/).
- **Computation.** The gain is the market value at the deemed sale less the cost; the ordinary share rules in skatteloven §§ 10-30 to 10-36 apply so far as they fit _(§ 10-70 fifth paragraph)_. Under those rules a personal shareholder's gain, after unused shielding, is multiplied by 1.72 and taxed at 22% _(skatteloven § 10-31 first paragraph; Forskuddsutskrivingen 2026)_. For assets you owned when you moved to Norway, the entry value starts at market value when you became resident; returning residents must adjust for relevant gains/losses from earlier Norwegian-residence periods that were not taxed here _(§ 10-70 sixth paragraph)_. [Tax Act § 10-70 and cited share rules](https://lovdata.no/dokument/NL/lov/1999-03-26-14/*); [dated exit regimes and exceptions](https://www.skatteetaten.no/en/person/to-from-norway/from/tax-when-you-move-abroad/exit-tax/).
- **Deferral for 12 years.** You can be given 12 years to pay, counted from the end of the year the exit tax is timed to. Security is required. For a move to another EEA state security is required only where there is a real risk the tax cannot be collected; Switzerland is outside the EEA, so the general rule applies _(§ 10-70 seventh paragraph)_. Security is, for example, a pledge of shares or real estate _(Skatteetaten, Exit tax)_. You choose how to pay: [Tax Act § 10-70 and cited share rules](https://lovdata.no/dokument/NL/lov/1999-03-26-14/*); [dated exit regimes and exceptions](https://www.skatteetaten.no/en/person/to-from-norway/from/tax-when-you-move-abroad/exit-tax/).
  - a) 1/12 of the tax each year (in words on the page); or [Tax Act § 10-70 and cited share rules](https://lovdata.no/dokument/NL/lov/1999-03-26-14/*); [dated exit regimes and exceptions](https://www.skatteetaten.no/en/person/to-from-norway/from/tax-when-you-move-abroad/exit-tax/).
  - b) all of it at the end of the period, with interest from the date the tax would have been due without deferral until payment _(§ 10-70 seventh paragraph letters a and b)_. [Tax Act § 10-70 and cited share rules](https://lovdata.no/dokument/NL/lov/1999-03-26-14/*); [dated exit regimes and exceptions](https://www.skatteetaten.no/en/person/to-from-norway/from/tax-when-you-move-abroad/exit-tax/).
- **When the deferral ends early.** On sale, on your death, on a transfer to a non-resident, and when a partnership in scope stops its business. Death does not end the deferral if your personal heirs take over your duties under § 10-70 _(§ 10-70 eighth paragraph)_. A withdrawal from a share savings account beyond the deposits ends the deferral for a proportional amount _(§ 10-70 eighth paragraph, third sentence)_. [Tax Act § 10-70 and cited share rules](https://lovdata.no/dokument/NL/lov/1999-03-26-14/*); [dated exit regimes and exceptions](https://www.skatteetaten.no/en/person/to-from-norway/from/tax-when-you-move-abroad/exit-tax/).
- **Dividend rule, for events in the regime applying from 7 October 2024.** A dividend or other distribution on an asset that carries exit tax ends the deferral for an amount equal to the distribution multiplied by 0.7 _(§ 10-70 eighth paragraph, fourth sentence)_. Its implementing cap can limit repayment so distribution tax plus exit-tax repayment does not exceed 100% of that distribution; establish the actual paid taxes before using that cap. You cannot empty the company while the exit tax is deferred. [Tax Act § 10-70 and cited share rules](https://lovdata.no/dokument/NL/lov/1999-03-26-14/*); [dated exit regimes and exceptions](https://www.skatteetaten.no/en/person/to-from-norway/from/tax-when-you-move-abroad/exit-tax/).
- **Return within 12 years cancels it.** If you become resident in Norway again within 12 years after the end of the year the exit tax is timed to, the tax and accrued interest lapse for the assets you still own _(§ 10-70 ninth paragraph)_. If you return later than that, having paid the exit tax in full, the cost of the assets you still own is adjusted by the change in value that was taxed on exit _(same paragraph)_. [Tax Act § 10-70 and cited share rules](https://lovdata.no/dokument/NL/lov/1999-03-26-14/*); [dated exit regimes and exceptions](https://www.skatteetaten.no/en/person/to-from-norway/from/tax-when-you-move-abroad/exit-tax/).
- **Information duty.** You must give the tax office all information relevant to the exit tax without being asked and without undue delay; a breach ends the deferral _(§ 10-70 eleventh paragraph)_. [Tax Act § 10-70 and cited share rules](https://lovdata.no/dokument/NL/lov/1999-03-26-14/*); [dated exit regimes and exceptions](https://www.skatteetaten.no/en/person/to-from-norway/from/tax-when-you-move-abroad/exit-tax/).
- **Long reopening window.** Where exit tax under § 10-70 first paragraph was not assessed, the ordinary reopening limit is 15 years after the end of the tax period; the death/estate and prior-decision limits in § 12-6(3)-(4) must also be checked _(skatteforvaltningsloven § 12-6 first paragraph)_. [Tax Administration Act § 12-6](https://lovdata.no/dokument/NL/lov/2016-05-27-14).

**Planning reads.**
- **The question is "pay when?", not "wait it out".** Compare paying in twelfths (option a) with paying at the end (option b, where the law sets interest), against liquidity and the chance you return. [Tax Act § 10-70 and cited share rules](https://lovdata.no/dokument/NL/lov/1999-03-26-14/*); [dated exit regimes and exceptions](https://www.skatteetaten.no/en/person/to-from-norway/from/tax-when-you-move-abroad/exit-tax/).
- **Pre-departure steps** (realising gains or losses while resident, reorganising) are tested against the general anti-avoidance rule, which requires both a main tax-advantage purpose and an overall assessment that the arrangement cannot be accepted for taxation, before applying its economic-content or alternative reconstruction rules _(skatteloven § 13-2)_. [Tax Act § 10-70 and cited share rules](https://lovdata.no/dokument/NL/lov/1999-03-26-14/*); [dated exit regimes and exceptions](https://www.skatteetaten.no/en/person/to-from-norway/from/tax-when-you-move-abroad/exit-tax/).
- **The return option is real.** A founder unsure about the move can defer, live in Switzerland, and cancel the charge for assets still owned by becoming resident again within 12 years after the end of the exit-tax year, subject to the distribution/basis adjustments in § 10-70(9). [Tax Act § 10-70 and cited share rules](https://lovdata.no/dokument/NL/lov/1999-03-26-14/*); [dated exit regimes and exceptions](https://www.skatteetaten.no/en/person/to-from-norway/from/tax-when-you-move-abroad/exit-tax/).


### Part 4: What stays Norwegian while (and after) you leave

- **Dividends from Norwegian companies.** Norway's domestic withholding tax is 25% _(Stortingets skattevedtak 2026 § 3-5 third paragraph)_. Under the treaty, Norway's tax is capped at 15% of gross dividends when the beneficial owner is a qualifying Swiss-resident individual; a beneficial-owner Swiss company, excluding a partnership, holding at least 10% of capital directly is exempt. The PE/fixed-base connection in art. 10(6) switches off those limits _(treaty art. 10 para. 2)_. Switzerland gives relief for the Norwegian tax on request, by credit, a lump-sum reduction or a partial exemption _(treaty art. 23 para. 2 letter b)_. [Treaty arts. 4-7, 10, 13, 16, 18, 22-23, 28a](https://www.fedlex.admin.ch/filestore/fedlex.data.admin.ch/eli/cc/1989/1356_1356_1356/20201026/de/pdf-a/fedlex-data-admin-ch-eli-cc-1989-1356_1356_1356-20201026-de-pdf-a.pdf); [domestic tax scope and company exit rules](https://lovdata.no/dokument/NL/lov/1999-03-26-14/*); [2026 domestic withholding rate](https://lovdata.no/dokument/STV/forskrift/2025-12-18-2747).
- **Large holdings: the 5-year rule.** Norway may tax a gain on selling a holding of more than 25% in a Norwegian company if the seller was resident in Norway at some point in the five years before the sale and is not taxed on the gain in Switzerland _(treaty art. 13 para. 5)_. The treaty reserves a taxing right; it does not by itself establish a domestic tax charge. Check continuing domestic residence and any actual domestic/exit-tax basis before computing a second Norwegian tax. Private-investor status and Swiss non-taxation must be established, not assumed. [Treaty arts. 4-7, 10, 13, 16, 18, 22-23, 28a](https://www.fedlex.admin.ch/filestore/fedlex.data.admin.ch/eli/cc/1989/1356_1356_1356/20201026/de/pdf-a/fedlex-data-admin-ch-eli-cc-1989-1356_1356_1356-20201026-de-pdf-a.pdf); [domestic tax scope and company exit rules](https://lovdata.no/dokument/NL/lov/1999-03-26-14/*); [2026 domestic withholding rate](https://lovdata.no/dokument/STV/forskrift/2025-12-18-2747).
- **Norwegian real estate.** Income, gains and wealth from real estate in Norway can be taxed in Norway _(treaty arts. 6, 13 para. 1, 22 para. 1)_. [Treaty arts. 4-7, 10, 13, 16, 18, 22-23, 28a](https://www.fedlex.admin.ch/filestore/fedlex.data.admin.ch/eli/cc/1989/1356_1356_1356/20201026/de/pdf-a/fedlex-data-admin-ch-eli-cc-1989-1356_1356_1356-20201026-de-pdf-a.pdf); [domestic tax scope and company exit rules](https://lovdata.no/dokument/NL/lov/1999-03-26-14/*); [2026 domestic withholding rate](https://lovdata.no/dokument/STV/forskrift/2025-12-18-2747).
- **The company may follow you.** Under the text applicable in 2026, a company incorporated under Norwegian law is resident in Norway, and a foreign company with its effective management in Norway is too _(skatteloven § 2-2 seventh paragraph)_. If both states treat the company as resident, the treaty gives residence to the state of its place of effective management _(treaty art. 4 para. 5)_. If a Norwegian company becomes resident in Switzerland, which is outside the EEA, its assets are taxed as if sold the day before _(skatteloven § 10-71 first and second paragraphs)_. Even short of that, a place of management in Switzerland is a permanent establishment _(treaty art. 5 para. 2 letter a)_, and Switzerland can tax the profits attributable to it _(treaty art. 7 para. 1)_. Keep real management (board meetings, decisions, minutes) in Norway if the company is to stay Norwegian. [Treaty arts. 4-7, 10, 13, 16, 18, 22-23, 28a](https://www.fedlex.admin.ch/filestore/fedlex.data.admin.ch/eli/cc/1989/1356_1356_1356/20201026/de/pdf-a/fedlex-data-admin-ch-eli-cc-1989-1356_1356_1356-20201026-de-pdf-a.pdf); [domestic tax scope and company exit rules](https://lovdata.no/dokument/NL/lov/1999-03-26-14/*); [2026 domestic withholding rate](https://lovdata.no/dokument/STV/forskrift/2025-12-18-2747).
- **Board fees** paid by a Norwegian company to a Swiss-resident director can be taxed in Norway _(treaty art. 16)_. ### Part 5: The Swiss landing

- **Ordinary taxation in a canton** is the default. Income and wealth tax rates differ by canton; this skill does not give cantonal rates. [DBG arts. 14, 16](https://www.fedlex.admin.ch/filestore/fedlex.data.admin.ch/eli/cc/1991/1184_1184_1184/20260902/de/pdf-a/fedlex-data-admin-ch-eli-cc-1991-1184_1184_1184-20260902-de-pdf-a.pdf); [StHG arts. 6, 7](https://www.fedlex.admin.ch/filestore/fedlex.data.admin.ch/eli/cc/1991/1256_1256_1256/20250101/de/pdf-a/fedlex-data-admin-ch-eli-cc-1991-1256_1256_1256-20250101-de-pdf-a.pdf); [Treaty art. 4(4)](https://www.fedlex.admin.ch/filestore/fedlex.data.admin.ch/eli/cc/1989/1356_1356_1356/20201026/de/pdf-a/fedlex-data-admin-ch-eli-cc-1989-1356_1356_1356-20201026-de-pdf-a.pdf).
- **Lump-sum taxation (Besteuerung nach dem Aufwand, forfait).** Available to individuals who are not Swiss citizens, who become fully taxable in Switzerland for the first time or after at least 10 years away (in words on the statute page), and who do not work in Switzerland; spouses living in a legally and actually unseparated marriage must both qualify _(DBG art. 14 para. 1)_. For the federal tax, the base is annual living costs, but at least the highest of CHF 435,000 (from 1 January 2026), 7 times annual rent/rental value for a person with their own household, or 3 times annual board/lodging charges for other taxpayers, or the gross-income control sum listed in the law (the multipliers are in words on the page) _(DBG art. 14 para. 3)_. Cantons may offer it and set their own minimum _(StHG art. 6 paras. 1 and 3)_. [DBG arts. 14, 16](https://www.fedlex.admin.ch/filestore/fedlex.data.admin.ch/eli/cc/1991/1184_1184_1184/20260902/de/pdf-a/fedlex-data-admin-ch-eli-cc-1991-1184_1184_1184-20260902-de-pdf-a.pdf); [StHG arts. 6, 7](https://www.fedlex.admin.ch/filestore/fedlex.data.admin.ch/eli/cc/1991/1256_1256_1256/20250101/de/pdf-a/fedlex-data-admin-ch-eli-cc-1991-1256_1256_1256-20250101-de-pdf-a.pdf); [Treaty art. 4(4)](https://www.fedlex.admin.ch/filestore/fedlex.data.admin.ch/eli/cc/1989/1356_1356_1356/20201026/de/pdf-a/fedlex-data-admin-ch-eli-cc-1989-1356_1356_1356-20201026-de-pdf-a.pdf).
- **The Norwegian catch.** Under the treaty, a person who is not subject in Switzerland to the generally levied taxes on all income from Norway that Swiss law taxes generally is not treated as a Swiss resident for the treaty _(treaty art. 4 para. 4)_. A plain lump-sum resident therefore cannot rely on the treaty: no tie-breaker during the 3-year tail and no 15% dividend cap. Swiss law has a modified lump sum for this case: where another state grants relief only if Switzerland taxes the income, the base also includes all income from that state that the treaty assigns to Switzerland _(DBG art. 14 para. 5; StHG art. 6 para. 7)_. Price the modified lump sum against ordinary cantonal taxation; for owners whose assets are mostly Norwegian, ordinary taxation can come out lower. [DBG arts. 14, 16](https://www.fedlex.admin.ch/filestore/fedlex.data.admin.ch/eli/cc/1991/1184_1184_1184/20260902/de/pdf-a/fedlex-data-admin-ch-eli-cc-1991-1184_1184_1184-20260902-de-pdf-a.pdf); [StHG arts. 6, 7](https://www.fedlex.admin.ch/filestore/fedlex.data.admin.ch/eli/cc/1991/1256_1256_1256/20250101/de/pdf-a/fedlex-data-admin-ch-eli-cc-1991-1256_1256_1256-20250101-de-pdf-a.pdf); [Treaty art. 4(4)](https://www.fedlex.admin.ch/filestore/fedlex.data.admin.ch/eli/cc/1989/1356_1356_1356/20201026/de/pdf-a/fedlex-data-admin-ch-eli-cc-1989-1356_1356_1356-20201026-de-pdf-a.pdf).
- **No Swiss tax on private capital gains** on movable assets _(DBG art. 16 para. 3; StHG art. 7 para. 4 letter b)_. Do not promise exemption for all post-move growth. Establish private movable-asset status and the Norwegian domestic/treaty position; business assets, real-estate-connected disposals, the treaty’s substantial-holding reservation and exit-tax events need separate analysis. [DBG arts. 14, 16](https://www.fedlex.admin.ch/filestore/fedlex.data.admin.ch/eli/cc/1991/1184_1184_1184/20260902/de/pdf-a/fedlex-data-admin-ch-eli-cc-1991-1184_1184_1184-20260902-de-pdf-a.pdf); [StHG arts. 6, 7](https://www.fedlex.admin.ch/filestore/fedlex.data.admin.ch/eli/cc/1991/1256_1256_1256/20250101/de/pdf-a/fedlex-data-admin-ch-eli-cc-1991-1256_1256_1256-20250101-de-pdf-a.pdf); [Treaty art. 4(4)](https://www.fedlex.admin.ch/filestore/fedlex.data.admin.ch/eli/cc/1989/1356_1356_1356/20201026/de/pdf-a/fedlex-data-admin-ch-eli-cc-1989-1356_1356_1356-20201026-de-pdf-a.pdf).

**Before departure**
1. Count your Norwegian residence years (10 or more means the 3-year tail applies); plan the 61-day budget and the dwelling question for 3 full income years. [Tax Act §§ 2-1, 10-70](https://lovdata.no/dokument/NL/lov/1999-03-26-14/*); [Treaty](https://www.fedlex.admin.ch/filestore/fedlex.data.admin.ch/eli/cc/1989/1356_1356_1356/20201026/de/pdf-a/fedlex-data-admin-ch-eli-cc-1989-1356_1356_1356-20201026-de-pdf-a.pdf); [exit-tax reporting](https://www.skatteetaten.no/en/person/to-from-norway/from/tax-when-you-move-abroad/exit-tax/); [emigration claim](https://www.skatteetaten.no/en/person/to-from-norway/from/tax-when-you-move-abroad/tax-emigration/).

[Tax Act §§ 2-1, 10-70](https://lovdata.no/dokument/NL/lov/1999-03-26-14/*); [Treaty](https://www.fedlex.admin.ch/filestore/fedlex.data.admin.ch/eli/cc/1989/1356_1356_1356/20201026/de/pdf-a/fedlex-data-admin-ch-eli-cc-1989-1356_1356_1356-20201026-de-pdf-a.pdf); [exit-tax reporting](https://www.skatteetaten.no/en/person/to-from-norway/from/tax-when-you-move-abroad/exit-tax/); [emigration claim](https://www.skatteetaten.no/en/person/to-from-norway/from/tax-when-you-move-abroad/tax-emigration/).

### Swiss move versus an EEA move: losses change the exit-tax base

Assume a 2026 move with covered ordinary shares: positive latent gains of NOK 5,000,000 and a separate latent loss of NOK 500,000, after established cost and unused shielding, with no other adjustments. For Switzerland, ignore the loss and deduct NOK 3,000,000: taxable underlying gain NOK 2,000,000 and estimated share tax NOK 756,800. For the hypothetical EEA alternative, net the loss first, then deduct the same amount: underlying gain NOK 1,500,000 and estimated tax NOK 567,600. The derived difference is NOK 189,200. This compares the loss rule, not two complete relocation plans; treaty residence, valuation, earlier Norwegian residence and actual deferral/security remain separate. ## When to refuse or refer

- Do not compute a final move-year assessment without residence/treaty dates, asset valuations, cost and established private-versus-business classification.
- Refer the exact year-end wealth assessment during the domestic residence tail, earlier-period moves, inheritance, company migration and overlapping domestic/exit tax before filing.
- Establish the actual domestic charge before treating a treaty reservation as a tax bill. Do not promise that all post-move gains escape tax.
- Swiss cantonal rates, immigration permission, professional-trader classification, payment/security acceptance and the modified lump-sum ruling need their own case-specific evidence.
