## Transition and filing

Law 127-Z art. 4(5) applies the new schedule where the statutory payment deadline falls after 30 January 2026. MNS 33616 explicitly confirms the month/activity mapping above. Taxpayers losing eligibility must correct Q1 returns by 31 March 2026. Newly eligible construction/taxi electing from 1 January must file the Q1 return by 31 January and pay January by 1 February 2026. These are the statutory dates; apply General Code art. 4 separately for nonworking days.

For January only, qualifying entrepreneur retail at trading places, exhibitions/sales, fairs and itinerant/peddling trade for fewer than 15 days uses coefficient 0.5. Current art. 342(6) is repealed: this reduction does not continue into February-December. Art. 4(5) separately preserves the January coefficient for specified ordinary individual sales, with its stated mixed-activity exclusion.

## Relief and computation rules

- Art. 340(1.1): first registration gives 25% reduction for the first three months starting with the month business begins, through the last day of the expiry month.
- Art. 340(1.2)-(1.5): 20% for the stated retirement, disability, three-or-more minor children and disabled-child grounds. Apply the exact status, documentation, start and end rules. The disabled-child ground includes qualifying group I/II disability since childhood, not every adult disabled child.
- Art. 340(1.6): 100% for qualifying group I/II disabled parents raising minors or full-time students, with the spouse, if any, also group I/II disabled. Required proof and month timing apply.
- Art. 340(2): multiple simultaneous grounds within (1.1)-(1.5) give a total 45% reduction. Do not compound percentages or add them without limit.
- Art. 340(3)-(4): entrepreneur proof accompanies the first relevant reporting-period return when relief is used. Relief applies to one chosen trading object/place/service object, or one eligible activity without those objects. Do not discount every location/activity.
- Art. 340(5)-(6): ordinary individuals can apply relief across their qualifying 3.1 activities, but cannot apply it to their short-stay activity. Distinguish them from registered entrepreneurs.
- Art. 342(1)-(2): use business-location rates, except taxi uses the entrepreneur's tax-registration location.
- Art. 342(3)-(7): mixed retail groups use the highest applicable product rate. Each trading object/place is charged separately, except the precise no-other-persons-involved trading-place/itinerant/peddling rule in (5), which uses the highest location rate. Vending machines are trading objects and are not peddling.
- Art. 342(9), (11), (13): several non-short-stay services use the highest service rate. Charge short stay for each property separately and add it to the other service charge.
- Art. 342(14): surcharge = 6% × max(0, quarter gross revenue − 40 × quarter fixed tax before relief). Use actual chargeable units, activity months and aggregation. Relief does not reduce this threshold.
- Art. 342(15)-(25): apply shipment/performance revenue dates and the separate other-income rules. Check the taxi dispatcher exception, uncollected income at a regime transition, and revenue-share allocation of other income that cannot be assigned to a regime.
- Arts. 342(26)-(29), 343(1)-(3): ordinary return by the first day of the quarter and payment by the first day of each month; first annual start uses the working day before activity. Amend changed conditions before the change. Both surcharge return and payment for Q1/Q2/Q3/Q4 fall by 1 July / 1 October / 1 January / 1 April, including after regime exit.
- Art. 342(30): proration requires the application/documents for art. 344(1.2), (1.3) or (1.5) absence grounds. It is not a voluntary part-month election. Multiply the supported monthly charge by active days / calendar days.
- Ordinary individuals, arts. 342(31)-(40), 343(4)-(5): advance activity notice; authority assessment; internet activity uses tax-registration location; highest-rate combination generally excludes short stay, which is charged separately per property. The professional-income-to-single-tax transition has its own proration rule.
