# UK–Gibraltar: investment income and capital gains

## Scope and effective periods

The agreement entered into force on **24 March 2020**. Withholding provisions apply to amounts paid or credited from **1 May 2020** in both territories. UK income tax and capital gains tax apply from the assessment year beginning **6 April 2020**, and corporation tax from the financial year beginning **1 April 2020**. Gibraltar income tax applies to assessment years beginning from **1 July 2020**, and corporation tax to accounting periods beginning from that date. These are different commencement tests. [HMRC status page](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/gibraltar-tax-treaties); [Article 29](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/gibraltar-tax-treaties/2019-uk-gibraltar-double-taxation-agreement-in-force#article-29-entry-into-force).

This Guide covers investment-income source-tax restrictions and capital-gain allocation, not all agreement articles. Determine the domestic charge first, then the agreement's restriction. An agreement ceiling does not impose a tax absent under domestic law. A source exemption does not establish residence-country exemption or eliminate the distributing company's tax. The existing named taxes are Gibraltar income and corporation tax and UK income, corporation and capital gains tax. [Articles 2 and 10–12](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/gibraltar-tax-treaties/2019-uk-gibraltar-double-taxation-agreement-in-force).

## Establish entitlement before using a rate

Article 1 covers residents of one or both territories. For a wholly or partly transparent entity or arrangement, income is treated as income of a resident only to the extent that the territory treats it as that resident's income for tax purposes. Establish the actual recipient and beneficial owner; an intermediary's address is insufficient. [Article 1; Articles 10–12](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/gibraltar-tax-treaties/2019-uk-gibraltar-double-taxation-agreement-in-force).

Article 4(1) requires liability to tax by domicile, residence, management, incorporation or a similar criterion, and excludes a person liable there only on local-source income or gains. Article 4(2) expressly includes established pension schemes and qualifying exclusively religious, charitable, scientific, cultural or educational organisations despite full or partial exemption. Do not assume that a registration certificate alone proves treaty residence; the interaction with a particular Gibraltar taxpayer's tax status must be documented. [Article 4](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/gibraltar-tax-treaties/2019-uk-gibraltar-double-taxation-agreement-in-force#article-4-resident).

For an individual resident in both territories, apply the sequence: permanent home; if available in both, centre of vital interests; if that cannot be determined or neither has a home, habitual abode; if habitual abode is in both or neither, competent-authority agreement. **There is no nationality tie-breaker in this text.** For a dual-resident non-individual, competent authorities endeavour to agree residence taking account of effective management, incorporation and other relevant factors. Without agreement there is no agreement relief or exemption except as and to the extent authorities agree. [Article 4(3)–(4)](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/gibraltar-tax-treaties/2019-uk-gibraltar-double-taxation-agreement-in-force#article-4-resident).

## Dividends: ordinary exemption and property distributions

When the beneficial owner is resident in the other territory, Article 10(2)(a) ordinarily exempts dividends in the paying company's territory. Article 10(2)(b) instead allows source tax capped at **15% of gross dividends** where an investment vehicle pays from income or gains derived directly or indirectly from immovable property, distributes most of that income annually, and that property income is exempt from tax. If the beneficial owner is a pension scheme established in the other territory, the exemption applies instead. This is a specific investment-vehicle exception, not a 15% rate for all property-company dividends. [Article 10(1)–(3)](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/gibraltar-tax-treaties/2019-uk-gibraltar-double-taxation-agreement-in-force#article-10-dividends).

The agreement's pension-scheme definition requires general income-tax exemption and operation to administer/provide pension or retirement benefits or earn income for such arrangements. Obtain evidence of those conditions, not just a fund's name. If the dividend holding is effectively connected with the beneficial owner's permanent establishment in the paying company's territory, Article 7 applies instead of Article 10(1)–(2). [Articles 3(1)(k), 10(4)](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/gibraltar-tax-treaties/2019-uk-gibraltar-double-taxation-agreement-in-force).

## Interest: recipient or payer qualification is essential

Article 11(2) gives exclusive residence-territory taxation only where the beneficial owner is resident there **and at least one Article 11(3) condition is met**. The qualifying recipient categories are:

- That territory, its political subdivisions, local authorities, central bank or statutory bodies.
- An individual.
- A company whose principal share class has substantial and regular trading on a recognised stock exchange.
- A company with **less than 25%** of its shares or other rights owned directly or indirectly by persons not resident in that territory.
- A pension scheme, bank or building society.
- Another financial institution unrelated to and dealing wholly independently with the payer, meeting the article's definition: substantially earning profits by raising debt finance in financial markets or taking interest-bearing deposits and using those funds to provide finance.
- Another person where the source-territory competent authority makes the specified determination that obtaining Article 11 benefits is not a principal purpose of its establishment, acquisition, maintenance or operations.

Alternatively, the payer can qualify as a territory, political subdivision, local authority or statutory body. The statutory-body extension includes institutions wholly or mainly government-owned as agreed by competent-authority exchange of letters; do not invent an agreed list. [Article 11(3)–(4)](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/gibraltar-tax-treaties/2019-uk-gibraltar-double-taxation-agreement-in-force#article-11-interest).

If none of those routes is established, Article 11(1)–(2) does not supply a fallback reduced percentage: source taxation under domestic law remains permitted. The authority-determination route is not a self-certification. A bank qualifies as a named interest recipient; this does not automatically qualify it for royalty relief. [Articles 11(2)–(3), 12(2)–(3)](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/gibraltar-tax-treaties/2019-uk-gibraltar-double-taxation-agreement-in-force).

## Royalties: a different eligibility list

Article 12(2) also requires the beneficial owner to be resident in the other territory and at least one paragraph (3) condition. Its listed recipients are: that territory, political subdivisions, local authorities or statutory bodies; individuals; companies with substantial regular trading in the principal share class on a recognised stock exchange; companies with **less than 25%** non-resident direct or indirect ownership of shares or other rights; and other persons receiving the prescribed competent-authority principal-purpose determination. Unlike interest, the royalty list has **no separate pension, bank or independent financial-institution category and no qualifying government-payer alternative**. A recipient must establish an actual listed route. [Article 12(2)–(4)](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/gibraltar-tax-treaties/2019-uk-gibraltar-double-taxation-agreement-in-force#article-12-royalties).

Royalties cover consideration for use or rights to use copyright (including cinematograph films), patents, trademarks, designs/models, plans, secret formulae/processes and industrial, commercial or scientific know-how. Classify the contract; a software or service invoice label alone is not a treaty classification. The Protocol defines recognised stock exchanges for Articles 11–13, including named exchanges and exchanges agreed by the authorities. Verify the actual venue and trading conditions, not only the company's listed status. [Article 12(5); Protocol paragraph 2](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/gibraltar-tax-treaties/2019-uk-gibraltar-double-taxation-agreement-in-force).

## Source, permanent establishments and related parties

For interest and royalties, source normally follows payer residence. Where a permanent establishment incurred the relevant indebtedness or liability and bears the payment, source follows that establishment instead, regardless of payer residence. If the beneficial owner's debt claim or royalty right/property is effectively connected with its permanent establishment in the source territory, Article 7 replaces the investment-income exemption rules. [Articles 11(6)–(7), 12(6)–(7)](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/gibraltar-tax-treaties/2019-uk-gibraltar-double-taxation-agreement-in-force).

Where a special relationship makes the payment exceed the amount independent parties would agree, Articles 11 and 12 protect only the independent amount. The excess remains taxable under each territory's law with regard to the agreement's other provisions. Do not apply a zero treaty rate to an entire related-party payment without checking its amount. [Articles 11(8), 12(8)](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/gibraltar-tax-treaties/2019-uk-gibraltar-double-taxation-agreement-in-force).

## Worked examples and boundary checks

**Property distribution:** assume all entitlement conditions are proved and a £20,000 dividend falls within Article 10(2)(b). Its source-tax ceiling is **£3,000**. If domestic tax before relief is assumed to be £4,000, treaty-limited tax is £3,000 and the potential reduction is £1,000. This does not prescribe a domestic rate or promise an automatic refund. A qualifying pension scheme instead has a zero source ceiling. [Article 10(2)](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/gibraltar-tax-treaties/2019-uk-gibraltar-double-taxation-agreement-in-force#article-10-dividends).

```oa-check
name: UK Gibraltar property dividend treaty ceiling
inputs:
  gross_dividend: 20000
  assumed_domestic_tax: 4000
steps:
  - treaty_ceiling: gross_dividend * 0.15
  - limited_tax: min(assumed_domestic_tax, treaty_ceiling)
  - potential_reduction: assumed_domestic_tax - limited_tax
  - qualifying_pension_ceiling: gross_dividend * 0
expected:
  treaty_ceiling: 3000
  limited_tax: 3000
  potential_reduction: 1000
  qualifying_pension_ceiling: 0
```

**Ownership boundary:** assume a single equal-right share class and verified complete direct/indirect ownership. Of 1,000 shares, 249 owned by non-residents gives **24.9%**, satisfying this particular less-than-25% limb. At 250 shares, **25% fails** it. Failure of that limb does not rule out a different listed recipient route, and passing it does not establish residence, beneficial ownership or anti-abuse compliance. Do not use this simple share count for unequal rights without analysis. [Articles 11(3)(a)(iv), 12(3)(d)](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/gibraltar-tax-treaties/2019-uk-gibraltar-double-taxation-agreement-in-force).

```oa-check
name: UK Gibraltar strict nonresident ownership boundary
inputs:
  total_equal_right_shares: 1000
steps:
  - below_boundary_percent: 249 / total_equal_right_shares * 100
  - at_boundary_percent: 250 / total_equal_right_shares * 100
expected:
  below_boundary_percent: 24.9
  at_boundary_percent: 25
```

**Excess interest:** assume verified interest of £12,000, independent-party amount £9,000, and exemption eligibility for that independent amount. Article 11 protects £9,000; the remaining **£3,000** requires separate domestic/agreement analysis. No tax rate for that excess is assumed. [Article 11(8)](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/gibraltar-tax-treaties/2019-uk-gibraltar-double-taxation-agreement-in-force#article-11-interest).

```oa-check
name: UK Gibraltar related party interest excess
inputs:
  interest_paid: 12000
  independent_amount: 9000
steps:
  - excess_for_separate_analysis: interest_paid - independent_amount
expected:
  excess_for_separate_analysis: 3000
```

## Capital gains: classify the disposed asset before allocating tax

Article 13(1) allows the territory where immovable property is situated to tax gains on its disposal by a resident of the other territory. Article 6 defines immovable property primarily by the law where it is situated, with specified inclusions such as usufruct and natural-resource payment rights; ships, boats and aircraft are excluded from that definition. Establish the legal interest sold and its location. Treaty permission to tax does not establish a domestic charge, allowable cost or filing deadline. [Articles 6 and 13(1)](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/gibraltar-tax-treaties/2019-uk-gibraltar-double-taxation-agreement-in-force).

### Property-rich shares, partnerships and trusts

Article 13(2) permits the property territory to tax gains from shares or comparable interests, including partnership or trust interests, deriving **more than 50% of their value directly or indirectly** from immovable property in that territory, unless paragraph (3)'s exclusion applies. This is an asset-value derivation test, not the seller's ownership percentage. Do not substitute turnover, rental-income share, or the dividend withholding ceiling. The paragraph does not specify a 365-day testing window; a time or valuation rule needs its own verified basis. [Article 13(2)](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/gibraltar-tax-treaties/2019-uk-gibraltar-double-taxation-agreement-in-force#article-13-capital-gains).

Paragraph (3) excludes interests with **substantial and regular trading on a recognised stock exchange**, but removes that exclusion for interests in the specified investment vehicle distributing property-derived income/gains where most of that income is exempt and distributed annually. A bare listing is insufficient, and the investment-vehicle exception must still be checked for traded interests. Use the Protocol's recognised-exchange definition. [Article 13(3); Protocol paragraph 2](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/gibraltar-tax-treaties/2019-uk-gibraltar-double-taxation-agreement-in-force).

**Boundary example:** assume a verified £10 million value of the interests and a properly established value derivation of £5 million from property in the other territory. Exactly **50%** fails the more-than-50% limb. At £5.1 million, **51%** passes that limb. In both cases the denominator and attribution are assumed verified, not established by simply adding book values. Passing the limb alone does not settle the traded-interest exclusion or domestic tax.

```oa-check
name: UK Gibraltar property rich interest value boundary
inputs:
  established_interest_value: 10000000
  property_derived_value_at_boundary: 5000000
  property_derived_value_above_boundary: 5100000
steps:
  - at_boundary_percent: property_derived_value_at_boundary / established_interest_value * 100
  - above_boundary_percent: property_derived_value_above_boundary / established_interest_value * 100
expected:
  at_boundary_percent: 50
  above_boundary_percent: 51
```

**Classification example:** assume interests derive 70% of value from relevant property and trade substantially and regularly on a recognised exchange. An ordinary entity outside paragraph (3)'s investment-vehicle exception has excluded interests for paragraph (2). If it meets the specified property-investment-vehicle conditions instead, exchange trading does not provide that exclusion. Then paragraph (2) permits property-territory taxation, subject to the agreement's other rules. Do not apply the **15% dividend** ceiling to the disposal gain: Article 13 specifies taxing rights, not that percentage. [Articles 10(2)(b), 13(2)–(3)](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/gibraltar-tax-treaties/2019-uk-gibraltar-double-taxation-agreement-in-force).

### Permanent establishments, ships and residual gains

Article 13(4) permits the permanent-establishment territory to tax gains on movable property forming part of that establishment's business property, including disposal of the establishment itself, alone or with the enterprise. Article 13(5) allocates gains from international-traffic ships/aircraft, and movable property pertaining to their operation, exclusively to the territory of the enterprise operating them. A passive ownership case must not be assumed to satisfy the operating-enterprise condition. [Article 13(4)–(5)](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/gibraltar-tax-treaties/2019-uk-gibraltar-double-taxation-agreement-in-force#article-13-capital-gains).

Other gains fall under Article 13(6)'s exclusive allocation to the alienator's residence territory only after excluding paragraphs (1), (2), (4) and (5). Also screen Article 21, which overrides other provisions for defined offshore activities and permits taxation of the specified exploration/exploitation rights, offshore property and related share gains. Article 28's anti-abuse and conditional remittance rules remain relevant. [Articles 13(6), 21(1)–(2), (6), 28](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/gibraltar-tax-treaties/2019-uk-gibraltar-double-taxation-agreement-in-force).

For every disposal, retain residence evidence, the sale contract, asset/right classification, ownership chain, supported value derivation, exchange/trading evidence where relevant, investment-vehicle facts, permanent-establishment/offshore analysis and both domestic computations. The [Gibraltar residential-disposals Guide](gi-residential-property-disposals.md) describes a particular domestic regime; neither it nor this treaty allocation establishes that all capital receipts are exempt or taxable. Treaty classification of that domestic regime, valuation dates/methods, domestic reporting, losses and relief claims require further verification.

## Anti-abuse and dispute procedure

Article 28 denies an item-specific benefit where obtaining it was a principal purpose of an arrangement or transaction, unless granting it accords with the relevant provisions' object and purpose. Paragraph (2) provides a request for competent-authority consideration of benefits that would have arisen without the arrangement; it is not automatic reinstatement. Where an individual's residence-territory law taxes the income/gain by remittance or receipt rather than its full amount, paragraph (3) limits source relief to the amount taxed there. Apply this conditional text to verified current domestic facts, not an assumed continuing remittance regime. [Article 28](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/gibraltar-tax-treaties/2019-uk-gibraltar-double-taxation-agreement-in-force#article-28-entitlement-to-benefits).

A person alleging taxation contrary to the agreement can present a case to either competent authority within **three years from the first notification** of the relevant action, irrespective of domestic remedies. Authorities endeavour to resolve justified cases; an agreed solution overrides domestic time limits. Article 24(5) provides requested arbitration for unresolved issues after **two years from presentation to the other territory's authority**, excluding issues already decided by a court or administrative tribunal. It also specifies acceptance/binding-effect conditions and requires authorities to settle its mode of application. This Guide has not verified that operational arrangement; neither clock is an automatic payment suspension or a domestic refund deadline. [Article 24](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/gibraltar-tax-treaties/2019-uk-gibraltar-double-taxation-agreement-in-force#article-24-mutual-agreement-procedure).

HMRC’s [DT8152 summary](https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/double-taxation-relief/dt8152) records no arbitration, conflicting with Article 24(5) in the published agreement. The [employment and pensions Guide](gi-uk-employment-pensions-treaty.md) records this and related pension discrepancies. The agreement provision above is verified, but its operational arrangement and the reason for the summary entry remain unresolved.

## Company UK-interest relief: published application evidence

HMRC states that company treaty withholding relief is not automatic: the ordinary certified route uses **DT Company** with residence certification, unless the Treaty Passport Scheme applies. Relief at source and repayment of prior deductions are distinct requests; retain tax vouchers even where not submitted initially. HMRC decides entitlement and authorises the allowed treatment. [Company relief guidance](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/double-taxation-relief-for-companies).

The [current DT Company entry page](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/international-tax-double-taxation-treaty-relief-form-dt-company) links its interactive form and warns that progress cannot be saved. Assemble the evidence before starting. This Guide verifies that published entry route, not every field or the final submission instructions inside a completed interactive application. No form has been submitted here.

Gibraltar's [ITO checklist](https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi/uploads/Income%20Tax%20Office/docs/Supporting%20documentation%20for%20UK%20tax%20treaty%20relief%20application.pdf) concerns its processing of **DT Company requests for relief at source on UK interest**. It calls for incorporation and constitutional documents, Registrar-issued good standing, and the finance agreement. Directors must provide a letter with an affidavit or equivalent sworn statement covering:

- All Board meetings for the relevant accounting period held in/from Gibraltar and directors' ordinary Gibraltar residence under section 74.
- Territorial taxation, Article 4 residence, sole Gibraltar residence and absence of another similar claim or treaty-residence position elsewhere.
- Treaty-person/resident status, beneficial ownership and the particular **Article 11(3)(a)** recipient classification relied upon.
- Where borrowing funds the loan, an arm's-length interest spread, including the stated contemporaneous OECD-method basis for connected-party arrangements.

Read the full two-page checklist before preparing declarations. Do not sign facts that have not been established, or infer that this administrative checklist rewrites Article 4 for every taxpayer. Its stated scope does not establish the procedure for individuals, royalties, other relief categories or reverse-direction payments. [ITO checklist](https://www.gibraltar.gov.gi/uploads/Income%20Tax%20Office/docs/Supporting%20documentation%20for%20UK%20tax%20treaty%20relief%20application.pdf).

A Gibraltar evidence packet and an HMRC application are not the same as an issued relief direction. Retain each decision, its covered payments and dates, and the payer's resulting treatment. For a funded related-party loan, reconcile the declared spread with the Article 11(8) independent-amount analysis above; a company label or an affidavit alone does not prove eligibility.

**Separate royalty route:** HMRC says a UK company can give royalty relief without prior HMRC authorisation where it reasonably believes the overseas payee qualifies under a double-taxation arrangement, with payment details included in its Company Tax Return. This does not remove Article 12 eligibility requirements or authorise the same approach for interest. Detailed statutory conditions, return fields and correction steps remain to be verified before applying that route. [HMRC royalty section](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/double-taxation-relief-for-companies).

Exact Gibraltar lodgement mechanics, claim limitation periods, Treaty Passport operation, individual/REIT procedures and treatment of inconsistent residence facts remain open. The directors' declaration must be supported by records and reviewed for the particular company; it is not a form of professional attestation of this Guide.

## Relief file and remaining verification

For each payment, retain: both parties and beneficial-owner evidence; residence analysis; payment date and classification; gross amount and domestic source tax; recipient-category proof, ownership chain and rights where relevant; any authority determination; permanent-establishment and independent-price analysis; principal-purpose assessment; and the actual relief application, authority response and repayment record. This is a recommended working-paper structure, not an official form list.

Before applying relief at payment or requesting repayment, verify the current domestic form, authorisation, evidence and deadline in the source territory. The company UK-interest route is partly documented above; other procedures, exact claim deadlines and penalties are not established by this Guide. [Employment and pensions](gi-uk-employment-pensions-treaty.md) are now separately drafted with remaining questions explicit. [Business profits and permanent establishments](gi-corporate-tax.md#uk-treaty-permanent-establishments-and-business-profits) are separately drafted. Domestic capital-gain computations and filing, full double-tax-credit coordination and other treaty topics remain separate work. See [domestic Gibraltar foreign-tax relief](gi-foreign-tax-relief.md) and [Spain residence agreement](gi-spain-tax-residence.md), which have different scope. Independent source review and professional attestation remain pending; this draft does not establish comprehensive Gibraltar coverage.

For the verified Gibraltar MAP submission route and its distinction from domestic appeals and collection postponement, see [assessment appeals and MAP](gi-income-tax-assessment-appeals.md#treaty-map-a-separate-submission-and-collection-decision).
