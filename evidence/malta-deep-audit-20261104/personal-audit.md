# Malta personal tax, payroll, social security and orchestrator audit

Read date: **4 November 2026**. Public repository reviewed at **ab692cc2ef1c6306de9435b63448294d30fc4052**. READ-ONLY audit: no Guide changes, commits, PRs or external messages. All repository line references below refer to that revision, under `skills/`.

## Conclusion

**Do not use the two Malta orchestrators to prepare a filing package in their present form.** They call the rental-income TA24 the self-employed income tax return, prescribe unsupported form boxes, use the wrong provisional-tax benchmark year and repeatedly use quarterly social-security scheduling. The corrected underlying Guides do not make this contradictory workflow safe. The moving-to-Malta corridor also contains a material remittance-basis eligibility error. The main personal-tax Guides are substantially better but are not all-clear: there is a real statute/MTCA rate-table discrepancy, a missed operative filing extension, some unqualified crypto income-recognition instructions, and readily resolvable source gaps.

This is a deep findings audit of nine assigned files, **not certification that every sentence is legally correct**. It separates confirmed defects, authority discrepancies and remaining unverified scopes. Sources were actually fetched using the web tool during this audit; an old access refusal is not evidence of present unavailability.

## Confirmed findings and unsafe instructions

**P1-A. Wrong annual return and invented boxes.** `orchestrator/mt-freelance-intake.md` lines 103, 145, 200–203, 260–263, 289, 347, 502, 508 and `orchestrator/mt-return-assembly.md` lines 3, 50–53, 59–90, 124, 143–171, 223–226, 266–272, 282, 297 call TA24 the self-employed annual return. Assembly line 51 says “TA24 self-employed return (annual)”. S1 identifies TA24 as the rental final-tax form. The prescribed TA24 Boxes 1–40, business expenses, contribution deduction and progressive tax computation do not belong to the described form. This is a broken filing workflow, not merely a naming issue. Hold both orchestrators; use the actual year-specific personal income tax return and verify each real field before rebuilding.

**P1-B. Wrong provisional-tax benchmark year.** Assembly lines 53, 88–90, 185 and 189 use the latest year's final liability for next year's PT and imply first business year 2025 means first PT in 2026. Intake line 263 says “Expected 2025 provisional tax (based on 2024): EUR 4,200 -- matches payments”. S2/S3 establish the ordinary two-year basis-year lag: 2026 PT uses basis 2024 / YA2025; 2025 PT uses basis 2023 / YA2024. A new business starting in 2025 without prior benchmark liability ordinarily first pays PT in 2027. Check an actual notice and existing taxable history. There is also a self-contained arithmetic error: intake's 800 + 1,200 + 2,000 payments total 4,000, not its asserted 4,200. Use the correct PT Guide rather than this fallback.

**P1-C. Wrong Class 2 payment frequency.** Intake lines 40, 213, 250–251 describe quarterly payments; line 251 says “Class 2 quarterly payments: EUR 470.34 x 4 = EUR 1,881.36 (SA minimum)”. Assembly lines 52, 179, 231, 235, 239 repeat quarterly and Q1/Q3/Q4 labels. S4 article 11 and S19 establish three four-monthly periods ending April, August and December. Four observed bank payments do not prove annual liability settled. The 470.34 figure is also not tied to an official 2025 table. Reconcile actual contribution weeks and period bills. Multiplication is correct; its legal scheduling assumption is not.

**P1-D. False remittance-basis eligibility.** `malta/moving-to-malta-tax-residence.md` line 113 says “Long-term residents keep the basis”; lines 22 and 37 describe any resident non-dom as eligible without the exceptions. S7 section 1.2 and S8 article 4 exclude statutory long-term residents and holders of the specifically defined permanent residence certificate/card. Spending many years in Malta is different from obtaining that legal status. MPRP immigration status must not automatically be equated with the separate statutory exclusion. The current statement can wrongly exclude worldwide income and gains. Add the actual status and spouse tests, then route to the detailed non-dom Guide.

**P1-E. Missed operative filing AND payment extension.** `malta-income-tax.md` lines 22, 356, 377; `mt-non-dom.md` line 290; `mt-crypto-tax.md` line 332; `mt-estimated-tax.md` line 217; assembly line 223 use 30 June 2026, or make the applicable extension an unknown online-filing possibility. S10 sets 31 July 2026 for both manual/electronic YA2026 individual returns AND tax due. Retain the general statutory 30 June rule but overlay the actual year-specific notice. Do not start July 2026 interest or penalties from the earlier date. The hypothetical eight-month late-return example remains in the same 50-euro penalty band after a roughly one-month shift, but its origin date is still wrong.

**P1-F. Crypto is incorrectly made a universal cash-receipt basis.** `mt-crypto-tax.md` lines 51, 137–142, 284–285, 343 prescribe income when received, including business fees; line 137 labels this “Statute”. Checklist line 343 commands that mining, staking, lending, airdrops and crypto pay are valued at receipt and taxed, even though its own airdrop row admits some gifts are not income. S12 preserves ordinary recognition rules. Crypto payment does not automatically defer accrued business fees until wallet receipt. Separate valuation at a taxable event from recognition and classification. Keep uncertain DeFi heads expressly held; this is not a conclusion that staking is generally untaxed.

**P2-G. Nomad foreign-tax reporting exemption lacks conditions.** `mt-non-dom.md` line 248 says foreign tax of at least 10%, proved to Residency Malta Agency, means the income need not be reported. S13 section 3 requires reporting authorised-work income if there is other chargeable income/gains OR joint computation with a spouse; it also specifies the documentation deadline of 31 March following the basis year. Qualify the hypothetical variant and include those conditions. Its core 10% arithmetic and twelve-month period are correct.

**P2-H. Unsupported categorical home-office denial; flawed reconciliation.** Intake line 308 says a shared space “does not qualify for a home office deduction under Article 14”. S8's general expense test does not establish a separate-room prohibition. No such official administrative prohibition was found in this pass. Mark it unsupported rather than asserting a statutory exclusion. Assembly lines 65–69 and 266 demand business income equal VAT turnover within one euro, despite admitting timing, exempt and foreign-income differences. Use an explained reconciliation bridge, not forced equality. Formal VAT analysis belongs to the VAT auditor.

**P2-I. Personal Class 2 deduction has no demonstrated authority.** Assembly lines 52, 78, 166, 181, 272, 282 instruct personal contributions into a TA24 Box 20 deduction. This contradicts the current income-tax Guide's memo-only treatment. S8's general deduction framework did not yield a specific personal Class 2 deduction. Important limitation: absence of an identified provision is weaker evidence than an express authority disallowance; this pass did not fetch an express MTCA personal-Class-2 disallowance statement. Treat the assembly instruction as unsupported and unsafe. Keep the existing memo-only computation unless a positive legal basis is established. Do not confuse an employer's staff contribution costs with a sole trader's own contributions.

**P2-J. Stale dependency and wrong citation.** Assembly lines 53, 184, 310 call `mt-estimated-tax` a Q4 stub, but it is version 2.1 with 356 lines and substantive rules/tests. The fallback points to personal-tax step 5, which now adds other income rather than computing PT. Moving Guide line 207 identifies article 56(1)(c) as the 5,000-euro minimum provision; the relevant provision is 56(27). Article 56(1)(c) is the non-resident scale.

**P2-K. Corridor oversimplifies exceptions.** Moving Guide lines 69–70 call HSI the only route taking local salary out of progressive rates, but lines 75–76 immediately describe the nomad exception. Remove that exclusivity. Lines 124 and 173 summarise all sales within five years as 5%, omitting project/development/related-party conditions which the detailed personal-tax Guide itself recognises. This is an incompleteness finding, not a full certification of property-transfer rules. Use concise conditional routing rather than an automatic property exit cost.

## Authority discrepancies and resolvable holds

**D1. 10,270 versus 10,275 is a real source conflict.** Personal-tax lines 69–72, 368 and payroll lines 89–92, 204, 257 use 10,270 for the one-child parent 35% subtraction. S8 still prints that figure. S9's amended 13 April 2026 communication and current official table print 10,275. At income 70,000, the two results are 14,230 and 14,225. Both arithmetic results are correct for their stated input. Do not claim legislation was amended from the administrative table alone, or describe 10,275 as merely a software convention. Record both sources and hold the implementation choice for legal/authority resolution.

**D2. Current contribution tables are accessible.** Payroll lines 23, 129, 135, 208–212 and SSC lines 26–28, 59, 124–141 honestly refuse current figures, but leave essential 2026 payroll incomplete. S5/S6 now provide annual tables. Class 2 SA is 36.18, SC 73.56 / 83.89 by birth cohort; Class 1 B is 22.94, maxima 49.04 / 55.93, maternity maxima 1.47 / 1.68. The complete update must also cover band boundaries, categories and contribution weeks. Do not reuse 2024 amounts. SSC line 73's old statutory reduced student provision has no age cap; S5's current official footnote specifies under 24. Record that eligibility discrepancy explicitly. S19 confirms first-year SA operational payments, narrowing SSC line 154's unknown. S19 also contains a stale 1% late-charge paragraph, which must not override the 2026 Act.

**D3. Administrative guidance closes several routine gaps.** Non-dom lines 128, 130, 197 hold foreign-income and DTR questions; S7 section 6 explains the couple threshold and reduction by DTR. Separate assessment details still need precision. Moving line 101 asserts a general “worst-first” mixed-account ordering rule. S7 section 5.4 instead establishes proof of capital and a presumption for ordinary living remittances; it does not create a universal worst-first rule. Moving line 215 says the HSI increase date is unsettled, but S14 expressly specifies 2031, then 2036. Payroll lines 159–165 refuse bonus figures, which S17 publishes; distinguish them from weekly COLA. Crypto line 25 describes unavailable guidelines, but S12 fetched successfully. Reading that 2018 guidance does not settle every modern DeFi/NFT issue.
